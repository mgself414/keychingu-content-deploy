📌 Entry · Visa · Trip Prep

Do I need a visa to enter Korea?
Many nationalities can enter visa-free; how long you can stay depends on your passport. The K-ETA (Electronic Travel Authorization) is temporarily waived from January 1 to December 31, 2026 (Ministry of Justice K-ETA notice). To see whether the waiver covers you, scan your passport on the k-eta.go.kr application screen. If it does not, apply — the fee is 10,000 KRW and review usually takes up to 72 hours, so apply a few days before you fly. If you do not hold a K-ETA, submit the free e-Arrival Card at e-arrivalcard.go.kr from 3 days before arrival.
When is the best season to travel?
Spring (April) + fall (October) are the top picks — cherry blossoms, autumn foliage, and pleasant weather. Summer (Jun–Aug) brings monsoon and heat; winter (Dec–Feb) is below freezing but offers illuminations and skiing. Avoid: the long holidays (early May, Chuseok, early October), when flights and hotels fill up early and prices rise.
How much money should I exchange?
Most places take cards — carry a little cash for street stalls and markets. City exchange offices (Myeongdong, Hongdae) usually give better rates than the airport, so exchange only a little there. Foreign credit cards (VISA/Master) are accepted almost everywhere; AMEX less so.

🚇 Transit · Getting Around

How do I get from the airport to the city?
Incheon Airport (ICN) → Seoul Station: AREX express (43 min, 13,000 KRW reserved ahead; 18,500 KRW walk-up) or all-stop (about 59 min, 4,750 KRW with T-money). Gimpo Airport (GMP) connects directly to Lines 5 and 9. With heavy luggage, take an airport limousine bus (17,000 KRW) or a KakaoT taxi (the fare depends on traffic and time of day). More: Incheon Airport City-Transfer Full Guide.
How do I ride the subway and buses?
A rechargeable T-money card (4,000 KRW at convenience stores) is the easiest — usable on subways, buses, taxis, and in convenience stores. For apps, use Naver Map or KakaoMap. Google Maps shows places, but its transit routing is unreliable. Top it up at station machines or convenience stores — have some cash, as card top-ups are not always possible.
How do I call a taxi?
The KakaoT app is the standard (Korea's Uber). It has an English UI; register a card or pay cash. Hailing on the street is fine too — all metered. No tipping culture. Seoul late-night surcharge: 20% (10–11 p.m. and 2–4 a.m.), 40% (11 p.m.–2 a.m.).
How do I travel between cities?
The KTX high-speed rail is the standard — Seoul → Busan in about 2h30 (54,400 KRW, standard class). Since September 2026, SRT trains run under the KTX name, so trains from Suseo are KTX too. Express buses cost less than KTX but take longer. Book at letskorail.com or the Korail app.

📱 Connectivity · Payments · Practical

How do I get internet / a SIM?
Three options: ① eSIM — usually the best value; activate before landing. ② Physical SIM — from an airport booth or city store. ③ Pocket Wi-Fi — handy for groups. "Unlimited" plans often slow down after a daily data cap — check the plan. More: SIM · eSIM · Roaming Guide.
How does food delivery work in Korea?
Baemin and Coupang Eats are the main delivery apps and can deliver to hotels and Airbnbs. Some delivery apps require Korean SMS verification — ask your hotel for help or use the app's international version. No tipping. A delivery fee is usually added.
I don't speak Korean — is that okay?
Tourist hotspots (Hongdae, Gangnam, Myeongdong, Itaewon) have English menus and basic English is fine. At local restaurants, use the Papago translation app or point at a photo menu. Subway and airport signs include English. In emergencies, call 1330 (24h tourist info in English) or 112 (police).
How do payments work at Korean stores?
VISA/Master credit cards work almost everywhere. Some street stalls and traditional markets are cash-only. Mobile wallets such as Apple Pay work only at some shops — always carry a physical card. No tipping. Prices include VAT.

🍴 Food · Restaurants · Culture

Which Korean foods are must-tries?
Top 5 for foreigners: ① Korean BBQ ② kimchi jjigae ③ bibimbap ④ Korean fried chicken + beer ⑤ tteokbokki. Bonus: naengmyeon (summer), samgyetang, ganjang gejang, street food (bungeoppang, hotteok), Korean desserts (bingsu, yakgwa). More: Korean Food Essentials.
Do I need restaurant reservations?
Fine dining, hotspot cafes, and popular BBQ places require reservations. Regular restaurants are walk-in. Reservation app: Catch Table is the standard (partial English UI, needs a Korean number). Without a Korean number, join the tablet waiting list at the door or ask your hotel to book by phone.
Are there vegan / halal / gluten-free restaurants?
Itaewon, Hongdae, and Yeonnam-dong have many vegan, halal, and gluten-free restaurants. Apps: Happy Cow (vegan), Crescent Rating (halal). Note that Korean dishes commonly use anchovy or shrimp stock, so always confirm in advance. More: Vegan · Halal Guide.
Any Korean etiquette I should know?
① Elders eat first / ② pour drinks with two hands / ③ take off shoes (hanok, jjimjilbang, some restaurants) / ④ keep right and stay quiet on the subway / ⑤ ask before taking photos / ⑥ no tipping / ⑦ no smoking on streets outside designated zones.

🛍 Shopping · Experiences

How do I get a tax refund?
Purchases of 15,000 KRW or more per store with a foreign passport qualify — get an instant in-store refund or an airport refund. Look for the Tax Free sign and show your passport. In-store refunds are capped at 1,000,000 KRW per purchase, 5,000,000 KRW per trip. Larger refunds are handled at the airport kiosk after security. Keep the goods unused until you leave.
Where can I buy K-pop / idol goods?
Hongdae, Myeongdong and Gangnam have K-pop and character goods shops — check that a store is open before you go. For albums and photocards, try large bookstores and record shops. More: K-pop Fan Guide.
Where can I try on hanbok?
Hanbok rental shops near Gyeongbokgung and Changdeokgung. Wearing hanbok (jacket plus skirt or trousers) gets you free entry to Gyeongbokgung. Bukchon Hanok Village and Insadong are the classic photo spots. More: Hanbok Experience Guide.
What about jjimjilbang / spa experiences?
Pick a large jjimjilbang and check that it is open, and its hours, before you go. Some run 24 hours — expect sheep-head towels, sikhye, sleeping rooms, sauna rooms, and baths. More: Complete Jjimjilbang Guide.

🛟 Emergency · Practical

Emergencies · tourist information?
112 (police) / 119 (fire·ambulance, English available) / 1330 (24h tourist info in English, Chinese, Japanese). Save your embassy's emergency contact before departure, and keep your travel insurance details handy.
