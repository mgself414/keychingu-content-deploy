FAQ — Frequently Asked Questions

The 20 questions foreign travelers ask most, before and after a Seoul trip

📌 Entry · Visa · Trip Prep

Do I need a visa to enter Korea?
Most of the US, Japan, EU, and Southeast Asia get a 90-day visa-free stay. But since 2021 a K-ETA (Electronic Travel Authorization) must be applied for in advance. Apply up to 72 hours before departure at k-eta.go.kr (about 10,000 KRW, valid 2 years). Since 2024 some countries have a temporary exemption in effect, so always check the latest info.
When is the best season to travel?
Spring (April) + fall (October) are the overwhelming top picks — cherry blossoms, autumn foliage, and pleasant weather. Summer (Jun–Aug) brings monsoon and heat; winter (Dec–Feb) is below freezing but offers illuminations and skiing. Avoid: the golden holidays (early May, Chuseok in September, early October) when flights and hotels cost 2–3× more.
How much money should I exchange?
50,000–100,000 KRW a day in cash is plenty — most places take cards + mobile pay. Exchange offices in Myeongdong and Insadong offer the best rates (3–5% better than the airport). Foreign credit cards (VISA/Master) are accepted almost everywhere; AMEX is accepted at limited places.

🚇 Transit · Getting Around

How do I get from the airport to the city?
Incheon Airport (ICN) → Seoul Station: AREX express (43 min, 9,500 KRW) or all-stop (58 min, 4,750 KRW). Gimpo Airport (GMP) connects directly to Lines 5 and 9 — 30 min / 1,500 KRW to the city. With heavy luggage, take a KAL limousine (17,000 KRW) or KakaoT (about 80,000 KRW call taxi). More: Incheon Airport City-Transfer Full Guide.
How do I ride the subway and buses?
A rechargeable T-money card (4,000 KRW at convenience stores) is the easiest — usable on subways, buses, taxis, and in stores. For apps, use Naver Map + Subway Korea. Google Maps works for walking/driving but its transit routing is unreliable. Besides T-money, contactless credit cards and Samsung/Apple Pay work at some gates.
How do I call a taxi?
The KakaoT app is the standard (Korea's Uber). It has an English UI; register a card or pay cash. Hailing on the street is fine too — all metered. International tourist taxis cost about 20% more than regular ones. No tipping culture. A 20% late-night surcharge applies (midnight–4 a.m.).
How do I travel between cities?
The KTX high-speed rail is the standard — Seoul → Busan in 2h30 (59,800 KRW). SRT is a private operator like KTX (departs Suseo, slightly cheaper). Express buses cost about half of KTX but take 1.5× as long. Book at letskorail.com (English supported).

📱 Connectivity · Payments · Practical

How do I get internet / a SIM?
Three options: ① Prepaid SIM — unlimited 4G / 5–30 days / 27,500–55,000 KRW (airport booth or pre-book). ② eSIM — compatible phones only, instant QR activation. ③ Pocket Wi-Fi — 4,500 KRW/day, good for groups. More: SIM · eSIM · Roaming Guide.
How does food delivery work in Korea?
Baemin is the standard (English UI available) — 24-hour delivery to hotels and Airbnbs. Coupang Eats is faster but mostly Korean. Baemin is more foreigner-friendly. No tipping. A 2,000–4,000 KRW delivery fee applies.
I don't speak Korean — is that okay?
Tourist hotspots (Hongdae, Gangnam, Myeongdong, Itaewon) have English menus and basic English is fine. At local restaurants, use the Papago translation app or point at a photo menu. Subway and airport signage is 100% in English. In emergencies, call 1330 (24h tourist info in English) or 112 (police, English).
How do payments work at Korean stores?
VISA/Master credit cards are accepted at 95% of stores. Some street stalls and traditional markets are cash-only. Samsung Pay, Apple Pay, Kakao Pay require a Korean phone number + Korean card (hard for foreigners). No tipping. Prices include VAT.

🍴 Food · Restaurants · Culture

Which Korean foods are must-tries?
Top 5 for foreigners: ① Korean BBQ ② kimchi jjigae ③ bibimbap ④ Korean fried chicken + beer ⑤ tteokbokki. Bonus: naengmyeon (summer), samgyetang, ganjang gejang, street food (bungeoppang, hotteok), Korean desserts (bingsu, yakgwa). More: Korean Food Essentials.
Do I need restaurant reservations?
Fine dining, hotspot cafes, and popular BBQ places require reservations. Regular restaurants are walk-in. Reservation app: Catch Table is the standard (partial English UI, needs a Korean number). Without a Korean number, book via your hotel concierge or Instagram DM.
Are there vegan / halal / gluten-free restaurants?
Itaewon, Hongdae, and Yeonnam-dong have many vegan, halal, and gluten-free restaurants. Apps: Happy Cow (vegan), Crescent Rating (halal). Note that Korean dishes commonly use anchovy or shrimp stock, so always confirm in advance. More: Vegan · Halal Guide.
Any Korean etiquette I should know?
① Elders eat first / ② pour drinks with two hands / ③ take off shoes (hanok, jjimjilbang, some restaurants) / ④ keep right and stay quiet on the subway / ⑤ ask before taking photos / ⑥ no tipping / ⑦ no smoking on streets outside designated zones.

🛍 Shopping · Experiences

How do I get a tax refund?
On purchases over 30,000 KRW with a foreign passport, you can get an instant in-store refund or an airport refund. Look for Global Blue / Global Tax Free stores. Show your passport at checkout → get a receipt + refund form. Airport refunds are at the refund counter past the departure gates. Just ask staff for "Tax Free."
Where can I buy K-pop / idol goods?
Hongdae (YG/SM HQ, KT&G Sangsangmadang) + Gangnam (SM Town COEX) + Myeongdong (Line Friends, Kakao Friends). For albums and photocards, try Hyundai Department Store Yeongdeungpo in Sinchon, or YES24 / Kyobo Bookstore Gangnam. More: K-pop Fan Guide.
Where can I try on hanbok?
Hanbok rentals at the entrances of Gyeongbokgung and Changdeokgung (about 15,000–30,000 KRW / 4 hours). Wearing hanbok gets you free palace entry. Bukchon Hanok Village and Insadong are the classic photo spots. More: Hanbok Experience Guide.
What about jjimjilbang / spa experiences?
Dragon Hill Spa (Yongsan), Spa Land (Jamsil), Hansol Jjimjilbang (Hongdae) are foreigner-friendly. Open 24 hours, entry 12,000–25,000 KRW — sheep-head towels, sikhye, sleeping rooms, sauna rooms, and pools. More: Complete Jjimjilbang Guide.

🛟 Emergency · Practical

Emergencies · tourist information?
112 (police, English available) / 119 (fire·ambulance) / 1330 (24h tourist info in English, Chinese, Japanese). Save your embassy's emergency contact before departure. Korea's emergency care is very fast and can process foreign medical insurance.
