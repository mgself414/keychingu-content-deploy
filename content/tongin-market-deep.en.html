Seochon Guide: Tongin Market and Dosirak Cafe, Seochon Village, Suseongdong Valley, Sajik Park and Sajikdan Altar, Seochon Lounge

Subtitle: Tongin Market (shops 07:00~21:00, Dosirak Cafe 11:00~15:00) · Seochon Village (always open, free) · Suseongdong Valley (always open, free) · Sajik Park (always open) · Sajikdan Altar (always open, free) · Seochon Lounge (free, closed Mondays and legal public holidays) — confirmed via Korea Tourism Organization tourism data (TourAPI)

🎯 How to Use This Guide

This guide gathers places to go in Seochon, west of Gyeongbokgung Palace (경복궁). They are Tongin Market (통인시장) and its Dosirak Cafe (도시락카페), which began in 1941, Seochon Village (서촌마을), where alleys and hanok remain, Suseongdong Valley (수성동계곡), which comes down from Inwangsan Mountain (인왕산), Sajik Park (사직공원), with the Joseon Sajikdan Altar (사직단), and the public hanok Seochon Lounge (서촌라운지). Apart from Tongin Market (varies by shop) and Seochon Lounge, Seochon Village, Suseongdong Valley, Sajik Park and Sajikdan Altar are registered as open every day of the year and always open. For the Bukchon and Ikseon-dong side, see the Jongno area guides, and to see several markets at once, see the #604 Seoul Traditional Market Beginner's Guide.

The operating details are copied from values registered in the Korea Tourism Organization tourism data (TourAPI). This guide covers only places registered in the Korea Tourism Organization tourism data.

📍 Market — Tongin Market

Tongin Market (통인시장) [Jahamun-ro 15-gil 18 (자하문로15길 18), Jongno-gu (종로구), ☎ 0507-1378-0940] began in June 1941 as a public market built for Japanese residents near Hyoja-dong (효자동). After the Korean War (6·25전쟁), as the population of Seochon grew, street stalls and shops appeared around the public market and it became a market. After being registered as a certified market in 2005, it was equipped with modernized facilities, and in 2010 it was selected as a "Seoul-style Cultural Market" by the Seoul Metropolitan Government and Jongno-gu.

According to the Korea Tourism Organization, there are more than 70 shops, and food businesses such as restaurants and side-dish shops are the most numerous. Next are vegetable, fruit, fish and meat shops, and there are also manufactured goods such as underwear and clothing repair shops. Since January 2012 it has run the Dosirak Cafe (도시락카페) 'Tong (通)'.

  • Opening hours: Market shops 07:00~21:00, Dosirak Cafe 11:00~15:00.
  • Closed days: Vary by shop. Parking: Available.

📍 Alleys and Valley — Seochon Village and Suseongdong Valley

Seochon Village (서촌마을) [Pirun-daero 45 (필운대로 45), Jongno-gu (종로구), ☎ Bukchon Village Information Center (북촌마을안내소) 02-2148-4161] is the name for the village west of Gyeongbokgung Palace (경복궁). Old shops, hanok and maze-like alleys remain, and there are pastel-colored murals and paintings here and there in the alleys. It is also described as known as a filming location for movies, dramas and music videos.

  • Opening hours: Always open. Closed days: Open every day of the year. Fee: Free. Parking: Not available.

Suseongdong Valley (수성동계곡) [Ogin-dong (옥인동), Jongno-gu (종로구), ☎ 02-2148-2844] is a valley that comes down from Inwangsan Mountain (인왕산) and joins Cheonggyecheon Stream (청계천). The sound of the water was loud and clear, so in the Joseon period this neighborhood is said to have been called Suseong-dong (水聲洞). It is included in the 'Jangdong Palgyeongcheop' (장동팔경첩), the album in which Gyeomjae Jeong Seon (겸재 정선) painted 8 scenic spots of Bugaksan Mountain (북악산) and Inwangsan Mountain (인왕산), and it was introduced as a scenic spot in Joseon-period geography books. It lost its scenery when the Ogin Model Apartment (옥인시범아파트) was built in 1971, but 40 years later the apartment was torn down and the area was designated a cultural heritage protection zone, and it regained its original appearance.

  • Opening hours: Always open. Closed days: Open every day of the year. Fee: Free. Parking: Not available.

📍 Altar of Joseon — Sajik Park and Sajikdan Altar

Sajik Park (사직공원) [Sajik-ro 89 (사직로 89), Jongno-gu (종로구), ☎ Jongno-gu Office Park Creation Team (종로구청 공원조성팀) 02-2148-2836] is a park on the southeastern foot of Inwangsan Mountain (인왕산), with an area of 188,710㎡. At its center is the Sajikdan Altar (사직단), first built in 1395 by King Taejo Yi Seong-gye (태조 이성계) of Joseon together with Jongmyo Shrine (종묘). It has a children's playground, statues of Yulgok Yi I (율곡 이이) and Shin Saimdang (신사임당), the archery range Hwanghakjeong (황학정), and the Seoul City Children's Library (시립 어린이 도서관). It is described as reaching the Inwangsan Mountain hiking trail if you walk up the Inwangsan path beside the park for about 5 minutes.

  • Opening hours: Always open. Closed days: Open every day of the year. Parking: 300 won per 5 minutes.

Sajikdan Altar (사직단) [Sajik-ro 89 (사직로 89), Jongno-gu (종로구), ☎ 02-739-7205] is an altar where rites were held for the gods of the land and the grain. While Jongmyo Shrine (종묘) enshrines the spirit tablets of kings and queens, Sajik enshrines the god of the land and the god of grain. With Gyeongbokgung Palace (경복궁) in the middle, Jongmyo is on the left and Sajik on the right. In the center is not a building but an empty altar, and there are 8 hongsalmun gates (홍살문), though even a royal tomb has only one. In the Joseon period only the king, the royal family and people with related duties could enter.

  • Opening hours: Always open. Closed days: Open every day of the year. Fee: Free. Parking: Not available.

📍 Resting in a Hanok — Seochon Lounge

Seochon Lounge (서촌라운지) [Pirun-daero 27-4 (필운대로 27-4), Jongno-gu (종로구), ☎ 02-736-7909] is a Seoul public hanok (서울 공공한옥). It is open to both Seoul citizens and people visiting Korea, and introduces hanok living culture. It has domestic and foreign books on hanok design and K-crafts, and its introduction says it hopes the visit will be a time to savor a new everyday life in Seoul's hanok with a cup of tea.

  • Opening hours: Tue~Thu and Sat~Sun 11:00~19:00, Fri 12:00~20:00.
  • Closed days: Every Monday, legal public holidays. Fee: Free.

⚠ 3 Things to Know Before You Go

  1. The Tongin Market Dosirak Cafe is open only 11:00~15:00 — the market shops are open 07:00~21:00.
  2. Seochon Lounge is closed on Mondays and legal public holidays — on Fridays it is open 12:00~20:00.
  3. Parking is not available at Seochon Village, Suseongdong Valley and Sajikdan Altar — Tongin Market and Sajik Park are registered as having parking.

⚙ FAQ 2

Q. When is the Tongin Market Dosirak Cafe open?

A. According to the Korea Tourism Organization registration, it is 11:00~15:00. It has been in operation since January 2012.

Q. Which places are free?

A. Seochon Village, Suseongdong Valley, Sajikdan Altar and Seochon Lounge are registered as free.
