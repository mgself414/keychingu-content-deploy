Korea Payment Survival Manual for Chinese-Speaking Travelers — Alipay Works, WeChat Doesn't, UnionPay Needs Prep First

Subtitle: Korea is a card paradise, but Chinese-speaking travelers' wallets run on different rules. Where Alipay works and where it doesn't, activating UnionPay for overseas use before you fly, why you can't rely on WeChat Pay, and your ATM/cash backup plan — one situational payment ladder.

ISSUE · #1333 · 2026-08-24

🎯 The Frame — "Card Paradise" and "My App" Are Two Different Things

Cards work almost anywhere in Korea. The problem is that Chinese-speaking travelers' main payment tools — Alipay, WeChat Pay, and UnionPay — each run on their own rules. The bottom line, one method at a time:

Method The reality in Korea
Alipay / AlipayHK Accepted at 120,000+ retail merchants — convenience stores, Olive Young (올리브영), department stores, and duty-free shops are all well covered. But transit (subway/bus) doesn't work
UnionPay The widest card acceptance of any option, plus ATM withdrawals — but overseas-use activation before departure is mandatory
WeChat Pay Limited acceptance — don't treat it as your primary method. Coverage tops out at some duty-free shops and large retailers
Cash (KRW) The last safety net for markets, old-school eateries, and small purchases — carry small bills

① Two Things to Prep Before You Fly — Skip These and You'll Get Stuck at the Airport

1. Activate UnionPay overseas use: A China-issued UnionPay card needs 境外用卡 (overseas card service) switched on in your banking app before it will pay or withdraw in Korea. Check your daily limits (payment and withdrawal) at the same time. It's a 5-minute fix before you leave — but once you're in Korea, even a call to your bank often can't undo it.

2. Check your Alipay balance and real-name verification: overseas payments only go smoothly on accounts with real-name verification completed. Hong Kong/Macau wallets like AlipayHK also work at the same merchants through the Alipay+ network.

② Alipay — Where It Works, Where It Doesn't

  • Where it works: convenience stores like CU and GS25, Olive Young, Starbucks, Shinsegae Department Store (신세계백화점), Lotte Duty Free (롯데면세점), and most of the tourist spending circuit. Say "Alipay" at the register and show your QR code — the clerk scans it (clerk-scan is the default flow in Korea).
  • Where it doesn't: subway turnstiles and bus card readers — transit is its own separate world. You'll need a T-money (티머니) transit card instead (#238). Plenty of market stalls and old-school eateries don't accept Alipay either.
  • If a payment fails: switching between clerk-scan (you show your QR) and customer-scan (you scan the store's QR) solves it about half the time.

③ UnionPay — Widest Acceptance, Two Traps

  • UnionPay acceptance in Korea runs into the millions of merchants — anywhere with a card terminal, payment typically goes through by PIN entry.
  • ATM withdrawals: you can withdraw KRW at UnionPay-marked ATMs in airports, subway stations, and convenience stores — some machines even offer a Chinese-language screen. Fees stack in two layers (your issuing bank plus the Korean ATM operator) — debit cards are generally cheaper.
  • Trap 1: overseas use not activated — solved in ① above.
  • Trap 2: DCC prompts at the ATM (billed in yuan) — if the screen shows a yuan amount and nudges you to confirm it, choose KRW instead; it works out cheaper. Full detail in the ATM deep dive, #1286.

④ Why You Can't Rely on WeChat Pay — Plus the Backup Ladder

WeChat Pay's merchant rollout in Korea has been slow, so "Alipay works but WeChat doesn't" is a common situation. Show up trusting your WeChat balance alone and you'll get stuck at the first convenience store. The real-world ladder goes like this:

1. Alipay QR → 2. UnionPay card → 3. Cash → (if none of those work) 4. A companion's Visa/Mastercard, or WOWPASS (#527)

⑤ Situational Payment Ladder — One-Page Summary

Situation First choice Backup
Convenience stores, Olive Young, department stores Alipay UnionPay card
Restaurants, cafes UnionPay card Alipay, cash
Traditional markets, old-school eateries, street stalls Cash Some accept cards
Subway, bus T-money (티머니) transit card (#238) Cash (a growing number of bus routes don't take cash — #1301)

Duty-free shops Alipay, UnionPay —
  • For cash, carry small bills, mainly ₩10,000 (~$7.50) notes — market vendors aren't thrilled about breaking a ₩50,000 (~$37.50) bill for change.
  • Tax refund: UnionPay cards are also linked for refund payouts — the process is covered in #1304.
