Rainy Day Seoul Indoor Museums Guide: National Palace Museum of Korea, National Folk Museum of Korea and National Children's Museum, National Museum of Korean Contemporary History, Seoul Museum of History, Seoul Museum of Craft Art, COEX Aquarium

Subtitle: National Palace Museum of Korea (free, open until 21:00 on Saturdays and the last Wednesday of each month) · National Folk Museum of Korea (free, Nov~Feb 09:00~17:00) · National Museum of Korean Contemporary History (free, open until 21:00 on Wednesdays and Saturdays) · Seoul Museum of History (free, closed Mondays, open if a holiday) · Seoul Museum of Craft Art (free, closed Mondays) · COEX Aquarium (adults 35,000 won, open year-round) — confirmed via Korea Tourism Organization tourism data (TourAPI)

🎯 How to Use This Guide

This guide gathers museums and an aquarium in Seoul where you can spend a rainy day indoors. They are the National Palace Museum of Korea (국립고궁박물관), linked with Gyeongbokgung (경복궁), the National Folk Museum of Korea (국립민속박물관) inside Gyeongbokgung, the National Museum of Korean Contemporary History (대한민국역사박물관) and the Seoul Museum of History (서울역사박물관) in Jongno-gu (종로구), the Seoul Museum of Craft Art (서울공예박물관) in Anguk-dong (안국동), and the COEX Aquarium (코엑스 아쿠아리움) in Gangnam (강남). Except for the COEX Aquarium, all are registered as having free admission. Closed days differ from place to place, so it helps to look at the table below first. The National Museum of Korea (국립중앙박물관) in Yongsan is in the #710 Yongsan and Itaewon Guide.

The operating details are copied from values registered in the Korea Tourism Organization tourism data (TourAPI). This guide covers only places registered in the Korea Tourism Organization tourism data.

📍 Jongno-gu — National Palace Museum of Korea and National Folk Museum of Korea

National Palace Museum of Korea (국립고궁박물관) [Hyoja-ro 12 (효자로 12), Jongno-gu, ☎ 02-3701-7500] is a museum that displays royal relics of the Joseon (조선) dynasty and the Korean Empire. It started in 1992 as the Court Relics Exhibition Hall, took its present name on August 15, 2005, and fully opened in November 2007.

  • Opening hours: Mon~Fri and Sun 09:30~17:30 (last entry 17:00). Saturdays and the last Wednesday of each month 09:30~21:00 (last entry 20:30).
  • Closed days: Last Monday of each month (the day after the holiday ends if it is a public holiday), January 1, Seollal and Chuseok days.
  • Fee: Free.

National Folk Museum of Korea and National Children's Museum (국립민속박물관과 어린이박물관) [Samcheong-ro 37 (삼청로 37), Jongno-gu, ☎ 02-3704-3114] is a folk life history museum inside Gyeongbokgung. It holds and displays about 4,000 traditional folk life items, and has 3 permanent exhibition halls on the themes of a Korean's day, year and lifetime. Outdoors there are statues of the twelve zodiac animals, jangseung (장승), a yeonja-bang-a (연자방아), and a "Street of Memories" recreating an alley of the 1970s~80s. The Children's Museum is by advance reservation, and it is registered that each person can visit once a day.

  • Opening hours: National Folk Museum of Korea Mar~Oct 09:00~18:00 (last entry 17:00), Nov~Feb 09:00~17:00 (last entry 16:00). Children's Museum 09:30~16:50.
  • Closed days: January 1, Seollal and Chuseok days. Fee: Free. Parking: Available.

📍 Around Sejong-ro, Jongno-gu — National Museum of Korean Contemporary History and Seoul Museum of History

National Museum of Korean Contemporary History (대한민국역사박물관) [Sejong-daero 198 (세종대로 198), Jongno-gu, ☎ 0507-1414-9202] is a museum covering the history of the Republic of Korea from the late 19th-century port-opening period to today, and it opened on December 26, 2012. According to the Korea Tourism Organization, it is the first national modern and contemporary history museum. It has 4 exhibition halls by period and a children's museum.

  • Opening hours: Mon, Tue, Thu, Fri and Sun 10:00~18:00 (last entry 17:30), Wed and Sat 10:00~21:00 (last entry 20:30).
  • Closed days: January 1, Seollal and Chuseok days. Fee: Free.

Seoul Museum of History (서울역사박물관) [Saemunan-ro 55 (새문안로 55), Jongno-gu, ☎ 02-724-0274~6] is a museum that shows how Seoul came to be and arrived at the present. It has permanent exhibitions on the roots of Seoul, the lives of Seoul people and the change into modern Seoul, and an exhibition of donated relics. It is registered that at the 1st-floor information desk you can borrow audio guides in Korean, English, Chinese and Japanese for free by showing ID.

  • Opening hours: 09:00~21:00 (last entry 20:30). Thursdays 09:00~18:00 (last entry 17:30).
  • Closed days: Every Monday, January 1. If Monday is a holiday, it opens as normal.
  • Fee: Free. Parking: For vehicles under 20 people, 1,000 won for the first hour, then 400 won every 5 minutes. For vehicles of 20 people or more, 3,000 won for the first hour, then 800 won every 5 minutes.

📍 Anguk-dong — Seoul Museum of Craft Art

Seoul Museum of Craft Art (서울공예박물관) [Yulgok-ro 3-gil 4 (율곡로3길 4), Jongno-gu, ☎ 02-6450-7000] is a craft museum that opened in July 2021 after renovating 5 buildings of the former Pungmun Girls' High School (풍문여고). According to the Korea Tourism Organization, it is the first public craft museum in Korea. It holds about 20,000 craft objects and craft materials, from traditional to contemporary. The museum site is where the house of Grand Prince Yeongeung (영응대군), a son of Sejong (세종), and the Anguk-dong Detached Palace (안국동 별궁), built for the royal wedding of Sunjong (순종), once stood.

  • Opening hours: 10:00~18:00. Closed days: Every Monday, January 1. Fee: Free.

📍 Gangnam — COEX Aquarium

COEX Aquarium (코엑스 아쿠아리움) [Yeongdong-daero 513 (영동대로 513), Gangnam-gu (강남구), ☎ 02-700-7200] is a large aquarium with 183 exhibition tanks and 3,500 tons of water. In its 16 theme zones you can see about 40,000 aquatic creatures of more than 650 species. According to the Korea Tourism Organization, it is home to the most species and the largest number of sharks in Korea.

  • Opening hours: 10:00~20:00 (last entry 19:00). Closed days: Open year-round.
  • Fee: Adults 35,000 won, children 31,000 won, couple ticket 70,000 won, family ticket 101,000 won. Free for children under 36 months.

⚠ 3 Things to Know Before You Go

  1. The Seoul Museum of History and the Seoul Museum of Craft Art are closed on Mondays — the Seoul Museum of History opens as normal if Monday is a holiday. The National Palace Museum of Korea is closed on the last Monday of each month, and if that Monday is a public holiday it is closed the day after the holiday ends.
  2. Some places are open into the evening — the Seoul Museum of History is open until 21:00 except Thursdays, the National Museum of Korean Contemporary History until 21:00 on Wednesdays and Saturdays, and the National Palace Museum of Korea until 21:00 on Saturdays and the last Wednesday of each month.
  3. The National Children's Museum at the National Folk Museum of Korea is by advance reservation — each person can visit once a day.

⚙ FAQ 2

Q. Which places can I enter for free?

A. According to the Korea Tourism Organization registration, the National Palace Museum of Korea, the National Folk Museum of Korea, the National Museum of Korean Contemporary History, the Seoul Museum of History and the Seoul Museum of Craft Art are all free.

Q. Which places have foreign-language guidance?

A. It is registered that at the Seoul Museum of History you can borrow audio guides in Korean, English, Chinese and Japanese for free.
