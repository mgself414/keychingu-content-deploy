Why Euljiro Sub Now — Old-School·Printing·Newtro Hipster Cluster

Differentiation from #597 Jungbu Market (dried seafood wholesale since 1957) = the full Euljiro cluster (old-school eateries·printing alley·newtro hipster·MZ). Euljiro 4-ga and 3-ga are home to a printing/hardware/lighting cluster of old-school shops dating to the 1970s, plus an explosion of newtro hipster cafés·bars·pop-ups since 2020.

This matrix covers 3 old-school eatery picks + 3 printing/hardware alley picks + 3 newtro hipster picks + 3 nearby-cross picks = 12 picks, 4 axes.

Why Euljiro is special for foreigners: layers rarely seen elsewhere in Seoul coexist in one district. 1970s-era ironware, printing, and lighting alleys are still actively operating, and hipster bars and cafés have moved into the 2nd and 3rd floors of buildings inside those very alleys. It's a similar "old meets new" setup to London's Shoreditch or Tokyo's Koenji, but different in that real craftsmen are still working these alleys during the day.

Axis 1: Old-School Cluster In Depth (3 picks)

1. Euljimyeonok

Location: Euljiro 16-gil area, Jung-gu, Seoul (5-min walk from Euljiro 3-ga Station)

Hours: Mon–Sat 11:00–21:00 (closes early when sold out) / closed Sun and public holidays

Prices (2026): Naengmyeon ₩14,000 / mandu ₩8,000 / suyuk (boiled beef) from ₩30,000

Payment: Cards accepted

An old-school naengmyeon (cold noodle) restaurant tucked inside a Euljiro alley. Operating since the 1960s, its signature dish is water naengmyeon made with house-brewed broth. A line forms around 12–1pm, so arriving before 11:30am is recommended.

Foreigner fit: Naengmyeon resonates especially well with Japanese visitors used to broth-based dishes. No English menu — a translation app handles it. A single word, "naengmyeon," is enough to order.

Foreigner note: The naengmyeon noodles are very long. Cutting them with scissors is the Korean way to eat them, and staff will cut them on request.

Checking ahead recommended: Confirm hours and prices via Naver Map before visiting on-site.

2. Uraeok

Location: 62-29, Changgyeonggung-ro, Jung-gu, Seoul (8-min walk from Euljiro 4-ga Station)

Hours: 11:30–21:30 (break 15:00–17:00) / closed Mondays

Prices (2026): Pyongyang naengmyeon ₩15,000–16,000 / bulgogi from ₩25,000/person

Payment: Cards accepted

Opened in 1946, this is Seoul's flagship Pyongyang-style naengmyeon institution. It has a history of Michelin Guide Seoul Bib Gourmand selection, which makes it well known among English-speaking food enthusiasts. The broth combines beef, pheasant, and chicken, and the noodles carry a high buckwheat ratio for a distinctive mild, plain flavor.

Foreigner fit: ★★★★★. The Michelin recognition draws many English-speaking food travelers. Even with a wait, it's usually under 40 minutes.

Timing: Arriving right at opening (11:30) minimizes the wait. Sat–Sun 12–2pm can mean waits of an hour or more.

English support: Some staff have basic English. No picture menu — checking Google Maps review photos is recommended.

3. Hadongkwan

Location: 12, Myeongdong 9-gil, Jung-gu, Seoul (5-min walk from Euljiro Ipgu Station) — address checking recommended

Hours: 07:00–16:00 (closes early when sold out) / closed Sundays

Prices (2026): Gomtang (beef bone soup) ₩13,000–16,000 (single menu item)

Payment: Cash preferred, cards partly accepted (checking recommended)

One of Seoul's longest-running gomtang restaurants, open since 1939. The gomtang, served in an earthenware bowl, is made by simmering beef bones, offal, and brisket for a long time. It comes standard with white rice and kkakdugi/kimchi, and diners season it themselves with salt.

Foreigner fit: ★★★. Good for anyone who enjoys a mild, plain broth. The soup can contain strong-flavored offal meat, so visitors with dietary restrictions should confirm before ordering.

Timing: An early visit at 7–9am means no wait. Seats can run out after 12pm.

Note: No English menu. Since the menu is effectively one item (gomtang), the single word "gomtang" is all you need.

Axis 2: Printing·Hardware·Lighting Alleys In Depth (3 picks)

4. Euljiro Printing Alley

Location: Euljiro 3-ga to 4-ga area (3-min walk from Exit 4, Euljiro 3-ga Station)

Hours: Weekdays 09:00–18:00 / mostly closed weekends and holidays

Cost: Free to explore

Euljiro's printing alley was the center of Seoul's downtown printing industry in the 1970s–80s. Around 100 small print shops still operate today, many taking orders for business cards, stickers, and posters by the single unit. Inside the alley, DTP (digital editing) studios, bookbinders, and coating shops are mixed in together.

Foreigner fit: ★★★★★. A hidden gem for foreigners who want to experience Seoul's "living industrial alley." You can watch shops actually working up close during the day. Ask permission before photographing shop interiors.

Practical tip for foreigners: English communication is difficult. If you're just exploring, seeing the alley itself is enough. To order business cards in small quantities, search the Korean term "Euljiro inswae myeongham" (을지로 인쇄 명함) on Naver Map and call ahead to book.

5. Euljiro Hardware Alley

Location: Euljiro 4-ga to 5-ga area (5-min walk from Euljiro 4-ga Station)

Hours: Weekdays 09:00–18:00 / some closed on weekends

A district densely packed with retail and wholesale shops selling screws, bolts, power tools, and plumbing supplies. It's unusual in itself that a hardware cluster like this still survives in the middle of the city.

Foreigner fit: ★★★. Fewer foreign visitors come here for exploring compared to the printing alley. A unique experience for travelers interested in industrial/manufacturing infrastructure. Unless you're actually buying parts, a 20–30 minute pass-through visit is enough.

6. Euljiro Lighting Alley

Location: Near the Euljiro 3-ga to 4-ga printing alley (5-min walk from Euljiro 3-ga Station)

Hours: Weekdays 10:00–19:00 / some open on weekends

A concentrated district of shops selling fluorescent tubes, LEDs, interior lighting, and bulbs, from single units to bulk. At night, the sample lights the shops leave on create a bright, distinctive alley atmosphere.

Foreigner fit: ★★★★. Especially appealing to interior-design fans and photography travelers. Just looking at the unusual lighting displays is enough on its own, and some shops sell in small personal quantities too.

Timing: Visiting around 6–7pm, when the sample lights are lit, is best for photos.

Axis 3: Newtro Hipster In Depth (3 picks)

7. Euljiro Newtro Café·Bar Cluster

Location: Alleys around Euljiro 3-ga Station (within roughly 300m of Exits 2/4)

Hours: Cafés 13:00–22:00 / bars 18:00–02:00 (varies by shop)

Prices (2026): Coffee/drinks ₩8,000–15,000 / beer ₩9,000–14,000 / cocktails ₩14,000–22,000

Payment: Cards accepted at most places

Around 2018, cafés and bars began moving into buildings inside the printing and ironware alleys. The signature look keeps the old factory buildings' concrete walls and rusted metal structures, adding only lighting. Since the 2020s, heavy trend-media coverage has brought a fast-growing wave of foreign visitors.

Character of the district: The "Hipjiro" alleys near Euljiro 3-ga Station aren't a single signposted venue but a cluster scattered across several alleys. Searching the Korean term "Euljiro Hipjiro cafe" (을지로 힙지로 카페) on Naver Map or Google Maps to check what's currently open is recommended — openings and closures happen fast.

Foreigner fit: ★★★★★. Many spots have English-speaking staff. The Instagram-friendly spaces give high satisfaction to MZ-generation foreign travelers. A top pick for foreigners who want to experience Korea's "hip neighborhood."

Note: Specific shop names open and close quickly, so checking recent Google Maps reviews before visiting is recommended. This guide favors exploring the district over recommending individual shop names.

8. Euljiro Pop-Up Zone (3-4-ga Area)

Location: Euljiro 3-4-ga alley area

Hours: Mostly weekends, schedule varies by event

Price: Free–paid (varies by event)

Since 2022, this has become one of the key hubs of Seoul's pop-up culture. Fashion brands, K-beauty companies, music labels, and independent artist groups temporarily rent empty warehouses or factory buildings in the Euljiro alleys for pop-up events lasting a few days to a few weeks.

Foreigner fit: ★★★★. A chance to experience Korean brand pop-ups in person. Schedules can be checked by following Instagram accounts such as @hipjiro (@힙지로) and @euljiropopup (@을지로팝업) (checking recommended — official status of these accounts is unclear, cross-check with a Google search).

Practical info: Hard to catch without knowing the schedule in advance. Checking Google Maps reviews or Instagram the day before for pop-ups currently running is recommended.

9. Euljiro Hidden Bar Alley

Location: Narrow alleys around Euljiro 3-ga Station

Hours: Nights 20:00–02:00 (varies by shop) / many closed Mondays

Prices (2026): Beer ₩9,000–14,000 / cocktails ₩15,000–25,000

Payment: Cards accepted

Many small bars here have no sign, a tiny sign, or sit behind an iron door and narrow stairs. These are the kind of places featured on social media as "hidden bar" or "no-sign bar." Because the spaces are small (10–20 seats), some require a reservation.

Foreigner fit: ★★★★. A growing number take English reservations and orders. Some don't admit walk-ins without a reservation, so checking via Instagram or Google Maps whether booking is required is recommended.

Note: To avoid fabricating specific shop names, none are listed here. Search the Korean term "Euljiro bar Hipjiro" (을지로 bar 힙지로) on Google Maps before visiting for the latest list of currently operating spots.

⚠️ Top 3 Mistakes Foreigners Make

Mistake 1: Expecting an English menu at old-school eateries

Most old-school spots like Euljimyeonok and Hadongkwan have Korean-only menus. Bring a translation app's camera feature (image translation), or, when the menu is effectively a single dish, order by holding up fingers ("one gomtang," for example). Uraeok has English-capable staff thanks to its Michelin-driven foot traffic.

Mistake 2: Visiting the printing and hardware alleys on a weekend

These alleys are made up of real, operating businesses. Most shutters stay down on weekends and holidays. Weekday 10am–5pm is when the alleys are actually alive.

Mistake 3: Showing up at a newtro bar at night without a reservation

Euljiro's hidden bars are small and many run on a reservation system. Especially on Friday and Saturday nights, many won't admit walk-ins. Checking Google Maps reviews or Instagram for the reservation method before visiting is essential.

Practical Info — Transport·Payment·Language

Transport

  • Base camp: Euljiro 3-ga Station (Lines 2/3) or Euljiro 4-ga Station (Lines 2/5)
  • Old-school eateries (Euljimyeonok, Uraeok): 5–10 min walk from Euljiro 3-4-ga stations
  • Hadongkwan: 5-min walk from Euljiro Ipgu Station (Line 2)
  • Printing·hardware alleys: 3-min walk from Exit 4, Euljiro 3-ga Station
  • Newtro cluster: within roughly 300m of Exits 2/4, Euljiro 3-ga Station

Movement tip: Euljiro 3-ga and 4-ga stations are a straight 15-minute walk apart. Using the two stations as base points, a day plan of old-school eateries (lunch) → printing/hardware alleys (afternoon) → newtro (evening/night) fits comfortably in one day.

Payment

  • Old-school eateries: mostly card-friendly / Hadongkwan prefers cash, checking recommended
  • Newtro cafés·bars: mostly card-friendly
  • Keep cash on hand: some old-school spots and small printing/hardware shops take cash only
  • ATMs: inside Euljiro 3-ga Station, and at nearby convenience stores (CU, GS25, 7-Eleven)

Language

  • Old-school eateries: Korean-centered. A translation app (camera mode) is essential
  • Newtro cafés·bars: many have English-speaking staff
  • Printing·hardware alleys: English communication is difficult. Not needed if you're just exploring
  • Emergency numbers: ☎ 1330 (tourist interpretation, 24h), ☎ 1339 (medical, English), ☎ 112 (police)

FAQ 6

Q1. Where should a first-time Euljiro visitor start?

Exit 4, Euljiro 3-ga Station. Lunch at a nearby old-school eatery (Euljimyeonok or Uraeok), the printing alley in the afternoon, then a newtro café/bar in the evening/night — that's the most efficient route.

Q2. Which feels more "Euljiro" — old-school or newtro?

Both are different sides of Euljiro. Old-school reflects the Euljiro of the 1950s–70s, while the newtro cluster is the Euljiro of the 2020s. If you have time, doing lunch at an old-school spot plus newtro at night lets you experience both layers of the district fully.

Q3. What's the price level?

An old-school meal runs ₩13,000–20,000 per person. Newtro café drinks are ₩8,000–15,000, bar drinks ₩12,000–25,000. Adding snacks at Gwangjang Market, a full day easily fits within a ₩50,000–80,000 per-person budget.

Q4. Can I order and communicate in English?

The newtro café·bar cluster is mostly English-friendly. Old-school spots are limited. A translation app handles most situations. The 1330 tourist interpretation line (24h, free) is also available.

Q5. Is it okay to visit alone?

Old-school eateries feel natural solo (counter seating available). Newtro bars see plenty of solo visitors too. Exploring the hardware/printing alleys is actually easier alone.

Foreigner Enthusiast 5 Tips

1. Base camp at Euljiro 3-ga Station (Lines 2/3) or 4-ga Station (Lines 2/5) — cover the whole district on foot between the two stations

2. Split into two courses: old-school (lunch) + newtro (night) — old-school is a lunchtime operation, newtro bars peak at night

3. Carry ₩20,000–30,000 in cash — some old-school and small alley businesses are cash-only

4. Visit the printing alley on a weekday morning — the alley is dead on weekends; weekday 10am–5pm is when Euljiro is alive

5. Always confirm reservation requirements for newtro bars — especially for Friday/Saturday visits, check Google Maps or Instagram the day before
