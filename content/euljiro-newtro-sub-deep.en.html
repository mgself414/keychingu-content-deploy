🎯 How to Use This Guide

This guide gathers 4 restaurants, tearooms and cafes and 6 alleys and markets around Euljiro in Seoul. The restaurants and cafes have opening hours and closed days registered, while most of the alleys and markets are registered as "varies by shop".

The operating details are copied from values registered in the Korea Tourism Organization tourism data (TourAPI). Menu prices are not in the Korea Tourism Organization data, so they are not listed.

📍 Old Restaurants — Wooraeok, Sansugapsan

Wooraeok (62-29 Changgyeonggung-ro, Jung-gu, ☎ 02-2265-0151) is a Pyongyang naengmyeon (Pyongyang-style cold noodles) specialist that opened in 1946. According to the Korea Tourism Organization, it was selected for the Michelin Guide in 2017, 2020 and 2023, and unlike other Pyongyang naengmyeon restaurants it adds a little more seasoning, so that first-timers can eat it without difficulty. There is also bulgogi to eat with the naengmyeon, and onmyeon (warm noodle soup) to eat hot.

  • Signature menu: Pyongyang naengmyeon. Also bibim naengmyeon, onmyeon, kimchi-mari naengmyeon and more.
  • Opening hours: 11:30~21:00 (last order 20:20). Closed days: Every Monday, January 1.
  • Parking: Possible. About 100 m from Exit 4 of Euljiro 4-ga Station (Lines 2 and 5).

Sansugapsan (24 Eulji-ro 20-gil, Injeon-dong 1-ga, Jung-gu, ☎ 02-2275-6654) is a sundae-gukbap (sundae and rice soup) specialist. According to the Korea Tourism Organization, its signature sundae-gukbap has a clean soup with no gamey smell and plenty of ingredients, and with the sundae set and the assorted sundae platter you can taste several kinds of daechang sundae at once. Euljiro Nogari Alley is close by, so it is a good place to look around together after a meal.

  • Signature menu: Sundae-gukbap. Also sundae set, assorted sundae platter, sulguk and more.
  • Opening hours: 11:30~21:30, prep time 15:00~17:00 (last order 14:30 for lunch, 20:30 for dinner). Closed days: Every Sunday.
  • Parking: Not possible. About 5 minutes on foot from Exit 10 of Euljiro 4-ga Station.
  • Dietary note: Registered menu items include sundae-gukbap, sundae set, assorted sundae platter and sulguk, and no vegetarian menu information is registered.

📍 Newtro Tearoom and Cafe — Eulji Dabang, Coffee Hanyakbang

Eulji Dabang (124-1 Eulji-ro, Euljiro 3-ga, Jung-gu, ☎ 02-2272-1886) is a tearoom that opened in 1985. According to the Korea Tourism Organization, its signature menu is ssanghwa-cha (Korean herbal tea) topped with an egg yolk, and it has kept running at its current spot because of redevelopment. It was one of the filming locations for BTS's 2021 Season's Greetings, so many young customers come.

  • Signature menu: Ssanghwa-cha. Also fresh fruit juice, iced coffee and more.
  • Opening hours: Mon~Sat 06:00~21:00, Sunday 09:00~20:00. Closed days: Open every day of the year.
  • Parking: Not possible. Near Exit 10 of Euljiro 3-ga Station.

Coffee Hanyakbang (16-6 Samil-daero 12-gil, Euljiro 2-ga, Jung-gu, ☎ 070-4148-4242) is, according to the Korea Tourism Organization, a cafe renovated on the site of Hyeminseo (혜민서), where Heo Jun (허준) treated the sick. Old props and antiques were left in place, so there are many spots for photos, and the coffee is made from hand-roasted beans. It is in a narrow alley, so it can be a little hard to find.

  • Signature menu: Filter coffee. Also Sujeonggwa-in-gabe, milk tea, cafe latte and more.
  • Opening hours: 1st floor weekdays 10:00~22:00, weekends 11:00~22:00 (last order 21:00), 2nd and 3rd floors 10:00~21:30. Closed days: Open every day of the year.
  • Parking: Not possible.

📍 Alleys and Markets — Nogari Alley, Printing Alley, Sewoon Sangga, Bangsan Market, Metal Furniture Street, Jungbu Market

Euljiro Nogari Alley (around 129 Eulji-ro, Jung-gu) is an alley leading to Exit 4 of Euljiro 3-ga Station. According to the Korea Tourism Organization, it began in November 1980 with the first nogari (dried young pollack) beer hall, Eulji OB Bear (을지 OB베어). Printing workers who finished night shifts drank draft beer with nogari, and shops opened one after another; now the plastic tables are full of customers every night. Besides nogari and draft beer, many shops serve side dishes such as fried chicken, stews and fried foods.

  • Opening hours and closed days: Vary by shop. Parking: Not possible.

Chungmu-ro and Euljiro Printing Alley (around 14 Toegye-ro 37-gil, Chungmu-ro 4-ga, Jung-gu) is, according to the Korea Tourism Organization, the leading printing district of Jung-gu, Seoul. On the Chungmu-ro side there are many commercial printing and digital printing businesses, and on the Euljiro side many old businesses that handle plate-making and printing machines. The two are within 10 minutes of each other on foot.

  • Opening hours and closed days: Vary by shop. Parking: Not possible.

Sewoon Sangga (159 Cheonggyecheon-ro, Jangsa-dong, Jongno-gu, ☎ 02-2271-2344), according to the Korea Tourism Organization, opened in 1968 as Korea's first general electronics market. It became Korea's first urban redevelopment site in 1966 and was designed by the architect Kim Swoo-geun (김수근). It sells electronic parts, audio equipment, lighting equipment and more, and has craftsmen who work on devices. It is the building that appeared as "Geumga Plaza" in the drama "Vincenzo".

  • Opening hours: 09:00~19:00 (may vary by store). Closed days: Every Sunday, public holidays (may vary by store). Hours and closed days are the values registered under the KTO 'Sewoon Electronics Market' entry.
  • Parking: Possible (1,000 won per 10 minutes).

Bangsan Market (20 Dongho-ro 37-gil, Jugyo-dong, Jung-gu, ☎ 02-2266-8765 Merchants Association) is, according to the Korea Tourism Organization, an industrial supplies market where you can buy printing, packaging, bookbinding and craft materials, along with stationery and fancy goods, all in one place. Small shops are clustered together, so it is easy to compare prices, and many designers and creators visit.

  • Opening hours and closed days: Vary by store. Parking: Possible.

Euljiro Metal Furniture Street (around 34-1 Changgyeonggung-ro, Yegwan-dong, Jung-gu) is, according to the Korea Tourism Organization, a furniture street with many commercial furniture shops. Many shops have their own factories, so you can buy direct, and when buying one or two pieces of office furniture it is about 10% cheaper than neighborhood furniture stores. It connects to the nearby lighting street.

  • Opening hours and closed days: Vary by shop. Parking: Possible. Restrooms: Available.

Seoul Jungbu Market (35 Eulji-ro 36-gil, Jung-gu, ☎ 02-2267-5617) is a market that opened on February 28, 1957. According to the Korea Tourism Organization, you can buy dried seafood such as laver, seaweed, yellow croaker, anchovies, squid, dried pollack and dried filefish cheaply.

  • Opening hours: 04:00~19:00 (varies by shop). Closed days: Vary by shop.
  • Parking: Not possible. Restrooms: Available.

⚠ 3 Things to Know Before You Go

  1. Closed days differ from place to place — Wooraeok is closed on Mondays and Sansugapsan on Sundays, and Sewoon Sangga is closed on Sundays and public holidays. Eulji Dabang and Coffee Hanyakbang are registered as open every day of the year.
  2. Sansugapsan has an afternoon prep time — 15:00~17:00 is prep time, and the last lunch order is 14:30.
  3. Many places have no parking — Sansugapsan, Eulji Dabang, Coffee Hanyakbang, Nogari Alley, Printing Alley and Jungbu Market are registered as no parking.

⚙ FAQ 2

Q. Which places can I go to early in the morning?

A. According to Korea Tourism Organization registered information, Jungbu Market opens at 04:00 and Eulji Dabang at 06:00 Monday to Saturday. Sewoon Sangga opens at 09:00, and the 1st floor of Coffee Hanyakbang at 10:00 on weekdays.

Q. Which places are open on Sundays?

A. Eulji Dabang (09:00~20:00) and Coffee Hanyakbang are registered as open every day of the year, and Wooraeok is registered as closed on Mondays. Sansugapsan and Sewoon Sangga are closed on Sundays.
