Korea Retro Train Station and Closed Station Guide — Culture Station Seoul 284, Hwarangdae Railway Park, Gyeongchun Line Forest Trail, Former Gimyujeong Station, Jeongdongjin Station, Chujeon Station, Hwabon Station and Seomjingang Train Village: 16 Places

Subtitle: 16 places chosen from the "Sentimental Train Stations" theme of the Ministry of Culture, Sports and Tourism and Korea Tourism Organization "Attraction Discovery 100×100" public vote · In Seoul, Culture Station Seoul 284 (free), Hwarangdae Railway Park (24 hours) and Gyeongchun Line Forest Trail (always open) · The two places with a registered admission or ride fee are Seomjingang Train Village (adults 5,000 won) and Wolmi Sea Train (weekday regular 11,000 won) (bicycle rental at Neungnae and parking at Hwarangdae cost extra) · The places registered as closed on Mondays are Culture Station Seoul 284, Former Gimyujeong Station (except the outdoor space), Wolmi Sea Train, Hwarangdae Station History Exhibition Hall, the Tram Library at Hwarangdae Railway Park, Gunsan Impi Station and Gomo Station Cultural Complex — confirmed via Korea Tourism Organization tourism data (TourAPI)

🎯 How to Use This Guide

This guide covers 16 of the 100 places chosen in the "Sentimental Train Stations" theme of the Ministry of Culture, Sports and Tourism and Korea Tourism Organization "Attraction Discovery 100×100" (명소발굴 100×100) (candidates recommended by travel experts, voted on by the public), whose operating information was confirmed in the Korea Tourism Organization tourism data. The ranks and vote counts in the table are the results of that vote. Where the name used in the vote differs from the name in the Korea Tourism Organization tourism data, the two were matched by address (for example, the vote's "Changwon Gyeonghwa Station Halt (창원 경화역 간이역)" is "Gyeonghwa Station Park (경화역공원)", "Wolmi Sea Station (월미바다역)" is "Wolmi Sea Train (월미바다열차)", and "Neungnae Station Closed Station (능내역 폐역)" is "Neungnae Station History and Culture Park (능내역사문화공원)"). Hwarangdae Station (화랑대역) combines two entries, "Hwarangdae Railway Park (화랑대 철도공원)" and "Hwarangdae Station History Exhibition Hall (화랑대역사 전시관)", into one item.

Most of them are former stations that were closed and turned into parks, exhibition halls or cultural spaces. Jeongdongjin Station (정동진역) is introduced as a station that sightseeing trains still serve. Wolmi Sea Train is registered not as a station but as a sightseeing monorail that circles Wolmido (월미도).

The two places with a registered admission or ride fee are Seomjingang Train Village and Wolmi Sea Train. The rest are registered as free or have no registered fee value. Bicycle rental at Neungnae Station History and Culture Park and parking at Hwarangdae Railway Park (beyond 2 hours) have separate charges. The operating details are copied from values registered in the Korea Tourism Organization tourism data (TourAPI).

📍 Seoul — Culture Station Seoul 284, Hwarangdae Railway Park and Gyeongchun Line Forest Trail

Culture Station Seoul 284 (문화역서울284) (Tongil-ro 1 (통일로 1), Jung-gu (중구), ☎ 02-3407-3500) is a complex cultural space that restored the old Seoul Station building. It began in 1900 as Namdaemun Station (남대문정차장), became Gyeongseong Station (경성역), and then Seoul Station, and after the old building closed in 2004 it opened on August 11, 2011. The exterior is a restoration of Gyeongseong Station as it was in 1925. There are exhibitions, performances, workshops and space tour programs.

  • Opening hours: Tuesday and Thursday~Sunday 10:00~18:00 (last entry 17:30), Wednesday 10:00~19:00 (last entry 18:30).
  • Closed days: Every Monday. It is closed while exhibitions are being prepared, so it is registered that you should check the exhibition dates before you go.
  • Fee: Free. It is registered that foreigners do not need a separate reservation. Parking: Not available.

Hwarangdae Railway Park (화랑대 철도공원) (Hwarang-ro 608 (화랑로 608), Nowon-gu (노원구), ☎ Nowon-gu Office Leisure City Division (노원구청 여가도시과) 02-2116-0668) is a park made around the old Hwarangdae Station (화랑대역). The Korea Tourism Organization introduction describes the station both as "Seoul's last halt" and as "the Gyeongchun Line's last halt." The tracks, which stopped running in 2010 after the Gyeongchun Line (경춘선) opened in 1939, were kept rather than removed. On display are a 1950s Mika locomotive, a narrow-gauge train, and trams from the Czech Republic and Hiroshima, Japan.

  • Opening hours: Open 24 hours. Tram Library (트램도서관) 10:00~19:00, cafe 11:00~21:00.
  • Closed days: Open year-round (the Tram Library is closed every Monday). Fee: Free. Parking: Free for 2 hours, then 150 won per 5 minutes.

Hwarangdae Station History Exhibition Hall (화랑대역사 전시관) (Hwarang-ro 610 (화랑로 610), Nowon-gu, ☎ 070-4179-3777) is the renovated old Hwarangdae Station, where the history of the Gyeongchun Line and railway materials are on display.

  • Opening hours: 10:00~18:00. Closed days: Every Monday, the day after public holidays, January 1, Seollal and Chuseok holidays. Fee: Free.

Gyeongchun Line Forest Trail (경춘선숲길) (Hagye-dong 107-2 (하계동 107-2), Nowon-gu, ☎ Gyeongchun Line Forest Trail Visitor Center (경춘선숲길 방문자센터) 02-3783-5977) is a path made by turning the old Gyeongchun Line tracks into a park. The Gyeongchun Line opened as a private railway on July 25, 1939 and ran its last train on December 20, 2010, and it became a park in stages from 2013.

  • Opening hours: Always open. Closed days: Open year-round.

📍 Gyeonggi·Incheon — Neungnae Station History and Culture Park and Wolmi Sea Train

Neungnae Station History and Culture Park (능내역사문화공원) (Dasan-ro 566-5 (다산로 566-5), Joan-myeon (조안면), Namyangju-si (남양주시), ☎ Namyangju City Hall Culture and Tourism Division (남양주시청 문화관광과) 031-590-4783) is Neungnae Station (능내역), which closed in December 2008, turned into an exhibition hall. The waiting room and the tracks are preserved, and there are props such as wooden chairs, a ticket office and photographs.

  • Opening hours: Always open. Closed days: Open year-round. Fee: Free.
  • Bicycle rental: Single bicycle 5,000 won for 1 hour, tandem bicycle 8,000 won for 1 hour.

Wolmi Sea Train (월미바다열차) (Jemulryang-ro 269 (제물량로 269), Jemulpo-gu (제물포구), Incheon, ☎ 032-450-7600) is a sightseeing monorail that circles Wolmido. The total running distance is 6.1km, and one loop takes about 42 minutes. The track is 7 to 18m above the ground.

  • Opening hours: Peak season (Apr~Oct) weekdays (Tue~Thu) 10:00~18:00, weekends (Fri~Sun and public holidays) 10:00~19:00. Off-season (Nov~Mar) 10:00~18:00.
  • Closed days: Every Monday.
  • Fee: Weekday regular 11,000 won, seniors and teenagers 8,000 won, children 7,000 won. Weekend regular 14,000 won, seniors and teenagers 10,000 won, children 8,000 won.
  • How to ride: It is registered that on your first ride you can board only at Wolmi Sea Station (월미바다역), and on later rides you can board at all 4 stations.

📍 Gangwon — Former Gimyujeong Station, Jeongdongjin Station, Taebaek Chujeon Station and Wonju Bangok Station

Former Gimyujeong Station (김유정역 폐역) (Gimyujeong-ro 1435 (김유정로 1435), Sindong-myeon (신동면), Chuncheon-si (춘천시), ☎ 033-261-7780) opened as Sinnam Station (신남역) in 1939 and was renamed Gimyujeong Station in 2004. According to the Korea Tourism Organization introduction, it was the first train station in Korea to use a person's name. When a new station building was built in 2010, the old station closed. An old Mugunghwa train remains on the tracks, and the inside of the train has been turned into a book cafe and a tourist information center.

  • Opening hours: Always open. Closed days: Every Monday (the outdoor space is open year-round). "Always open" in the opening hours field and "Monday" in the closed days field are both registered.
  • Fee: Free. Parking: Free.

Jeongdongjin Station (정동진역) (Jeongdongyeok-gil 17 (정동역길 17), Gangdong-myeon (강동면), Gangneung-si (강릉시), ☎ Sandglass Tourist Information Center (모래시계관광안내센터) 033-640-4536) is, according to the Korea Tourism Organization introduction, the station closest to the sea in the whole country. It began operating as an ordinary station in 1962, and in 1995 it became the setting of the drama Sandglass (모래시계) and became known as a sunrise spot.

  • Opening hours: It is registered that they differ by line. Closed days: Open year-round. Parking: Available.

Taebaek Chujeon Station (태백 추전역) (Ssaribat-gil 47-63 (싸리밭길 47-63), Taebaek-si (태백시), ☎ 033-553-8550) stands 855m above sea level. According to the Korea Tourism Organization introduction, it is the highest train station in Korea. It opened in 1973 as a station that shipped out coal. The rest area has stationmaster and station staff uniforms and caps, so you can try being a station worker.

  • Opening hours: Always open. Closed days: Open year-round. Fee: Free.

Wonju Bangok Station (원주 반곡역) (Dalmajung 3-gil 30 (달마중3길 30), Wonju-si (원주시), ☎ Wonju City Hall Tourism Division (원주시청 관광과) 033-733-1330) is a Jungang Line station building that began operating in 1941. It was registered as a Registered Cultural Heritage (등록문화유산) in 2005 and became a closed station in 2021 after a new Jungang Line route opened. In spring, cherry blossoms bloom around the station building.

  • Opening hours: Always open. Closed days: Open year-round. Fee: Free.

📍 Daegu — Hwabon Station and Gomo Station Cultural Complex

Hwabon Station (화본역) (Seongsangaeum-ro 711-9 (산성가음로 711-9), Sanseong-myeon (산성면), Gunwi-gun (군위군), ☎ Gunwi County Office Culture and Tourism Division (군위군청 문화관광과) 054-380-6065) began operating as an ordinary station on the Jungang Line on December 1, 1938. It became a closed station when the Jungang Line was discontinued in December 2024. The station building was built in 1936, and a 25m-high water tower remains. The waiting room displays historical photographs of the station, station staff caps and more.

  • Opening hours: 09:00~17:00. Closed days: Open year-round. Fee: Free.

Gomo Station Cultural Complex (고모역복합문화공간) (Gomo-ro 208 (고모로 208), Suseong-gu (수성구), ☎ 053-791-3334) is the closed Gomo Station (고모역) turned into a cultural space. It has the Gomo Museum (고모뮤지엄), divided into a history hall and a culture hall, plus a garden and a walking path.

  • Opening hours: Apr~Nov 10:00~18:00, Dec~Mar 10:00~17:30.
  • Closed days: Every Monday, January 1, the days of Seollal and Chuseok. Fee: Free. It is registered that group visits require a reservation on the website.

📍 Busan — Former Songjeong Station

Former Songjeong Station (송정역 구) (Songjeongjungang-ro 8beon-gil 60 (송정중앙로8번길 60), Haeundae-gu (해운대구), ☎ Haeundae Blueline Park (해운대 블루라인파크) 051-701-5548) is a station on the Donghae Nambu Line (동해남부선). It began operating as a halt in 1934 and became an ordinary station in 1941. It is registered as a Registered Cultural Heritage. It is now the starting and ending station of the Haeundae Beach Train (해운대 해변열차), which uses the old Donghae Nambu Line tracks.

  • Opening hours: 09:30~22:00 (may change depending on circumstances). Closed days: Open year-round. Parking: Free.

📍 Jeonbuk — Gunsan Impi Station and Former Seodo Station

Gunsan Impi Station (군산 임피역사) (Seowonseokgok-ro 37 (서원석곡로 37), Impi-myeon (임피면), Gunsan-si (군산시), ☎ 063-454-3923) is a building put up around 1936 as a Gunsan Line (군산선) station. The Korea Tourism Organization introduction says that during the Japanese colonial period the Gunsan Line was the route that carried the farm products of Jeollanam-do and Jeollabuk-do out to Japan through Gunsan Port. It now has a passenger car exhibition hall and a park, and it is presented together with the stories of the works of Chae Man-sik (채만식), a novelist from this area.

  • Opening hours: 10:00~18:00. Closed days: Every Monday. Fee: Free.

Former Seodo Station (구 서도역) (Seodo-gil 32 (서도길 32), Samae-myeon (사매면), Namwon-si (남원시), ☎ Namwon City Hall Regional Vitality Division (남원시청 지역활력과) 063-620-6684) was built in 1932 and, according to the Korea Tourism Organization introduction, is the oldest wooden-building closed station in Korea. It stopped handling passengers in 2004. It is the main setting of Choi Myeong-hui's (최명희) epic novel Honbul (혼불).

  • Opening hours: Always open. Closed days: Open year-round. Fee: Free.

📍 Jeonnam·Gyeongnam — Seomjingang Train Village and Gyeonghwa Station Park

Seomjingang Train Village (섬진강기차마을) (Gichamaeul-ro 232 (기차마을로 232), Ogok-myeon (오곡면), Gokseong-gun (곡성군), ☎ 061-362-7461) is at the old Gokseong Station (곡성역). A train restored to look like an old steam locomotive is introduced as making round trips over a 10km section from the old Gokseong Station (Seomjingang Train Village) to Gajeong Station (가정역).

  • Opening hours: 09:00~20:00 (last entry 18:00). Closed days: Open year-round.
  • Admission fee (individual): Adults 5,000 won, children and seniors 4,500 won. It is registered that you should check the website and by phone for details.

Gyeonghwa Station Park (경화역공원) (Jinhae-daero 649 (진해대로 649), Jinhae-gu (진해구), Changwon-si (창원시), ☎ 055-548-4000) is a park built around the closed Gyeonghwa Station (경화역). In spring, a tunnel of cherry blossoms forms along the railway.

  • Opening hours: Always open. Closed days: Open year-round.

⚠ 3 Things to Know Before You Go

  1. Many places are closed on Mondays — Culture Station Seoul 284, Former Gimyujeong Station (except the outdoor space), Wolmi Sea Train, Hwarangdae Station History Exhibition Hall, the Tram Library at Hwarangdae Railway Park, Gunsan Impi Station and Gomo Station Cultural Complex are registered as closed on Mondays.
  2. Culture Station Seoul 284 closes while exhibitions are being prepared — it is registered that you should check the exhibition dates before you go.
  3. For Wolmi Sea Train, board at Wolmi Sea Station on your first ride — weekday and weekend fares differ.

⚙ FAQ 3

Q. Which places can I reach from Seoul?

A. Culture Station Seoul 284 (a complex cultural space restoring the old Seoul Station building), Hwarangdae Railway Park and Hwarangdae Station History Exhibition Hall, and Gyeongchun Line Forest Trail. Admission is registered as free or has no registered value.

Q. Which places let me ride a train?

A. The places where train service is introduced are Jeongdongjin Station (sightseeing train), Former Songjeong Station (the starting and ending station of the Haeundae Beach Train), Seomjingang Train Village (a sightseeing train making round trips to Gajeong Station) and Wolmi Sea Train (sightseeing monorail). It is registered that the running times at Jeongdongjin Station differ by line.

Q. Which places cost money?

A. Seomjingang Train Village (adults 5,000 won) and Wolmi Sea Train (weekday regular 11,000 won, weekend regular 14,000 won). Bicycle rental at Neungnae Station History and Culture Park is 5,000 won for 1 hour for a single bicycle and 8,000 won for 1 hour for a tandem, and parking at Hwarangdae Railway Park is free for 2 hours and then 150 won per 5 minutes.
