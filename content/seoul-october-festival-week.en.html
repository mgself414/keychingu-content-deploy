Seoul's First Week of October Festival Week — A Two-Day National Foundation Day Holiday Brings the Gangnam Parade and Jamsu Bridge Gourmet Road Back to Back

Subtitle: Oct 3 (Sat) Gangnam Festival Grand Parade (Pikachu balloon) · Oct 4 (Sun) Car-Free Jamsu Bridge "Gourmet Road" · Oct 1–11 Goyang Fall Flower Festival · Oct 17–23 Seoul Silver Grass Festival preview — Seoul's 2026 limited-run event layer

🎯 Why "the First Week of October"

Seoul's classic fall spots — silver grass, foliage, ginkgo (#1313 · #1314) — really peak from mid-October on. But the first weekend of October 2026 is a special window the calendar happens to create: October 3 falls on a Saturday, which turns National Foundation Day (개천절) into a two-day holiday, and each of those two days gets its own one-off event — a Gangnam parade on Saturday that shuts down all of Dosan-daero (도산대로), and a food-truck street on Sunday across the car-free Jamsu Bridge (잠수교). Both happen once a year, and this is the last season Jamsu Bridge hosts it in the "Car-Free Jamsu Bridge" format.

This guide is a date-stamped calendar for how to string those two days together, plus what's nearby and what's coming the following week. The standing night views and Hangang River route (#586 · #702) are there whenever you go, so this piece covers only what layers on top during this window.

⚠ Note on timing: The events in this guide are a 2026-only schedule. Each one can change for rain or on-site conditions, so check the day-of announcement on the official channel (SOURCES below) before you head out.

🎈 Oct 3 (Sat) Gangnam Grand Parade — Two Hours When 2 km of Dosan-daero Becomes the Stage

This is Gangnam District's first-ever large-scale street parade. From 4 to 6 p.m., the procession runs from Cheongdam Intersection to the Dosan Park intersection — starting with a children's brass band and traditional processions, followed by large character balloons such as Pororo (뽀로로), game-themed parade floats, robots, fashion models, and street performances, and finishing with a giant Pikachu balloon procession and dance performance marking the Pokémon franchise's 30th anniversary. Residents from Gangnam's 22 neighborhoods march in the procession themselves, so it reads less like a "tourist show" and more like a neighborhood festival scaled way up.

  • Where to stand: The procession starts near Cheongdam and moves toward Dosan Park. The area near the Dosan Park intersection is where the finale (Pikachu) passes, so if the characters are your priority, post up there; if you want to catch the start, get to Cheongdam Intersection before 4 p.m.
  • Getting there: Dosan-daero is closed to vehicles during the parade. Skip the idea of a taxi or bus dropping you on the road — go by subway: Apgujeong Rodeo Station (Suin-Bundang Line) sits right at the Dosan Park intersection, and Cheongdam Station (청담역) sits right at Cheongdam Intersection, on Line 7.
  • Into the night: Once the parade ends, a family concert runs from 19:00 on the same Dosan-daero stage. From there, it flows naturally into dinner in the backstreets around Apgujeong Rodeo and Dosan Park — see #1088 for boutique shops, #1073 for Hannam (한남) night bars.
  • The festival itself runs three days, Oct 3–5, so even if you miss the parade, the street program continues on Sunday and Monday. Just note that the parade itself runs only once across the three days.

🍢 Oct 4 (Sun) Jamsu Bridge Gourmet Road — Food Trucks on a Car-Free Bridge, the Last Year for This Format

The Car-Free Jamsu Bridge Ttubeok-ttubeok Festival closes traffic on Jamsu Bridge and around Banpo Hangang Park every Sunday, 2–10 p.m., from September 6 through October 25 — a pedestrians-only festival. It's now in its fifth year as one of Seoul's signature Hangang River festivals, and even on a normal Sunday, roughly 30 food trucks called "Moonlight Dining" (달빛식당) set up on the bridge's north and south ends. October 4 is Gourmet Road week specifically — the food-truck lineup expands sharply to cover Korean, American, Vietnamese, Indian, and other international cuisines from the south end of the bridge to the north (organizers say roughly triple the usual count), and a separate large seating area for up to 800 people goes up just for that day.

There's one more reason this festival is special. Jamsu Bridge is set to become a fully pedestrian bridge in the second half of 2027, so this year is the final season for a festival called "Car-Free Jamsu Bridge." Walking across a bridge that still has painted traffic lanes under your feet ends after this fall.

  • Route: Subway to Express Bus Terminal Station (고속터미널역) on Lines 3, 7, 9, or Dongjak Station (동작역) on Lines 4, 9 → Banpo Hangang Park → the south end of Jamsu Bridge. The standard plan is to go in at 2 p.m., eat and walk, then move on to the Banpo Bridge Moonlight Rainbow Fountain (반포대교 달빛무지개분수) (#702) around sunset — check the day-of notice for the fountain schedule.
  • Until 10 p.m.: The Hangang night view (#586 · #703) from the bridge after dark is this festival's second half. Plan it as two parts — eat during the day, walk at night.
  • Rain and safety: Because this is a riverside event, it can be canceled same-day depending on rainfall or water level. Check the organizer's notice (SOURCES below) once in the morning.

🌼 Nearby Option — Oct 1–11 Goyang Fall Flower Festival (Ilsan Lake Park)

If you have an hour to spare outside Seoul, the Ilsan Lake Park flower festival runs during the same window. Chrysanthemum and cosmos flower beds paired with a walk around the lake are the standard combo, and it's a walk from Jeongbalsan Station (정발산역) on Line 3. Scheduling it on a weekday (Oct 1–2, or after Oct 5) that doesn't overlap with the parade or Jamsu Bridge lets you avoid weekend crowds. Nationwide, the Jara Island Flower Festa (자라섬 꽃 페스타) (Gapyeong, through Oct 5) and the Taean Fall Flower Expo (태안 가을꽃박람회) (through Nov 1) run around the same time, but this guide only covers what's reachable within a single day trip from Seoul.

🌾 Next Week's Preview — Oct 17–23 Seoul Silver Grass Festival (Haneul Park)

If you go to Haneul Park in the first week of October, the silver grass is there, but there's no lighting show yet. The 25th Seoul Silver Grass Festival runs October 17 (Sat) through 23 (Fri) at World Cup Park's Haneul Park, and this year it's again a nighttime festival with landscape lighting, a light show, photo zones, and busking. The seasonal guide to the silver grass itself (where it turns silver, how it differs from pink muhly) is in #1313, so all you need to remember here is the dates — if you're in Seoul during the first week and staying through the third, push your Haneul Park visit to after the 17th.

🗓 Two-Day Route — Making the Most of the National Foundation Day Holiday

Oct 3 (Sat): 3:30 p.m. Apgujeong Rodeo Station → viewing spot at the Dosan Park intersection → 16:00–18:00 parade (Pikachu finale) → dinner in the Dosan Park/Apgujeong Rodeo backstreets → 19:00 concert, or Hannam night bars (#1073).

Oct 4 (Sun): 2 p.m. Express Bus Terminal Station → Banpo Hangang Park and the Jamsu Bridge Gourmet Road → Moonlight Rainbow Fountain around sunset (#702) → night view on the bridge → wraps up at 22:00.

Rain backup: both events are outdoors. This happens to be a year with an unusually full slate of Seoul exhibitions from late September through October — KAWS solo exhibition (through Dec 27), Gaudí (through Oct 31), Martin Parr photography (through Oct 18), and more — so you can switch to an indoor layer.

⚠ 3 Common Mistakes

1. Trying to reach Dosan-daero by taxi during the parade — it's a closed zone, and you can't get in. Take the subway to Apgujeong Rodeo or Cheongdam Station instead.

2. Showing up at Jamsu Bridge on Saturday — this festival runs Sundays only. On Saturday, Oct 3, traffic on Jamsu Bridge runs as usual.

3. Expecting Haneul Park's night lighting show in the first week — the Silver Grass Festival doesn't start until Oct 17. In the first week, it's daytime silver grass only.

⚙ 3 FAQs

1. If you can only pick one day? — For international visitors, Oct 4's Jamsu Bridge Gourmet Road. It connects straight into the Hangang night view, and this is the last year for the "Car-Free Jamsu Bridge" format. If the characters and parade are your priority, go with Oct 3.

2. What about admission? — Both events are free to enter. Food trucks and craft experiences on Jamsu Bridge are paid.

3. How crowded is it, given the holiday? — Since it's a National Foundation Day holiday weekend, expect crowds at both. Arriving before 4 p.m. for the parade, or right when Jamsu Bridge opens at 2 p.m., is relatively the least crowded window.
