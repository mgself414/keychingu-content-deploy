Why Non-Coffee Drink Chains — the Cafe Republic's Second Refrigerator

Korea is the Cafe Republic (#1158) — so where should a traveler who doesn't drink coffee go? The answer is already on the street: bubble tea chains at every subway station, budget juice chains selling fresh fruit juice in the ₩2,000 (~$1.5) range, and milk tea brands that came from Taiwan and evolved Korean-style.

Bubble tea in particular is a fascinating case. Gong Cha (공차) is a Taiwanese brand, but a Korean company acquired the global headquarters, turning it into "a Taiwanese brand re-exported from Korea" — while homegrown chains like Amasvin (아마스빈) built their own genre out of coupon culture and jumbo sizes. This guide is the drinks piece of the chain series (#61 cafe, #1264 burger, #1265 ice cream) — how to pick a brand when you want something to drink that isn't coffee.

Brand Profiles — Starting with a One-Line Identity

🧋 Gong Cha — the Bubble Tea That Came from Taiwan and Was Raised by Korea

  • One line: The default name for bubble tea in Korea. Born in Taiwan, but the protagonist of a reverse-export story in which the Korean business acquired the global headquarters.
  • Signature: Black milk tea + pearls — the basic is the standard. Active seasonal smoothie and fruit tea lines.
  • What stands out: Found at subway stations and busy districts nationwide · sugar level (0/30/50/70/100%) and ice level customization built into ordering · a topping culture of adding pearls, milk foam, and aloe
  • Price range: ₩4,000~6,500 (~$3.0~4.9)
  • Foreigner fit: The "compare with back home" fun for Taiwanese and Chinese-speaking travelers. The seasonal menu differs from Gong Cha in your own country.

💙 Amasvin — the Homegrown Champion of Value and Coupons

  • One line: A homegrown bubble tea chain with 250+ stores nationwide. Blue signboards, jumbo sizes, and coupon culture make it the standard of student districts.
  • Signature: Brown sugar bubble milk tea · large-format (large and jumbo) options.
  • What stands out: Priced deliberately below Gong Cha · photogenic stores and packaging · dense around university districts
  • Price range: ₩3,000~5,500 (~$2.2~4.1)
  • Foreigner fit: "The bubble tea Korean students actually drink" — the local-experience axis.

🍵 Palgong Tea — the Milk Tea Artisan from Daegu

  • One line: A homegrown milk tea specialist that started at Palgongsan (팔공산) mountain in Daegu (대구) — a strong tea-leaf base has earned it a devoted following.
  • Signature: Royal milk tea · a brown sugar line.
  • What stands out: Evenly spread across the capital region and the provinces · positioned on tea-base quality · a quiet store mood
  • Foreigner fit: Travelers for whom the milk tea itself is the destination — tea flavor over sweetness.

🍹 Juicy — the Invention of the ₩2,000-Range Fresh Fruit Juice

  • One line: The chain that created the budget fresh fruit juice genre. The jumbo-size original that went viral for packing more fruit than the cup could hold.
  • Signature: Fresh fruit juice (strawberry and watermelon on a seasonal cycle) · a 1L jumbo option.
  • What stands out: A ₩2,000~4,000 (~$1.5~3.0) price band · university district and market locations · summer watermelon juice is a seasonal event
  • Foreigner fit: The best-value hydration during heatwave sightseeing (#905). Pairs with market browsing (the #59 gimbap and bunsik route).

🥤 Smoothie King — the Global Mutant on the Health Axis

  • One line: American-born but evolved its own menu in Korea — a health-drink axis that expanded through convenience store and subway station outlets.
  • Signature: Fruit smoothies + protein options.
  • What stands out: Many small in-station outlets · demand as a breakfast substitute
  • Foreigner fit: Travelers keeping up a workout routine or grabbing breakfast. Fun to compare the menu against Smoothie King back home.

🎯 What to Know Before Ordering — Common to All

  • 🎚️ Sugar and ice customization: The bubble tea chain standard — sugar at 0/30/50/70/100%, ice at none/less/regular. Selectable at both kiosks and counters. Instead of Taiwan's "微糖" labels, it's % numbers.
  • 🧋 Adding toppings: Pearls (tapioca), aloe, and milk foam run ₩500~1,000 (~$0.4~0.7) each. "Add pearls" is the basic grammar.
  • 💳 Payment: Cards OK. Membership app points require a Korean phone number — travelers can skip it with negligible loss.
  • 🥤 Size culture: Amasvin's large and Juicy's 1L are genuinely huge — one is plenty for two people.

Pick-by-Situation Guide 3

Situation 1. "I want to compare this with Taiwanese bubble tea"

  • Gong Cha → Amasvin, in that order. Check the seasonal-menu differences versus the homeland at Gong Cha, then the Korean-style brown sugar and jumbo-size evolution at Amasvin — 30% sugar makes a fair comparison baseline.

Situation 2. "Cheapest possible hydration while browsing a market in midsummer"

  • Juicy watermelon juice, 1L. Around ₩3,000 (~$2.2) and it hydrates two people — the standard combo for Gwangjang Market (광장시장) (#59) and the heatwave route (#905).

Situation 3. "I don't drink coffee, but I want the cafe mood"

  • Palgong Tea. A quiet store plus a rich royal milk tea — the non-coffee substitute for the #61 cafe chains.

5 Usage Tips — Getting 200% Out of Drink Chains

Tip 1. 30~50% sugar is the safe zone

Korean bubble tea at default (100%) is intensely sweet — go 50% your first time, 30% if you're sensitive to sugar. 0% is a perfectly respectable choice too.

Tip 2. Drink the pearls "fast"

Tapioca pearls harden as they cool — within an hour of takeout is the rule. Sending them to your hotel fridge is a bad idea.

Tip 3. The seasonal menu is the real menu

Gong Cha and Amasvin run spring strawberry, summer watermelon, and autumn sweet potato lines — checking the seasonal board before the regular menu is the Korean way.

Tip 4. In-station outlets have faster lines

Small subway-station outlets (Smoothie King, Gong Cha) turn over quickly, so the wait is shorter than at tourist-district stores — slot them into your transfers.

Tip 5. A drink-chain stamp rally takes half a day and ₩15,000

Gong Cha → Amasvin → Juicy back to back runs about ₩12,000 (~$9.0) — the non-coffee version of the chain series' cafe stamp rally (#61).

FAQ 6 — Drink Chain Questions Foreigners Actually Ask

Q1. Is Gong Cha a Korean brand or a Taiwanese brand?

  • Both are true. Born in Taiwan, but the Korean entity acquired the global headquarters — a unique history, which is why Korean Gong Cha's seasonal menu moves differently from the homeland's.

Q2. What sugar percentage is the local standard?

  • The MZ-generation standard is 30~50%. 100% requires bracing for "sweet" — just say the number when ordering and you'll be understood.

Q3. Besides bubble tea, what drinks feel distinctly Korean?

  • Juicy-style fresh fruit juice is a Korean invention, and the next axes are convenience store drink trends (#1262) and traditional drinks like sujeonggwa (수정과) cinnamon punch and sikhye (식혜) sweet rice drink.

Q4. I need something cheap to drink like water.

  • Juicy's ₩2,000-range juice or convenience store (#1261) 1+1 drinks — for hydration while sightseeing, these two channels are the value peak.

Q5. Do I need cash?

  • No — all 5 chains take cards and contactless. Only street-market juice stalls are safer with cash.

Q6. Do they open early in the morning?

  • In-station outlets (Smoothie King, Gong Cha) open around 7–8am, university district stores around 10–11am — for a morning drink, the station and convenience store channels are the answer.
