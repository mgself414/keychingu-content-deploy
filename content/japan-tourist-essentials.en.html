⚠ 3 Common Mistakes Japanese Tourists Make

1. "Isaac Toast isn't on Naver Maps" — Failure to search in Korean

The most common trap for first-time Japanese visitors. Isaac Toast (이삭토스트), a signature Japanese breakfast spot in Myeongdong (명동), has weak search results in Korean on Naver Maps. Many Koreans don't know the name itself, but it's widely featured as "イサックトースト (Isakku Tosuto)" on Japanese TV and TikTok. Search "イサックトースト" in Japanese on Google Maps → Myeongdong (명동) branch appears. It's recommended to bookmark it in advance on Google Maps in Japanese before arrival.

2. "Minari = Everywhere" — Wandering outside Seongsu and Neungdong

The minari (미나리, water celery) trend in Japan (for the past 2-3 years) + Japan's 15-year ban on yukhoe (육회, beef tartare) = minari + yukhoe combination is a must-eat when visiting Korea. However, foreigners who don't know the Korean term "Minari Gomtang (미나리 곰탕)" can easily get lost. Signature Minari Gomtang and Minari Yukhoe Bibimbap (미나리육회비빔밥) restaurants are concentrated in the Seongsu (성수)·Neungdong (능동) area (within walking distance of Ttukseom Station (뚝섬역, Line 7) and Children's Grand Park Station (어린이대공원역, Line 5)) — a K-Instagram hotspot frequently visited by Japanese celebrities. Search "미나리곰탕 능동" or "ミナリコムタン" on Google Maps.

3. "Lotte Mart is just a supermarket" — Not knowing foreigner-specific services and wandering in general aisles

A common pattern: on the last day of their trip, tourists drag their carriers into a regular supermarket and wander through general aisles looking for Korean snacks and souvenirs. In reality, Lotte Mart Seoul Station (롯데마트 서울역점) is equipped with 40%+ foreign sales, a dedicated foreigner section + international shipping + currency exchange + 3-hour free luggage storage + a rearranged merchandise display focused on souvenirs. You can store your carrier and do full shopping within 3 hours before heading to the airport. If you have a carrier, use the 1st-floor storage lockers first.

The Landscape of Japan's 'Guk-rul' Course

The number of Japanese tourists visiting Korea surged to approximately 2.8 million in 2024 and over 1 million in Q1 2025. This combines pre-COVID recovery + increased Korea-Japan LCC flights (~20 routes) + a weak yen + accumulated K-culture exposure. With a re-visit rate of 44.8% and an average of 7 visits (overwhelmingly #1 among foreign segments) — Japanese tourists follow a pattern of "multiple short, fulfilling repeat visits" rather than "one full experience." This is why everyday consumption experiences (cafes, street food, markets, pharmacies, supermarkets) are core to their itinerary, rather than nature or history.

The "Korean Bullet Tour (韓国弾丸ツアー / Hanguk Chongal Yeohaeng)" is a day-trip format that has become signature content on Japanese SNS and YouTube. Depart Tokyo (Narita, Haneda, Kansai) at 02:00 (2 AM) → Arrive Incheon Airport (인천공항) at 05:00 (5 AM) → 07:00 (7 AM) Anguk-dong (안국동) cafe → Myeongdong (명동) → Gwangjang Market (광장시장) → Seongsu (성수) → Lotte Mart (롯데마트) → 22:00 (10 PM) Incheon Airport → Depart Tokyo early morning. A full challenge of 12-14 hours. Short but a full experience of K-cafes + Myeongdong K-beauty + Gwangjang Market + Seongsu, covering all 4 pillars.

The 5 pillars of Anguk (안국)·Samcheong (삼청) Cafe + Myeongdong (명동) Core + Gwangjang Market (광장시장) + Seongsu (성수) + Lotte Mart Seoul Station (롯데마트 서울역점) form the true structure of the Japanese 'Guk-rul' Course. Gangnam (강남), Hongdae (홍대), and Itaewon (이태원) are lower priorities in the main Japanese tourist itinerary — what Japanese visitors crave when visiting Korea is the everyday scenery seen in K-dramas and Mukbangs, and that scenery is most vividly present in Anguk (안국), Myeongdong (명동), Gwangjang (광장), and Seongsu (성수).

From T6.6 cluster signal mining, the 12 signature Japanese tourist spots have an average review_count of 8,000+ + an average Japanese review frequency of 30%+ — estimated foreigner demand-supply gap of 600+ (within Top 3). Content coverage is less than 20% (while English/Chinese foreigner guides exist, a meta-guide specifically for the Japanese segment is lacking).

The true differentiator of Japan's 'Guk-rul' Course: Unlike other foreign segments (China, Southeast Asia, Europe), it targets enthusiasts who have already visited Korea an average of 7 times. This is a deep dive for repeat Japanese enthusiasts, different from the first-time foreigner meta-guide (#176 walkable). Many Japanese hotspots like Isaac Toast (이삭토스트), abalone porridge (전복죽), minari (미나리), and pharmacies are surprisingly unknown even to Koreans.

① Anguk (안국)·Samcheong (삼청) Cafe (2)

1. London Bagel Museum Anguk (런던베이글뮤지엄 안국)

  • Signature: #1 essential cafe for Japanese tourists. Japanese queues from 07:00 (7 AM) on weekdays. ₩93.8 billion in sales last year (Top among all Korean cafes). 3-minute walk from Anguk Station (안국역, Line 3).
  • Foreigner Friendliness: ★★★★★ — Strongest Japanese SNS·TikTok presence, English·Japanese menus
  • Recommended Menu: Signature bagels (Plain, Itta Olive, Parmesan), 6 cream cheese pairings
  • Tip: Opens at 07:00 (7 AM) on weekdays / Shorter queues before 09:00 (9 AM). Afternoons can have 1-2 hour waits. Jamsil (잠실) and Yeouido (여의도) branches have lower Japanese visitor ratios — Anguk (안국) main branch is the #1 Japanese 'Guk-rul' spot.

2. Cafe Onion Anguk (카페 어니언 안국)

  • Signature: 1920s hanok (한옥, traditional Korean house) cafe. 'Pandoro' (팡도르) is a Japanese signature in reviews. Staff fluent in Japanese.
  • Foreigner Friendliness: ★★★★★ — Hanok (한옥) + Japanese language support, #1 for Instagram
  • Recommended Menu: Pandoro (sugar powder bread), Signature Latte, Seasonal Desserts
  • Tip: 7-minute walk from Anguk Station (안국역, Line 3), 8 Yulgok-ro 3-gil — 5-minute walk from London Bagel Anguk (런던베이글 안국). Possible to combine these two cafes into one course. Take photos in the hanok (한옥) garden — a Japanese Instagram signature.

② Myeongdong (명동) Breakfast·Shopping (5)

3. Isaac Toast Myeongdong (이삭토스트 명동)

  • Signature: Koreans may not know it well, but all Japanese do. Strongest exposure on Japanese TV and TikTok. Weak search results on Naver Maps, Myeongdong (명동) branch appears when searching "イサックトースト" in Japanese on Google. About 70% of conversations in the store are in Japanese.
  • Foreigner Friendliness: ★★★★★ — Japanese cluster, staff accustomed to Japanese language support
  • Recommended Menu: Bacon Egg Toast, Ham Cheese Toast (sweet egg similar to Japanese egg dishes)
  • Tip: Within walking distance of Myeongdong Station (명동역, Line 4). Queues from 08:00-10:00 (8 AM - 10 AM) — Japanese pattern of first meal after early morning arrival. Good value for money at ₩4,500~6,500 (approx. $3.30-4.80 / 450-650 JPY) per person.

4. Myeongdong Abalone Porridge (명동 전복죽)

  • Signature: A signature breakfast for Japanese tourists — its savory, not salty taste fits Japanese palates. Japanese menu from the entrance.
  • Foreigner Friendliness: ★★★★☆ — Japanese menu, English language support
  • Recommended Menu: Basic Abalone Porridge (Jeonbokjuk) (₩17,000~) (approx. $12.50 / 90 RMB / 1,700 JPY), Vegetable Porridge, Pumpkin Porridge — Japanese introduction to Korean porridge.
  • Tip: Myeongdong (명동) core — within walking distance of Myeongdong Station (명동역, Line 4). Popular with Japanese tourists, often with queues even during off-peak hours (10:00-12:00 (10 AM - 12 PM) and 14:00-16:00 (2 PM - 4 PM)). Featured in Japanese media as a "韓国のお粥 (Korean porridge)" signature.

5. Olive Young Myeongdong Town (올리브영 명동타운점)

  • Signature: 95% foreign sales — #1 store nationwide. ₩1 trillion in foreign sales in 2024. #1 K-beauty shopping spot for Japanese.
  • Foreigner Friendliness: ★★★★★ — English·Chinese·Japanese guidance, foreign language support
  • Recommended Categories: Full K-beauty sets (skincare, makeup, body), seasonal limited editions, mask pack boxes
  • Tip: 5-minute walk from Myeongdong Station (명동역, Line 4) Exit 9. Peak hours are 17:00-21:00 (5 PM - 9 PM) — quieter in the morning and at lunchtime. Utilize tax-free designated aisles.

6. Daiso Myeongdong (다이소 명동역점)

  • Signature: 12-story mega-store — many foreigners. Especially the beauty corner is hot. Good value for money K-lifestyle shopping at ₩1,000-5,000.
  • Foreigner Friendliness: ★★★★★ — Foreign language guidance, clear price labeling
  • Recommended Categories: K-beauty value items, Korean character goods, kitchenware, souvenirs
  • Tip: Directly connected to Myeongdong Station (명동역, Line 4) Exit 6. A full 12-story tour takes 1 hour+ — #1 carrier shopping spot for Japanese. Tax-free available for purchases over ₩50,000.

7. Myeongdong Pharmacy District (명동 약국가) — +19 new in 6 months

  • Signature: 19 pharmacies rapidly increased in Myeongdong (명동) in the last 6 months. Foreign pharmacy sales reached ₩141.4 billion last year — +142% compared to the previous year. A signature shopping spot for Japanese.
  • Foreigner Friendliness: ★★★★☆ — Japanese guidance, pharmacists with foreign language support
  • Recommended Categories: Functional ointments·creams (atopy, acne), skincare (centella, cica), traditional Korean nutritional supplements, diet aids
  • Tip: Pharmacy cluster around Myeongdong (명동) Exit 6. Pharmacies ≠ regular cosmetics stores — pharmacist prescription and professional recommendations are high-value for Japanese. Olive Young (올리브영) (mask packs) + Pharmacy (functional skincare) combined shopping pattern.

③ Gwangjang Market (광장시장)·Seongsu (성수) Food Tour (3)

8. Jin Ok-hwa Halmae Dakhanmari Gwangjang (광장시장 진옥화 할매 닭한마리)

  • Signature: #1 for Japanese K-nabe (hot pot) enthusiasts. Similar to Japanese nabe but combined with Korean-style sauce and kalguksu (칼국수, knife-cut noodles). Abundant foreigner reviews.
  • Foreigner Friendliness: ★★★★★ — English·Japanese menus, many foreigners
  • Recommended Menu: Dakhanmari (닭한마리, whole chicken soup) (2-4 servings), Kalguksu (칼국수) noodles, extra garlic, tteok (떡, rice cake)
  • Tip: Gwangjang Market (광장시장) core — 5-minute walk from Jongno 5-ga Station (종로5가역, Line 1). Peak hours 18:00-21:00 (6 PM - 9 PM) → quieter during weekday lunch 11:00-14:00 (11 AM - 2 PM) or 14:00-17:00 (2 PM - 5 PM). Adding Korean-style garlic and sauce is the signature way Japanese enjoy it.

9. Gwangjang Market Street Food Trio (광장시장 마약김밥·꽈배기·빈대떡)

  • Signature: Japanese must-eat trio at Gwangjang Market (광장시장). Kkwabaegi (꽈배기, twisted donut) has a tteok (떡, rice cake) texture, making it a Japanese favorite. Classic Mayak Gimbap (마약김밥, "drug gimbap" with mustard + mini gimbap) + Bindaetteok (빈대떡, mung bean pancake).
  • Foreigner Friendliness: ★★★★★ — Abundant Japanese media exposure, photo menus
  • Recommended Menu: Kkwabaegi (꽈배기) (fast-moving queue), Mayak Gimbap (마약김밥) + Janchi Guksu (잔치국수, feast noodles), Nokdu Bindaetteok (녹두빈대떡, mung bean pancake) + Makgeolli (막걸리, Korean rice wine).
  • Tip: 3-minute walk from Jongno 5-ga Station (종로5가역, Line 1). Kkwabaegi (꽈배기) queue is long but moves fast — 15-20 minute wait. Bindaetteok (빈대떡) + Makgeolli (막걸리) is quieter on weekdays 14:00-17:00 (2 PM - 5 PM).

10. Neungdong Minari Gomtang·Minari Yukhoe Bibimbap (능동 미나리곰탕·미나리육회비빔밥)

  • Signature: Japanese minari (미나리, water celery) trend + 15-year ban on yukhoe (육회, beef tartare) = minari + yukhoe combination is a must-eat when visiting Korea. Frequently visited by Japanese celebrities.
  • Foreigner Friendliness: ★★★★☆ — Abundant Japanese reviews, English menu
  • Recommended Menu: Minari Yukhoe Bibimbap (미나리육회비빔밥) (sesame oil + minari + sweet and sour sauce), Minari Gomtang (미나리 곰탕) (fragrant + stops before becoming too strong)
  • Tip: Seongsu (성수)·Neungdong (능동) area — 10-minute walk from Children's Grand Park Station (어린이대공원역, Line 7) or 12-minute walk from Ttukseom Station (뚝섬역, Line 2). Japanese Instagram signature — recommended to enter popular restaurants before 13:00 (1 PM).

④ Lotte Mart (롯데마트)·Souvenirs (2)

11. Gwangjang Kimchi & Sesame Oil (광장시장 김치·참기름)

  • Signature: Japanese Gwangjang Market (광장시장) unexpected shopping item #1 — Kimchi (김치) + Sesame Oil (참기름). Kimchi (김치) is essential despite carrier packing difficulties, sesame oil products often have English·Japanese labels (reflecting foreign demand).
  • Foreigner Friendliness: ★★★★☆ — English·Japanese labels, vacuum packaging options
  • Recommended Categories: Vacuum-packed kimchi (kimchi (김치) whole cabbage, chonggak (총각) radish, kkakdugi (깍두기) diced radish), sesame oil (bottles, small sizes), sesame salt, gochujang (고추장, red chili paste)
  • Tip: Gwangjang Market (광장시장) kimchi (김치) corner + sesame oil (참기름) corner cluster. Recommended for last-day shopping before heading to the airport — due to kimchi (김치) freshness + smell. Olive Young (올리브영) + Cafe Onion (카페 어니언) also have branches inside Gwangjang Market (광장시장), allowing market shopping + cafe breaks.

12. Lotte Mart Seoul Station (롯데마트 서울역점)

  • Signature: 40%+ foreign sales — #1 last-minute shopping spot before heading to the airport. International shipping + currency exchange + international parcel service + 3-hour free luggage storage + merchandise display rearranged for souvenirs.
  • Foreigner Friendliness: ★★★★★ — English·Chinese·Japanese guidance, foreign language support, carrier-friendly
  • Recommended Categories: Korean snacks (Pepero, Mongshell Matcha, Choco Pie Matcha, Chaltteok Pie), Banana Milk, Biyott, sugar-free·zero-sugar snacks, beauty·nutritional supplements, Korean culture goods ('Eat Samgyeopsal' T-shirts)
  • Tip: 3-minute walk from Seoul Station (서울역, Line 1·4·Airport Railroad). 3-hour course before heading to the airport — carrier storage on the 1st floor + full shopping + currency exchange + international shipping options. Foreigner-specific aisle order = souvenirs → snacks → beverages → beauty → nutritional supplements.
