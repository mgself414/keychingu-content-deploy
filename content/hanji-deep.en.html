Korean Hanji Travel Guide — Jeonju Traditional Hanji Center, Jeonju Hanji Museum and Jeonju Hanji Culture Festival, Wanju Daeseung Hanji Village, Wonju Hanji Theme Park, Goesan Hanji Experience Museum, Andong Hanji, Seoul Jongi Nara Museum

Subtitle: Jeonju Traditional Hanji Center (전주전통한지원, closed Mondays, hanji-making experience) · Jeonju Hanji Museum (전주한지박물관, free, closed Sundays and Mondays) · Jeonju Hanji Culture Festival (전주한지문화축제, October 8~10, 2026, ended) · Wanju Daeseung Hanji Village (완주 대승한지마을, closed Mondays) · Wonju Hanji Theme Park (원주한지테마파크, closed Mondays) · Goesan Hanji Experience Museum (괴산 한지체험박물관, reservation-only experiences) · Andong Hanji (안동한지, hanji making 5,000 won) · Jongi Nara Museum (종이나라박물관, adults 2,000 won) — confirmed via Korea Tourism Organization tourism data (TourAPI)

🎯 How to Use This Guide

This guide gathers 7 museums, villages and factories plus 1 festival where you can see hanji (traditional Korean paper) and paper culture, or try making hanji yourself. The Jeonju Hanok Village area is also covered in #723 Jeonju Hanji Village and Hanok Village Guide and #384 Jeonju Guide.

The operating details are copied from values registered in the Korea Tourism Organization tourism data (TourAPI). Closed days differ from place to place, so check the table first. In particular, the Jeonju Hanji Museum is closed on both Sundays and Mondays.

📍 Jeonju — Jeonju Traditional Hanji Center, Jeonju Hanji Museum and Jeonju Hanji Culture Festival

The Jeonju Traditional Hanji Center (100-10 Hanji-gil, Wansan-gu, Jeonju-si (전주시 완산구 한지길 100-10), Pungnam-dong 3-ga (풍남동3가), ☎ 010-8959-7757) is, according to the Korea Tourism Organization, a place that reproduces traditional hanji-making techniques to produce hanji, and it exports more than 80% of the hanji it produces to Japan. You can learn the hanji-making process, including scooping the paper, adding patterns and drying.

  • Opening hours: 09:00~18:00. Closed days: Every Monday.
  • Experiences: Hanji doll crafts, cloisonné (chilbo) crafts, hanji-making (scooping) experience and more.
  • Parking: Not available.

The Jeonju Hanji Museum (59 Palbok-ro, Deokjin-gu, Jeonju-si (전주시 덕진구 팔복로 59), ☎ 063-210-8103) was, according to the Korea Tourism Organization, renamed from the paper museum to its current name in 2007. It holds hanji-related relics such as hanji crafts, hanji-making tools, old documents and old books, and it changes the theme of its special exhibition every year.

  • Opening hours: 09:00~17:00 (last entry 16:30).
  • Closed days: Every Monday, Sundays, January 1, Seollal and Chuseok holidays.
  • Admission: Free.

Jeonju Hanji Culture Festival — 2026

The Jeonju Hanji Culture Festival began in 1997 and was held in 2026 on October 8~10 at the Korea Traditional Culture Center (한국전통문화전당) (20 Hyeonmu 1-gil, Wansan-gu, Jeonju-si (전주시 완산구 현무1길 20)) (☎ 063-281-1553).

  • Hours: 10:00~20:00. Admission: Free.
  • Programs: According to the information registered with the Korea Tourism Organization, they included a Hanji Culture Zone (experiences and sales), a Hanji Experiment Zone, a Hanji Play Zone, a hanji lantern exhibition, an exhibition of award-winning works from the 32nd National Hanji Craft Contest, an exhibition from the 6th National Children's Hanji Art Contest and more.

📍 Wanju — Daeseung Hanji Village

Wanju Daeseung Hanji Village (18 Bogeom-gil, Soyang-myeon, Wanju-gun (완주군 소양면 보검길 18), ☎ 063-242-1001) is, according to the Korea Tourism Organization, a village with 9 hanji factories and more than 10 holders of hanji-production skills. It has a Hanji Life History Exhibition Hall and an experience center where you can make paper yourself or make hanji crafts.

  • Opening hours: 09:00~18:00, winter season (November~February) 09:00~17:30.
  • Closed days: Every Monday.
  • Experiences: Papermaking experience, making a framed hanji sheet, making hanji rubber shoes, making kites and more (all ages).
  • Parking: Available.

📍 Wonju — Wonju Hanji Theme Park

Wonju Hanji Theme Park (151 Hanji Gongwon-gil, Musil-dong, Wonju-si (원주시 한지공원길 151, 무실동), ☎ 033-734-4739) is a hanji culture complex that opened on September 9, 2010, and has been run under contract by the Hanji Culture Foundation (한지문화재단) since 2011. According to the Korea Tourism Organization, the first floor has a Hanji History Room where you can look at the history and making process of hanji, and an art shop where you can see products made from hanji. With a reservation, you can join a hanji experience program.

  • Opening hours: 09:00~18:00.
  • Closed days: Every Monday, January 1, Seollal and Chuseok holidays.
  • Experiences: Hanji experience, hanji scooping and more.
  • Parking: Available.

📍 Goesan — Hanji Experience Museum

The Goesan Hanji Experience Museum (233 Wonpung-ro, Yeonpung-myeon, Goesan-gun (괴산군 연풍면 원풍로 233), ☎ 043-832-3223) is, according to the Korea Tourism Organization, the learning center of Sinpung Hanji (신풍한지), which makes hanji from domestic paper mulberry. With a reservation you can try traditional hanji scooping, wildflower paper scooping, making hanji coasters, tearing hanji and decorating with it, and more. A hanji museum run by director Ahn Chi-yong (안치용), a hanji master and holder of intangible heritage, is also on the site, where you can see hanji-related relics such as hanji-making tools.

  • Opening hours: 09:00~18:00. Closed days: Every Monday.
  • Experiences: The experience program is decided after a phone consultation.
  • Parking: Available.

📍 Andong — Andong Hanji

Andong Hanji (13 Nabau-gil, Pungsan-eup, Andong-si (안동시 풍산읍 나바우길 13), ☎ 054-858-7007) is, according to the Korea Tourism Organization, a traditional hanji factory at the entrance to Hahoe Village, and it makes more than 60 kinds of paper from domestic paper mulberry, including calligraphy paper (hwaseonji), window paper (changhoji), backing paper (baejeopji), book paper (chaekji) and colored hanji.

  • Opening hours: 09:00~17:00.
  • Closed days: Open year-round (only factory tours are available on weekends).
  • Experiences: Hanji making 5,000 won (all ages).
  • Parking: Available (about 50 small cars).

📍 Seoul — Jongi Nara Museum

The Jongi Nara Museum (166 Jangchungdan-ro, Jung-gu, Seoul (서울 중구 장충단로 166), Jangchungdong 1-ga (장충동1가), ☎ 02-2279-7901) is, according to the Korea Tourism Organization, a museum built to preserve, exhibit and teach origami and paper culture. The visit is registered as taking about 40 minutes.

  • Opening hours: 09:30~18:00 (last entry 17:30).
  • Closed days: Every Sunday, public holidays.
  • Admission: Adults 2,000 won, teenagers and children 1,500 won, seniors (ages 65 and over) 1,000 won. Free for ages 36 months and under and others.

⚠ 3 Things to Know Before You Go

  1. Many places are closed on Mondays — the Jeonju Traditional Hanji Center, Jeonju Hanji Museum, Wanju Daeseung Hanji Village, Wonju Hanji Theme Park and Goesan Hanji Experience Museum are all registered as closed every Monday. Andong Hanji is open year-round.
  2. The Seoul Jongi Nara Museum and the Jeonju Hanji Museum are closed on Sundays — the Jongi Nara Museum is also closed on public holidays.
  3. Some places require a reservation or consultation for experiences — Wonju Hanji Theme Park takes reservations, and the Goesan Hanji Experience Museum decides the experience after a phone consultation.

⚙ FAQ 2

Q. Where can I scoop hanji myself?

A. The places with hanji-scooping or papermaking experiences in the Korea Tourism Organization registered information are the Jeonju Traditional Hanji Center (hanji scooping), Wanju Daeseung Hanji Village (papermaking experience), Wonju Hanji Theme Park (hanji scooping), the Goesan Hanji Experience Museum (traditional hanji scooping) and Andong Hanji (hanji making 5,000 won).

Q. Is there an admission fee?

A. The Jongi Nara Museum is registered at 2,000 won for adults, and the Jeonju Hanji Museum and the Jeonju Hanji Culture Festival (ended) are registered as free. Andong Hanji has only the experience fee (hanji making 5,000 won) registered, and the rest have no admission item in the Korea Tourism Organization registered information.
