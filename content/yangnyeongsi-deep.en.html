🎯 How to Use This Guide

From Exit 2 of Jegi-dong Station (제기동역) on Line 1, you can link Seoul Yangnyeongsi (서울약령시) (the herbal medicine street), the Seoul Korean Medicine Promotion Center (서울한방진흥센터) (museum and foot bath) and Gyeongdong Market (경동시장) (groceries, night market, Starbucks Gyeongdong 1960 (스타벅스 경동1960)) into a half-day walking route. Hours and addresses below are only the values read on 2026-10-07 from the official pages of Seoul City's "Naesonane Seoul", Dongdaemun-gu and the Seoul Korean Medicine Promotion Center. Anything the official pages do not post, such as the museum fee, a Monday closure or the foot-bath price, is marked "unconfirmed." We dropped three claims from the older guide because they differ from the official record: the "600-year history," "moved from Gwanghwamun before the 1960s," and "Jegi-dong Station Exit 1."

🌿 Seoul Yangnyeongsi — Korea's Largest Herbal Market

  • Seoul City's official description: the largest herbal market in Korea, with about 1,000 related shops, and about two-thirds of Korea's herbal materials and about three-quarters of Seoul's ginseng and honey pass through it (Naesonane Seoul, 2023).
  • History (official): in the 1970s, herbal merchants from Jongno 4-ga and 5-ga (Jongno (종로)) moved here; the area was recognized as Seoul Yangnyeongsi in 1995 and designated a culture-and-tourism market in 2013.
  • Open 09:00–18:00 at 10 Yangnyeong Jungang-ro. It is a wholesale street, so for small quantities go to shops with a retail sign. Prices are not posted, so we list none.
  • Tip for foreign visitors: import rules for herbal medicine and health food differ by country, so check your own customs rules before you fly home (this guide does not state any rules).

🏛 Seoul Korean Medicine Promotion Center — Museum of Oriental Medicine + Foot Bath

  • Run by Dongdaemun-gu. Mar–Oct 10:00–18:00, Nov–Feb 10:00–17:00; closed Jan 1, Lunar New Year and Chuseok (official; the Chuseok closure on 9/25 in 2026 was announced). We could not read from the official page whether it closes on Mondays or what the admission fee is — check on site.
  • Exhibits: 360 medicinal herbs and 420 artifacts. Programs (official list): herbal foot-bath experience, Bojewon (보제원) Korean medicine experience and Bojewon mobile clinic. The foot-bath fee is not posted officially (a secondary source says 6,000 won).
  • The older guide's "free herbal tea tasting, Sasang constitution consultation and English-speaking staff" are not on the official page, so we dropped them.
  • 26 Yangnyeong Jungang-ro, Jegi-dong Station Exit 2.

🛒 Gyeongdong Market — Day Market, Night Market, Gyeongdong 1960

  • Opened in 1960, 08:30–18:00 (same on weekdays and weekends), 3 Gosanja-ro 36-gil, 381 m from Jegi-dong Station Exit 2 (Seoul City and Dongdaemun-gu, official). A grocery market for herbs, dried fish, vegetables, fruit, rice cakes and more.
  • Gyeongdong 1960 Night Market (경동1960 야시장): Fri–Sun 18:00–23:00 (Seoul City, official). See the herbal street by day and come back in the evening to make it a full day.
  • Starbucks Gyeongdong 1960 (스타벅스 경동1960): a store on floors 3–4, converted from the Gyeongdong Theater that closed in 1994 (Seoul City, official). Sources split between 09:00–23:00 and 09:00–22:00, so the hours are unconfirmed.
  • Gyeongdong Gwangseong Arcade (경동광성상가): founded in 1948, mostly food (Dongdaemun-gu, official). The older guide's "clothing and general-goods arcade" was wrong.

🚇 Getting There · Half-Day Route

  • Jegi-dong Station (Line 1) Exit 2 → Seoul Yangnyeongsi street (30 min) → Seoul Korean Medicine Promotion Center (1 hour including the foot bath) → Gyeongdong Market (groceries and snacks, 1 hour) → on Fri–Sun, Gyeongdong 1960 Night Market (from 18:00).
  • The official pages do not say whether the museum closes on Mondays, so call ahead. A secondary source says many herbal shops close on Sundays, so weekday or Saturday daytime is the safer choice.

⚠ 5 Common Mistakes

1. Leaving by Exit 1 — the herbal street, museum and Gyeongdong Market are all by Exit 2 (official).

2. Arriving at the museum at 09:00 — it opens at 10:00 (and closes at 17:00 in Nov–Feb).

3. Visiting the herbal street on a Sunday — many shops are closed (secondary source). Go on a weekday or Saturday daytime.

4. Expecting wholesale prices for small amounts — buy at shops with a retail sign.

5. Carrying herbal medicine to any country — check your own customs rules first.

⚙ FAQ 6

1. What is the museum admission fee? — Not on the official page. Check on site (a secondary source says it is a small amount).

2. What about the foot bath? — The herbal foot-bath experience is on the official program list. The fee is not posted.

3. When is the night market? — Gyeongdong 1960 Night Market, Fri–Sun 18:00–23:00.

4. Until what time is Starbucks Gyeongdong 1960 open? — Unconfirmed (sources conflict: 09:00–22:00 vs. 23:00). Check the store page.

5. What about English? — Whether the museum has English guidance or staff is not posted officially. Bring a translation app.

6. Why is it called the "largest in Korea"? — That is the wording in Seoul City's official material (about 1,000 shops, about two-thirds of Korea's herbal materials).
