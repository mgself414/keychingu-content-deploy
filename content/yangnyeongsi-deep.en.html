Why Yangnyeongsi Now — a 9-Market Cluster + Korea's #1 Herbal Medicine District

The Cheongnyangni (청량리)·Jegi-dong (제기동) area is an integrated cluster of 9 markets — Gyeongdong (경동) and Cheongnyangni general markets, agricultural and fisheries, fruit, the Yangnyeongsi herbal-medicine district, and more. Gyeongdong Market's Yangnyeongsi (약령시) = Korea's #1 wholesale herbal-medicine district. Cross-fit with K-Medical tourism (#404·#593).

There are two reasons foreigners are drawn to this area. First, 9 markets sit packed within a 500m radius of a single subway station, so you can cover all of them in a day. Second, Yangnyeongsi isn't a tourist reenactment — it's a living, working market run by real herbalists and wholesalers with 600 years of history. The Joseon-era Yangnyeongsi near Gwanghwamun (광화문) relocated to Jegi-dong in the 1960s and continues to operate there today.

This matrix breaks down into 3 Yangnyeongsi herbal-medicine picks + 3 Cheongnyangni market picks + 3 agriculture/fisheries/dawn picks + 3 foreigner/experience picks = 12 picks across 4 axes.

12 Picks, 4 Axes

Axis 1: Yangnyeongsi Herbal Medicine (3 Picks) — Korea's #1

1. Gyeongdong Market Yangnyeongsi

Korea's largest wholesale herbal-medicine market. Centered on Yangnyeongjungang-ro (약령중앙로) in Dongdaemun-gu (동대문구). 600 years of history, roughly 1,000 herbal-medicine shops and wholesalers packed together. Ginseng·red ginseng·herbal medicine·oriental health foods available at wholesale prices.

  • Hours: Weekdays and Saturday, 08:00-18:00. Sundays vary by shop (many closed, confirm on-site).
  • Prices (as of 2026): Herbal ingredients sold individually — red ginseng, 100g: ₩30,000-80,000; danggwi (당귀, angelica root), 100g: ₩5,000-15,000; gugija (구기자, goji berry), 100g: ₩4,000-10,000 (varies by grade and origin). Full prescribed herbal-medicine packages: ₩250,000-420,000 (includes clinic prescription).
  • Foreigner fit: As a wholesale market, Korean is the default language. Few vendors speak a foreign language. Visiting the Oriental Medicine Promotion Center first, then moving here, is recommended.
  • Timing: 10am-12pm is uncrowded with vendors more attentive. Most close by evening.
  • Common foreigner mistake: Some shops require a minimum wholesale quantity. If you want a retail (small) purchase, confirm with somae doemnikka? (소매 됩니까?, "Can I buy retail?")

2. Seoul Oriental Medicine Promotion Center

26 Yangnyeongjungang-ro, Dongdaemun-gu. The top first stop for foreigners new to Yangnyeongsi. Full cultural hall, hands-on programs, and foreign-language guidance.

  • Hours: Tue-Sun 09:00-18:00, closed Mondays.
  • Experience programs: Herbal-tea tastings, ingredient-identification activities, herbal foot baths, and sasang-constitution (사상체질, a traditional Korean body-type theory) consultations. Some programs cost ₩3,000-10,000.
  • English availability: 1-2 English-speaking staff on hand (varies by visit time; English brochures available).
  • Foreigner fit: ★★★★★. Foreign-language signage, free exhibit areas, and herbal-ingredient displays make it the ideal entry point. No prior knowledge of oriental medicine needed.
  • Access: Jegi-dong Station, Exit 2, 5-min walk.

3. Yangnyeongsi Clinic Cluster

Dozens of oriental-medicine clinics cluster in and around Gyeongdong Market's Yangnyeongsi. Herbal prescriptions, acupuncture, and sasang-constitution consultations available.

  • Hours: Varies by clinic, generally 10:00-18:00.
  • Foreigner care: Few clinics offer foreign-language service. Ask the Oriental Medicine Promotion Center's front desk for a clinic with interpretation support.
  • Prices: First consultation ₩30,000-50,000 / acupuncture session ₩30,000-80,000 / full herbal-medicine course ₩250,000-500,000.
  • Tip: Interpretation and booking support available when bundled with a medical-tourism package (#404).

Axis 2: Cheongnyangni Markets (3 Picks)

4. Cheongnyangni General Market

The administrative hub of the 9 markets around Cheongnyangni Station. Less a single market than a cluster of adjoining markets. Mixed clothing·dried goods·fruit·seafood·produce.

  • Hours: 06:00-20:00 (evening food stalls run until 22:00).
  • Foreigner fit: ★★★☆☆. A market for daily local life; foreign-language support is limited. Best for browsing and soaking up the atmosphere.
  • Price level: Roughly 60-80% of standard retail on groceries (for bulk purchases).
  • Timing: Lively 7-9am; some stalls start closing after 2pm.

5. Cheongnyangni Traditional Market

Adjacent to the General Market. An alleyway market centered on long-established shops. Scattered general goods·prepared food·old-school restaurants.

  • Hours: Varies by shop, typically 07:00-18:00.
  • Foreigner fit: ★★★☆☆. Deeply tied to local daily life. Suited to photography and atmosphere exploration.
  • Note: Only one shared restroom in the market. Check before you go.

6. Cheongnyangni Gwangseong Mall

An indoor arcade-style shopping center inside the Cheongnyangni market area. Centered on clothing·general goods·household items.

  • Hours: 10:00-18:00, some shops closed Sundays.
  • Foreigner fit: ★★☆☆☆. More useful for understanding the market's layout than for shopping itself.

Axis 3: Agriculture, Fisheries & Dawn (3 Picks)

7. Cheongnyangni Agricultural & Marine Products Market

70 stalls across 4 zones. Vegetables·fruit·seafood·dried goods·seasonings. Wholesale and retail side by side.

  • Hours: 04:00-17:00 (04:00-08:00 is peak wholesale time).
  • Prices: 20-40% below retail. Further negotiation possible for bulk purchases.
  • Foreigner fit: ★★★☆☆. A dawn visit gives a firsthand look at local life. After 9am, casual retail shopping gets easier.
  • Access: 3-min walk from Cheongnyangni Station.
  • Cross: Pairs well with the #587 dawn-market series.

8. Cheongnyangni Fisheries Market

The seafood section within the Agricultural & Marine Products Market. A mix of live fish and dried goods.

  • Hours: 05:00-16:00.
  • Tip: For dried goods specifically, Jungbu Market (중부시장, #597) has deeper specialization. This market is better for fresh seafood.

9. Cheongnyangni Fruit Market

Arguably Seoul's #1 for value-for-money fruit. Retail built directly on wholesale volume.

  • Hours: 05:00-18:00.
  • Sample prices (as of 2026, seasonal): Strawberries, 1kg: ₩5,000-10,000 / apples, 5kg: ₩12,000-20,000 / Korean melons (참외), 5kg: ₩8,000-15,000.
  • Foreigner fit: ★★★★☆. Low language barrier. You can buy just by pointing. Small quantities are generally fine too.
  • Timing: 7-10am for peak freshness.

Axis 4: Foreigners & Experience (3 Picks)

10. Foreign-Language Pharmacies & Yangnyeongsi Culture Experience

Numerous pharmacies around Yangnyeongsi serve foreign languages (EN/CN/JP) — a safe channel for buying herbal-based health foods: red ginseng extract, gongjindan (공진단), and more.

  • Gongjindan prices (as of 2026): 1 pill: ₩30,000-80,000 (pharmacy-sold, quality varies widely by grade — confirm the source).
  • Red ginseng extract: ₩50,000-150,000 (varies by volume/brand).
  • Common foreigner mistake: Buying without confirming ingredients/dosage can mean customs trouble back home. Ask the pharmacist for an English ingredient list.

11. K-Medical Medical-Tourism Cross (#404·#593)

A growing trend of foreigners linking the Yangnyeongsi cluster into medical-tourism packages. A one-day course combining clinic treatment + Yangnyeongsi ingredient shopping + a cultural experience at the Promotion Center.

  • Booking channel: Seoul Medical Tourism Information Center (서울의료관광정보센터) — near a general hospital with a Line 1/2 transfer, English support. Confirm on-site.
  • Interpretation support: Dongdaemun-gu Public Health Center's (동대문구 보건소) foreign-patient medical interpretation service (advance booking, free). Confirm on-site.

12. Dried Goods & Seasoning Shops (#597 Cross)

Dried-goods and seasoning shops inside Gyeongdong Market. Smaller selection than Jungbu Market (#597), but convenient to combine with a Yangnyeongsi visit.

  • Popular items: Dried chili peppers·sesame oil·perilla oil·anchovies·dried kelp.
  • Packaging: Vacuum-sealed packing available on request; check with your airline about carry-on/checked-bag rules before flying home.

3 Common Mistakes Foreigners Make

Mistake 1: Visiting Yangnyeongsi on a Sunday

Many Yangnyeongsi herbal-medicine shops and clinics close on Sundays. If you're visiting on a weekend, pick Saturday, or confirm the Seoul Oriental Medicine Promotion Center (open Tue-Sun) is open before planning your route.

Mistake 2: Buying Herbal Medicine/Health Foods Without Checking Export Rules

Customs regulations for herbal ingredients and health supplements vary by country. Animal-derived ingredients in particular (deer antler, bear bile, etc.) may fall under CITES, and some countries require pesticide-residue certification or import permits even for plant extracts. Ask the pharmacist for an English ingredient list before buying, and check your home country's customs rules.

Mistake 3: Trying to Buy Small Quantities at a Wholesale Market

Some stalls at Cheongnyangni Agricultural & Marine Products Market and Gyeongdong Yangnyeongsi have minimum-quantity or minimum-spend requirements. Confirm whether retail purchases are possible first. Retail-focused stalls tend to be marked separately or positioned near the entrance.

Practical Info

Transportation

  • Yangnyeongsi: Line 1, Jegi-dong Station, Exit 1, 3-min walk (into Yangnyeongsi), Exit 2, 5-min walk (to the Oriental Medicine Promotion Center).
  • Cheongnyangni market cluster: Line 1·Gyeongui-Jungang Line·Gyeongchun Line·Suin-Bundang Line·KTX all serve Cheongnyangni Station — a major greater-Seoul rail hub, excellent access.
  • Connecting the two zones: Jegi-dong Station to Cheongnyangni Station is 1 stop on Line 1 (2 min). About a 15-minute walk if you go on foot.

Payment

  • Oriental Medicine Promotion Center and large herbal-medicine shops: cards mostly accepted.
  • Small herbal-medicine shops·street stalls·parts of the produce market: cash preferred. Carry ₩50,000-100,000 in cash as a buffer.
  • The Cheongnyangni market zone: cash-heavy. ATMs available inside Cheongnyangni Station.

Language

  • English: limited support at the Oriental Medicine Promotion Center and a handful of foreign-language pharmacies.
  • Translation apps: Papago (파파고) and Google Translate's camera-translation feature can read herbal-ingredient names and price tags in real time.
  • Emergency: ☎ 1330 (tourist interpretation, free 24h in English·Chinese·Japanese·Vietnamese).

Herbal-Medicine Export Guide

  • Plant-based herbal ingredients — ginseng, danggwi, hwanggi (황기, astragalus root), and similar: most countries allow small, personal-use quantities. Must be dried and sealed.
  • Animal-derived ingredients — deer antler, sea-dog kidney (해구신), bear bile, and similar: may fall under CITES. Confirm ingredients before buying.
  • Extracts and capsules: as finished, processed products, these generally travel more easily. Check for an English label.
  • Gongjindan: confirm whether it contains musk (사향). Musk is a CITES-regulated substance.
  • Rule of thumb: if unsure, call ☎ 1330 and ask in English before you buy.

FAQ 5

Q1. First Visit to Yangnyeongsi — Where to Start?

Start at the Seoul Oriental Medicine Promotion Center (Jegi-dong Station, Exit 2, 5-min walk). The English brochures and exhibit space give you the basics of oriental medicine before you head into Yangnyeongsi's shops — it makes the rest of the visit much easier. There's also a free herbal-tea tasting area.

Q2. I Want an Herbal Prescription — Is There a Foreign-Language Clinic?

The Oriental Medicine Promotion Center's front desk can point you to a clinic with foreign-language support. Alternatively, the Dongdaemun-gu Public Health Center's foreign-patient medical interpretation service (advance booking, free) can accompany you to a clinic visit. Confirm on-site.

Q3. What's the Price Range?

  • Small herbal-ingredient purchases: ₩5,000-30,000 (by item)
  • Full prescribed herbal-medicine course: ₩250,000-420,000
  • Red ginseng extract: ₩50,000-150,000
  • Gongjindan, 1 pill: ₩30,000-80,000 (varies by grade)
  • Small fruit-market purchases: ₩5,000-15,000
  • Produce: 20-40% below retail

Q4. Is a Dawn Visit Worth It?

Cheongnyangni Agricultural & Marine Products Market peaks at 04:00-08:00. If experiencing the local dawn-market atmosphere is your goal, this window fits. Visit Yangnyeongsi and the Promotion Center after 9am.

Q5. Can I Cover Both Yangnyeongsi and the Cheongnyangni Markets in One Day?

Yes. Suggested route: 09:00 Oriental Medicine Promotion Center → 10:00 Gyeongdong Yangnyeongsi → 12:00 lunch in the market → 13:30 Cheongnyangni General Market·Fruit Market → 15:00 Agricultural & Marine Products Market → 16:00 wrap up. The two zones are 1 subway stop (or a 15-minute walk) apart.

5 Tips for Foreign Enthusiasts

1. Line 1 Jegi-dong Station (for Yangnyeongsi) / Cheongnyangni Station (for the market cluster) — the two stations are 1 stop apart.

2. Start at the Seoul Oriental Medicine Promotion Center — the most efficient entry point into Yangnyeongsi. Foreign-language guidance available.

3. At a foreign-language pharmacy, always request an English ingredient list — for customs on the way home.

4. Bring ₩50,000-100,000 in cash — small market shops prefer cash.

5. Try the dawn course (#587 cross) — 04:00-08:00 at Cheongnyangni Agricultural & Marine Products Market is the best window for the authentic local experience.
