Korea Lodging First Night, the Zero-Panic Manual — Shoes, Boiler Panel, Trash Bags, Recycling

Subtitle: The first 10 minutes after you unlock the door at an Airbnb, guesthouse, or hanok stay — taking off your shoes at the entrance, the wall panel's three buttons ([ Heating ], [ Hot Water ], [ Away ]), the pay-as-you-throw trash bag and the 3 ways food waste gets sorted, staying quiet after 10pm, and where to leave your bag at checkout. Whatever varies by neighborhood is flagged "lodging instructions take priority"

ISSUE 06 · #1380 · 2026-09-17 · korea-first-timer series

🎯 How to Read This Manual — The Tag Convention

This reuses the tag convention from #1270 (the kiosk manual).

  • Signage and instruction-sheet text goes in quotes: "Please drain food waste before disposal"
  • A button you need to press goes in brackets: [ Hot Water ]
  • A hands-on action is bold: tie off the bag opening before you put it out

One angle only. Booking your lodging, the check-in app, and luggage storage are different manuals — here it's only what you have to do inside, after the door closes: boiler, trash, noise, bathroom. Every item is tagged by whether it's a rule or a custom ([Rule] = law or ordinance, [Custom] = almost everyone does it, [Optional] = your call). Whatever varies by neighborhood, the lodging's own instructions take priority over this manual.

① The First 10 Minutes — Master Flow

Step Situation What to Do Trap
1 Entrance Take off your shoes and put on the indoor slippers The moment you step in with your shoes still on, the host's read on you changes
2 Find the instructions Photograph the Korean/English instruction sheet on the fridge, door, or table Trash, the wifi password, and the boiler are usually all right there
3 Wall panel Locate the boiler remote, check the current mode (②) In winter, "Away" mode leaves the room cold — switch to "Heating"
4 Trash Locate the trash bag and food-waste bin (③) No bag on hand? Buy one at a convenience store (only your own neighborhood's bag works)
5 Bathroom Check the toilet-paper sign and bidet buttons (⑤) No sign posted usually means you can flush it
6 Night Stay quiet after 10pm (④) Apartment-style lodging carries footsteps and door slams down to the floor below

② Boiler Panel — Just Three Buttons to Know

Heating in Korean lodging is almost always underfloor hot-water heating, ondol (온돌), and a small wall panel (the remote) controls the boiler. Button names and screens differ by manufacturer (Navien, Kiturami, Rinnai, Daesung Celtic), but the basics are the same.

Button Meaning When
[ Heating ] (난방) Turns on floor heating. Adjust room or ondol (water) temperature with ▲▼ The room is cold
[ Hot Water ] (온수) Turns on hot water for the shower and taps only Summer or a warm day — hot water without heating
[ Away ] (외출) Minimum-run mode (freeze protection), saves energy Leaving the place empty, or a winter checkout
[ Reservation ] (예약) / [ Timer ] (타이머) Runs automatically on a schedule You only want it on at night
[ Power ] (전원) Turns everything off Don't turn this off in winter — the pipes can freeze
  • No hot water [Custom] — "[ Hot Water ]" is off, or the hot-water temperature is set low. Hot-water temperature usually adjusts within a 30–60°C range (based on Navien models). Turn it on and wait 1–2 minutes.
  • The floor's too hot — the ondol temperature is set too high. Turn it down with ▼, or switch to "Indoor" (실내) mode.
  • Winter, going out [Custom] — don't turn it off, use "[ Away ]". Most manufacturers build in "auto-run below a set temperature" for freeze protection.
  • Can't read the screen — you only need to find four words: Heating, Hot Water, Away, Reservation. They're all in the ⑦ survival words below.

③ Trash — 3 Types of Bags, the Neighborhood Sets the Rules

Type What Goes In Where to Buy Tag
General trash Seoul generally uses a white pay-as-you-throw bag (jongnyangje bongtu / 종량제 봉투) (check the exact color and size at a convenience store). 10L, 20L, and so on Convenience stores, marts — only your specific district's bag is valid [Rule]
Food waste Dedicated bag / paid sticker / RFID bin (card tag), whichever your neighborhood uses Bags and stickers at convenience stores; RFID uses the lodging's own card [Rule]
Recycling 4 categories — paper, plastic, cans/bottles, vinyl — sorted into a clear bag Any clear bag [Rule]
  • General [Rule] — Without the pay-as-you-throw bag, it won't get collected, and illegal dumping carries a fine (varies by district, roughly ₩100,000 on a first offense). The bag's price is set by the district (e.g., ₩490 for a 20L bag in Gangdong-gu).
  • Food waste (eumsingmul sseuregi / 음식물 쓰레기) [Rule] — "Please drain food waste before disposal" is the one rule everywhere. Vinyl, toothpicks, bottle caps, bones, and shells don't go in. The method varies district by district in Seoul (RFID bins are used in some areas like Geumcheon, Yeongdeungpo, Seocho, and Songpa) — the lodging's instructions take priority.
  • Recycling (jaehwaryong / 재활용) [Rule] — Sort the 4 categories into a clear bag. Apartments have a designated spot; single-family homes and villas put it out front (curbside). Evening to midnight is the customary [Custom] window (e.g., 7pm–midnight in Jung-gu), and the day of the week varies by neighborhood — the lodging's instructions take priority.
  • At checkout [Custom] — Tie off the bag opening and leave it where the instructions point. If the host says "just leave it," leave it as is.

④ Noise & Neighbors — 10pm Is the Line

  • After 10pm [Optional] — Keep footsteps, the washing machine, and the front door quiet. Apartments and villas are sensitive to noise between floors; guesthouses expect a lower conversation volume in shared spaces.
  • The front door [Optional] — Digital-lock doors are heavy and tend to slam. Close it by hand.
  • Hallways & elevators [Custom] — A light nod if you pass a neighbor. Skipping it isn't rude either.

⑤ Bathroom — Toilet Paper and the Bidet

  • Toilet paper [Custom] — Public-restroom trash bins disappeared after 2018, and most lodging expects you to flush it. The exception is an older building with a "toilet paper goes in the bin" sign — the sign has the answer (#1277).
  • Bidet [Custom] — the side panel by the toilet has [ Rinse ] (back), [ Bidet ] (front), [ Dry ], and [ Stop ]. Press it before you sit and water sprays — press after you sit.
  • Drain smell [Optional] — A place left empty for a while can have a dried-out drain trap, which smells. Running the water once usually clears it.

⑥ 30-Second Checkout

1. Winter: set the boiler to "[ Away ]." Summer: just turn off "[ Hot Water ]" (follow the sign if it says otherwise).

2. Tie off the trash bag and leave it at the designated spot.

3. Check the windows and gas; leave the keys or card where the instructions say.

⑧ 3 Panic Scenarios

1. The shower water is cold — "[ Hot Water ]" is off on the panel. Turn it on, raise the temperature with ▲, and try again after 1–2 minutes. Still nothing? Message the host: "Onsu-ga an nawayo" (온수가 안 나와요) — no hot water is coming out.

2. The convenience store says the bag you bought doesn't work — it's another district's bag. Buy one printed with your lodging's district name (e.g., Mapo-gu). Tell the clerk: "I dongne jongnyangje bongtu juseyo" (이 동네 종량제 봉투 주세요) — the local pay-as-you-throw bag, please.

3. You don't know where to put food waste — with no sign posted, asking the host is fastest. Keep it drained in a bag until you hear back. Don't mix it into the general-trash bag.

⑨ Plan B — When You're Lost

  • If the panel's too much, just turn on "[ Heating ]" alone and set it to 22–24°C. Leave everything else alone.
  • Not sure which bag you need? At a convenience store, say: "Ilban sseuregi bongtu 20 liteo juseyo" (일반 쓰레기 봉투 20리터 주세요) — a 20-liter general-trash bag, please — and you'll get that store's local bag.
  • Worried about noise? After 10pm, walk in socks, no slippers.
