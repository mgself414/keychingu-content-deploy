Eating Vegetarian or Vegan in Korea — Hidden Animal-Derived Ingredients, 19 Allergen Labels to Read on Packaging, and How to Use Seoul City's Vegetarian Restaurant List

Even vegetable gimbap can contain ham, eggs and crab-flavored fish cake. Packaged food has 19 labeled allergens, and 10 of them are animal-derived ingredients. 🎯 Who This Is For, and Why This Guide

This guide is made for foreign travelers who do not eat some or all of meat, fish, eggs and dairy products in Korea. It is written so that you can choose food even if you do not know Korean.

This guide is not a restaurant list. It explains how to choose food. It covers the seven things below.

  • Hidden animal-derived ingredients: ingredients that go into food even when it looks like a vegetable dish.
  • Words on packaging: the 19 allergen labels.
  • Seoul City's list of vegetarian restaurants: how to use a list with 838 entries.
  • Temple food (사찰음식): what it prohibits and what it does not prohibit.
  • Certification marks: the 3 criteria of the Korea Agency of Vegan Certification and Services (한국비건인증원).
  • Fast food and convenience stores: Lotteria (롯데리아), Subway (서브웨이) and CU.
  • In-flight meals: the Korean Air (대한항공) request deadline.

Only one restaurant name is given. Only one experience center is given. The list of vegetarian restaurants in Seoul, the request card you show in a restaurant, and temple food and Templestay are in the other guides under Related below.

① Hidden Animal-Derived Ingredients in Korean Food

What this section says is what the official Korea Tourism Organization English website article "Tips for Vegetarians" (read 2026-11-26) writes. The article says it was last revised in October 2023.

1. Broth

The Korea Tourism Organization writes that "Korean food is often prepared with meat and fish broth". Food that shows only vegetables can also contain broth.

2. Kimchi

The Korea Tourism Organization writes that "kimchi seasoning most often contains salted seafood, fish sauce, meat broth".

Regional kimchi can also contain "ingredients such as abalone, cutlass fish, and pork".

Kimchi often comes as a side dish (banchan, 반찬) in Korean restaurants. Even when it looks like a vegetable side dish, it can contain the ingredients above.

3. Bibimbap

Bibimbap is a dish of vegetables and rice mixed with gochujang (고추장) or soy sauce. The Korea Tourism Organization writes: "Most bibimbap menus include meat and eggs as toppings so ask to exclude these ingredients when you order."

4. Jeon (savory pancake)

Jeon is food in which ingredients are coated in batter and fried in oil. The Korea Tourism Organization writes: "some recipes may call for adding eggs in the batter."

5. Vegetable Gimbap

The Korea Tourism Organization writes: "even vegetable gimbap may contain ham, eggs, and crab-flavored meat". Even if the name has "vegetable" in it, look at the filling.

6. Eggs as a Garnish

The Korea Tourism Organization writes: "eggs may be placed on top as a garnish."

Phrases to Use When Ordering

When you ask for an ingredient to be left out, say it like this.

  • "Gogi ppae juseyo (고기 빼 주세요)." — Please leave out the meat.
  • "Dalgyal ppae juseyo (달걀 빼 주세요)." — Please leave out the egg.

A request card you can show to staff on a screen, and more phrases, are in #1300.

② What to Read on Packaging — The 19 Allergen Labels

The official guide of Food Safety Korea (식품안전나라) (posted 2024-07-19, read 2026-11-26) gives 19 items as the allergen labeling targets. On the allergen labeling of packaged food, look for the Korean below.

No. Korean label to look for Meaning Animal-derived ingredient
1 Eggs (난류; legal wording: 알류, poultry only) Eggs of poultry such as chicken ○
2 Milk (우유) Cow's milk ○
3 Buckwheat (메밀) Buckwheat —
4 Peanuts (땅콩) Peanuts —
5 Soybeans (대두) Beans —
6 Wheat (밀) Wheat —
7 Mackerel (고등어) Mackerel ○
8 Crab (게) Crab ○
9 Shrimp (새우) Shrimp ○
10 Pork (돼지고기) Meat of a pig ○
11 Peach (복숭아) Peach —
12 Tomato (토마토) Tomato —
13 Sulfites (아황산류) Sulfites —
14 Walnuts (호두) Walnuts —
15 Chicken (닭고기) Meat of a chicken ○
16 Beef (쇠고기) Meat of a cow ○
17 Squid (오징어) Squid ○
18 Shellfish (조개류; legal wording: includes 굴, 전복, 홍합) Shellfish such as oyster, abalone and mussel ○
19 Pine nuts (잣) Pine nuts —

The items are the 19 in the Food Safety Korea post (2024-07-19), and the legal wording shown in the table is the wording of Attached Table 2 of the Enforcement Rule of the Act on Labeling and Advertising of Food, etc. (식품 등의 표시·광고에 관한 법률 시행규칙).

There are 10 animal-derived ingredients. They are eggs (난류), milk (우유), mackerel (고등어), crab (게), shrimp (새우), pork (돼지고기), chicken (닭고기), beef (쇠고기), squid (오징어) and shellfish (조개류).

This labeling is for allergies. The list has only 19 items. Honey (꿀), anchovy (멸치) and salmon (연어) are not on this list. So even when the allergen labeling has no animal-derived word, it does not mean the food has no animal-derived ingredient. Do not judge by the allergen labeling alone. This is this guide's own advice.

③ How to Use Seoul City's Vegetarian Restaurant List

The Seoul Metropolitan Government (서울특별시) has posted the "Status of Vegetarian Restaurants" list on its website. The values below are what we saw by opening that screen directly on 2026-11-26.

  • Address: func.seoul.go.kr/user/vegetarian/restaurantList.do
  • Number of entries: the screen says "838 entries in total" (총 838건).
  • Columns of the table: serial number (연번), business name (상호명), business type (업종), contact (연락처), district (자치구), address (소재지), menu items (판매메뉴) with the vegetarian type (채식유형).
  • Language: the screen is in Korean.

3 Ways to Search

  1. Pick a district (gu, 자치구). Pick one of the 25 districts of Seoul. If you pick the district where your lodging is, places nearby come up.
  2. Pick a business type (업종). There are 11 options: cafe (카페); Indian/Middle Eastern (인도/중동); bar (술집); Korean (한식); bakery (베이커리); Southeast Asian (동남아); Chinese (중식); Western (양식); snack food (분식); Japanese (일식); and Korean and Western combined (한식,양식).
  3. Enter a search word. The search word is matched against business name, contact, address and menu items.

The screen also has a button to download an Excel file.

The Vegetarian Type Attached to a Menu

This list attaches the vegetarian type to menus, not to restaurants. When you press "View" (보기) on each row, the menu name and the type in parentheses appear.

The list screen has no definition of the types. A Seoul City article of 2021-01-19 (Media Hub) explained the types as follows.

Korean label How to read it Meaning
vegan (비건) bigeon "The stage that allows no animal-derived food and no food that comes from animals in any form"
lacto (락토) rakto "Vegetarianism that allows the intake of dairy products"
ovo (오보) obo "Vegetarianism that does not allow meat, fish, poultry or dairy products but eats animal eggs"
pesco (페스코) peseuko "Vegetarianism that does not allow meat but allows dairy products, eggs, seafood and fish"
regular (일반식) ilbansik "Usually run as a non-vegan menu. A vegetarian option can be chosen at the customer's request"
vegan option available (비건가능) bigeon-ganeung The article gives no definition

Some menus carry two tags, such as pesco + vegan option available (페스코,비건가능) or regular + vegan option available (일반식,비건가능). Do not read vegan option available (비건가능) as vegan (비건). Treat it as a menu where you have to ask when ordering to have it made vegan. This is this guide's own advice.

Examples of how it is written on the screen are Ria Miracle Burger (리아미라클버거) and Awesome Burger (어썸버거), each followed by the type tag vegan (비건).

What You Cannot Tell from This List

The list screen has no column that filters for vegetarian-only restaurants. Only the vegetarian type is attached to each menu. So a restaurant on the list can also have menus that are not vegetarian. Look at the menu name and the type in parentheses, not the restaurant name. This is this guide's own advice.

When Seoul City announced this list in January 2021, it carried 948 places (Seoul City Media Hub article, 2021-01-19).

The screen is in Korean, so turn on your browser's translation function when you look at it. This is this guide's own advice.

④ Temple Food Is Not the Same as Vegan

The Official Definition

The official introduction on the temple food website run by the Cultural Corps of Korean Buddhism (한국불교문화사업단) (read 2026-11-26) says: "In temple food, all animal-derived foods except dairy products and the 'osinchae' (오신채) are prohibited."

In this sentence, dairy products are left out of the prohibition. If you do not eat dairy products, ask about the ingredients when you eat temple food too. This is this guide's own advice.

The Korea Tourism Organization's "Tips for Vegetarians" says temple food is the food of monks in training and that it "does not use any meat at all".

The Five Pungent Vegetables (osinchae, 오신채)

The same website explains osinchae as "five vegetables that give a pungent taste". The five are below.

  • Green onion (파)
  • Garlic (마늘)
  • Chives (부추)
  • Wild chive, dallae (달래)
  • Asafoetida, heunggeo (흥거)

Temple food does not use these five. The five written in this definition are green onion, garlic, chives, wild chive and asafoetida. Onion (양파) is not written there.

⑤ Certification Marks

The 3 Criteria of the Korea Agency of Vegan Certification and Services

The certification guide on the official website of the Korea Agency of Vegan Certification and Services (한국비건인증원) (read 2026-11-26) gives three criteria.

  1. "Animal-derived raw materials must not be used."
  2. "Experiments using animals must not be done."
  3. "Cross-contamination must be prevented before, during and after the product production process."

The Korea Tourism Organization's Guidance

The Korea Tourism Organization's "Tips for Vegetarians" says to "look for a certified vegan logo".

The three criteria above are for products that agency has certified. This guide does not print a picture of the mark. Allergen labeling covers only 19 ingredients.

On the sandwich menu of the official Subway website (read 2026-11-26), there is no menu marked as plant-based or vegan. CU

The official BGF Retail (BGF리테일) press release (2023-01-25, read 2026-11-26) says: "In 2019, CU created a 'vegetarian' brand, the first among convenience stores, and has continued to release some 40 vegetarian ready-to-eat meals, from lunch boxes to pasta." The brand name is CU "Chaesikjuui" (채식주의), a vegetarian line.

The products this press release announced are the "vegetarian Chinese-style set lunch box (채식 중화 정식 도시락)", the "vegetarian basil-filled sandwich (채식 바질 가득 샌드위치)" and the "vegetarian egg-mayo tofu-pouch rice balls (채식 채란마요 유부초밥)". The press release calls the "egg mayo sandwich and tofu-pouch rice balls (에그 마요 샌드위치와 유부초밥)" "100% plant-based food using substitute eggs". The lunch box does not have this wording. The press release says nothing on whether milk and honey are used.

The CU vegetarian press release this guide opened is dated 2023-01-25. These are product names from January 2023, so they may differ from what is on the store shelf now.

When you buy packaged food at a convenience store, look at the allergen labeling in ② and the certification marks in ⑤ together. This is this guide's own advice.

⑧ In-Flight Meals — Korean Air

These are the values in the special meal (특별 기내식) guide on the official Korean Air website (read 2026-11-26).

  • Vegetarian in-flight meals: Korean Air says: "We offer a variety of customized vegetarian meals for our vegetarian passengers."
  • Request deadline: "please request a special meal at least 24 hours before departure."
  • Codeshare flights (공동운항편): "For codeshare flights, requests must be made at least 48 hours before departure."

⚠ 6 Common Mistakes

  1. Thinking of kimchi only as a vegetable side dish. Kimchi seasoning often contains salted seafood, fish sauce or meat broth. Regional kimchi can also contain abalone, cutlassfish and pork.
  2. Buying by the name "vegetable gimbap" alone. Even vegetable gimbap can contain ham, eggs and crab-flavored fish cake.
  3. Ordering bibimbap as it is. Most bibimbap menus put meat and eggs on top as toppings. When you order, say "Gogi ppae juseyo (고기 빼 주세요)." and "Dalgyal ppae juseyo (달걀 빼 주세요)."
  4. Thinking food is vegan when the allergen labeling has no animal-derived word. Only 19 items are labeled. Honey, anchovy and salmon are not on this list.
  5. Thinking temple food is the same as vegan. In the official definition of temple food, dairy products are left out of the prohibition.
  6. Thinking a restaurant on the Seoul City list is a vegetarian-only restaurant. The list has no column that filters for vegetarian-only restaurants. Look at the vegetarian type attached to each menu.

⚙ 5 FAQs

  1. If I order a vegetable dish in Korea, does it have no animal-derived ingredient? — No. The Korea Tourism Organization writes that Korean food is often prepared with meat and fish broth. Jeon can have eggs in the batter. Eggs can also be put on top of food as a garnish.
  2. What do I look at on packaged food? — In the allergen labeling, look for the 10 items: eggs (난류), milk (우유), mackerel (고등어), crab (게), shrimp (새우), pork (돼지고기), chicken (닭고기), beef (쇠고기), squid (오징어) and shellfish (조개류). This labeling covers only 19 ingredients. Also look for whether there is a certified vegan logo.
  3. How do I find restaurants with vegetarian menus in Seoul? — On the Seoul City "Status of Vegetarian Restaurants" list (func.seoul.go.kr/user/vegetarian/restaurantList.do), choose by district, business type and search word. The 2026-11-26 screen carried 838 entries in total. When you press "View" (보기) on each row, the menu name and the type in parentheses appear. The types are vegan (비건), lacto (락토), ovo (오보), pesco (페스코), regular (일반식) and vegan option available (비건가능). Vegan option available (비건가능) is different from vegan (비건). The screen is in Korean.
  4. Is temple food vegan? — It is not the same. The temple food website of the Cultural Corps of Korean Buddhism says "all animal-derived foods except dairy products and the 'osinchae' are prohibited". Dairy products are not prohibited. The osinchae, green onion (파), garlic (마늘), chives (부추), wild chive (달래) and asafoetida (흥거), are not used.
  5. By when do I request a Korean Air vegetarian in-flight meal? — By 24 hours before departure. For codeshare flights, by 48 hours before departure.
