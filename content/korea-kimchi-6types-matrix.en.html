Korean Kimchi 6 Types Matrix — UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage Hansik #1

Subtitle: For Foreign Tourists: 6 Types of Kimchi (Baechu, Kkakdugi, Chonggak, Yeolmu, Nabak, Dongchimi) + Seasonal & Regional Signatures + Beginner's Guide

ISSUE 03 · #822 · 2026-06-08

🎯 Why a Korean Kimchi Matrix?

Foreign tourists' pain point with Korean kimchi = "Only knowing baechu-kimchi". Kimjang (김장, the culture of making kimchi) = UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity (inscribed in 2013, official name "Kimjang, making and sharing kimchi in the Republic of Korea"). However, kimchi itself is not inscribed — Kimjang (김장, the tradition of making and sharing) is the inscribed heritage. Kimchi (김치) = Globally recognized Hansik (한식, Korean food) #1 + an essential banchan (반찬, free Korean side dishes) for Korean meals. Even though it's all "kimchi," baechu-kimchi (배추김치, Korea's #1), kkakdugi (깍두기, cubed radish), chonggak-kimchi (총각김치, whole young radish), yeolmu-kimchi (열무김치, young summer radish), nabak-kimchi (나박김치, water kimchi), and dongchimi (동치미, winter water kimchi) are six completely different dishes. Foreigners often (a) only know baechu-kimchi (배추김치) and haven't experienced other types, (b) don't know the varying spice levels, (c) are unaware of fermentation stage differences, and (d) don't understand the meaning of Kimjang (김장) culture. This guide provides a 6-type matrix + regional and seasonal signatures + a priority list for foreign beginners.

🏛 UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage — Kimjang (김장) 2013

Definition of Kimjang (김장)

  • Inscribed as UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity in 2013
  • Official name: "Kimjang, making and sharing kimchi in the Republic of Korea"
  • ⚠ Kimchi (김치) itself is not inscribed — Kimjang (김장, the tradition of making and sharing) is the inscribed heritage.
  • Kimjang (김장) = A Korean cultural practice where families and communities gather to make large quantities of kimchi (김치) for winter.
  • Timing: November (from Ipdong (입동, beginning of winter) to Soseol (소설, first snow)) when the average temperature is below 4°C (39°F).
  • Meaning: Community, generational transmission, seasonal food culture
  • An average of 50-100 heads of napa cabbage are used for Kimjang (김장) per household.

Kimjang (김장) Experience for Foreigners

  • Bukchon Hanok Village (북촌 한옥마을, traditional Korean village) Kimjang (김장) Experience (Autumn, November) — #1 for foreign enthusiasts.
  • Jeonju Hanok Village (전주 한옥마을) Kimjang (김장) Experience — Traditional and authentic.
  • Museum Kimchikan (김치 박물관) (Seoul) = The #1 place for kimchi (김치) history and experience.

🥬 Kimchi (김치) 6 Types Deep Dive

🥬 Baechu-kimchi (배추김치) — Korea's #1 Signature

  • Signature: Korea's #1 kimchi (김치), representative of UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage, #1 free banchan (반찬) at restaurants.
  • Types:
  • Pogigimchi (포기김치, Whole): Whole head of napa cabbage.
  • Mak-kimchi (막김치, Cut): Napa cabbage cut into pieces (for faster fermentation).
  • Baek-kimchi (백김치, White): White kimchi (김치) without chili powder (good for beginners).
  • Fermentation Stages:
  • Geotjeori (겉절이, 1-2 days): Mildly spicy, fresh.
  • Igeun Kimchi (익은 김치, 1-2 weeks): Standard fermentation.
  • Mukeunji (묵은지, 6+ months): Deep sour taste, popular for jjigae (찌개, stew) and bokkeum (볶음, stir-fry).
  • Foreigner-friendly: ★★★★★ (#1 free banchan (반찬) at Korean restaurants).
  • Recommended for beginners: Baek-kimchi (백김치) (not spicy) → Geotjeori (겉절이) → Igeun Kimchi (익은 김치) → Mukeunji (묵은지).

🥕 Kkakdugi (깍두기)

  • Signature: Cubed radish (사각 cube), #1 pairing for gukbap (국밥, Korean rice soup).
  • Ingredients: Radish + chili powder + saeujeot (새우젓, salted shrimp) + garlic, ginger, sugar.
  • Foreigner-friendly: ★★★★★ (Crunchy, refreshing, standard at gukbap (국밥) restaurants).
  • Recommended pairing: Seolleongtang (설렁탕, ox bone soup), Gomtang (곰탕, beef bone soup), Dwaeji-gukbap (돼지국밥, pork rice soup), BBQ.

🥒 Chonggak-kimchi (총각김치)

  • Signature: Whole chonggakmu (총각무, small young radish).
  • Ingredients: Chonggakmu (총각무) + chili powder + jeotgal (젓갈, salted seafood) + garlic.
  • Foreigner-friendly: ★★★★ (Whole, crunchy + spicy).
  • Recommended pairing: Makgeolli (막걸리, Korean rice wine), Soju (소주, Korean distilled spirit), beer (as anju (안주, drinking snack)).

🌱 Yeolmu-kimchi (열무김치) — Summer #1

  • Signature: Yeolmu (열무, young summer radish leaves) + light.
  • Ingredients: Yeolmu (열무) + chili powder + glutinous rice paste + garlic.
  • Foreigner-friendly: ★★★★ (Light for summer).
  • Recommended pairing: Yeolmu-guksu (열무국수, cold noodles with young summer radish kimchi) — Summer's #1 / Bibimbap (비빔밥, mixed rice with vegetables).

💧 Nabak-kimchi (나박김치) — Beginner-friendly

  • Signature: Mul-kimchi (물김치, water kimchi) + thinly sliced radish and napa cabbage.
  • Ingredients: Radish, napa cabbage, water, a little chili powder, garlic.
  • Foreigner-friendly: ★★★★ (Not spicy, refreshing broth).
  • Recommended pairing: Tteok (떡, rice cakes), Mandu (만두, dumplings), royal court cuisine (mild pairing).

❄ Dongchimi (동치미) — Mild for Beginners

  • Signature: Gyeoul Mul-kimchi (겨울 물김치, winter water kimchi) + not spicy.
  • Ingredients: Radish + water + salt + pear + garlic (no or very little chili powder).
  • Foreigner-friendly: ★★★★★ (Not spicy, #1 refreshing broth).
  • Recommended pairing: Memil-guksu (메밀국수, buckwheat noodles) (Dongchimi-guksu (동치미국수) uses dongchimi broth) / Pyeongyang Naengmyeon (평양냉면, Pyeongyang-style cold noodles).

⚠ 5 Common Mistakes by Foreigners

1. "Kimchi (김치) = only baechu-kimchi (배추김치)": There are 6 types in total. Dongchimi (동치미), Baek-kimchi (백김치), Nabak-kimchi (나박김치) are not spicy.

2. "All kimchi (김치) is spicy": Baek-kimchi (백김치) and Dongchimi (동치미) are not spicy. They are top priorities for beginners.

3. "Kimjang (김장) = simply making kimchi (김치)": Kimjang (김장) = UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity (2013) representing community and generational transmission culture.

4. "All kimchi (김치) has the same fermentation stage": Geotjeori (겉절이, 1-2 days) ↔ Igeun Kimchi (익은 김치, 1-2 weeks) ↔ Mukeunji (묵은지, 6+ months) are completely different stages.

5. "Kimchi (김치) at Korean restaurants = free once": Restaurant kimchi (김치) = free, unlimited refills. It's okay to ask for "Kimchi jom deo juseyo (김치 좀 더 주세요)" (More kimchi, please).

⚙ 8 FAQs

1. Top priority for beginners: Baek-kimchi (백김치) or Dongchimi (동치미) (not spicy) → Baechu-kimchi Geotjeori (배추김치 겉절이) → Igeun Kimchi (익은 김치) stages.

2. Gukbap (국밥) pairing: Kkakdugi (깍두기) (#1) — provided free at all gukbap (국밥) restaurants.

3. Top for summer: Yeolmu-kimchi (열무김치) + Nabak-kimchi (나박김치) + Dongchimi (동치미) (refreshing).

4. Kimjang (김장) experience: Bukchon (북촌) and Jeonju Hanok Village (전주 한옥마을) Kimjang (김장) experience (Autumn, November).

5. Uses for Mukeunji (묵은지): Kimchi-jjigae (김치찌개, kimchi stew), Kimchi-bokkeumbap (김치볶음밥, kimchi fried rice), Mukeunji-gimbap (묵은지 김밥, aged kimchi rice rolls), Mukeunji-ramyeon (묵은지 라면, aged kimchi ramen).

6. Allergies: Saeujeot (새우젓, salted shrimp) (crustaceans), garlic, ginger, chili powder, soy (brewed soy sauce) should be declared in advance.

7. Vegetarian/Halal/Kosher: Regular kimchi (김치) = contains saeujeot (새우젓, salted shrimp) and myeolchi-aekjeot (멸치액젓, anchovy sauce) (not vegetarian/not halal). Temple food kimchi (사찰음식 김치) = vegetarian OK ([#800](800_korean_temple_food_guide.md) for reference).

8. VAT refund: Food and beverages are generally not eligible for refunds.

⚠ Medical, Safety, and Dietary Disclaimer

Kimchi (김치) = fermented food (high in sodium, often spicy). Pregnant women, those with high blood pressure, or kidney patients should be mindful of sodium intake. For children, Baek-kimchi (백김치) and Dongchimi (동치미) (not spicy) are recommended. Declare allergies (saeujeot (새우젓, salted shrimp), myeolchi-aekjeot (멸치액젓, anchovy sauce), crustaceans, garlic, chili powder) in advance. In case of suspected food poisoning, call ☎ 1339 (medical, English available). This guide provides general information and is not medical advice.

Dietary restriction fallback:

  • Halal/Kosher: Regular kimchi (김치) = not halal/not kosher (contains saeujeot (새우젓, salted shrimp)). → Temple food kimchi (사찰음식 김치) = vegetarian, halal, and kosher options available ([#800](800_korean_temple_food_guide.md)).
  • Vegetarian/Vegan: Regular kimchi (김치) = not vegetarian (contains saeujeot (새우젓, salted shrimp) and myeolchi-aekjeot (멸치액젓, anchovy sauce)). → Temple food kimchi (사찰음식 김치) OR separate vegan kimchi (김치) (soy sauce/bean-based) options.
  • Gluten-free: Regular kimchi (김치) = generally gluten-free (if soy sauce is excluded). Baek-kimchi (백김치) and Dongchimi (동치미) = OK.

✈️ Taking Kimchi (김치) from Korea (Foreigner Pain Point #1)

  • Carry-on prohibited — Kimchi (김치) is classified as liquid (>100ml liquids only allowed in checked baggage).
  • Checked baggage OK — Vacuum-sealed or airtight containers are essential.
  • Recommended places to buy:
  • Incheon International Airport (인천공항) Duty-Free — Vacuum-sealed kimchi (김치) (pick up right before departure).
  • Myeongdong (명동) / Gangnam (강남) Hansik (한식) gift shops — Foreigner-friendly vacuum-sealed packaging.
  • E-Mart (이마트) / Lotte Mart (롯데마트) kimchi (김치) corners — Cost-effective vacuum-sealed packaging.
  • Price anchor: Vacuum-sealed kimchi (김치) 1kg = ₩15,000 (~$11 / ~80 RMB / ~1,600 JPY) - ₩30,000 (Duty-Free) / ₩8,000 - ₩15,000 (Mart).
  • Note: Some countries (e.g., Australia, New Zealand) restrict the import of solid foods — be sure to check your country's regulations in advance.
