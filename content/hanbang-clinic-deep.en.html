Why Hanbang Now — K-Medical Health Tourism + Yangnyeongsi Culture

Korean hanbang (한방) is the world's #1 authentic Korean traditional medicine + Yangnyeongsi (약령시, herbal-medicine markets in Seoul and Daegu) + a top-tier segment of K-Medical health tourism. It's the #1 enthusiast destination for the CN, Middle Eastern, and JP segments (cross-ref #325·#404·#538). This matrix covers 3 Yangnyeongsi picks + 3 Gangnam hanbang picks + 3 general hanbang hospital picks + 3 foreign-visitor experience picks = 12 picks across 4 axes.

Foreign arrivals for Korean-medicine health tourism topped 120,000 per year as of 2023 (Korea Tourism Organization medical tourism statistics), with CN, JP, and Middle Eastern visitors together accounting for 60% of the total. Foreign visitors choose hanbang for three main reasons. First, non-surgical treatments like acupuncture, cupping, and herbal medicine are already familiar in many source cultures (CN, JP, Southeast Asia). Second, it offers strong value for visitors dealing with chronic fatigue, musculoskeletal pain, or beauty goals. Third, only in Seoul can visitors experience a herbal-medicine market with centuries of history — Yangnyeongsi (Gyeongdong Market, 경동시장) — in person.

12 Picks, 4 Axes — Institution Deep Dive

Axis 1: Yangnyeongsi·Cheongnyangni Hanbang (3 picks)

#1 Seoul Oriental Medicine Promotion Center

  • Location: 26 Yangnyeong-jungang-ro, Dongdaemun-gu, Seoul (Lines 1·6 Dongdaemun Station Exit 2, 5-min walk / Line 1 Cheongnyangni Station, 10-min walk)
  • Hours: Tue-Sun 10:00-18:00, closed Mondays (as of 2026; confirm before visiting — kmedi.ddm.go.kr)
  • Experiences: herbal-plant exhibits·hanbang foot-bath experience·foreign-language brochures·Yangnyeongsi tour guidance. Programs run roughly ₩5,000-20,000.
  • Foreign-visitor fit: English, Chinese, and Japanese brochures always available. Can refer visitors to foreign-language-capable pharmacies. Ideal first stop for exploring Yangnyeongsi.
  • Booking: advance booking recommended for groups (10+), individuals can walk in.
  • Accessibility: wheelchair accessible. Connected on foot to nearby Gyeongdong Market and the Cheongnyangni herbal-medicine cluster.

#2 Gyeongdong Market Yangnyeongsi

  • Location: Gosanja-ro, Dongdaemun-gu, Seoul (between Line 1 Cheongnyangni Station and Line 6 Korea Univ. Station)
  • Scale: Korea's largest herbal-medicine wholesale market. Over 600 types of herbal ingredients traded. Cross-ref #365.
  • Shopping tips: buy small quantities of key ingredients like red ginseng, ginseng, angelica root (당귀), and astragalus (황기). At wholesale prices, some items run 30-50% cheaper than at a regular pharmacy (varies by item — confirm before buying).
  • Note for foreign visitors: most vendors communicate mainly in Korean. If you want English support, link up with the Seoul Oriental Medicine Promotion Center (#1) and bring a tour guide.
  • Hours: most shops 09:00-18:00, shorter hours on Sundays. Many shops closed Mondays.

#3 Cheongnyangni Herbal-Medicine Cluster

  • Location: the herbal-medicine street near Cheongnyangni Station, Dongdaemun-gu, Seoul
  • Features: a dense cluster of herbal pharmacies specializing in tonic prescriptions and compounding. A 1-month tonic supply runs ₩250,000-420,000 (per dailymedi's 2025 pricing reference, for in-house compounding).
  • Note for foreign visitors: consultations and prescriptions are handled mainly in Korean. Foreign visitors should bring an interpreter. Before accepting a prescription, confirm the practitioner is a licensed Doctor of Korean Medicine (look for the "한의원" sign, which is mandatory).

Axis 2: Gangnam·Cheongdam Hanbang (3 picks)

#4 Jaseng Hospital of Korean Medicine, Gangnam Main Branch

  • Location: Gangnam-daero, Gangnam-gu, Seoul (Gangnam Station Exit 3, 3-min walk)
  • Features: Korea's #1 spine and joint hanbang specialist. Runs a foreign medical-tourism coordinator program. Full English-language website.
  • Fees (2026): first visit ₩30,000-80,000; additional treatments like acupuncture and pharmacopuncture run roughly ₩30,000-100,000 per session (confirm — jaseng.co.kr/international).
  • Foreign-visitor fit: English, Chinese, and Japanese coordinators. Medical interpretation available with advance request. Custom treatment packages for foreign patients available on inquiry.
  • Booking: website (jaseng.co.kr) / direct line to the International Clinic / KakaoTalk channel: Jaseng Hospital of Korean Medicine (자생한방병원).
  • Insurance: foreign patients pay out of pocket (not covered by Korean national health insurance). Check directly with your travel-insurance provider on coverage. English-language treatment certificates and receipts available.

#5 Cheongdam Hanbang Clinic Cluster

  • Location: Cheongdam-dong area, Gangnam-gu, Seoul (near Line 7 Cheongdam Station)
  • Features: growing numbers of foreign visitors coming for beauty acupuncture, hanbang weight-management programs, and skin treatments. Beauty acupuncture runs ₩50,000-150,000 per session; hanbang diet program packages run ₩300,000-1,000,000+ (varies by clinic).
  • Foreign-visitor fit: some clinics offer English or Chinese consultation. Confirm foreign-language support with the specific clinic before visiting.
  • Booking: phone or KakaoTalk booking with each individual clinic.

#6 Gangnam Herbal Tonic Clinics

  • Location: Nonhyeon·Sinsa area, Gangnam-gu, Seoul
  • Features: tonic prescriptions from hanbang clinics in the Gangnam area. A 1-month supply runs ₩250,000-420,000 (in-house compounding). Similar price range to the Cheongnyangni cluster (#3), with the advantage of Gangnam's accessibility.
  • Note for foreign visitors: a consultation with a licensed Doctor of Korean Medicine is required before a prescription. Confirm foreign-language support by contacting the clinic directly.

Axis 3: General Hanbang Hospitals (3 picks)

#7 Jaseng Hospital of Korean Medicine, Gangbuk (Sinnae)

  • Location: Sinnae-dong, Jungnang-gu, Seoul (near Gyeongui-Jungang·Gyeongchun Line Sinnae Station)
  • Features: the same foreign-coordinator system as the Gangnam main branch. Wait times can be shorter than at the Gangnam branch.
  • Booking: jaseng.co.kr / International Clinic phone line.
  • Access: requires a subway transfer from Gangnam (30-40 minutes).

#8 Dongguk University Hanbang Hospital

  • Location: Pildong-ro 1-gil, Jung-gu, Seoul (Chungmuro Station Exit 5, 10-min walk)
  • Features: a university-affiliated general hanbang hospital. Runs specialized departments including internal hanbang medicine, acupuncture & moxibustion, and hanbang rehabilitation medicine. An officially designated medical-tourism partner institution.
  • Foreign-visitor fit: runs a foreign-language medical interpretation service (advance request required). English-language treatment certificates available.
  • Booking: Dongguk University Hanbang Hospital website / main phone line. Look for the dedicated foreign medical-tourism consultation desk.
  • Fees: varies by department and treatment. Foreign patients are billed at the non-covered (out-of-pocket) rate. Consult before visiting.

#9 Kyung Hee University Hanbang Hospital

  • Location: 26 Hoegi-ro, Dongdaemun-gu, Seoul (Line 1·Gyeongui-Jungang Line Hoegi Station, 5-min walk / Kyung Hee Univ. Station)
  • Features: Korea's first college of Korean medicine. A core K-Medical health-tourism hub. Specializes in a Sasang constitutional-medicine clinic and constitution diagnosis. Runs a foreign-language clinic.
  • Foreign-visitor fit: English, Chinese, and Japanese medical interpretation available. Runs a foreign medical-tourism coordinator program. Maintains a network of partner institutions abroad.
  • Booking: the International Clinic on the Kyung Hee University Hanbang Hospital website (advance booking strongly recommended). Same-day walk-in registration can mean a long wait.
  • Signature: the best institution for the Sasang constitutional diagnosis (#12). Classifies patients into 4 constitution types — Taeyangin, Taeeumin, Soyangin, Soeumin (태양인·태음인·소양인·소음인) — with a tailored prescription for each.

Axis 4: Foreign-Visitor Experiences·Culture (3 picks)

#10 Acupuncture Experience (₩30,000-80,000/session)

  • What it is: visit a hanbang clinic → diagnosis from a Doctor of Korean Medicine → acupuncture treatment. 30-80 minutes per session, ₩30,000-80,000.
  • Why it's the #1 draw for foreign enthusiasts: non-invasive, short, accessible anywhere in Seoul. A familiar practice across CN, JP, and Western cultures alike.
  • How to choose: clinics with foreign-language support = Jaseng (#4·#7)·Kyung Hee (#9)·Dongguk (#8). Ordinary neighborhood clinics operate mainly in Korean.
  • Caution: always confirm the practitioner is a licensed Doctor of Korean Medicine (check for the "한의원" sign and a posted license). Be sure to distinguish licensed clinics from unlicensed look-alike businesses.

#11 Full Cupping·Moxibustion·Pharmacopuncture Set

  • What it is: a combination of acupuncture + cupping (부항) + moxibustion (뜸) + pharmacopuncture (약침). 1-2 hours, ₩50,000-120,000.
  • Foreign-visitor fit: popular with JP and Western enthusiasts of authentic wellness. Cupping marks (lasting 2-3 days) should be explained to visitors in advance.
  • Booking: advance booking recommended at a foreign-language-capable clinic. Some clinics accept same-day walk-ins, but expect a wait.

#12 Sasang Constitutional Diagnosis

  • What it is: diagnosis into one of 4 constitution types (Taeyang, Taeeum, Soyang, Soeum) based on Lee Je-ma's (이제마) Sasang constitutional medicine, followed by a tailored herbal-medicine and diet prescription. ₩30,000-60,000 per session.
  • Best institution: the Sasang Constitutional Medicine Clinic at Kyung Hee University Hanbang Hospital (#9).
  • Foreign-visitor fit: high interest from the CN and Middle Eastern segments. English-language explanatory materials and diagnosis reports available (at Kyung Hee — confirm before visiting).
  • Time required: 1-1.5 hours including diagnosis. Expect about half a day if you also get same-day prescription and compounding.

⚠️ 3 Common Mistakes Foreign Visitors Make

Mistake 1: Confusing a "Hanuiwon" (한의원, Licensed Clinic) with a "Hanyakbang" (한약방, Herbal Pharmacy)

A hanuiwon (한의원) staffed by a licensed Doctor of Korean Medicine can diagnose, prescribe, and treat. A hanyakbang (한약방, herbal pharmacy) — sometimes called hanyakguk (한약국) — on the other hand specializes in compounding against a prescription. If your visit is medical in purpose, you need to see a licensed doctor at a hanuiwon. Check that the sign explicitly reads hanuiwon (한의원).

Mistake 2: Assuming Travel Insurance Will Cover It

Hanbang treatment for foreign visitors is entirely out-of-pocket. It isn't covered by Korean national health insurance, and travel insurance policies frequently list "alternative medicine·hanbang" as an exclusion. Check your policy terms before you depart. English-language treatment certificates and receipts are available from Jaseng, Kyung Hee, and Dongguk.

Mistake 3: Showing Up at a General Hanbang Hospital Without a Booking

At Kyung Hee (#9) and Dongguk (#8), a same-day walk-in without a booking can mean a wait of 2-3 hours or more. Foreign medical-tourism coordinator connections and foreign-language interpretation also require advance requests. Book online at least 3-5 days before your visit.

Practical Info — Transport, Booking, Language, and Insurance

Transport

  • Yangnyeongsi cluster (#1·#2·#3): Line 1 Cheongnyangni Station, or Lines 1·6 Dongdaemun Station. 5-10 min walk to the Seoul Oriental Medicine Promotion Center.
  • Gangnam cluster (#4·#5·#6): Line 2 Gangnam Station (3-min walk to Jaseng #4). The Cheongdam cluster is at Line 7 Cheongdam Station.
  • Kyung Hee University Hanbang Hospital (#9): Line 1·Gyeongui-Jungang Line Hoegi Station, 5-min walk.
  • Dongguk University Hanbang Hospital (#8): Lines 3·4 Chungmuro Station Exit 5, 10-min walk.

Booking

  • Jaseng Hospital of Korean Medicine: jaseng.co.kr > International Clinic / KakaoTalk channel "Jaseng Hospital of Korean Medicine"
  • Kyung Hee University Hanbang Hospital: khu.ac.kr (Hanbang Hospital > International Clinic)
  • Dongguk University Hanbang Hospital: website or main phone line (advance consultation recommended for foreign patients)
  • Seoul Oriental Medicine Promotion Center: kmedi.ddm.go.kr (group booking / individual walk-in)
  • Ordinary clinics: modoodoc.com (searchable in English for hanbang clinics) / search Kakao Map for a clinic and book by phone

Language

  • Best for English: Jaseng (#4·#7), Kyung Hee (#9), Dongguk (#8)
  • Chinese/Japanese available: Jaseng's International Clinic, Kyung Hee's foreign-language clinic (advance request)
  • Yangnyeongsi·ordinary clinics: mainly Korean. Use a translation app (Papago·Google Translate) or bring an interpreter.
  • Tourist interpretation hotline: ☎ 1330 (Korea Tourism Organization, 24h, English·Chinese·Japanese·Russian)

Insurance and Costs

  • Foreign visitors pay entirely out of pocket (not covered). No Korean national health insurance coverage.
  • English treatment certificates·receipts: issued by Jaseng, Kyung Hee, and Dongguk. Needed for overseas insurance claims.
  • Whether travel insurance covers hanbang treatment varies by insurer — confirm directly before departure.
  • modoodoc (modoodoc.com): an English-language booking and payment platform. Lists some hanbang clinics.

FAQ 5

Q1. What order should a first-time foreign visitor follow for hanbang?

Start with the cultural experience: Seoul Oriental Medicine Promotion Center (#1) → Gyeongdong Yangnyeongsi (#2) → acupuncture experience (#10). If your purpose is medical, booking the International Clinic at Jaseng Hospital of Korean Medicine (#4) is the #1 choice — it has English coordinators, English-language paperwork, and a proven route for foreign patients.

Q2. How much does hanbang treatment cost for a foreign visitor?

  • Tonic (1-month supply): ₩250,000-420,000
  • Acupuncture, 1 session: ₩30,000-80,000
  • Full cupping·moxibustion·pharmacopuncture set: ₩50,000-120,000
  • Sasang constitutional diagnosis: ₩30,000-60,000
  • First visit at a general hanbang hospital: ₩30,000-80,000 + treatment cost

Q3. Where can I get treated in English?

Institutions with confirmed English coordinators: Jaseng Hospital of Korean Medicine (both Gangnam and Gangbuk), Kyung Hee University Hanbang Hospital (International Clinic), Dongguk University Hanbang Hospital (medical-tourism team, advance request). Yangnyeongsi and ordinary clinics operate mainly in Korean.

Q4. How do I combine this into a 1-week K-Medical health-tourism course?

See the cross-refs #325 (Middle East persona)·#404·#538 (medical tourism). Combine Jaseng (spine·joint treatment) + Kyung Hee (constitution diagnosis·tonic) + Yangnyeongsi culture (#1·#2) for a 3-4 day full hanbang course. If working through a medical-tourism agency, confirm the pickup and interpretation package separately.

Q5. Can I buy herbal medicine (a tonic) to bring home?

You can buy raw ingredients at Gyeongdong Yangnyeongsi (#2) or at pharmacies linked with the Seoul Oriental Medicine Promotion Center (#1). That said, customs rules for bringing finished herbal products or concentrated extracts abroad vary by country. Animal-derived ingredients in particular (like deer antler, 녹용) may be banned imports in some countries, so check your home country's customs regulations before you depart.

Season, 5 Axes

  • Spring (Mar-May): tonic season 1 (fatigue recovery·immune support). Spring herbal-medicine demand peaks — book early.
  • Summer (Jun-Aug): heat-relief hanbang (fatigue from excess AC·digestive treatment). Acupuncture demand rises in summer.
  • Fall (Sep-Nov): tonic season 2 (building strength for winter). Peak season at Gyeongdong Yangnyeongsi. The busiest season for foreign enthusiasts.
  • Winter (Dec-Feb): restorative herbal medicine·warming joint treatments. Demand rises for moxibustion and cupping.
  • Monsoon season (Jun-Jul): more inquiries about hanbang treatment for humidity-related joint pain.
