1. What Is Tteok (떡)? — A Quick Primer

1-1. Tteok Basics

Tteok (떡) is Korea's traditional rice cake, made by steaming and kneading glutinous rice (찹쌀) or regular short-grain rice (멥쌀). Unlike Western bread, it contains no wheat flour and is naturally gluten-free. The defining characteristic is its chewy, yielding texture — ranging from lightly sweet to subtly savory depending on the variety.

Sharing tteok is a cornerstone of Korean social life. It appears on the table during Seollal (설날, Lunar New Year) and Chuseok (추석, Korean Thanksgiving), as well as on birthdays, housewarming days, and other milestones. Gift-packaged sets are widely available, especially at premium shops. In recent years, tteok cafes have emerged across Seoul (서울), making tteok a natural companion to coffee and modern dessert culture.

1-2. Must-Know Varieties

Name Character
Injeolmi (인절미) Glutinous rice cake rolled in roasted soybean powder — the gold standard of chewy + nutty
Gyeongdan (경단) Small round balls of glutinous rice dough coated in fillings: red bean, soybean, or sesame
Songpyeon (송편) Half-moon shaped rice cake, the iconic Chuseok (추석) food; filled with sesame or red bean paste
Jeolpyeon (절편) Flat, patterned rice cake, pressed with a decorative stamp; mild and soft
Baekseolgi (백설기) Pure steamed white rice cake with nothing added — clean, fluffy, almost melts on the tongue
Chapsaltteok (찹쌀떡) Round, plump glutinous rice cake filled with red bean paste or cream; similar to Japanese mochi, popular with international visitors
Yaksik (약식) Glutinous rice steamed with jujubes, chestnuts, pine nuts, soy sauce, and sugar — sweet and slightly savory
Ssuktteok (쑥떡) Mugwort rice cake, vivid green, with a distinct herbal fragrance

Jongno (종로) and Jung-gu (중구) are the historic heart of Seoul (서울), home to Insadong (인사동), Euljiro (을지로), and Changdeokgung Palace (창덕궁). The tteok shops here sit directly on popular tourist itineraries — old neighborhood stalwarts alongside modern tteok cafes.

Jongno Tteokjip (종로떡집) · Nagwon Tteokjip (낙원떡집) — Positioned directly across the street from each other in front of Nagwon Arcade (낙원상가), these two shops have anchored this block for decades. There is nothing flashy here — just the kind of no-frills, local-first neighborhood tteok shop where regulars stop in on their way to work. A natural detour if you are walking Jongno (종로).

Dongbyeongsangryeon (동병상련) — A uniquely designed space: tteok cafe on the ground floor, production kitchen in the basement. Visitors can glimpse the tteok-making process while eating upstairs. Well-suited for experience-oriented travelers. The location connects naturally to Ikseon-dong (익선동) and Changdeokgung Palace (창덕궁).

Ajindang Tteok Sangjeom (아진당 떡상점) — A polished, Instagram-friendly tteok cafe tucked into the Ikseon-dong (익선동) alley. Seasonal menus and a carefully arranged interior make it accessible to younger crowds and first-time tteok visitors alike.

Hangwa Miuisik Annyeong Insadong (한과미의식 안녕인사동점) — Located on the main Insadong (인사동) street, this shop slots easily into a shopping-and-sightseeing loop. In addition to tteok, it stocks traditional hangwa (한과) — Korean confections including gangjung (강정, puffed rice and grain snacks) and jeonggwa (정과, candied fruits and roots). Packaging is gift-ready, making it a strong pick for souvenirs.

Gyeonggi Tteokjip Lotte Main (경기떡집 롯데백화점본점) — Inside the food hall of Lotte Department Store (롯데백화점) on Euljiro (을지로), this outlet offers department-store-grade quality control and presentation. Convenient for visitors already on a Myeongdong (명동) shopping run.

Yakhyeon Tteokbang (약현떡방) — A tteok cafe on the quiet Jungnim-ro (중림로) backstreet of Jung-gu (중구). Sits close to Yakhy eon Cathedral (약현성당) and the Seosomun (서소문) historical park area — a good add-on for visitors exploring this part of the city.

Dosuhyang (도수향) — Situated near Seolleung Station (선릉역) and Samsung Station (삼성역) in the heart of Gangnam (강남). The surrounding office density means this shop sees regular demand for bulk and corporate gift orders.

Cheongdam Ibuk Injeolmi (청담이북인절미) — As the name suggests, injeolmi (인절미) is the star here. Located in the Cheongdam-dong (청담동) alley, just minutes from Seoul's luxury fashion district — a convenient detour after browsing designer boutiques.

3. Best Times & Seasons

3-1. Morning Snack

In Korea, tteok has traditionally doubled as a light morning snack or a quick meal substitute. Varieties like baekseolgi (백설기), jeolpyeon (절편), and injeolmi (인절미) — lower in sugar and soft in texture — work especially well early in the day. Many neighborhood shops have freshly steamed tteok ready from opening, so checking hours before you go is worthwhile.

3-2. Weekend Afternoon Dessert

The tteok cafes — Dongbyeongsangryeon (동병상련), Ajindang Tteok Sangjeom (아진당 떡상점), and Yakhyeon Tteokbang (약현떡방) — are designed for sitting down with tteok alongside coffee or traditional Korean tea (전통 차). They slot naturally into a weekend afternoon cafe crawl and double as a rest stop mid-sightseeing.

3-3. Holiday Seasons (Seollal & Chuseok)

Demand for gift-packaged tteok sets spikes dramatically around Seollal (설날, January–February) and Chuseok (추석, September–October). If you are visiting during these periods, popular items at Baeknyeon Hwapyeon (백년화편), Gyeonggi Tteokjip (경기떡집), and Hangwa Miuisik (한과미의식) sell out quickly. Visiting early in the holiday window or placing an online order in advance is the safer strategy.

4. Ordering, Packaging & Gift Tips

General price range: Neighborhood shops are affordable; department-store outlets and branded gift sets sit at a premium. Exact prices vary per shop — check in person or via Naver Maps (네이버 지도).

Requesting gift packaging: If you want something wrapped as a gift, simply say "선물 포장 해주세요" (pronounced: seonmul pojiang haejuseyo). Most shops will oblige. Department-store outlets — Gyeonggi Tteokjip Lotte Main (경기떡집 롯데백화점본점) and Tteokhamji Jamsil Main (떡함지 잠실본점) — provide high-standard packaging as a default.

Things to keep in mind:

  • Tteok contains few or no preservatives. Same-day consumption is strongly recommended. Chapsaltteok (찹쌀떡) and injeolmi (인절미) in particular need refrigeration and harden quickly at room temperature.
  • Some shops offer freezer-stable products — confirm at the time of purchase.
  • If you plan to carry tteok on a flight, verify whether the specific variety can withstand cabin temperatures and travel time before you buy.

Language: Most neighborhood tteok shops have no English menu. Show a photo, use a translation app, or point — it works. The staff is generally used to navigating the language gap.

6. Etiquette & Watch-outs

Freshness and same-day eating: The rule of thumb for most tteok is: made today, eaten today. Tteok hardens and loses its texture quickly. Practically speaking, it makes more sense to buy on your last day in Seoul or close to your accommodation, rather than on day one of a long trip.

Freezing: Some chapsaltteok (찹쌀떡) and injeolmi (인절미) can be frozen and thawed successfully. That said, whether freezing is appropriate and how to thaw correctly varies by shop — always confirm when purchasing.

Summer heat: From June through August, Seoul's temperatures regularly push past 30°C. Tteok left at room temperature on a summer afternoon can deteriorate within hours. Refrigerate immediately after purchase during this period.

Market-based shops: Shops inside traditional markets — like Myeongga Tteokjip (명가떡집) in Jamsil Saemaeul Market (잠실 새마을 시장) — operate on the market's opening schedule, which may differ from typical retail hours. Check before going.

Photography in small shops: In compact neighborhood tteok shops, pushing for photo angles can create friction with other customers. Tteok cafes and larger spaces are more photography-friendly — read the room in smaller spots.
