Why Galchi Alley Sub Matters Now — A Parent Guide Plus a Standalone Braised Hairtail Enthusiast Pick

The Namdaemun old-school eateries 12-pick guide covered Galchi Alley — Jungang Galchi (중앙갈치) and Huirak Galchi (희락갈치) — with just 3 picks. This sub-deep dive expands that into a standalone 12-pick matrix devoted entirely to Galchi Alley (갈치골목): 3 braised hairtail stall picks + 3 grilled hairtail stall picks + 3 nearby cluster picks + 3 foreign enthusiast picks.

Galchi Alley matters to foreign visitors for more than just good food. Thick-cut whole hairtail, slow-braised in gochujang with potato and radish, is a dish that Japanese and Western travelers alike describe as something you can "only eat in Korea." Formed in the 1970s, Namdaemun's Galchi Alley remains the country's only cluster where an entire alley is devoted to nothing but hairtail restaurants.

⚠️ Top 3 Mistakes Foreign Visitors Make

1. Planning a Sunday visit → running into a closed shop

A number of Galchi Alley shops close or shorten their hours on Sundays. If you're visiting on a Sunday, calling ahead the day before or checking real-time hours on Naver Map is essential. Magnae Hoejip (막내회집) nearby tends to be relatively reliable on Sundays, though it's still worth confirming.

2. Trying to walk straight in during peak hours (12:00–14:00) — 30+ minute wait

Ever since it aired on TV (including the show Restaurants with a Line, Season 2), Jungang Galchi has had a lunchtime line even on weekdays. Arriving before 11:30, or coming back after 14:00, is the practical move.

3. Ordering without distinguishing braised from grilled — getting a dish you didn't expect

Galchi-jorim (조림 = braised/stewed) and galchi-gui (구이 = grilled) are completely different dishes. Jorim is simmered in a seasoned sauce; gui is grilled with salt or soy seasoning. Ordering by gesture alone, without a translation app, leads to confusion. Setting up a translation app like DeepL or Papago in advance is recommended.

12 Picks Across 4 Axes

Axis 1: Braised Hairtail Stalls (3 picks) — the alley's flagship dish

1. Jungang Galchi (중앙갈치식당)

  • Highlights: 56 years in business, featured on the TV show Restaurants with a Line, Season 2, the flagship shop of Galchi Alley
  • Location: The middle of Galchi Alley (5–7 minute walk from Exit 5, Hoehyeon Station, heading toward Namdaemun Market)
  • Hours: Daily 08:30–21:00 (no break time; check on Sunday operation)
  • Prices: Galchi-jorim, roughly ₩12,000–16,000 per portion (2026, subject to change — check on-site)
  • Foreigner fit: A top pick for Japanese and English-speaking enthusiasts; its TV credential drives strong visibility on foreign travel blogs
  • English: Has a photo menu board, basic English ordering possible (worth confirming)
  • Timing: Recommended before 11:30 or after 14:30
  • Access: Exit 5, Hoehyeon Station → toward Namdaemun Market's C and D sections → into Galchi Alley

2. Huirak Galchi (희락갈치)

  • Highlights: 50-plus years in business, featured on the parent Namdaemun old-school eateries 12-pick list, one of the alley's two flagship shops alongside Jungang Galchi
  • Hours: Check ahead (irregular closures possible, typical of an old-school shop)
  • Prices: Galchi-jorim, roughly ₩12,000–18,000 per portion (2026, subject to change)
  • Foreigner fit: A fit for foreign visitors after that genuine old-school feel that comes with 50 years of history
  • English: Basic ordering possible; bring a translation app
  • Timing: Avoid the 12:00–14:00 peak; weekday mornings or after 14:00 are recommended

3. General Galchi Alley stalls — alley hopping

  • Highlights: Many shops throughout the alley run both galchi-jorim and galchi-gui. A solid alternative when Jungang Galchi or Huirak Galchi have long lines.
  • Access tip: Shops further into the alley tend to have shorter waits than ones near the entrance. Check the menu board and price before sitting down.
  • Foreigner fit: If you want the hairtail experience without a wait, alley hopping is the practical choice.
  • Price range: ₩12,000–20,000 depending on the shop (2026, varies — check the menu board)

Axis 2: Grilled Hairtail Stalls (3 picks)

4. Grilled hairtail specialists (whole galchi-gui)

  • Highlights: Whole hairtail grilled with salt or soy seasoning. Unlike the braised version, it has a clean, straightforward fish flavor.
  • Foreigner fit: A more familiar option for Western travelers who find braised fish unfamiliar. Japanese travelers often prefer the grilled version too.
  • Price range: ₩14,000–20,000 per portion (2026, worth confirming)
  • Note: Grilled specialists are fewer in number than braised shops within the alley — check the signage as you enter.

5. Grilled-plus-braised combo shops

  • Highlights: Shops that let you order half-grilled, half-braised in one dish — a good way for first-time visitors to compare both styles.
  • Ordering tip: Try "Half grilled, half braised," or use a translation app to ask whether half-and-half orders are available.
  • Foreigner fit: Ideal when you're not sure which style you'll prefer. If you're dining solo, confirm ahead which shops offer the combo.

6. Soy-glazed grilled hairtail

  • Highlights: Grilled with a soy-based seasoning instead of salt, for a sweet-and-salty flavor.
  • Foreigner fit: Familiar territory for Japanese and Chinese travelers used to soy-based sauces. Pairs especially well with rice thanks to its bolder seasoning.
  • Note: Not every shop in the alley offers the soy option — confirm before ordering.

Axis 3: Nearby Cluster (3 picks) — Namdaemun cross

Within a 5–10 minute walk of Galchi Alley, these are shops included in the Namdaemun old-school eatery route — useful when the hairtail line is long, or when you're building a full-day course.

7. Magnae Hoejip (막내회집)

  • Highlights: A raw fish (hoe) specialist, featured on the parent Namdaemun old-school eatery 12-pick list
  • Foreigner fit: A good alternative for visitors after raw fish rather than braised hairtail. Relatively reliable on Sundays too (still worth confirming)
  • Combine with: Wait out the Galchi Alley line here, or hit the hairtail shop first and finish with raw fish

8. Bugwon Myeonok (부원면옥)

  • Highlights: A pyongyang naengmyeon (cold buckwheat noodle) specialist, featured on the parent Namdaemun old-school eatery 12-pick list
  • Foreigner fit: A course that finishes hairtail lunch with cold noodles. A natural galchi-plus-naengmyeon pairing during the hot season (summer)
  • Timing: Around the lunch peak, 12:00–14:00. Naengmyeon lines can get longer in summer.

9. Eunho Sikdang (은호식당)

  • Highlights: An oxtail soup specialist, featured on the parent Namdaemun old-school eatery 12-pick list
  • Foreigner fit: A gentler alternative for travelers wary of galchi-jorim's strong spice. A taste of traditional Korean bone-broth soup.
  • Timing: Works for both breakfast and lunch

Axis 4: Foreign Enthusiast Bases (3 picks)

10. The authentic Japanese/English-speaking hairtail enthusiast route

  • Route: Exit 5, Hoehyeon Station → Jungang Galchi or Huirak Galchi in Galchi Alley → browse inside Namdaemun Market → back to Hoehyeon Station
  • Time needed: 2–3 hours including lunch
  • Foreigner fit: Namdaemun's Galchi Alley shows up repeatedly on Japanese- and English-language travel blogs as a "Seoul authentic old-school food bucket-list" stop

11. Hoehyeon Station Exit 5 base camp

  • Highlights: An exit within walking distance of Namdaemun Market, Galchi Alley, Namsan, and Sungnyemun (숭례문)
  • How to use it: Browse Namdaemun Market before your Galchi Alley visit, then walk to Sungnyemun afterward for a natural follow-on
  • Transport info: Seoul Subway Line 4, Hoehyeon Station, Exit 5. T-money card or credit card tap-to-ride both work.

12. The Namdaemun Market and Galchi Alley full set (Namdaemun cross)

  • Route: Exit 5, Hoehyeon Station → Namdaemun Market entrance → Galchi Alley → the Namdaemun old-school route (Bugwon Myeonok, Magnae Hoejip, and others) → Namdaemun Market shopping (clothing, glasses, dried seafood) → back to Hoehyeon Station
  • Time needed: Half a day to a full day (4–6 hours)
  • Foreigner fit: A single-district experience combining old-school food, traditional market shopping, and history (Sungnyemun) in one route

Practical Info

Transport

  • Subway: Line 4, Hoehyeon Station, Exit 5 → 5-minute walk (to Galchi Alley)
  • Bus: Many routes pass through Myeongdong and Namdaemun Market (worth confirming)
  • Taxi: Saying "Namdaemun Market Galchi Alley" or "Namdaemun Market Galchi Alley" in English is understood
  • Naver Map: Search "Namdaemun Galchi Alley" to find the alley entrance

Payment

  • Card vs. cash: Some old-school shops in Namdaemun's Galchi Alley prefer cash. Bring a small amount of cash before visiting (around ₩50,000)
  • Checking card acceptance: Ask "Can I use a card?" at the counter before sitting down. Larger old-school shops (such as Jungang Galchi) often accept cards, but confirm first
  • ATM: Available inside Namdaemun Market and near the currency exchange counters at Hoehyeon Station

Language

  • Translation apps: Install DeepL, Papago, or Google Translate ahead of time. Papago specializes in Korean and is especially accurate with food terms.
  • Ordering keywords:
  • Galchi-jorim: "Galchi-jorim" (braised hairtail fish)
  • Galchi-gui: "Galchi-gui" (grilled hairtail fish)
  • One portion: "il-in-bun" (one portion)
  • Extra: "chu-ga" (extra/more)
  • English menu: Many shops have photo menus. If not, use a translation app to scan the menu board.

VAT refund

  • Some shops inside Namdaemun Market qualify for Tax Refund. Restaurants mostly don't. Check when shopping for clothing or general goods inside the market.

FAQ 6

Q1. First time in Galchi Alley — where should I go?

Jungang Galchi (56 years, TV-certified) is the top pick. If the line is long, try Huirak Galchi or hop to a shop further into the alley. Whichever shop you choose, the galchi-jorim quality is on par across these old-school spots.

Q2. What should I budget for?

Galchi-jorim runs ₩12,000–18,000 per portion, galchi-gui ₩14,000–20,000 (2026 basis). Budgeting ₩15,000–22,000 per person including side dishes is reasonable. Old-school pricing can shift — check the menu board directly.

Q3. Can I order in English?

A photo menu plus translation app combo is more realistic than full English service. You can order by saying "Galchi-jorim" or pointing at the photo menu. Some staff speak basic English, but it isn't guaranteed.

Q4. Is Sunday a good day to visit?

Some shops close or shorten hours on Sundays. Checking real-time hours on Naver Map or calling the day before is essential. On a Sunday visit, nearby Magnae Hoejip is a relatively safe alternative, though still worth confirming.

Q5. What if the lunch peak wait is too long?

Shift to the quiet window, 14:00–17:00. An early lunch before 11:30 a.m. often gets you in without a wait. Or hop to a shop further into the alley.

Q6. Is this just a one-alley visit, or can it fill a half-day?

Galchi Alley alone takes 1–2 hours. To stretch it into a half-day course, add shopping inside Namdaemun Market (clothing, glasses, dried seafood, imported foods) plus Sungnyemun (a 10-minute walk). The full Namdaemun set — Bugwon Myeonok, Magnae Hoejip, Eunho Sikdang included — makes a full-day course.

Course 1 — Hairtail-focused (2–3 hours)

Exit 5, Hoehyeon Station → Jungang Galchi (before 11:30 or after 14:30) → Huirak Galchi or alley hopping → back to Hoehyeon Station

Course 2 — Hairtail plus Namdaemun Market half-day (4–5 hours)

Exit 5, Hoehyeon Station → Galchi Alley (lunch or early dinner) → shopping inside Namdaemun Market → a walk to Sungnyemun → head toward Myeongdong or return

Course 3 — Hairtail plus the full Namdaemun old-school set (full day, 6–7 hours)

Morning: Bugwon Myeonok (naengmyeon lunch) → afternoon: Galchi Alley (14:00–16:00, quiet) → Magnae Hoejip (raw fish) → Eunho Sikdang (oxtail soup for dinner) → back to Hoehyeon Station

Autumn (September–November) is hairtail's peak season. Its fat content rises in the fall, deepening the flavor of both braised and grilled versions. Foreign enthusiasts will find an autumn visit the most practical choice.

💡 5 Practical Tips for Foreign Visitors

1. Jungang Galchi, 56 years, TV-certified — searching these two keywords on foreign travel blogs turns up plenty of reviews. Reading 3–5 reviews before visiting helps set expectations

2. Hoehyeon Station Exit 5 — Galchi Alley, Namdaemun Market, Sungnyemun, and Namsan are all reachable from this exit. Use a T-money card or a tap-to-ride credit card

3. Bring a small amount of cash — some old-school shops prefer cash. ATMs are available at Hoehyeon Station and inside Namdaemun Market

4. Install the Papago translation app — its menu-board camera translation is practical. Distinguishing galchi-jorim from galchi-gui is the key thing to get right

5. Time it for autumn — hairtail is in season September–November; avoid the wait by visiting before 11:30 a.m. or after 14:30
