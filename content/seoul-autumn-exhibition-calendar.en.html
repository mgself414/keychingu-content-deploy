Seoul Fall Exhibition Calendar 2026 — KAWS, Do Ho Suh, Gaudí, Martin Parr: 10 Date-Stamped Picks From October Into Early Winter

Subtitle: The 10 exhibitions actually running in Seoul from late September through November 2026, ordered by closing date — KAWS's first Korean museum solo show (through 12/27), Do Ho Suh's Tate Modern-toured show (through 2027/2/9), the Gaudí death-centenary exhibition (through 10/31), the Martin Parr retrospective (through 10/18, free), plus three Asia-first solo shows at Leeum, Amorepacific, and SeMA. A rainy-day indoor layer, and a half-day afternoon course to slot between festival weekends.

🎯 Why a "Fall Exhibition Calendar"

Seoul's museums and galleries already have standing guides (#350 Seoul Art Map, #1258 Subway Museum Line). But fall 2026 has an unusually stacked exhibition lineup — Brooklyn artist KAWS's first Korean museum solo show, Do Ho Suh's largest-ever domestic solo exhibition after touring Tate Modern, the official Gaudí death-centenary world tour, and "Asia's first solo exhibition" billed separately by Amorepacific Museum of Art, Leeum, and the Seoul Museum of Art (SeMA) — all overlapping in one season. For a foreign traveler, the question isn't "which museum is better," it's "what's actually open the week I'm in Seoul."

This piece answers only that question — permanent-collection write-ups stay in the standing guides, and this one pins down dates, locations, admission, night hours, and booking channels in a table. If the first-week-of-October festivals (#1375) get rained out, this table becomes the indoor backup.

⚠ Note on timing: This is a 2026-only calendar. Hours, closed days, and admission follow each official website (checked 2026-09-17); confirm each museum's notice before visiting around Chuseok (10/3–10/5) or any temporary closures. Values marked "unconfirmed" aren't specified on the official page and need on-site confirmation.

🅰 The Ones That Close in October First — Martin Parr, Gaudí, SONGEUN

  • Martin Parr (through 10/18, free): A retrospective spanning 50 years of British documentary photographer Martin Parr's work — 14 series, 500-plus photographs, 90 photobooks — filling the entire building at SeMA Photo Museum. Free admission is the deciding factor here; the only real cost is the travel time out to Changdong. It's roughly 40 minutes from downtown via Changdong Station on Lines 1 and 4, and it stays open until 20:00 on weekdays, so evenings work too. For anyone into photography, this is the best-value scene in Seoul this fall.
  • Gaudí (through 10/31, ₩27,000): The Seoul stop of the Gaudí Foundation's official world tour, at Sinsa House, open until 20:00 daily. It's the priciest ticket on this list, but the Fever app is the only booking channel that takes English and international cards, which actually makes it the easiest one for foreign visitors. Book online, swap for a physical ticket on-site, and a QR audio guide comes with it. Closed on Chuseok itself, 9/25.
  • SONGEUN (through 10/10, free): A 25th-anniversary group show for the SONGEUN Art Award on Dosan-daero in Cheongdam — 179 contemporary Korean artists under one roof, which makes it the fast way to see "what's happening in young Korean art right now." Closed Sundays and holidays, and it shuts at 18:30, so pair it with Apgujeong Rodeo/Cheongdam shopping (#1088) on a weekday afternoon.

🅱 Running Into December — KAWS, Koo Jeong A, Lee Kangso, and the Original-Art Shows

  • KAWS (through 12/27, ₩12,000): Brooklyn artist KAWS's first Korean museum solo show. Twenty works, including large-scale new paintings and sculptures, at Space K Seoul in Magok. Tue–Sun 10:00–18:00, closed Mon. Magok sits out near Magongnaru on Lines 5 and 9, which makes it easy to slot around a Gimpo Airport landing or takeoff (#1285) — and for character/street-art fans, this exhibition alone justifies the trip out.
  • Koo Jeong A (through 12/27): At Leeum Museum of Art M2 in Hannam-dong, 35 works from early-1990s pieces through a new Leeum-commissioned work. Booking opens 14 days before your visit date, so check ticket.leeum.org the moment your travel dates are set — weekends fill fast. Closed Mon, Lunar New Year, and Chuseok; closes at 18:00 (ticketing until 17:30).
  • Lee Kangso (through 11/1, ₩20,000): At Lotte Museum in Lotte World Tower, Jamsil. A large-scale solo show for a first-generation pioneer of Korean experimental art, and since it's in the same building cluster as Jamsil shopping and Lotte World (#1088, #586 Jamsil night views), it costs zero extra travel. Last admission 18:30.
  • GROUND SEESAW East original-art shows (through 11/29): On the 2nd floor of Eastfall in Gwangjin-gu, original-art exhibitions from illustrators Lee Ki-hoon and Cho Eun. Wall text may lean Korean-only — English signage is unconfirmed — so set your expectations for a "go to see the art itself" visit. Pricing follows the Interpark NOL listing.

🅲 The Big Three Running Into Winter — Do Ho Suh, Jonas Wood, Lynn Hershman Leeson

  • Do Ho Suh (through 2027/2/9, ₩8,000): At MMCA Seoul, Samcheong-dong. His largest domestic solo exhibition after touring Tate Modern, at ₩8,000 with night hours until 21:00 on Wed and Sat — the only exhibition on this list that scores well on night access, price, and scale all at once. The Seoul Box section opens separately on 12/18, so December visitors can catch both parts. Walking distance from Bukchon galleries and hanok dining (#908).
  • Jonas Wood (through 2027/2/28, ₩18,000): At the Amorepacific Museum of Art (APMA) in Yongsan, the LA artist's Asia-first solo exhibition and a 20-plus-year retrospective. Booking works only through APMA's own site, and with Monday closures and a 17:30 ticketing cutoff, this isn't a drop-in — pin a date and go. The building itself (David Chipperfield-designed) is worth the visit on its own and overlaps with the #1258 line.
  • Lynn Hershman Leeson (10/21–2027/2/21): At SeMA's Seosomun Main Building. Asia's first museum solo show for an American artist who has worked with media, AI, and identity for 60 years. Only viewable from the third week of October on, and it stays open until 21:00 on Fridays. Check the post-opening notice for admission and English signage.

🗓 A Two-Day Route — The Museums Cluster Into Two Axes

Day 1 · Northern Downtown Axis (Wed or Sat recommended): Afternoon at MMCA's Do Ho Suh in Samcheong-dong → a walk through Bukchon alleys and galleries (#908) → evening at SeMA in Seosomun (Lynn Hershman Leeson from 10/21 on; open until 21:00 on Friday). Since Do Ho Suh runs until 21:00 on Wed and Sat, you can flip the order and do Seosomun first.

Day 2 · Southern Riverside Axis: Morning at Leeum's Koo Jeong A in Hannam-dong (booking required) → one stop by foot or bus to APMA's Jonas Wood in Yongsan → evening at Gaudí in Sinsa-dong (until 20:00, closes 10/31). Wrap up at a Hannam night bar (#1073).

Two separate half-days: ① West — KAWS in Magok + Martin Parr in Changdong (both sit on the edge of the city; pairing them in one day means about two hours of just travel, so treat them as one-each) ② East — Lotte Museum's Lee Kangso in Jamsil + GROUND SEESAW's original-art show in Gwangjin (Line 2, Jamsil ↔ Konkuk University).

Rain-day switch: If the 10/3–10/4 festival week (#1375) gets rained out, use the Day 1 axis (Samcheong-dong/Seosomun) as your indoor backup as-is.

⚠ 3 Common Mistakes

1. Going to museums on Monday — Martin Parr, KAWS, Koo Jeong A, Jonas Wood, and Lynn Hershman Leeson are all closed Mondays. Save Monday for Gaudí (open daily), Lotte Museum, GROUND SEESAW, or SONGEUN (closed Sundays, so it's open on Mondays).

2. Counting on walk-in tickets — Leeum runs a 14-days-ahead booking system, and APMA sells only through its own site. Weekend walk-ins can be unavailable or sold out. Gaudí is the opposite case — app (Fever) booking is actually the easiest route for foreign visitors.

3. Visiting Seosomun before the third week of October — Lynn Hershman Leeson opens 10/21. Go to the Seosomun Main Building before that, and this exhibition simply isn't there yet.

⚙ 3 FAQs

1. If you can only pick one? — For a first-time foreign visitor, Do Ho Suh (Samcheong-dong): ₩8,000, open until 21:00 Wed/Sat, his largest-scale show, and it's right next to Bukchon. If photography or documentary work is more your thing, go with the free Martin Parr (through 10/18).

2. What if the booking app is Korean-only? — Gaudí's Fever app is confirmed to work in English with international cards. The other museum sites lean toward domestic cards and Naver Pay, so payment with a foreign-issued card is unconfirmed — prioritizing free exhibitions (SONGEUN, Martin Parr) and venues that accept walk-in purchases (MMCA, Space K) lowers the risk.

3. Where can you go in the evening? — MMCA Seoul until 21:00 Wed/Sat, SeMA Seosomun until 21:00 Fri (20:00 Tue–Thu), Gaudí until 20:00 daily, Martin Parr until 20:00 on weekdays. Everything else closes at 18:00–19:00.
