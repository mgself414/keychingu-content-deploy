Incheon Market Guide — Juan Singi Market and Singi Tongbo, Dongincheon Songhyeon Market, Sinpo International Market, Chinatown

Subtitle: Singi Market (신기시장), 1 Singi Tongbo = 500 won · Songhyeon Market (송현시장), 1960, closed on the 2nd Tuesday of each month · Sinpo International Market (신포국제시장), 10:00–21:00, open year-round · Incheon Chinatown (인천 차이나타운), always open — confirmed via Korea Tourism Organization tourism data (TourAPI)

🎯 How to Use This Guide

Markets in Incheon are spread across different neighborhoods. Singi Market is in Juan-dong (주안동), Michuhol-gu (미추홀구). Songhyeon Market is north of Dongincheon Station (동인천역). Sinpo International Market goes back to the port-opening era. And Chinatown, though not a market, is covered together because it has so much food.

This guide covers only these four places, and the operating details are copied from values registered in the Korea Tourism Organization tourism data (TourAPI). The food alley by alley at Sinpo International Market is covered separately in #623 Incheon Sinpo International Market.

🪙 Singi Market — The Coin-Shaped Currency "Singi Tongbo"

Singi Market at 6 Singi-gil 58beon-gil, Michuhol-gu, Incheon (인천 미추홀구 신기길58번길 6), Juan-dong, ☎ 032-865-5424, is a culture and tourism market that many foreign tourists also visit. It began in the 1970s when people sold vegetables from their garden plots at the foot of Munhaksan (문학산), and an alley market grew from there.

  • Singi Tongbo (신기통보): A currency made for this market only, shaped like an old coin (yeopjeon). You exchange it at 500 won per coin and use it for snacks and in the food alley.
  • What they sell: Food, clothing, sundries, seafood, and local agricultural specialties.
  • Opening hours and closed days: Vary by stall.
  • Facilities: Restrooms and parking are available.

🌲 Songhyeon Market — The Arcade Market North of Dongincheon Station

Incheon Songhyeon Market at 18 Hwadojin-ro 44beon-gil, Jemulpo-gu, Incheon (인천 제물포구 화도진로44번길 18), ☎ 032-773-2368, is a traditional market north of Dongincheon Station. After the Korean War (6·25), displaced people and migrants gathered here, and it formed naturally in the early 1960s (the opening year registered with the tourism organization is 1960).

  • Closed days: The 2nd Tuesday of each month. Opening hours vary by stall.
  • What they sell: Agricultural, marine, and livestock products, plus sundries.
  • Facilities: It was selected as a culture and tourism market in 2008 and an arcade was installed over the alley. There is a park nearby for a rest.
  • The name: "Songhyeon" comes from Songhyeon (松峴), the Chinese-character spelling of "Solgogae" (솔고개), a pine hill between Songnim-dong (송림동) and Songhyeon-dong (송현동).
  • Convenience: Restrooms are available, and parking is free.

🍗 Sinpo International Market — Incheon's First Modern Permanent Market

Sinpo International Market at 7-40 Sinpo-dong, Jemulpo-gu, Incheon (인천 제물포구 신포동 7-40), ☎ Sinpo International Market Support Center 032-764-0415, is Incheon's first modern permanent market, with a history of 100-plus years. In the early port-opening years it was called Sinpo Market, meaning "new port," and it took its current name in 2010.

  • Opening hours: 10:00–21:00, varying by stall. Open year-round (varies by stall).
  • What to try: Dakgangjeong (닭강정, sweet crispy fried chicken), gonggalppang (공갈빵, hollow bread), jjolmyeon (쫄면, chewy cold noodles), and five-color dumplings. Dakgangjeong is known to have been first developed here.
  • Zones: Divided into vegetables and fruit, a seafood alley, a side-dish alley, and an alteration alley.
  • Facilities: Restrooms and parking are available.

🏮 Incheon Chinatown — The Street Where Jajangmyeon Was Born

Incheon Chinatown at 20 Chinatown-ro 59beon-gil, Jemulpo-gu, Incheon (인천 제물포구 차이나타운로59번길 20), information ☎ Incheon Station Tourist Information Center 032-777-1330, came about when Incheon Port opened in 1883 and, in 1884, this area was designated a Qing-dynasty extraterritorial zone.

  • Opening hours: Always open, year-round.
  • What to try: This is where jajangmyeon (짜장면) was born. There are also white jajang, lamb skewers, gonggalppang, and oven-baked dumplings.
  • For the Chinatown alleys and Gonghwachun (공화춘), see #1012 Incheon Chinatown + Gonghwachun.

⚠ 3 Common Mistakes

1. Thinking Singi Market is in Dongincheon — Singi Market is in Juan-dong, Michuhol-gu, while Songhyeon Market is north of Dongincheon Station.

2. Treating Singi Tongbo only as a souvenir — You exchange it at 500 won per coin and use it like money in the market's food alley.

3. Visiting Songhyeon Market on a Tuesday — It is closed on the 2nd Tuesday of each month.

⚙ FAQ 2

Q. Where do I go to eat dakgangjeong?

A. Sinpo International Market. It is known as the place where dakgangjeong was first developed, and it is open 10:00–21:00.

Q. Can I use a card?

A. Whether cards are accepted is not registered in the tourism organization's data for the markets in this guide. At Singi Market, you can exchange money for Singi Tongbo and use that.
