Busan K-pop Pilgrimage — Walking as a Fan Through BTS's Hometown

Subtitle: If the Seoul K-pop tour (#221) is a city of agency headquarters and merch shops, Busan is a city of roots. It's the hometown of two BTS members, Jimin and Jungkook, where a father's cafe and childhood alleyways are still standing exactly as they were. How not to get fooled by a lookalike sign, fan etiquette in residential neighborhoods, a rising-generation pilgrimage site (BOYNEXTDOOR), and how to combine it all with a concert trip — this piece maps out the grammar of stanning in Busan.

ISSUE · #1323 · 2026-08-23

🎯 Why Busan

Two of BTS's seven members — Jimin and Jungkook — are from Busan. If Seoul's K-pop pilgrimage sites are "the company" (HYBE, SM, JYP), Busan's are "hometown": a cafe a father runs, the walk to elementary school, the neighborhood of childhood. That makes a Busan pilgrimage a different grammar from Seoul's — a trip through traces of everyday life instead of flashy exhibition halls, which makes etiquette matter even more. It's official enough a theme that Busan's own tourism site (visitbusan) features the pilgrimage course directly.

① The BTS Axis — Jimin and Jungkook's Busan

Jimin: His Father's Cafe — ⚠ Watch the Sign

  • The original cafe run by Jimin's father is ZM-illennial (formerly 'Magnate'). It changed its name over a trademark issue, and there's even an official notice that a nearby shop still using the 'Magnate' sign is a separate business with no connection to the family. If you're a fan, go to ZM-illennial — that's the single most important line in this piece.
  • Fan events run around discharge dates and anniversaries, and there's even a mailbox for letters from overseas ARMY — this is a living, active pilgrimage site.

Jungkook: Mandeok-dong (만덕동), Buk-gu (북구) — His Childhood Neighborhood

  • Lego Village (레고마을) — a small apartment complex built in 1986 whose colorful rooftops look like Lego blocks. It's become the symbolic spot among fans for the neighborhood where Jungkook grew up.
  • School spots like Baekyang Elementary (백양초) and Baekyang Middle School (백양중) are real schools with actual students — look quietly from outside, and go no further than that.

Together: The Gamcheon Culture Village Mural

  • Gamcheon Culture Village (감천문화마을) has a mural of Jungkook and Jimin holding roses — a point where Busan's signature photo spot overlaps with fandom, making it the easiest to fold into a regular sightseeing route.

② The Rising-Generation Axis — BOYNEXTDOOR's BASECOFFEE

BASECOFFEE (베이스커피), a basement cafe you reach by walking down a stairway in Bokcheon-dong (복천동), Dongnae (동래), has become a fan pilgrimage site after it became known that it's run by the family of BOYNEXTDOOR's LEEHAN. Post-its from fans cover the walls, traces are everywhere, and if you look carefully there's even a signature — this is what a 4th-generation fandom pilgrimage looks like as it's forming. It sits right next to Dongnae Eupseong (동래읍성) and Bokcheon Museum (복천박물관), so it folds naturally into a Dongnae walking route.

③ The Event Axis — Asiad Is a K-pop Stadium

Busan Asiad Main Stadium (아시아드 주경기장) is now a fixture on the K-pop calendar:

  • 2022, BTS 'Yet to Come in BUSAN' — a free concert held to support Busan's Expo bid, the day the whole city turned purple.
  • June 2026, BTS Busan Concert — the Busan stop of the full-group comeback tour.
  • September 19, 2026, The Fact Music Awards — an awards show with a lineup including RIIZE and Choi Yena.

There's one pattern here: combine the concert trip with the pilgrimage. Build it as Mandeok and Gamcheon the day before the show, Asiad on the day, and the Dongnae cafe axis the day after, and you've got a full 2-night, 3-day trip (see #1114 for the skeleton).

🙏 Five Lines of Fan Etiquette (The Real Point of This Piece)

1. At a cafe pilgrimage site, order something first — don't just walk in for photos and leave.

2. Lego Village and residential streets are where actual residents live — no photographing front doors or windows, and keep your voice down.

3. Schools are outside-only. No entering the grounds, for any reason.

4. Post-its and guestbooks go only where the shop has set up space for them.

5. No graffiti on murals or signs — leave them as they are, for the next fan.

🗺 Sample One-Day Route

Time Route
