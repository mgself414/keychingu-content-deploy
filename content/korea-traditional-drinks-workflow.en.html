Korean Traditional Drink Ordering & Tea Ceremony Workflow — From Hanok Cafes to Temple Tea Rooms

> Subtitle: A 5-Step Workflow for Foreign Tourists + Dado (Tea Ceremony), Pairing, and Instagram Photo Tips

ISSUE 03 · #841 · 2026-06-09

🎯 Why a Korean Traditional Drink Workflow Guide

Even if you know the [#840 Korean Traditional Drinks 6-Type Matrix](840_korean_traditional_drinks_matrix.md), ordering, drinking, and tea ceremony etiquette are separate. Korean traditional drinks can be found in (a) hanok cafe (a cafe in a traditional Korean house) = walk-in in Insadong (인사동) or Ikseondong (익선동) / some require reservations (b) sachal chasil (temple tea room) = Jingwansa (진관사) or Haeinsa (해in사) templestay (a Buddhist temple stay program) packages (c) MZ cafe (a cafe targeting Millennials and Gen Z) = kiosk ordering / yakgwa (traditional Korean honey cookie) + tea pairing (d) hotel hanjeongsik (Korean full-course meal) restaurant = dessert course after a hanjeongsik. Foreigners (1) don't know dado (Korean tea ceremony) etiquette (6 steps) (2) don't know sikhye (sweet Korean rice punch) vs. sujeonggwa (cinnamon punch) pairing (3) don't know how to hold a teacup (4) don't know if sachal chasil (temple tea room) is only for templestay. This guide provides a 5-step workflow + dado etiquette + pairing + English phrases.

🍵 Dado (다도) Etiquette: 6 Steps

Traditional Korean Dado (다도)

1. Pouring Tea: Teapot → teacup (small amount, multiple cups)

  • Two Hands: Teapot = two hands (traditional)

2. Aroma: Bring teacup close to nose = enjoy the aroma (5 seconds)

3. Taste: First sip = hold briefly in mouth (check taste with entire tongue)

4. Pairing: Tea + dagwa (다과) (yakgwa (약과), dasik (다식), gukhwa (국화), jjat (잣))

5. Aftertaste: Enjoy the lingering aroma after drinking tea

6. Closing: Empty cup = receive and place with two hands

Key Etiquette for Foreigners

  • Teacup = Two Hands: Receive and place with two hands (traditional)
  • No Sound: Avoid slurping or smacking lips (traditional etiquette)
  • Don't Fill Teacup Completely: Fill tea to 70% (traditional emphasis on empty space)

🌶 English Phrases for Foreigners

  • "녹차 한 잔" = "One green tea, please"
  • "식혜 + 약과 페어" = "Sikhye (식혜) with yakgwa (약과), please"
  • "수정과 한 잔" = "One sujeonggwa (수정과), please"
  • "사진 찍어도 돼요?" = "Can I take a photo?"
  • "이거 카페인 있어요?" = "Does this have caffeine?"
  • "카드 돼요?" = "Card accepted?"
  • "포장이요" = "To go, please"

⚠ 7 Common Mistakes by Foreigners

1. "Teacup = One Hand": Korean dado (다도) = hold and place with two hands (traditional).

2. "Fill Tea Completely": Tea = fill to 70% (traditional emphasis on empty space).

3. "Sound = OK": Slurping or smacking lips = avoid in traditional etiquette.

4. "Sikhye (식혜) = Alcohol": Sikhye (식혜) = non-alcoholic fermented sweet drink. OK for children and pregnant women.

5. "Tea = After Dessert": Korean tradition = tea + yakgwa (약과) paired simultaneously (not dessert after tea).

6. "Sachal Chasil (사찰 차실) = Templestay (템플스테이) Only": Some temples = allow foreign visitors to simply drink tea. Check with the venue in advance.

7. "Pregnant Women = All Teas OK": Green tea caffeine, omija (오미자) uterine contractions, large amounts of ginger caution. Sikhye (식혜) and daechu-cha (대추차) are OK.

⚙ 8 FAQs

1. First Traditional Drink = Where: Hanok cafe (한옥 카페) (Insadong (인사동) or Ikseondong (익선동)) is #1 priority (atmosphere + tradition).

2. Instagram/Vlog: Hanok cafe (한옥 카페) (Insadong (인사동) vlog #1) + Sachal chasil (사찰 차실) (serene) + MZ sikhye latte (MZ 식혜 라떼).

3. MZ Trends: Sikhye Latte, Yakgwa Latte, Matcha Latte, Sujeonggwa Bingsu (수정과 빙수).

4. Pregnant Women: Sikhye (식혜), daechu-cha (대추차) (caffeine-free OK) / Green tea = moderate caffeine (≤300mg/day) / Large amounts of omija (오미자) or ginger-based drinks (sujeonggwa (수정과), daechu-cha (대추차)) = consult a medical professional.

5. Halal/Kosher/Vegetarian: All 6 types are alcohol-free and animal-ingredient-free = Halal/Kosher/Vegetarian OK (check for honey content).

6. Allergies: Advise in advance about nuts, pine nuts, honey, cinnamon, chrysanthemum, omija (오미자).

7. Payment/Tips: Hanok (한옥)/MZ cafe (MZ 카페) = card OK / Some temples = cash only / No tipping.

8. VAT Refund: Food and beverages generally not refundable / Tea products at duty-free shops = refundable.

⚠ Medical, Safety, and Dietary Disclaimer

Traditional drinks = general food (not medicine). Caffeine guidelines for pregnant women (WHO/KFDA): ≤ 200-300mg per day / Green tea 1 cup approx. 25-35mg / Daechu-cha (대추차) and sikhye (식혜) = caffeine-free OK / For drinks with large amounts of ginger, cinnamon, or herbal ingredients (sujeonggwa (수정과), daechu-cha (대추차)) = medical consultation recommended / Omija (오미자) = uterine contractions have been reported, so checking with the venue is essential. Children = sikhye (식혜), daechu-cha (대추차), green tea OK / Honey = not for infants under 12 months (botulism). Sikhye (식혜) and diabetic patients = caution with high sugar content. Advise in advance about allergies (nuts, pine nuts, honey, cinnamon, chrysanthemum, omija (오미자)). In case of suspected food poisoning, call ☎ 1339 (Medical, English). This guide provides general information and is not medical advice.

Venue Information Disclaimer: Hanok (한옥), sachal (사찰), MZ, and hotel venue operations may change — check NAVER/Kakao map or official websites before visiting.

Dietary Restriction Fallback:

  • Halal/Kosher: All 6 types are alcohol-free = Halal/Kosher possible (check for honey content). → Sachal chasil (사찰 차실) #1.
  • Vegetarian/Vegan: All 6 types are vegetarian OK (not vegan if honey is included) / Sikhye (식혜) and green tea = vegan OK.
  • Gluten-free: All 6 types are gluten-free OK.
