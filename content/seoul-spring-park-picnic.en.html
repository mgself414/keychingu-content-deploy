Why Spring Park Picnics Now

Seoul's spring picnic scene is the top pick for MZ-generation enthusiasts — a full set of Seoul Forest (서울숲), Olympic Park, the Hangang River, Namsan, cherry blossoms, and chimaek (fried chicken and beer). The roughly seven weeks from late March to mid-May are peak picnic season. Admission is free almost everywhere, a convenience-store meal runs about 10,000 won, and one picnic mat is all you need for half a day.

In-Depth Guide to the Real Parks

1. Seoul Forest

Location: 273 Ttukseom-ro, Seongdong-gu, Seoul

Admission: Free (open 24 hours)

Access: 3-minute walk from Suin-Bundang Line Seoul Forest Station Exit 3

Size: About 430,000 pyeong — Seoul's largest downtown park

There are two spring highlights. The dawn redwood trail photographs best in early April, right as the fresh green comes in. The deer sanctuary (a habitat for spotted deer) is open 10am–4pm, free admission. The main lawn fills with picnic mats on weekend afternoons.

Foreigner fit: Full English signage throughout. Seven restrooms, English-labeled. A 15-minute walk connects to the Seongsu-dong cafe district.

Recommended timing: Weekdays 10am–2pm (low crowds). On weekends, arrive before 9am.

Amenities: Bike rental — 3 Ddareungi (따릉이) stations — 2 cafes, a convenience store (CU) about a 5-minute walk away.

2. Olympic Park

Location: 424 Olympic-ro, Songpa-gu, Seoul

Admission: Free (24 hours)

Access: 5-minute walk from Line 5 Olympic Park Station Exit 3 / 5-minute walk from Line 9 Hanseongbaekje Station Exit 1

Enjoy the 1988 Seoul Olympics heritage site — some of the original sports facilities remain — alongside the park itself. The spring highlights are the canola flower field (May) and a large lawn plaza. The zelkova tree known on Instagram as the "Lone Tree" (나홀로 나무) stands inside Olympic Park — best photographed in the fresh-green season after cherry blossoms fade, late April to May.

Foreigner fit: English park maps at the entrance kiosk. 1988 Olympics history panels carry EN/JP/CN text.

Recommended timing: Canola flowers peak May 1–15. For the Lone Tree photo, early morning (7–9am) backlight is recommended.

Amenities: Bike rental, one convenience store (GS25) inside the park, 10+ restrooms, many picnic tables.

3. Namsan Park

Location: Spanning Jung-gu and Yongsan-gu, Seoul (the whole of Namsan mountain is parkland)

Admission: The park is free. N Seoul Tower observation deck costs 21,000 won (2026), separately.

Access: 5 minutes by Namsan bus (route 02 or 03) from Line 4 Myeong-dong Station Exit 3 / or by Namsan cable car, 13,500 won (2026)

The spring cherry blossom trail runs along a 1–2km stretch on Namsan's north slope, facing Myeong-dong. It peaks April 1–15. Namsan Hanok Village (a Seoul-designated folk cultural heritage site) offers free viewing of five traditional houses plus spring experience programs — the schedule changes yearly, so check in advance.

Foreigner fit: Both the cable car and the bus are easy for foreign visitors. Namsan Hanok Village signage is in EN/JP/CN. Four or five hanbok rental shops sit nearby (rental 20,000–30,000 won, 2026).

Recommended timing: Weekends during peak bloom are extremely crowded. Weekday mornings 9–11am, or evenings 6–8pm for the night view too, are recommended.

Amenities: Many restrooms, benches and picnic spots scattered mid-mountain, CU and GS25 near Myeong-dong Station.

4. Banpo Hangang Park

Location: 40 Sinbanpo-ro 11-gil, Seocho-gu, Seoul (Banpo district)

Admission: Free (24 hours)

Access: 5-minute walk from Line 9 Gubanpo Station Exit 1 / 8-minute walk from Line 7 Banpo Station

The Rainbow Fountain (무지개분수) — officially the Moonlight Rainbow Fountain on Banpo Bridge — holds the Guinness record for the world's longest bridge fountain. Spring operating schedule: April–October, 2–3 shows daily (exact times per official Seoul city announcement — check on-site). The Jamwon-district cherry blossom path peaks in early April and is the most accessible of the riverside blossoms.

Foreigner fit: Chicken-and-beer delivery is available — enter the park address in Baemin or Coupang Eats. Multiple GS25 and CU convenience stores nearby.

Recommended timing: Fountain night show plus night view combo, 8–10pm. Weekend daytime during cherry blossom season sees less crowding than evenings.

Amenities: Kiosks, restrooms, a bike path connected to the Hangang bike route, Ddareungi bike-share stations.

5. Yeouido Hangang Park + Yunjungno

Location: Yeouido-dong, Yeongdeungpo-gu, Seoul (Yeouido Hangang Park) / Yunjungno cherry blossom road around Yeouido's perimeter

Admission: Free (24 hours)

Access: 5-minute walk from Line 5/9 Yeouinaru Station Exit 1

Korea's largest cherry blossom destination. Yunjungno (윤중로) — the road circling Yeouido's edge — has a roughly 5.5km blossom tunnel, peaking on average April 1–10. During the Spring Flower Festival (held annually late March to early April, hosted by Seoul city), the lawn plaza of the Hangang park fills with busking and outdoor events.

Foreigner fit: Adjacent to the Yeouido financial district, with thorough English signage. Hangang bike rental — Ddareungi plus in-park rental shops.

Recommended timing: Weekday mornings 8–10am during peak bloom for the best photo light and lowest crowds. Weekend afternoons are extremely crowded.

Amenities: In-park kiosks and cafes, restrooms, bike rental, a wide lawn picnic zone.

⚠️ 3 Common Mistakes Foreigners Make

1. Showing up at noon–3pm on a weekend without a plan

Popular parks like Seoul Forest, Yeouido, and Banpo Hangang often run out of picnic-mat space during weekend lunch hours. A weekday, or a weekend morning before 9am, is the realistic choice.

2. Assuming you can buy picnic gear at the park

Park kiosks sell drinks and ice cream, but not picnic mats, with a few exceptions in some Hangang districts. It's more reliable to buy one in advance — a nearby Daiso (10–15 min walk) or a vinyl mat from a convenience store (2,000–3,000 won, 2026).

3. Picnicking on the grass in white clothes without checking the weather

Seoul's April daily temperature swing is 10–15°C — chilly mornings, warm afternoons, so layering is essential. If the lawn is damp after rain the day before, you'll get wet without a waterproof mat.

Practical Info for Foreigners

Transport

  • The subway is your base: Seoul Forest (Suin-Bundang Line), Olympic Park (Line 5), Yeouido (Line 5/9), Banpo (Line 9), and Children's Grand Park (Line 7) are all within 5–10 minutes of a station. Subway English signage is 100% covered.
  • Ddareungi (Seoul's public bike-share): App or station kiosk. 1,000 won per hour (2026). The Hangang bike path is ideal for moving between parks. Foreign credit cards work.
  • Bus: Namsan uses the 02/03 circular shuttle bus (1,200 won, 2026). T-money or credit card accepted.

Mats & Picnic Gear

  • Convenience-store mats: CU, GS25, and 7-Eleven sell vinyl picnic mats for 2,000–3,000 won (2026). Small enough to pack in a travel bag.
  • Daiso: A 10–15 minute walk from almost anywhere downtown. Picnic mats (2,000–5,000 won, 2026), paper cups, and disposable utensils are all available.
  • Food: A CU or GS25 rice-ball-and-lunch-box set runs 5,000–8,000 won (2026). In Hangang park delivery zones, entering the park address in a delivery app gets you chicken or pizza delivered.

Restrooms

Seoul Forest, Olympic Park, and the Hangang parks each have 7+ restrooms, generally well maintained. Toilet paper is sometimes stocked, sometimes not — bring small tissues just in case.

FAQ

Q1. First spring picnic in Seoul — where do I start?

Seoul Forest is the top recommendation. It's an easy subway ride (direct to Seoul Forest Station), free, and you can cover all three zones — the deer sanctuary, the lawn plaza, and the dawn redwood forest — in half a day. A 15-minute walk after the picnic connects you to Seongsu-dong cafes for a complete full-day course.

Q2. When does the cherry blossom picnic peak?

Seoul's average cherry blossom peak is April 1–10, shifting 3–5 days depending on that year's weather. For real-time bloom updates, check the Korea Meteorological Administration (weather.go.kr) or Seoul city's official social media in Korean, then run it through Google Translate — that's the fastest route.

Q3. How much does a full picnic cost?

Budget version: a 3,000 won mat + a 6,000 won convenience-store lunch box + a 3,000 won drink = about 12,000 won (2026) for a half-day picnic. Add chicken delivery and it runs 20,000–25,000 won per person. Admission is free at all the major parks.

Q4. What if it rains?

You can still picnic under the roofed pergolas at the Hangang park rest areas. If rain is forecast, switch to one of Seoul Forest's two in-park cafes or a nearby Seongsu-dong cafe. Some enthusiasts say Namsan's cherry blossoms photograph beautifully even in the rain.

Q5. Which parks are best with kids?

Children's Grand Park (direct Line 7 access, free admission, a combined zoo and amusement park) or Seoul Grand Park (Gwacheon, 5,000 won admission) are the top family picks. Olympic Park also works well with kids thanks to its wide lawns and bike rentals.

Tip 5

1. Weekdays 10am–2pm have the lowest crowds and the best light for photos.

2. The signature Instagram shot: Seoul Forest's dawn redwoods paired with hanbok (#379·#753).

3. Clothing: spring layering and sneakers are essential — you'll be moving across grass and dirt a lot.

4. Bring your own lunch box and mat from a convenience store or Daiso — don't count on the park kiosks.

5. Use Ddareungi (#741) to move between Hangang parks — it saves on transport and doubles as exercise.
