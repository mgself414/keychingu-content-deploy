🎯 How to Use This Guide

This guide gathers places where you can watch and learn nongak (농악) (farmers' music), pungmul (풍물) and Namsadang Nori (남사당놀이), played on percussion such as the kkwaenggwari (꽹과리), jing (징), janggu (장구) and buk (북). It covers 4 places in Anseong (안성) (a Namsadang performance hall, a regular performance, a tomb and a festival), 4 nongak training halls in Jeolla (전라도), and 1 Gochang nongak festival. A program for learning samul nori (사물놀이) is registered at Yeonggwang Udo Nongak Training Hall (영광 우도농악전수관).

The operating details are copied from values registered in the Korea Tourism Organization tourism data (TourAPI). This guide covers only places registered in the Korea Tourism Organization tourism data.

📍 Anseong — Namsadang Performance Hall, Gombaengi-teotda Regular Performance, Baudeogi's Tomb and Baudeogi Festival

The Anseong Namsadang Baudeogi Pungmul Troupe regular performance 'Gombaengi-teotda (곰뱅이텄다)' (198-2 Namsadang-ro (남사당로), Bogae-myeon (보개면), Anseong-si, at the Anseong Namsadang Performance Hall (안성 남사당 공연장), ☎ 031-678-2518) is a regular performance of Anseong City that has run since 2003. The 2026 performance of the Anseong City Namsadang Baudeogi Pungmul Troupe (안성시립 남사당 바우덕이 풍물단), subtitled 'The Spirit of the Marketplace Shakes Gyeongbokgung (경복궁)!', reworks the six madang of Namsadang Nori with a modern sensibility.

  • Performance period and time: April 4 to November 29, 2026, every Saturday and Sunday at 14:00. About 90 minutes. Age: All ages.
  • Fee: Adults 12,000 won, teenagers 6,000 won, children 3,000 won. 50% discount for Anseong residents and seniors.
  • The six madang: pungmul (풍물) (taepyeongso (태평소), kkwaenggwari, jing, janggu, buk, sogo (소고)), eoreum (어름) (tightrope walking and witty banter on a rope 3m high and 10m long), beona (버나) (the skill of spinning a sieve frame or a bowl on a stick or a pipe), salpan (살판) (acrobatics performed with the body), deotboegi (덧뵈기) (mask play), deolmi (덜미) (traditional puppet play).

The Anseong Namsadang Performance Hall (안성 남사당 공연장) (same address, ☎ 031-678-2518) is a round performance hall built for traditional play performances including Namsadang Nori. It has more than 700 seats, a 20m round stage and a 13m lift stage. The outdoor performance ground has 1,000 seats.

  • Opening hours: Weekends (April to November) 14:00~16:00. Closed days: Weekdays.
  • Fee: Adults 12,000 won, teenagers 6,000 won, children 3,000 won. 50% discount for recipients of basic livelihood benefits, people with disabilities, persons of national merit, single-parent families, seniors (age 65 and over), Anseong residents and others.
  • Pets: Not allowed.

The Anseong Namsadang Baudeogi's Tomb (안성 남사당 바우덕이 묘) (1279-76 Seongjin-ro (성진로), Seoun-myeon (서운면), Anseong-si, ☎ Anseong City cultural heritage office 031-678-5889) is the resting place of Baudeogi (바우덕이) of Namsadang (1848~1870). Her real name was Kim Am-deok (김암덕), and in the late Joseon period she learned skills such as Buddhist chanting, sogo dance and tightrope walking in Buldang-gol (불당골), Cheongnyong-ri (청룡리), Seoun-myeon, and made her name. According to the Korea Tourism Organization introduction, when Heungseon Daewongun (흥선대원군) renovated and expanded Gyeongbokgung in the 2nd year of King Gojong (고종) (1865), he summoned the Anseong Namsadang troupe to stage a play, and for comforting the laborers she was awarded an okgwanja (옥관자), a rank ornament equivalent to the senior third rank (정3품). Anseong Namsadang pungmul nori (남사당풍물놀이) was designated Gyeonggi-do Intangible Heritage No. 21 on September 30, 1997, and local notables (유지) of Anseong found the abandoned tomb, restored it and set up a gravestone in 1990.

  • Opening hours: Always open. Closed days: Open year-round.
  • Parking: Available.

The 2026 Anseongmatchum Namsadang Baudeogi Festival (2026 안성맞춤 남사당 바우덕이축제) (198 Namsadang-ro, Bogae-myeon, Anseong-si, ☎ 031-678-5414) is a festival themed on Namsadang Nori, a UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity. According to the Korea Tourism Organization introduction, Baudeogi was the first female kkokdusoe (꼭두쇠) of Joseon. In 2026 it was held October 2~5 at Anseongmatchum Land (안성맞춤랜드) and around Anseongcheon (안성천), from 10:00 to 21:00, and admission was free.

  • Programs: Namsadang Nori and traditional wedding performances, Anseong Old Marketplace (안성옛장터), Baudeogi Theme Park (바우덕이테마파크) experiences, Baudeogi Light Festival (바우덕이빛축제), the THE NEXT Baudeogi contest and more were held.

📍 Jeolla Nongak Training Halls — Imsil Pilbong, Yeonggwang Udo, Gokseong Jukdong, Hwasun Hancheon

The Pilbong Nongak Training Hall (필봉농악전수관) (272 Gangun-ro (강운로), Gangjin-myeon (강진면), Imsil-gun, ☎ 063-643-1902/2901) is a training hall built where Pilbong Nongak (필봉농악) began. It has a pungmul exhibition hall (풍물전시관) that shows the history of Pilbong Nongak and the Pilbong Hanok Stay (필봉 한옥스테이), where you experience traditional culture. It has performance facilities and lodging such as the Pilbong Indoor Theater (필봉 실내극장), which holds about 300 people, the dome-shaped performance hall Gutsandae (굿산대) and the Hanok Madang (한옥마당). According to the Korea Tourism Organization introduction, more than 30,000 people visit Pilbong Culture Village (필봉문화촌) every year to learn, experience or watch Pilbong Nongak.

  • Closed days: Open year-round. Parking: Free.
  • Experience fees: Learning pungmul and sogo play: students 10,000 won, adults 11,000 won. Making a hanji (한지) lantern and beona: students 17,000 won, adults 18,000 won. Learning nanta (난타): students 10,000 won, adults 11,000 won. Learning ganggangsullae (강강술래) and a traditional play madang: students 10,000 won, adults 11,000 won.

The Udo Nongak Training Hall (우도농악전수관) (11 Mulmu-ro 1-gil (물무로1길), Yeonggwang-eup (영광읍), Yeonggwang-gun, ☎ Udo Nongak Preservation Society (우도농악보존회) 061-351-2666) is a place that preserves and transmits the intangible heritage Udo Nongak (우도농악). Udo Nongak carries on the Yeongmujang (영무장) nongak tradition of Yeonggwang, Gochang (고창), Jangseong (장성), Hampyeong (함평) and other places on the southwest coast of Jeolla. According to the Korea Tourism Organization introduction, nongak was inscribed on the UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage list. The training hall runs an e-mobility samul nori program in which you learn samul nori while riding a Segway, and carries out activities such as stage performances and one-person changmuggeuk (창무극).

  • Opening hours: 08:30~17:30 (Saturday 09:00~18:00). Closed days: Sunday.
  • Pets: Not allowed.

The Jukdong Nongak Training Hall (죽동농악전수교육관) (46-1 Jukdong-gil (죽동길), Gokseong-eup (곡성읍), Gokseong-gun, ☎ 061-363-6000) is a place that teaches and carries on Gokseong Jukdong Nongak (곡성 죽동농악). Gokseong Jukdong Nongak was designated an intangible cultural heritage on April 19, 2002. It was built in 2007 as two buildings, one and two stories, nongak education takes place there regularly, and folk play equipment is on display. At the small performance space in front of the building, Jukdong Nongak is performed when there are events.

  • Opening hours: Always open. Closed days: Open year-round.
  • Fee: Free.

The Hancheon Nongak Training Hall (한천농악전수회관) (720 Kimsatgat-ro (김삿갓로), Dongbok-myeon (동복면), Hwasun-gun, ☎ 061-370-1225) is a place for transmission education and performances of Hancheon Nongak (한천농악). It is a two-story building built in 1996 with lodging and a practice room. Hancheon Nongak became widely known after winning the Presidential Prize at the 1964 National Folk Arts Contest (전국민속경연대회), and was designated an intangible cultural heritage in 1979.

  • Opening hours and closed days: Vary with the performance schedule.
  • Fee: Free.

📍 Gochang — Gochang Nongak Kkotdaerim Festival

The 7th Gochang Nongak Kkotdaerim Festival (제7회 고창농악 꽃대림축제) (106 Hyangsan 1-gil (향산1길), Seongsong-myeon (성송면), Gochang-gun, at the Gochang Nongak Training Hall (고창농악전수관), ☎ 063-562-2044) is a performing arts festival that carries on the Kkotdaerim-gut (꽃대림굿) of Gochang Nongak (고창농악), an intangible heritage of Jeonbuk Special Self-Governing Province. Kkotdaerim-gut was a communal ritual in which villagers held a gut together around Chilseok (칠석) on the lunar calendar to ease the year's toil and pray for abundance in the rest of the year. In 2026 it was held August 28~30 around the Gochang Nongak Training Hall, from 11:00 to 23:30, and admission was free.

  • Programs: Kkotdaerim Dangsan-gut (당산굿), Gochang Nongak Pungjang-gut (풍장굿), pan-gut (판굿) and japsaek (잡색), Balttal (발탈), a National Intangible Heritage, Gumi Mueul Nongak (구미무을농악), a Korea-Japan international exchange cooperation performance, a flea market and more were held.

⚠ 3 Things to Know Before You Go

  1. The Anseong regular performance is held only on weekends — 'Gombaengi-teotda' runs April 4 to November 29, 2026, every Saturday and Sunday at 14:00, and the performance hall is closed on weekdays.
  2. Two festivals have finished their 2026 schedule — the Gochang Nongak Kkotdaerim Festival was held August 28~30 and the Baudeogi Festival October 2~5.
  3. Each training hall operates differently — Jukdong Nongak Training Hall is always open, Udo Nongak Training Hall is closed on Sunday, and Hancheon Nongak Training Hall opens according to its performance schedule.

⚙ FAQ 2

Q. When can I see a Namsadang Nori performance?

A. According to the Korea Tourism Organization registered information, the regular performance 'Gombaengi-teotda' at the Anseong Namsadang Performance Hall is held April 4 to November 29, 2026, every Saturday and Sunday at 14:00 (about 90 minutes). The fee is 12,000 won for adults, 6,000 won for teenagers and 3,000 won for children.

Q. Where can I learn nongak and samul nori myself?

A. They are Pilbong Nongak Training Hall (learning pungmul and sogo play, adults 11,000 won), Udo Nongak Training Hall (e-mobility samul nori program) and Jukdong Nongak Training Hall (nongak education).
