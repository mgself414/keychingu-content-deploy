Autumn Nights at the Palace — Starlight Tours, Moonlight Tours, and the Royal Culture Festival: The Complete Guide to Programs You Can't Enter Without a Reservation

Subtitle: A completely different world from daytime Gyeongbokgung (#37) — a guide to the palace night and experience programs that run only in autumn. Gyeongbokgung Starlight Night Tour (Byeolbit-yahaeng, 경복궁 별빛야행, confirmed 9/2–10/24), Changdeokgung Moonlight Tour (Dalbit-gihaeng, 창덕궁 달빛기행), and the autumn Royal Palace Culture Festival (궁중문화축전, confirmed 10/7–11), plus a breakdown of Korea's application → lottery → purchase ticketing system, and a pre-trip prep checklist. Every single one runs on reservation only — that's why this guide comes "in advance."

🎯 Why "Night" at the Palace — And Why Read This Now

Seoul's five grand palaces are open to daytime visitors any time, for just a few thousand won. But the palace at night is a different animal — it opens only through premium, small-group, reservation-only programs that come with expert guides and royal court refreshments, and the autumn sessions are competitive enough that even Koreans have to win a lottery draw (추첨) to get in. There's no walking up on the day as a foreign traveler — applying and buying tickets weeks before your trip is the only way in.

① Confirmed 2026 Autumn Schedule (Web-Verified)

1. Gyeongbokgung Starlight Night Tour (Byeolbit-yahaeng, 경복궁 별빛야행) — Sep 2 – Oct 24 (Confirmed · Rest Sep 30 – Oct 4)

  • The flagship night program: a guided walk through Gyeongbokgung (경복궁) after dark that includes a Suragan (수라간, the palace's royal kitchen) experience and royal court refreshments (궁중 다과). ₩60,000 per person · two sessions a day (18:20/19:30, 110 minutes) · during the rest period (Sep 30–Oct 4), a royal court music concert runs in its place.
  • Ticketing flow (based on the actual 2026 autumn session): application window (8/14–20) → lottery results (8/24) → winners' advance purchase window (8/25–27) → remaining-seats general sale, first-come first-served, opens 8/28 at 14:00 (confirmed). If you missed the application window, this general sale is your second shot — after that, watch for a returned ticket (취소표) (the cancellation deadline is 17:00, two days before the visit, so seats tend to open up right before then).

2. Autumn Royal Palace Culture Festival (Gungjung Munhwa Chukjeon, 궁중문화축전) — Oct 7 – Oct 11 (Confirmed)

  • The biggest palace event of the year, held simultaneously across five palaces and a royal shrine — Gyeongbokgung, Changdeokgung, Deoksugung, Changgyeonggung, and Jongmyo. It mixes free programs with reservation-based ones, making it the lowest-barrier way to catch a palace's autumn night atmosphere without any advance booking at all.

3. Changdeokgung Moonlight Tour (Dalbit-gihaeng, 창덕궁 달빛기행) — Sep 10 – Oct 17 (Confirmed · Thu–Sun)

  • A night tour that goes as far as the Huwon (후원, Changdeokgung's Secret Garden) under moonlight. ₩30,000 per person · six sessions a day (Part 1 around 19:10, Part 2 around 20:00, staggered entry, 100 minutes) · the most competitive program on the list.
  • Ticketing (confirmed for the 2026 autumn session): Ticketlink (티켓링크) application (through 8/26) → lottery results 8/28 at 17:00 → winners' purchase window (8/29 14:00 – 9/2, up to 2 tickets) → remaining-seats general sale, first-come first-served, 9/3 at 14:00. If you don't win the lottery, this 9/3 sale is your real shot.
  • ★ Four foreign-language-guide-only sessions — Sep 20, Sep 27, Oct 4, Oct 11 (English, Chinese, Japanese): If you're a foreign traveler, make these four dates your top priority — they're the only sessions where the guiding actually reaches you.

4. Other Autumn Palace Experiences (Announced Per Session)

  • Gyeongbokgung Saeng-gwabang (생과방, Sep 3 – Nov 3, ₩15,000) — a tasting experience for royal court sweets and tea in the Sojubang hall. The lowest price point among the autumn programs.
  • Gyeongbokgung Suragan Tasting Experience (수라간 시식공감, Sep 17 – Sep 26, ₩25,000) — a royal-cuisine tasting program, a short ten-day window.
  • Changgyeonggung Yayeon (창경궁 야연, an evening performance program), and more — quick to sell out once a session opens.

② How Korea's Ticketing System Works — Application → Lottery → Purchase

This is the part that trips up foreign visitors the most. It isn't first-come, first-served:

1. Application (응모): Apply through the application page during a set window — this step doesn't involve any payment.

2. Lottery (추첨): Winners are drawn at random from applicants — check the results on the announcement date.

3. Advance purchase window (선예매): Only winners can pay, within a set window (miss it, and your slot is automatically forfeited and released as a returned ticket).

4. Returned tickets (취소표): Leftover slots after the payment deadline sometimes go on general sale — a second chance for anyone who didn't win the lottery.

  • Since purchases run through domestic Korean ticket platforms, foreign cards and overseas sign-ups sometimes get blocked — some sessions set aside a separate quota for foreign visitors, so check the announcement for a "Foreigner" line first. If you're locked out, having a Korean friend or your hotel concierge book on your behalf is the realistic workaround.

🧳 Prep Checklist (Starting 4–6 Weeks Before Your Trip)

  • [ ] Follow the announcement channels — check the Royal Palace Culture Festival and Royal Palaces and Tombs Center (궁능유적본부) official pages regularly starting 6 weeks out (application windows are usually open for under a week)
  • [ ] Calendar all three ticketing dates — application, results, and advance purchase (miss even one, and your shot is gone)
  • [ ] Check for a foreign-visitor quota — look for a "Foreigner" line in the announcement first; if there isn't one, have a backup plan for booking through someone local
  • [ ] Have a lottery-loss Plan B — set an alert for returned-ticket releases, and line up a free Royal Palace Culture Festival program as a backup
  • [ ] On the day: night palaces keep lighting minimal — flash photography is customarily off-limits, wear comfortable walking shoes, and bring a layer for the cool autumn night air
  • [ ] Pair it with a daytime palace visit — an early morning at Gyeongbokgung (경복궁, see the early-morning strategy in #37) and Bukchon (북촌, #38) in the morning, then the night program in the evening, makes for a complete day
