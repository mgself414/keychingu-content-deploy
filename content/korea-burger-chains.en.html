Why K-Burgers — The Brand With More Stores Than McDonald's

The Korean burger market holds a twist most foreigners never see coming. The No. 1 chain by store count isn't McDonald's (맥도날드) — it's Mom's Touch, which has overtaken the global players with 1,400+ stores nationwide. And Lotteria, which has been reinventing the burger for Korean palates since 1979, created the Bulgogi Burger (불고기버거) and the Shrimp Burger (새우버거) — the originals that show exactly what "Koreanizing the burger" means.

In other words, in Korea a burger is not "a taste you already know." Whole chicken-thigh patties, bulgogi sauce, shrimp patties, budget handmade-style builds — burgers you have never had back home are within a five-minute walk. Following chicken (#65) and cafes (#61), this guide answers the chain series' final question: which burger sign should you walk into?

Brand Profiles — Start With the One-Line Identity

🟠 Mom's Touch (맘스터치) — No. 1 by Store Count · King of the Whole-Thigh Patty

  • One line: A homegrown Korean brand and the nation's No. 1 by store count (1,400+). The burger brand Korea's MZ generation loves most.
  • Signature: The Cyber Burger (싸이버거) — a thick patty made from a whole chicken thigh, deep-fried in one piece. Not a pressed patty; it's literally "a piece of fried chicken inside a burger."
  • What sets it apart: Cooked to order (piping hot and crispy) · strong chicken sides too (crossover with #65) · overwhelming portion for the price
  • Price range: Burger only ₩4,000~6,000 (~$3.0~4.5) · set ₩7,000~9,000 (~$5.2~6.7)
  • Fit for visitors: The No. 1 pick for "a burger you can only eat in Korea." Bonus fun in the spicy options (seasonal lines like White Garlic (화이트갈릭) and Unbelievable (언빌리버블)).

🔴 Lotteria (롯데리아) — The 1979 Original · The Textbook of Burger Koreanization

  • One line: Korea's longest-running burger chain (1979~). It survived the global-chain onslaught by going thoroughly Korean in flavor.
  • Signature: The Bulgogi Burger — the original bulgogi-marinade-sauce patty. The Shrimp Burger — a whole-shrimp patty. Lotteria set the Korean standard for both.
  • What sets it apart: It's everywhere, including small provincial towns · a spirit of limited-edition experimentation (buzz-makers like the Squid Burger (오징어버거) and the Gundaeria (군대리아)) · many 24h locations
  • Price range: Burger only ₩4,500~7,000 (~$3.4~5.2) · set ₩7,500~10,000 (~$5.6~7.5)
  • Fit for visitors: A cultural experience — tasting "the Koreanization of the burger." The bulgogi + shrimp combo is the starter course.

🟡 No Brand Burger (노브랜드버거) — Shinsegae's Value-for-Money Trendsetter

  • One line: The burger version of E-mart (이마트)'s No Brand label (2019~). The face of budget dining, expanding at breakneck speed on a "Value for Money" concept.
  • Signature: The NBB Signature (NBB 시그니처) line — handmade-burger-style builds at a low price.
  • What sets it apart: Pricing engineered so sets start in the ₩5,000s · minimalist yellow signage · ties into the E-mart / convenience-store ecosystem (#1261)
  • Price range: Burger only ₩2,500~5,000 (~$1.9~3.7) · set ₩5,000~7,500 (~$3.7~5.6)
  • Fit for visitors: A full meal for budget travelers. Experience Korean prices firsthand: "burger + drink + fries for ₩6,000."

🟢 Frank Burger (프랭크버거) — The Emerging Budget Handmade Style

  • One line: An emerging chain that expanded explosively in the 2020s. Handmade-burger style at rock-bottom prices — multiplying fast around university districts and residential areas.
  • Signature: Handmade-style lines like the whole-shrimp burger and double cheese, in the ₩3,000~5,000 range.
  • What sets it apart: Small stores, takeout-focused · clustered in university areas (Sinchon (신촌), Hongdae (홍대), etc.)
  • Fit for visitors: A brand you meet in "the neighborhood" rather than tourist zones — a taste of local commercial streets.

🌍 Global Chains' Korea-Only Menus — Signs You Know, Menus You Don't

There is exactly one reason to visit McDonald's or Burger King (버거킹) in Korea when you have them at home: the Korea-exclusive menus.

  • McDonald's: The Bulgogi Burger — a permanent Korea-only menu item. The McSpicy Shanghai Burger (맥스파이시 상하이버거) is also tuned to Korean-style heat.
  • Burger King: Korea-born limited Whoppers like the Quattro Cheese Whopper (콰트로치즈와퍼) drop periodically.
  • Shake Shack (쉐이크쉑) · Five Guys (파이브가이즈): Premium global chains — present in major districts like Gangnam (강남), but the lineup matches what you have at home, so they're excluded from this edition.

🎯 Know This Before Ordering — Common to All Chains

  • 🖥️ Kiosk ordering is the default: Most stores use self-service kiosks — there is an English (EN) language button (supported at Mom's Touch, Lotteria, and most global chains). Menus are pictorial, so you can order without knowing the language.
  • 💳 Foreign cards: Kiosk acceptance of foreign cards varies by store — if yours fails, switch to ordering at the counter, which solves it most of the time.
  • 🥤 Set culture: A "set" = burger + fries + drink. Same as an American "combo." Drink refills are generally not a thing (don't expect bottomless refills).
  • 🕐 Hours: Lotteria and McDonald's run many 24h stores; Mom's Touch and No Brand Burger mostly close at 22:00~23:00.

Scenario Picks — 3 Situations

Scenario 1. "Just one burger I can only eat in Korea"

  • A Mom's Touch Cyber Burger set. The whole-thigh patty is a build no global chain anywhere has — take on the spicy sauce line to complete the experience.
  • Alternative: the Lotteria Bulgogi Burger + Shrimp Burger double (two burgers alone cost about the same as one set).

Scenario 2. "A meal on a ₩6,000 (~$4.5) budget"

  • No Brand Burger or Frank Burger. Sets in the ₩5,000s — together with convenience store lunch boxes (#1262), one of the two pillars of budget travel.

Scenario 3. "I want a burger in the middle of the night"

  • Lotteria and McDonald's 24h stores. Locations on main streets near stations and nightlife districts are the safe bet. Late at night, walking in beats delivery apps.

5 Usage Tips — Get 200% Out of K-Burgers

Tip 1. Choose by patty, not by sign

Whole thigh = Mom's Touch, bulgogi · shrimp = Lotteria, value = No Brand · Frank. If you know what you want, the brand is the answer (same principle as convenience stores, #1261).

Tip 2. At Mom's Touch, the wait is normal

Everything is cooked to order, so a 5~10 minute wait is standard. The temperature of a just-made Cyber Burger is the reward — if you're in a hurry, go Lotteria or a global chain.

Tip 3. At global chains, order only the exclusives

McDonald's Bulgogi Burger, Burger King's Korea-only Whoppers — "an unknown menu under a known sign" is the rule. Ordering the same thing you get at home isn't traveling.

Tip 4. Find the English button on the kiosk first

Look for the EN/ENG button in the corner of the first screen — and if there isn't one, the pictures are enough. The set-upgrade popups (side swaps · large sets) are safe to SKIP.

Tip 5. A burger crawl is a half-day itinerary too

Cyber Burger → Bulgogi Burger → NBB Signature, one per stop — the same travel method as the cafe crawl in the chain series (#61).

FAQ 6 — K-Burger Questions Foreigners Actually Ask

Q1. What is Mom's Touch and why is it so famous?

  • A homegrown Korean brand that's No. 1 by store count. The Cyber Burger's whole-fried chicken-thigh patty is completely different from anything at the global chains — "the burger made by the nation of fried chicken (#65)."

Q2. What is a Bulgogi Burger?

  • A burger with a patty coated in Korean bulgogi marinade (a sweet-savory soy-based sauce). Lotteria is the original, and McDonald's Korea carries one too — comparing the two versions is part of the fun.

Q3. Do sets come with drink refills?

  • Generally, no. Korean fast food has no refill culture — a few stores are exceptions, but the standard is not to expect it.

Q4. What if the kiosk has no English?

  • The pictorial menu is enough to order, and you can always switch to the counter. Finger pointing plus "set-eu (세트)" gets the job done.

Q5. Are there vegetarian options?

  • Limited. Lotteria has experimented with plant-based patties (the Miracle Burger (미라클버거), etc.), but availability isn't guaranteed — for dedicated vegetarian eating, see #799/#801 (vegan guides).

Q6. Can I get delivery?

  • All four are on delivery apps (Baedal Minjok (배달의민족), etc.) — but the apps require a Korean phone number and card (#1149 delivery culture). For travelers, walking into a store is faster.
