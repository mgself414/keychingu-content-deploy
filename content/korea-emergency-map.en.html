Korea Emergency and Safety Guide: Where to Turn in a Hurry — 112, 119 and 1330, Finding ER and Pharmacies, Lost Items, Disaster Alerts

Subtitle: Police 112 · Fire and ambulance 119 (1339 is for infectious disease consultation) · Tourist interpretation 1330 by phone in 8 languages, by chat in 4 languages (Russian, Vietnamese, Thai and Malay/Indonesian 08:00~19:00) · ERs through E-Gen, 156 Moonlight Children's Hospitals nationwide open until 12 a.m. (midnight) · Seoul subway lost-and-found centers weekdays 09:00~18:00 · Incheon Airport lost-and-found office 07:00~21:30 · Disaster alerts in 5 foreign languages — checked with the Korea Tourism Organization (VisitKorea), National Fire Agency (소방청), National Emergency Medical Center (중앙응급의료센터), Seoul Metro (서울교통공사), Incheon International Airport Corporation (인천국제공항공사) and Ministry of the Interior and Safety (행정안전부)

🎯 How to use this guide

It is two in the morning in your hotel room, you have a rising fever, and your phone screen shows a disaster alert in Korean you have never seen before. What you need then is not a long explanation but which number to press and where to go. This guide sorts urgent situations into five branches: emergency calls, when you are sick, pharmacies, when you lose something, and disaster alerts.

The figures and operating hours are only the values read on each organization's and hospital's website on 2026-11-02. For each branch, the step-by-step procedure continues in a dedicated manual below.

📞 Emergency calls: memorize just three numbers

  • 112 — Police. For reporting crimes and accidents.
  • 119 — Fire and ambulance. A medical emergency is also 119. For foreigners and others who find it hard to report by voice, text, photo and video reporting has been available since 2011 (Korea.kr policy briefing (정책브리핑)). When you report, say the location → symptoms → age and existing conditions → contact number, in that order, and do not hang up; follow the instructions (National Fire Agency (소방청)).
  • 1339 — Consultation when an infectious disease is suspected (Korea Tourism Organization (VisitKorea)). For a medical emergency where someone is injured or has collapsed, call 119.
  • 1330 — Tourist interpretation helpline. Open every day of the year. By phone it covers 8 languages (Korean, Japanese, English, Chinese, Russian, Vietnamese, Thai, Malay/Indonesian), and by text chat 4 languages (Korean, English, Japanese, Chinese). Russian, Vietnamese, Thai and Malay/Indonesian are available 08:00~19:00, by phone only (Korea Tourism Organization (VisitKorea) service standard). The hours for the other 4 languages are not stated in the official notice — for a late-night emergency, start with 119 or 112, not 1330. From abroad dial +82-2-1330; internet voice calls made over Wi-Fi are free, and you can also ask through the 1330 messenger, KakaoTalk, Facebook, LINE and WhatsApp (Korea Tourism Organization (VisitKorea)).
  • 120 — Seoul Dasan Call Center foreign-language consultation. English, Chinese, Japanese, Vietnamese and Mongolian, weekdays 09:00~18:00 only. Press 02-120, then choose 9 (Seoul Dasan Call Center 120 (120다산콜재단)).

📸 Save 112, 119 and 1330 in your phone contacts now. For the 1330 screens, see #1299 How to Use 1330.

🏥 When you are sick: find an ER through E-Gen, and see a university hospital's international clinic for foreign-language care

  • Finding an ER: Use E-Gen from the National Emergency Medical Center (중앙응급의료센터) to find ERs by map, region or radius. Each hospital shows its phone number and ER operating status (National Emergency Medical Center (중앙응급의료센터)).
  • When a child is sick at night: Moonlight Children's Hospitals (달빛어린이병원) see patients at night and on holidays, 365 days a year, and the portal notice says until 12 a.m. (midnight). As of November 2026, there are 156 nationwide. Closing times may differ by hospital, so check before you go (National Emergency Medical Center (중앙응급의료센터)). It is a pediatric night clinic, not an ER.
  • Getting care in a foreign language: Many large hospitals run an international clinic center. Most are closed on weekends and public holidays, so check before you go (Korea Tourism Organization (VisitKorea)).
  • Seoul Asan Medical Center (서울아산병원): interpretation in English, Japanese, Chinese, Russian, Mongolian, Arabic and Vietnamese (Seoul Asan Medical Center (서울아산병원)).
  • Severance Hospital (세브란스병원): interpretation in English, Chinese, Japanese, Russian, Arabic and Mongolian (Severance Hospital (세브란스병원)).
  • Samsung Medical Center (삼성서울병원): International Healthcare Center +82-2-3410-0200 (Samsung Medical Center (삼성서울병원)).

For the order of steps from check-in to payment and pharmacy with just your passport, see #1307 Hospital Visit Procedure.

💊 Pharmacies: at night and on holidays, search first

  • Holiday and late-night pharmacies: On the Korean Pharmaceutical Association's (대한약사회) Holiday Pharmacy finder (휴일지킴이약국) (pharm114), find open pharmacies by map or region, and look up the list of public late-night pharmacies (공공심야약국) separately. The site is in Korean, so paste the address into a map app (Korean Pharmaceutical Association (대한약사회)).
  • Convenience store medicines: The safe household medicines (안전상비의약품) sold at convenience stores are 13 items on the Ministry of Food and Drug Safety (식약처) list — fever and pain relievers such as Tylenol Tablets 500mg (타이레놀정500mg) and Tylenol Tablets 160mg (타이레놀정160mg), cold medicine such as Pancol-A oral liquid (판콜에이내복액), digestive aids such as Bearse tablets (베아제정), and patches such as Shinshin Pas Arex (신신파스아렉스) (Ministry of Food and Drug Safety (식품의약품안전처)).
  • For a card that shows your symptoms in one sentence, see #1278 Buying Medicine at a Pharmacy.

🧳 When you lose something: start with the lost-and-found center where you rode

  • Seoul subway: Lost-and-found centers are divided by line — Lines 1 and 2 at Seoul City Hall Station (시청역) 02-6110-1122, Lines 3 and 4 at Chungmuro Station (충무로역) 02-6110-3344, Lines 5 and 8 at Wangsimni Station (왕십리역) 02-6311-6765, Lines 6 and 7 at Taereung Station (태릉입구역) 02-6311-6766, Line 9 (Eonju to VHS Medical Center) at Sports Complex Station (종합운동장역) 02-2656-0787. If you do not know which center, call the main number 1577-1234. Weekdays 09:00~18:00, closed Saturdays, Sundays and public holidays. Items registered at a station are handled by the lost-and-found center within 7 days; if unclaimed, they are passed to the police station and kept for 6 months (Seoul Metro (서울교통공사)).
  • Incheon Airport: Lost-and-found office Terminal 1, basement level 1, west side 032-741-3110, Terminal 2, basement level 1, east side 032-741-8988. 07:00~21:30, every day of the year. To report a theft inside Terminal 1 call 032-740-0112; for other inquiries, the airport information desk 1577-2600 (Incheon International Airport Corporation (인천국제공항공사)).
  • For the counters of taxis, buses and hotels, and for reporting a loss to the police, see #1392 Lost Item Manual.

📲 Disaster alerts: to get them in a foreign language, install the app

  • Disaster alerts sent to Korean phones are in Korean by default. Foreigners can install the Ministry of the Interior and Safety's (행정안전부) Emergency Ready App separately to see disaster and safety information on English, Chinese and Japanese screens (Ministry of the Interior and Safety (행정안전부)).
  • Foreign-language disaster alert notices cover 5 languages: English, Chinese, Vietnamese, Thai and Japanese. Turn on notifications in the app settings (Ministry of the Interior and Safety (행정안전부), 2024-09).

🧰 Step by step by situation: follow it as written

  1. Someone is injured or has collapsed: 119 → if you do not know the address, read out nearby signs or building names and turn on your phone's location (GPS) → describe symptoms briefly, such as "not breathing, unconscious, bleeding" → do not hang up and follow the instructions.
  2. A fever at night: confused, struggling to breathe or having convulsions → call 119 right away. Otherwise, if it is mild, a convenience store fever reducer; for a child, a Moonlight Children's Hospital (after checking its hours); if you are worried, the nearest ER on E-Gen.
  3. You lost your wallet or passport: the lost-and-found center of the transport you rode → go to the nearest police station (police box) and report the loss — do not use 112 unless it is an emergency → for a passport, contact your embassy.
  4. A disaster alert arrived: if you have the app, check the same alert in a foreign language in the Emergency Ready App. If you do not have the app, read the area name and the action instructions in the alert with a translation app's camera. If it is urgent, 119.

⚠ 4 common mistakes

  1. "If I am sick, 1339" — 1339 is the infectious disease consultation number. For an emergency, 119.
  2. "1330 is every language, 24 hours" — Russian, Vietnamese, Thai and Malay/Indonesian are 08:00~19:00 and phone only. Text chat is Korean, English, Japanese and Chinese only.
  3. "120 foreign-language consultation is anytime" — Foreign-language consultation is weekdays 09:00~18:00 only.
  4. "Subway lost-and-found centers are open on weekends too" — Weekdays 09:00~18:00 only. If you lost something on a weekend, first ask station staff to register it.

⚙ FAQ 4

Q1. Can I report to 119 in English?

We could not confirm from the official notice whether English service is guaranteed. Say your location and symptoms briefly and do not hang up. If speaking is hard, 119 also takes reports by text, photo and video (Korea.kr policy briefing (정책브리핑)).

Q2. Which hospitals treat patients in foreign languages?

Large hospitals with international clinic centers, such as Seoul Asan Medical Center (서울아산병원), Severance Hospital (세브란스병원) and Samsung Medical Center (삼성서울병원). Most are closed on weekends and public holidays, so for night and holiday emergencies, find an ER on E-Gen.

Q3. What medicine can I buy at a convenience store?

The 13 items of safe household medicines (안전상비의약품) set by the Ministry of Food and Drug Safety (식약처) — basic medicines such as fever and pain relievers, cold medicine, digestive aids and patches. For medicine that needs a prescription, go to a pharmacy.

Q4. Can I receive disaster alerts in English?

If you install the Emergency Ready App, you receive them in English, Chinese, Vietnamese, Thai and Japanese (Ministry of the Interior and Safety (행정안전부)).
