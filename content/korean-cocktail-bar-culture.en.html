Korean Cocktail Bar Culture — Why Seoul Became the ‘Cocktail Capital’ of Asia

Bar Cham·Zest·Traditional liquor cocktail·Asia's 50 Best — Korean cocktail bar culture explainer for foreigners. Rather than asking “Which bar is best?”, we are asking “Why has Seoul suddenly become a cocktail city that the world is paying attention to?”

When many foreigners think of Korean alcohol, soju, beer, and makgeolli first come to mind. However, when you come to Seoul and go into a cocktail bar, you will be surprised twice. First, cocktails made with Korean ingredients such as Jeju tangerines and Chungju apples are served at a bar in a converted Hanok. Second, its completeness and service are world-class. In fact, bars in Seoul sweep the top rankings of Asia's 50 Best Bars every year, and one bar is even ranked among the World's 50 Best Bars (as of 2025 — recommended). This article is not about “where to drink” (→ cross-link), but how Seoul emerged as the cocktail capital of Asia in just 10 years explains the background and meaning from a foreigner’s perspective. ⚠️ 3 misunderstandings foreigners often make

1. "Koreans only drink soju and beer" ❌ — Traditional drinking parties (→ #1140 company dinners and drinking) still exist, but in Seoul, there is a cocktail bar scene that is on a completely different level. It is also a city of gourmet cocktails that bar critics from around the world visit every year.

2. "Cocktails are a Western culture, so shouldn't they copy Korean ones?" ❌ — Rather, it's the opposite. The global differentiation of Seoul bars is traditional liquor and Korean ingredients. Local products such as makgeolli, soju, traditional fruit wine, Jeju tangerines, and Chungju apples are mixed into cocktails to create “a drink that can only be enjoyed in Korea.”

3. "High-end bars will be expensive and unfriendly" ❌ — Prices range from 20,000 to 25,000 won per drink, which is more reasonable than Tokyo and Hong Kong (recommend checking), and the Seoul bar scene has a reputation for friendly and knowledgeable hospitality. Even if you don't know much about cocktails, the bartender will ask you what you like and make recommendations.

① Seoul has become the ‘cocktail capital of Asia’

Asia's 50 Best Bars is the most authoritative annual list of Asia's best bars. In the 2025 announcement, several bars in Seoul were included in both the main (1st to 50th) and expanded lists (51st to 100th) (more than 7 in total — recommended to check with the official list). On a single city basis, it is on par with Tokyo, Singapore, and Hong Kong.

In particular, Zest in Cheongdam-dong ranked 2nd in Asia's 50 Best Bars 2025, and also ranked high (16th — recommended to check) in World's 50 Best Bars. It is a symbolic event that goes beyond Asia and is recognized on the world stage, putting a Korean bar at the top of the global rankings. Considering that there was virtually no scene like this in Seoul just 10 years ago, this speed is surprising.

Rank/year changes every year. Before your visit, check the official list of Asia's 50 Best Bars (theworlds50best.com) for the most up-to-date information.

② Why did it come to mind ① — The weapon called ‘traditional liquor cocktail’

The first secret to making Seoul's bar scene world-class is Korea's unique ingredients. Instead of copying Western bars, they created their own identity with alcohol and ingredients that are unique to Korea.

A representative place is Bar Cham in Seochon in Jongno. In a space converted to a hanok, the menu is divided into six regions across the country and cocktails are made using traditional liquor and products from each region — tangerines for the Jeju section, pears and apples for the southern section, and so on. Just drinking it is an experience that takes you around Korean geography and history. This farm-to-bar approach — connecting directly with local farmers and breweries to use seasonal ingredients — has become the common language of the Seoul scene.

Makgeolli, soju, and traditional fruit wine (→ #136 Makgeolli · #203 Korean alcohol) being used as a cocktail base right alongside gin and whiskey is a sight you won't find in any other city in the world — it's uniquely Seoul's.

③ Why Did It Take Off ② — Zero Waste and Sustainability

The second secret is sustainability. Zest, the bar that's come closest to the world's #1 spot, is known for more than just great cocktails — it's known for an operation that leaves almost nothing to waste.

  • Reusing ingredients: Scraps that would normally be thrown away — bread ends, fruit peels — get put back into cocktails.
  • Local and self-grown: Ingredients are sourced as close to the bar as possible, and some are grown in-house (like getting honey from urban beekeeping — details worth confirming).
  • Minimalism: Labels are stripped off and clutter is removed so the focus stays purely on the spirit and the flavor.

By showing that this philosophy leads to better flavor and creativity, not just 'feel-good marketing,' sustainability has become a new benchmark for the entire Asian bar scene.

④ Why Did It Take Off ③ — World-Class Bartenders and a 'Team' Culture

The third secret is people. Seoul has several bartenders at the level of world-competition champions. Figures like Im Byung-jin of Bar Cham and Eom Do-hwan of Le Chamber(르 챔버) reached the top of global cocktail competitions such as Diageo World Class (year and competition worth confirming), and they were the first generation to push the Seoul scene's technical level up a notch.

Another hallmark is a structure where a single 'team' grows several bars at once. One team runs multiple bars with different concepts and develops talent across them — for example, the team behind Cricket(크리켓) and Pine & Co(파인앤코), or the team that opened Bar Cham, Pomme(뽐), and Cham in Season(참앤시즌) one after another. A virtuous cycle — a bartender who worked at one legendary bar, Alice (앨리스), goes independent and opens another must-visit spot (like Zest) — has helped the scene grow quickly.

⑤ From Speakeasies to Science Labs — a Diversity of Concepts

Another draw of Seoul's cocktail bars is the diversity of spaces and concepts. They're not just selling drinks — they're selling an experience.

  • Hidden entrances (speakeasies): In Cheongdam, Le Chamber's door only opens once you push a button hidden in a bookshelf, and Alice is entered through a secret door inside a flower shop.
  • Period concepts: Charles H (찰스 H), at the Four Seasons Hotel near Gwanghwamun, recreates a 1920s-30s Art Deco speakeasy.
  • Spatial concepts: Villa Records (빌라레코드), in a Gangnam basement, is a vinyl listening lounge with 1960s-70s space-age vibes, while MIXOLOGY(믹솔로지) treats cocktails like a science experiment.
  • Korean-rooted depth: Gong-Gan (공간), in the Bukchon hanok village, unpacks childhood memories through cocktails and practices upcycling — and made it onto Asia's 50 Best for it.

The same 'one cocktail' becomes a completely different experience depending on what story it's wrapped in — this competition in storytelling has raised the level of the whole Seoul scene.

⑥ From Gangnam to Jongno and Eunpyeong — the Scene Spreads

In its early days, Seoul's cocktail bars were concentrated in Gangnam (Cheongdam, Apgujeong). Pioneers like Le Chamber, Alice, and Zest opened up the scene in this area. Now the center of gravity has spread across the whole city — to Jongno (Seochon, Bukchon, Anguk), where tradition still lives; to Mapo (Gongdeok), with its younger sensibility; and even to the foot of Bukhansan Mountain in Eunpyeong-gu. Bars far out on the edge of the city sometimes get named "the place bartenders recommend most." The fact that good bars aren't trapped in one neighborhood but have spread across the whole city shows just how deep and thick the scene has become.

⑦ How Foreigners Can Enjoy Seoul's Cocktail Bars

  • Reservations: Popular bars (especially ones on Asia's 50 Best) often have long lines or require reservations. It's best to book ahead through a reservation app like Catchtable, an Instagram DM, or a phone call.
  • English: The more famous the bar, the better the English service. Even if you don't know much about cocktails, just tell the bartender what flavors you like (refreshing/smoky/not too sweet) and they'll recommend something.
  • Cost: A cocktail runs roughly 20,000~25,000 won, with hotel bars and tasting courses running higher (worth confirming the timing and exchange rate). Service charge is usually included, so no separate tip is needed.
  • Manners: Many bars have few seats, so keeping to your reservation and party size matters. For a big group, pick a spacious bar (like Villa Records). Bring your passport for ID checks (19 and over).
  • Route: Neighborhoods like Cheongdam in Gangnam and Seochon in Jongno, where several bars cluster together, are great for bar hopping.

Specific bar lists and routes are covered in a separate location guide (Seoul Craft Cocktail Bar Guide).

❓ Frequently Asked Questions

1. Can I go even if I don't know much about cocktails? — Yes. Seoul's bar scene is known for its hospitality. Just tell the bartender your preferences and they'll recommend something tailored to you. The same goes for menu-less bars.

2. Are soju and makgeolli cocktails actually good? — The deep flavor of traditional liquor pairs well with cocktails. It's a combination you can only try in Korea, so it's well worth trying.

3. Is it okay to go alone? — Yes — taking a seat at the bar counter makes a solo visit feel natural, and it's a great way to chat with the bartender.

4. Do only the expensive places count? — No. Neighborhood bars (like Notes) can also deliver world-class quality. The average level across the Seoul scene itself is high.

5. When's the best time to go? — Weeknights (Sunday to Thursday) are quieter, while Friday and Saturday get busy. For popular bars, arriving right at opening makes it easier to get a seat.
