Korean NAVER Map Directions, No-Panic Procedure — Follow the Screens Exactly

① How to Read This Procedure — Screen Notation Rules

This piece also uses the same convention as the #1270 kiosk piece. Instead of "use NAVER Map," it writes down exactly "what appears on screen and which button to press."

  • Buttons on screen appear in brackets: [ Directions ]
  • Screen titles and prompts appear in corner brackets: 「Gilchatgi (길찾기)」
  • Hand actions appear bold: "tap the search bar at the top"

② Why NAVER Instead of Google Maps

South Korean security regulations block detailed map data from being exported overseas, so Google Maps only handles some driving directions and effectively doesn't support public transit or walking directions. NAVER Map (Kakao Map is on par), by contrast, is the domestic source of truth — real-time subway and bus data, even walking shortcuts. The bigger picture of Korea's digital infrastructure is in #080 (arrival essentials) — but the conclusion is all you need: for directions in Korea, it's NAVER.

③ Switching the Language to English — 3 Steps

The app opens entirely in Korean the first time. Do this first:

1. Tap [ ☰ Menu (메뉴) ] at the top-left or bottom

2. Tap [ Settings (설정) ]

3. [ Language (언어) ] → select [ English ]

The whole app switches to English instantly, no restart needed. Every screen described from here on assumes English.

④ Search Procedure — When You Can't Type a Korean Business Name

  • Typing the romanized name as-is in the search bar at the top still works ("Gyeongbokgung," "Myeongdong").
  • If you don't know the store's name, break through with an English category search instead: type a generic term like coffee, convenience store, or pharmacy and a list of nearby candidates comes up.
  • Check distance and star ratings in the candidate list, tap one → moves to the detail screen.

⑤ Directions Master Flow — 4 Steps

1. On the destination's detail screen, tap the blue [ Directions ] button

2. Four transport-mode tabs sit side by side at the top: 🚗 Car 🚇 Public Transit 🚶 Walk 🚴 Bike — switch by tapping

3. Route candidates for the selected mode appear as a list (sorted by travel time)

4. Tap the route you want → enters the turn-by-turn detail screen

⑥ How to Read the Results Screen — Exit Numbers Are the Key Info

This is where first-time map app users get confused the most. Reproducing the screen layout exactly:

```

🚇 34min · 2 transfers · ₩1,500

① Head toward Gangnam Station (강남역) [ Exit 3 (3번 출구) ] — 3-min walk

↓ Board the Sinbundang Line (신분당선) · next train in 4 min · 4 stops

② Get off at Sinnonhyeon Station (신논현역) → [ Exit 2 (2번 출구) ]

↓ 5-min walk

🏁 Arrival (도착)

```

  • Public transit = subway + bus combined: a single route mixes subway and bus together. The screen tells you every time you need to transfer.
  • Exit numbers: it doesn't matter if you can't read the station name. Just look at the number in something like [ Exit 3 (3번 출구) ] and find that number — that's the single most important piece of information this app gives you.
  • Wait time: shown in minutes, like "next train in 4 min."
  • Number of stops: counted as "4 stops" — how many stops until you get off.
  • Walking mode catches underground-passage and through-building shortcuts — it's often the actual shortest route, rather than a long detour above ground.

⑦ Stair-Avoidance Mode — Essential for Rolling Luggage and Older Travelers

Since August 2025, public transit routes have had a [ Exclude Stairs (계단 제외) ] option. Turn it on and elevator locations show up on the map, with the route itself recalculated to pass through elevators. If you're pulling luggage or find stairs difficult, turn this on before you start, no exceptions.

⑧ Save, Share & 10 Survival Words

  • The heart/bookmark icon at the top of the results screen → saves it as a favorite, so you can pull it right back up from the menu later.
  • The share icon → sends the route link via KakaoTalk or messages. Whoever receives it can open it straight in a browser, even without the app.

⑨ 3 Panic Scenarios

1. The result is different from Google Maps — that's normal. As covered in ② above, Google Maps doesn't have Korea's public transit and walking data. Trust NAVER Map's results.

2. Zero search results — most likely a romanization spelling difference ("Gwanghwamun" versus "Kwanghwamun," for example). Try shortening the spelling, or work around it with the category search from ④.

3. GPS jumps around — your position teleporting around in dense high-rise areas or underground is common. Walk a few steps and check the screen again — it settles down.

⑩ Plan B — If NAVER Map Isn't Working

  • Kakao Map: data coverage is nearly on par. If a result looks off, cross-check by searching it again on Kakao Map.
  • Papago Lens: for when the problem is a sign or notice board rather than the map app itself — point the camera at it for real-time translation. See #1230 for app setup.
