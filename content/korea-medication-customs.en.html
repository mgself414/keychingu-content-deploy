Bringing Medication Into Korea — Can I Bring My Prescriptions?

Subtitle: A 3-category breakdown of what you can bring in — everyday OTC meds, general prescription drugs, and narcotic/psychotropic-controlled medication — plus the pre-approval process, paperwork, and customs declaration in practice

⚠️ This guide is not medical or legal advice. Import rules can change, so confirm the latest regulations with the MFDS (식약처) and Korea Customs Service (관세청) official channels before you travel. Talk to your prescribing doctor about anything medication-specific.

🎯 Why This Matters

"I'll just pack my meds and go" doesn't always hold up. Korea requires pre-departure approval from the MFDS for medication containing narcotic or psychotropic substances (마약류·향정신성) — things like some ADHD medications, sleep aids, and strong painkillers — and bringing them in without approval isn't just a fine: it can be prosecuted as a violation of the Narcotics Control Act (마약류관리법). Reddit threads show the same question on repeat, unanswered anywhere clearly — "traveling through 4 countries with immunosuppressants, do I need to declare them?" This guide gives you the 3-category checklist that answers it — final confirmation always goes through official channels.

★ At a Glance — 3 Categories

Category Examples What You Need Pre-Approval
① Everyday OTC Medication Painkillers, cold medicine, allergy meds Keep original packaging None (personal-use amount)
② General Prescription Drugs Blood pressure meds, diabetes meds, immunosuppressants English prescription or doctor's letter None — roughly a 3-month personal-use supply is the usual benchmark
③ Narcotic/Psychotropic-Controlled Medication Some ADHD meds (amphetamine-class), some sleep aids, opioid painkillers Pre-approval from the MFDS Required — apply 2–3 weeks before travel (review typically takes ~10 days)

Not sure if your medication falls under ③? — Check by active ingredient, not brand name; the same use case can fall into a different category depending on the compound. If you're not sure, ask the MFDS directly — "I didn't know" isn't a valid excuse.

📋 Category-by-Category Checklist

① Everyday OTC Medication — Usually No Issue

  • Personal-use quantities of OTC medication need no special procedure to bring in.
  • Keep the original packaging and label — moving pills into an unmarked container is what raises unnecessary suspicion.
  • Korean pharmacies also carry plenty of equivalents if you run out (see #1278, the pharmacy guide).

② General Prescription Drugs — Paperwork Is Your Insurance

  • Carry an English-language prescription or doctor's letter (listing the active ingredient, dosage, and reason) alongside your medication. You often won't be asked for it, but if you are, this document settles things on the spot.
  • Quantity should match personal use — roughly a 3-month supply is the usual benchmark. Anything well beyond your trip length can raise suspicion of resale intent.
  • Carry-on, not checked luggage — if it's lost, restarting the same prescription in Korea means starting over from a hospital visit (#1307).

③ Narcotic/Psychotropic-Controlled Medication — No Entry Without Pre-Approval

  • Apply online in advance for MFDS narcotic carry-in approval — attach your prescription and diagnosis documents, and apply 2–3 weeks before travel (review typically takes ~10 days).
  • Print the approval letter and carry it — present it at the customs declaration counter on arrival.
  • Bringing it in without approval: treated as a violation of the Narcotics Control Act — criminal penalties and deportation are both on the table. "It's a small amount" doesn't work as a defense here.

🛃 Entry Declaration in Practice

  • On the e-Arrival customs declaration (세관 신고) (#1289), list declarable items honestly — Category ③ items go through the declaration lane along with your approval letter.
  • Even with nothing to declare, keep your Category ② paperwork somewhere easy to grab — pull it out immediately if you're flagged for inspection.
  • Injectable or refrigerated medication (like insulin) is governed separately by airline carry-on rules and customs import rules — check both.

⚠ 3 Common Mistakes

1. "Same brand name, so it should be fine" — the same brand can use different active ingredients by country. Always check by active ingredient, never brand name.

2. "I'll just toss the pills in a ziplock bag" — unlabeled pills mean a longer conversation at inspection even for Category ① meds. Original packaging is the answer.

3. "I'll just buy it here instead" — buying the same medication in Korea often requires a local doctor's prescription, and the compound itself may not even be sold domestically. Pack your full trip supply plus a few extra days as a buffer.

⚙ 3 FAQs

1. What about vitamins and supplements? — Generally treated like Category ①, though bulk/wholesale quantities can draw customs scrutiny. Personal-use amounts are fine.

2. What about CBD products? — Korea is strict on cannabis-derived compounds. THC-containing products are barred entirely, and CBD isn't safe either — don't bring it.

3. What if I missed the approval window? — If your trip is impossible without a Category ③ medication, adjust your travel dates rather than risk bringing it in unapproved. The risk isn't worth gambling the whole trip on.
