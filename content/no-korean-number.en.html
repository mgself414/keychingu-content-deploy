No Korean Phone Number, the Zero-Panic Manual — What Blocks You and How to Go Around

Subtitle: Starting June 2026, Naver opened passport verification so you can reserve, order, and pay on the map without a number (pop-up store bookings included) · Catchtable Global, NOL World, and YES24 Global just need an email and an overseas card · A data-only eSIM has no number attached — if you need one, you have to buy a plan that includes voice · Places like Coupang and train ticketing are still clearly blocked

ISSUE 06 · #1394 · 2026-09-18 · korea-first-timer series

🎯 How to Read This Manual — Notation Conventions

The most common wall in Korea travel isn't language — it's "I don't have a Korean number to receive the verification code on." You get stuck on the last step of a booking screen, an app sign-up won't go through, a payment bounces. This piece writes down only one thing: where you get blocked and how to go around it.

  • A button on a screen goes in brackets: [ Verify with Passport ]
  • Screen titles and notices go in guillemets: 「Please enter your mobile phone number」
  • A hands-on action is bold: lay the passport flat and scan it
  • A sentence to show staff exactly as-is goes in code: 한국 번호가 없어요 (hanguk beonho-ga eopseoyo)

⚠ Notation Principle: Policies for individual services change often. This guide only states what was confirmed via an official announcement or official page as of September 2026; a service that couldn't be confirmed is left as "check on site." Declaring an unconfirmed service "works" or "doesn't work" as fact throws off your travel plan.

① The Big Picture — One Wall Came Down in 2026

For years, the wall for foreign travelers was mobile identity verification (PASS). The system required a number registered with a Korean carrier, so short-term visitors without a foreigner registration card got stuck at train ticketing and many bookings.

Then on June 4, 2026, Naver opened passport verification. A passport issued abroad is enough to complete identity verification, and it currently applies to reservations, orders, and payment on Naver Map. That means you can now book restaurants and pop-up stores on Naver Map without a Korean number.

That said, this doesn't unlock everything. An alternative to PASS itself (passport check, video-call verification) is being pushed forward and hasn't been fully rolled out yet. So it still comes down to each service.

One thing that matters for ticketing: even if you booked through the global version, your passport's English name and the name registered on the platform must match exactly when staff check ID at the venue. A single letter off is a problem.

③ Naver Passport Verification — What It Actually Does

This is the most useful route for travelers right now.

1. Try to make a reservation or order in the Naver Map app.

2. On the identity verification screen, choose passport verification.

3. Lay the passport flat and scan it. A passport issued abroad works.

4. Once verification is done, it moves you into the booking and payment. You can pay with an overseas card.

The scope is Naver Map's reservations, orders, and payment, and it's been announced that this will expand in stages. It doesn't apply to every Naver service.

Tip for foreign visitors — It helps to set up a Naver account ahead of time. That said, there's no official recommendation saying you must make one before you leave your country.

④ If You Want to Visit a Pop-Up Store

Popular Korean pop-up stores often take reservations through the Naver Reservation (Naver Map) channel. Dedicated platforms (POPPLY and others) mostly handle schedule listings and curation.

  • Once the passport verification in ③ opened up, this channel opened to foreign visitors too. You can try booking without a number.
  • This piece couldn't confirm the split between reserved slots and walk-in queues — it's safer to assume a popular pop-up has both a reservation list and a walk-in line and plan around that.
  • Pop-ups run for short periods and change often. How to find pop-up-heavy neighborhoods like Seongsu-dong is in #001.

Tip for foreign visitors — Merch often comes with a per-person quantity limit. If you're traveling with others, it's safer for each person to book separately.

⑤ If You Still Need a Number — Pick a Plan That Actually Issues One

This is where most people get confused. Buying an eSIM doesn't automatically mean you get a number.

Plan Type Number What You Can Use
Data-only eSIM/SIM No number (or receive-only) Internet, maps, messaging
Voice-included prepaid plan A Korean number is issued Everything above + SMS verification, calls

For example, KT's foreigner-only prepaid plans (the LTE Welcome series) run around 35,000 won or 55,000 won for 30 days and come with data plus 200 minutes of voice, 200 texts, and a Korean number. If you actually need verification, pick a plan like this that includes voice.

  • A short-term SIM's number can't be transferred — you get a newly issued number.

⚠ 3 Common Mistakes

1. Buying a data-only eSIM and expecting verification to work — with no number, text verification won't go through. If you need verification, get a voice-included plan.

2. Kept trying on the Korean-facing site — tickets have a separate global version (NOL World, Melon Global, YES24 Global). There, email is all it takes.

3. Registering under a different name than your passport — you'll get stuck at ID check at the venue. Enter your name exactly as printed in your passport.

⚙ 3 FAQs

1. Can I travel Korea without a number? — Getting around, maps, taxis, and payment mostly work fine. What gets blocked is some bookings and domestic-only apps.

2. How far does Naver's passport verification go? — As of September 2026, it covers reservations, orders, and payment on Naver Map. It's been announced that this will expand in stages.

3. Can I borrow a friend's Korean number? — It can work for things like joining a waitlist, but it's not recommended for a booking that involves payment or identity checks.
