Why a K-Drama Mania 1-Night Trip

K-drama filming-location pilgrimages rank among the top one or two travel motivations for foreign visitors. Since Netflix and Disney+ went global, the fan base for Goblin (도깨비), Parasite, Itaewon Class, Squid Game, and Winter Sonata has expanded rapidly among overseas millennials and Gen Z in their 20s to 40s. The strength of a 1-night itinerary is that it links downtown filming locations (same day) and out-of-town locations (overnight, then an early departure) into one continuous flow.

Seoul alone covers Parasite — Jahamun (자하문) and Cheongun-dong (청운동) — Itaewon Class (Itaewon), and Moonlight Drawn by Clouds (Gyeongbokgung Palace and Changdeokgung Palace). With the KTX, same-day trips reach as far as Jeongdongjin in Gangneung (Goblin) and Jeonju Hanok Village (Moonlight Drawn by Clouds). Setting a 1-night budget around KRW 400,000-500,000 covers lodging, transport, admission, and meals in full.

3 Common Mistakes Foreign Visitors Make

1. Mistaking a filming location for the exact space shown on screen

The Parasite stairs (the Cheongun-dong steps above the Jahamun Tunnel) are some distance from the semi-basement neighborhood shown in the film. Filming locations often capture only a specific exterior or alley shot, and the interior or surroundings can differ from what appears on screen. Watching the relevant scene on a streaming platform before visiting helps manage expectations.

2. Trying to combine Namiseom (남이섬) and Jeongdongjin (정동진) in a single day

Namiseom (Gapyeong) and Jeongdongjin (Gangneung) lie in different directions from Seoul. Combining both in one day eats up 6-7 hours in transit alone. For a 1-night itinerary, split them across two days (Namiseom on day one, Jeongdongjin by KTX on day two) or pick just one.

3. Visiting Gyeongbokgung Palace (경복궁) and Changdeokgung Palace (창덕궁) late in the afternoon

Both palaces close early (Gyeongbokgung at 18:00; Changdeokgung's rear garden tour requires advance reservation). Visitors often confuse hanbok rental return times (usually 17:30) with the palace closing time and end up unable to enter while still wearing hanbok. Arriving before 10:00 is recommended.

12 Picks Across 4 Axes — A Deep Dive into Real Filming Locations

Axis 1: Goblin and Mr. Sunshine (3 picks)

1. Incheon Yeongjongdo (영종도) — Goblin Filming Location (#743)

Location: Yeongjongdo area, Jung-gu, Incheon (get off at Yeongjong Station on the Airport Railroad)

Production: tvN's Goblin (2016-2017, starring Gong Yoo and Kim Go-eun). The coastal road and breakwater area of Yeongjongdo stood in for the Quebec, Canada scenes between the Goblin and Ji Eun-tak. Rather than filming in Canada, the production used Incheon's Yeongjongdo coastline, and the sunset-hour seascape matches the drama's atmosphere closely.

Access: About 30 minutes by airport limousine or taxi from Terminal 1 of Incheon International Airport. Get off at Yeongjong Station on the Airport Railroad and continue by taxi. Direct limousine buses also run from Hongdae and Sinchon in Seoul.

Admission: The public coastal area is free. Nearby Eulwangni and Wangsan beaches are also free.

Foreigner fit: Easy to pair with a first stop right after landing at Incheon Airport, or as a last stop the day before departure. English signage is limited, so switch Naver Map to English before heading out.

Best timing: The golden hour is about an hour before sunset (varies by season). Summer sunset runs 19:30-20:00; winter sunset runs 17:30-18:00.

Etiquette: Residential areas and the coastal tourist zone overlap here, so avoid loud noise and night-time photography with flash or lighting rigs.

2. Gangneung Jeongdongjin — Goblin and a World-Famous Sunrise Spot (#743)

Location: 17 Jeongdongjin-ro, Gangneung, Gangwon State (1-minute walk from Jeongdongjin Station)

Production: tvN's Goblin (2016-2017). Many of the beach scenes between the Goblin (Gong Yoo) and the Grim Reaper (Lee Dong-wook), along with Ji Eun-tak's scenes watching the sunrise alone, were filmed on Jeongdongjin Beach. Jeongdongjin Station has reportedly been listed in Guinness World Records as the train station closest to the sea (worth confirming), and it draws foreign visitors as an attraction in its own right.

Access: From Cheongnyangni Station in Seoul, take the KTX or ITX-Saemaeul to Jeongdongjin Station (about 2 hours to 2 hours 30 minutes). A Mugunghwa transfer is available at Gangneung Station. By rental car, take the Yeongdong Expressway to Gangneung IC.

Admission: The beach and the station plaza are free. The nearby Jeongdongjin Time Museum charges admission (check the adult rate in advance).

Foreigner fit: English information is available in part, with English signage inside the station. Arriving in time for sunrise is the key challenge, so staying overnight in Gangneung and heading out before dawn is the practical approach.

Best timing: Arrive 30 minutes before sunrise. Summer sunrise runs 05:00-05:30; winter sunrise runs 07:30-08:00 (check the exact time by season in real time).

Etiquette: Many visitors arrive in the early hours, so move quietly. Cooking and drinking on the sand are prohibited under Gangneung's beach ordinance.

3. Incheon Songdo (송도) — Mr. Sunshine and BTS's "DNA" (#743·#727·#732)

Location: Songdo-dong area, Yeonsu-gu, Incheon (Central Park Station, Incheon Subway Line 1)

Production: tvN's Mr. Sunshine (2018, starring Lee Byung-hun). The waterfront area of Songdo Central Park in Incheon's Songdo International City, along with the exteriors of several modern-style buildings, appears in the drama. BTS's "DNA" music video was also filmed around Songdo (cross-ref [#732]).

Access: From Hongik University Station on Seoul Subway Line 2, take the Airport Railroad, transfer at Gyeyang Station, and ride Incheon Subway Line 1 to Central Park Station (about 1 hour). Direct express buses from Yeouido or Gangnam in Seoul take about 1 hour 20 minutes.

Admission: Central Park is free. Tribowl (an outdoor performance venue and plaza) is free. Some themed experience facilities charge a fee.

Foreigner fit: This new town has relatively well-developed English signage. Cafes, convenience stores, and restaurants with English menus cluster nearby. The night view is also excellent, making it a spot where K-pop fans and drama fans overlap.

Best timing: Evening for the night view (19:00-22:00), or a morning walk. Weekend afternoons get crowded.

Etiquette: Check the dedicated bike lanes in the park. Some areas are off-limits to the water-boat zone.

Axis 2: Parasite and Itaewon (3 picks)

4. Jahamun and Cheongun-dong — Parasite's Academy Award Filming Location (#743)

Location: Cheongun-dong and Jahamun-ro area, Jongno-gu, Seoul (above the Jahamun Tunnel, toward Okin-dong)

Production: Bong Joon-ho's film Parasite (2019, winner of 4 Academy Awards). The scene of the Kim family (led by Song Kang-ho) descending the stairs on a rainy night, and the semi-basement alley, were filmed in the real alleys of Cheongun-dong and Okin-dong. Note that the semi-basement interior is a set, not an actual location.

Access: Gyeongbokgung Station, Subway Line 3, Exit 3, then a 15-20 minute walk (uphill). Bus routes 1020 and 7022 stop at Jahamun Tunnel. A taxi is recommended given the narrow alleys.

Admission: The alleys and stairs are free (public roads). This is a residential area, so entering private property is prohibited.

Foreigner fit: No English signage on site. Download the visitkorea Parasite filming-location course or a relevant blog map before visiting. It also pairs well with nearby Cheong Wa Dae, the former presidential residence open to the public since 2022.

Best timing: Visit during the day. At night, pass quietly through this residential area.

Etiquette: This is a real residential neighborhood. Do not photograph residents' windows or interiors without permission. No littering.

5. Itaewon Class Filming Locations (#710·#743)

Location: Itaewon-dong area, Yongsan-gu, Seoul (Itaewon Station, Subway Line 6)

Production: JTBC's Itaewon Class (2020, starring Park Seo-joon and Kim Da-mi). The lead character Park Sae-ro-yi's pub "Danbam" is set in Itaewon, with the World Food Street and Gyeongnidan-gil near Itaewon Station Exit 1 forming the main backdrop. The pub set itself was built at the broadcaster's studio, but several alleys around Itaewon were used for filming.

Access: Right at Itaewon Station Exit 1, Subway Line 6. Within 30 minutes of central Seoul.

Admission: The streets and alleys are free. Individual restaurants and bars charge separately.

Foreigner fit: English support here is top-tier. Itaewon has the highest concentration of foreign residents in Seoul, with nearly 100% of restaurants offering English menus. Card payment works everywhere without cash. It's also a hub for halal restaurants and vegetarian menus, covering a wide range of dietary needs.

Best timing: Evening into night (18:00-23:00) captures the atmosphere closest to the drama. Weekend nights get very crowded.

Etiquette: Itaewon is a major foreign-resident district in Seoul. Avoid loud noise after drinking and stay out of residential areas. Gyeongnidan-gil, toward Hangangjin Station, mixes housing with commercial space.

6. Gwangjang Market (광장시장) — Squid Game and Street Food (#743·#776)

Location: 88 Changgyeonggung-ro, Jongno-gu, Seoul (Subway Line 1 or 2, City Hall or Jonggak Station, or a 15-minute walk from Gwanghwamun Station on Line 5)

Production: The market is cited as a backdrop referenced in some scenes of Netflix's Squid Game (2021), and it has long featured in Korean food-vlog content and various K-dramas before that (worth confirming — the exact Squid Game filming location should be double-checked against official sources). Bindaetteok (mung-bean pancake), mayak gimbap (bite-sized seaweed rice rolls), and yukhoe bibimbap (raw beef with rice) are considered must-try foods for foreign visitors here.

Access: Jonggak Station Exit 11, Subway Line 1, then a 10-minute walk. Or Jongno 3(sam)-ga Station Exit 12, Subway Line 5, then a 5-minute walk.

Admission: The market itself is free. Food runs KRW 3,000-15,000 per serving.

Foreigner fit: English support is partial. Vendors are used to taking orders by pointing or photo. Naver Map has a strong base of English-language reviews, so deciding what to eat beforehand is recommended.

Best timing: 10:00-18:00 (varies by stall). Lunch (11:30-13:30) is busiest; 15:00-17:00 has shorter lines.

Etiquette: There's no obligation to buy after sampling, but sitting down to eat implies an order. Ask vendors before photographing them.

Axis 3: Historical Dramas, Moonlight Drawn by Clouds, and Winter Sonata (3 picks)

7. Gyeongbokgung and Changdeokgung Palaces — Moonlight Drawn by Clouds and The Moon Embracing the Sun (#379·#743)

Location:

  • Gyeongbokgung Palace: 161 Sajik-ro, Jongno-gu, Seoul (Gyeongbokgung Station, Subway Line 3, Exit 5)
  • Changdeokgung Palace: 99 Yulgok-ro, Jongno-gu, Seoul (Anguk Station, Subway Line 3, Exit 3, 5-minute walk)

Production: tvN's Moonlight Drawn by Clouds (2016, starring Park Bo-gum and Kim Yoo-jung) and MBC's The Moon Embracing the Sun (2012), among many other historical dramas. Gyeongbokgung's Geunjeongjeon Hall and Gyeonghoeru Pavilion, along with Changdeokgung's Injeongjeon Hall and its rear garden (Huwon), are recurring backdrops for period dramas.

Access: Both palaces connect directly to the subway. Gyeongbokgung to Changdeokgung is a 20-minute walk, or take a bus.

Admission:

  • Gyeongbokgung: KRW 3,000 for adults, same rate for foreign visitors. Closed Mondays.
  • Changdeokgung: KRW 3,000 for adults (KRW 5,000 including the rear garden). The rear garden requires advance reservation (through the Cultural Heritage Administration's site; slots often sell out). Closed Mondays.

Foreigner fit: English, Japanese, and Chinese audio guides can be rented (at Gyeongbokgung's main gate). Foreign-language docent tours are available through Cultural Heritage Administration reservations (free or low-cost — confirm in advance). For hanbok rental, use a shop near Gyeongbokgung's university district or Bukchon Hanok Village (around KRW 15,000-30,000 for 3 hours).

Best timing: Entering before 10:00 is most comfortable. If renting hanbok, plan to return it before 17:30. Night openings (seasonal, spring and fall) require a separate reservation.

Etiquette: Wearing hanbok waives the admission fee (confirm for the current period). Touching or climbing on cultural heritage structures is strictly prohibited.

8. Jeonju Hanok Village (전주 한옥마을) — Moonlight Drawn by Clouds and Period-Drama Backdrops (#743·#723)

Location: Around 99 Girin-daero, Wansan-gu, Jeonju, North Jeolla Province (Jeonju Hanok Village Information Center)

Production: Known as a filming location for tvN's Moonlight Drawn by Clouds (2016), Jeonju Hanok Village preserves some 700 traditional hanok homes, making it the largest hanok cluster in Korea, and it has served as a backdrop for numerous period dramas and other productions.

Access: From Seoul, KTX to Jeonju Station takes 1 hour 40 minutes to 2 hours. From Jeonju Station, a city bus or taxi takes 20 minutes. KTX service from Suseo Station (formerly the SRT, integrated as of September 1, 2026) is also available.

Admission: The village streets are free. Individual experiences (hanbok, hanji paper craft, traditional food) cost extra, generally KRW 5,000-30,000.

Foreigner fit: English support is available at the information center. English-menu restaurants are increasing. Jeonju bibimbap and kongnamul gukbap (bean-sprout soup) are must-try foods for foreign visitors. Numerous hanbok rental shops operate within the village.

Best timing: 09:00-14:00 is quietest. Weekend and holiday afternoons get very crowded. A day trip from Seoul by KTX is feasible within half a day.

Etiquette: Real residents still live in the hanok village. Avoid noise and drinking late at night or early in the morning. Entering hanok courtyards, which are private property, is prohibited.

9. Namiseom — Winter Sonata (#743·#739)

Location: 1 Namiseom-gil, Namsan-myeon, Chuncheon, Gangwon State (accessed by boat from Gapyeong Station)

Production: KBS2's Winter Sonata (2002, starring Bae Yong-joon and Choi Ji-woo). Namiseom's birch tree path, ginkgo tree road, and lawn plaza became the drama's iconic backdrop, making the island a first-generation Hallyu pilgrimage site for fans across Japan, China, and Southeast Asia. Foreign visitors still make up around 40% of visitors today (Korea Tourism Organization data — worth confirming).

Access: From Yongsan or Cheongnyangni Station in Seoul, take the ITX-Cheongchun to Gapyeong Station (about 1 hour to 1 hour 10 minutes), then take the Gapyeong ferry terminal boat to Naminara Republic (Namiseom), about a 5-minute crossing. Alternatively, take a bus from Sangbong or Dong-Seoul Terminal in Seoul (about 1 hour 30 minutes) to Gapyeong Terminal, then a taxi to the ferry terminal.

Admission: KRW 13,000 for adults (includes the ferry and Naminara admission — check the current rate in real time).

Foreigner fit: English, Japanese, and Chinese signage covers the entire island. English-menu restaurants and English-language shuttle announcements are available. Visitors from more than 30 countries come each year, and staff are well-versed in multilingual service.

Best timing: Visit after the first ferry at 09:00. Fall foliage season (mid-October to early November) and a snowy winter (December-February) come closest to the Winter Sonata mood. Cherry blossom season (early April) is also popular.

Etiquette: Bicycles are available to rent for getting around the island. Pets are not allowed in some areas. The island follows a carry-out-your-trash policy.

Axis 4: Foreign Visitor Infrastructure and Seasons (3 picks)

10. Multilingual Information Infrastructure — KOCCA, KTO, visitkorea

The official support system that lets foreign visitors explore K-drama filming locations independently.

  • visitkorea (English): https://english.visitkorea.or.kr — regional filming-location course recommendations, downloadable English map PDFs
  • Seoul Tourism Organization (English): https://english.visitseoul.net — Seoul filming-location courses for Parasite, Itaewon, and Goblin
  • KOCCA Hallyu Tourism Information: provides a database of K-drama filming locations (worth confirming)
  • KTO 1330 Tourist Interpretation: 24-hour phone interpretation in English, Japanese, Chinese, and Russian

If a foreign visitor gets lost or needs information on the ground, calling 1330 connects them to real-time interpretation and guidance. It's free.

11. Foreign Visitor K-Drama 1-Night Passes — Klook, KKday

Klook and KKday sell K-drama filming-location tour packages. Prices range roughly from KRW 100,000 to KRW 500,000 depending on the itinerary (check current rates in real time).

Common package types:

  • Half-day tour: Parasite filming locations + Itaewon (about 4-5 hours, KRW 100,000-150,000 per person)
  • Full-day tour: Gyeongbokgung + Bukchon + Gwangjang Market (about 8 hours, KRW 150,000-200,000 per person)
  • 1-night full set: Seoul filming locations + Namiseom or Jeonju (2 days, KRW 350,000-500,000 per person)

Booking tip: Even visitors who don't speak Korean can book, pay, and enter with a QR code entirely in English through the Klook app. For packages that include a guide, confirm the English-guide option.

Practical Information

Transportation

  • Within Seoul: The subway covers everything. Use a T-money card (sold at convenience stores, no passport needed for foreign visitors) or a transit-card app. Base fare starts at KRW 1,550. English guidance is available on every line.
  • Seoul to Jeongdongjin: ITX-Saemaeul from Cheongnyangni Station, or KTX to Gangneung with a transfer. A round trip runs roughly KRW 80,000-120,000.
  • Seoul to Namiseom: ITX-Cheongchun to Gapyeong Station, one-way from KRW 6,500. The boat fare is separate.
  • Seoul to Jeonju: A KTX round trip runs roughly KRW 80,000-110,000.
  • Useful apps: Naver Map (English transit directions), KakaoT (taxi-hailing app), and the Korail app (train reservations).

Tours

  • Independent travel: Download a course PDF from visitseoul.net, switch Naver Map to English, and go.
  • Guided tours: Search Klook and KKday for half-day to 1-night packages with English-speaking guides. Advance booking is recommended, as peak-season slots sell out.
  • Private tours: Airbnb Experiences and Viator can connect you with local guides who specialize in K-drama tours.

Language

  • On the ground: Major tourist sites such as Itaewon, Gyeongbokgung, and Namiseom have English-speaking staff or complete signage.
  • Emergency: 1330 (tourist interpretation, 24 hours, Korean, English, Japanese, Chinese, Russian, Spanish, and more)
  • Translation apps: Papago (Korean to English, Japanese, and Chinese, with offline mode)

FAQ 3-5

Q1. If a first-time enthusiast only has one day, where should they start?

Itaewon (#5). English support is complete, the drama's backdrop alleys are attractions in themselves, and both food and shopping are covered. Arriving at 2 p.m. and finishing the evening at Gwangjang Market (#6) is the most efficient same-day combination.

Q2. Where can I find a multilingual guide?

The visitkorea English site offers downloadable regional audio-guide PDFs. Gyeongbokgung and Changdeokgung both rent on-site audio guides. Calling 1330 gets you real-time interpretation.

Q3. How much budget should I prepare?

Minimum same-day course: KRW 100,000 (transport, admission, and meals). A 1-night couple's full set: KRW 300,000-500,000. Add KRW 80,000-120,000 in transport if you extend to Namiseom or Jeongdongjin outside the city.

Q4. Can K-pop fans enjoy this trip too?

Yes — it pairs well with Songdo, the filming site of BTS's "DNA" (#3), and with public tapings of Music Bank and Inkigayo (KBS, MBC, SBS; separate applications required). Cross-ref [#387·#732].

Q5. What's the recommended watch order on Netflix before the trip?

1) Goblin (Yeongjongdo, Jeongdongjin) 2) Parasite (Cheongun-dong) 3) Itaewon Class (Itaewon) 4) Winter Sonata (Namiseom) 5) Moonlight Drawn by Clouds (Gyeongbokgung, Jeonju). Check real-time availability by streaming service.
