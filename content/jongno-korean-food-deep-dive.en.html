Foreigner Jongno Korean food deep dive

> 600 years of Joseon’s capital city — Insa-dong Korean food, Gwangjang Market soup, Euljiro long-established restaurant, royal Korean food 15 places Matrix

Jongno was the capital of Hanyang during the Joseon Dynasty for 600 years. Mecca of Korean food in the area between the three major palaces of Gyeongbokgung Palace, Changdeokgung Palace, and Changgyeonggung Palace. Gwangjang Market (Korea's oldest market, 1905), Insa-dong (Korean food cluster), Euljiro (old restaurant cluster), and royal Korean food near Changdeokgung Palace — the #1 Korean food deep dive for foreign segment·foodie·history buff personas. This guide includes 15 Korean restaurants in 4 regions + 1-day route matrix + Korean food etiquette.

1. Korean food scene in Jongno (foreigner fit fact)

600 years of Korean food history

  • 1392 Joseon Hanyang capital relocation → Jongno = center of the capital city
  • Royal Korean food, noble Korean food, and common Korean food are all clustered in Jongno.
  • Gwangjang Market opened in 1905 = Korea’s oldest permanent market
  • Insa-dong = noble residence in the late Joseon Dynasty → Korean food cluster
  • Euljiro = Long-established store cluster during Japanese colonial period and after liberation

4 types of Korean food (all available in Jongno)

  • Royal Korean food: Sinseonro, Gujeolpan, Japchae, Galbijjim (Royal Dynasty of the Joseon Dynasty)
  • Banga Korean Cuisine: 12-20 Korean dishes (good price)
  • Common people’s Korean food: Gukbap, noodles, stew, bibimbap (market)
  • Contemporary Korean food: Michelin store (modern reinterpretation)

7. Foreign fit tips

English language availability

  • Top: Korean House, Onzium, Tosokchon, Wooraeok, Bakgane Bindaetteok (English menu, foreigners’ favorite)
  • Middle class: Insa-dong Korean cuisine (partial menu in English)
  • Local: Gwangjang Market stall, Euljiro restaurant (picture menu, translation app)

Budget (per person per day)

  • Pattern A Korean deep: ₩80,000~150,000 (hop at various stores)
  • Pattern B Royal Court·Korean Settlement: ₩200,000~400,000 (Korea House + Onzium)
  • Pattern C market/long-established store: ₩50,000~100,000

By persona

  • American Persona: Pattern A (available for 1 day)
  • European Persona: Pattern B (court·art·history)
  • foodie/local: Pattern C (market/long-established store deep)
  • anniversary·special: Onzium + Korea House
  • K-drama fan: Gwangjang Market·Insa-dong (multiple filming locations)

By season

  • Spring (April-May): Insadong/Gwangjang Market + Palace Walk
  • Summer (June-August): Tosokchon Samgyetang, Pyongyang Naengmyeon, and Kongguksu
  • Fall (October-November): Hanjeongsik (fall ingredients, bunches, shad)
  • Winter (December-February): Gukbap, gomtang, and hot pot (Euljiro No-po)

8. Korean food etiquette (information on fit for foreigners)

Do

  • Adults and seniors start eating first
  • Receiving and giving drinks with both hands
  • Hold the rice bowl
  • Use chopsticks + spoon simultaneously (spoon for rice and soup)
  • Side dishes are OK to share and you can eat them all (free refills for most)
  • Hanjeongsik: Meaning of the number of concubines (12 concubines = noblemen, 5 concubines = commoners)

Don't

  • Blow your nose
  • Putting chopsticks on rice
  • Leaving food behind is a virtue
  • One hand when pouring alcohol (two hands recommended)

Payment·tips

  • In Korea, there is no tip culture (pay 100% of the bill)
  • It is common for one store to pay (X for each person)
  • Card + cash is OK. Market recommends cash.
