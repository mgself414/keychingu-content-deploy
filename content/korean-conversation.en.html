Situational Korean Phrasebook for Travel — Short Sentences to Say on the Spot, from Airport, Subway, Taxi and Restaurant to Pharmacy and Emergency

Subtitle: Airport, subway and bus, taxi, restaurant, cafe, convenience store, shopping, paying, asking directions, accommodation, pharmacy, emergency. For each of the 12 situations where you need Korean while traveling, we wrote short sentences people really use, in Hangul, romanized sound and English meaning. We also added what the other person is likely to ask back, so you can understand the reply and keep the conversation going.

ISSUE 03 · #537 · 2026-11-25

🎯 Who This Is For

This piece is for people who have no time to study Korean but want to say a few words themselves while traveling. It does not cover grammar or study methods. It only collects sentences to pull out and read on the spot. If you want to start studying Korean, see #432 Korean Guide and #403 Korean Language Academy.

How to read it:

  • The romanization follows the sound. It matches what you actually hear, not the official romanization rules. For example, '몇 번' is written myeot ppeon and '찾는' is written channeun. No hyphens are used, and spaces appear only where the Hangul has a space.
  • Every sentence is polite speech. Most end in 'yo (-요)', while 'gamsahamnida (감사합니다)' and 'joesonghamnida (죄송합니다)' end in 'mnida (-ㅂ니다)', a more formal ending. They sound natural even with someone you have just met.
  • If it is hard to say aloud, show the screen. Pull this page up on your phone and point to the sentence with your finger. The meaning gets across.
  • 'What the other person says' lists replies that come up often in each situation. If you hear them in advance, you will not be caught off guard when asked a question.

🧰 8 Basic Sentences for Anywhere

Say it Meaning
annyeonghaseyo (안녕하세요.) Hello.
gamsahamnida (감사합니다.) Thank you.
joesonghamnida / jeogiyo (죄송합니다. / 저기요.) Sorry. / Excuse me (to get attention).
igeo juseyo (이거 주세요.) This one, please.
gwaenchanayo (괜찮아요.) It's okay. / No, thank you.
jamsimanyo (잠시만요.) Just a moment. / Excuse me (passing by).
hangugeo jal motaeyo (한국어 잘 못해요.) I don't speak Korean well.
cheoncheonhi malhae juseyo (천천히 말해 주세요.) Please speak slowly.

'gwaenchanayo (괜찮아요)' is widely useful. When asked whether you need a bag or a receipt, a slight wave of the hand with "gwaenchanayo (괜찮아요)" is taken to mean "I don't need it."

① Airport

Say it Meaning
jim channeun gosi eodiyeyo (짐 찾는 곳이 어디예요?) Where is baggage claim?
gonghangcheoltto eodiseo tayo (공항철도 어디서 타요?) Where do I take the Airport Railroad?
gyotongkadeu eodiseo sayo (교통카드 어디서 사요?) Where can I buy a transit card?
yusim eodiseo sayo (유심 어디서 사요?) Where can I buy a SIM card?
i beoseu myeongdong gayo (이 버스 명동 가요?) Does this bus go to Myeongdong?

What the other person says

  • jeojjogeuro gaseyo (저쪽으로 가세요.) — Go that way.
  • jiha ilcheungieyo (지하 1층이에요.) — It's on the basement level 1 (B1).

② Subway·Bus

Say it Meaning
igeo ihoseon majayo (이거 2호선 맞아요?) Is this Line 2?
sicheongnyeok garyeomyeon eodiseo garatayo (시청역 가려면 어디서 갈아타요?) Where do I transfer to get to City Hall Station?
myeot ppeon chulguyeyo (몇 번 출구예요?) Which exit number?
yeogiseo naeryeoyo (여기서 내려요?) Do I get off here?
kadeuga an jjikyeoyo (카드가 안 찍혀요.) My card won't tap.

What the other person says

  • daeum nyeogeseo naeriseyo (다음 역에서 내리세요.) — Get off at the next station.
  • bandaepyeoneseo taseyo (반대편에서 타세요.) — Take it from the other side (opposite platform).

On a bus you must tap your card on the reader both when boarding and when getting off. If you do not tap when getting off, the transfer discount on the next vehicle is lost and the unpaid base fare may be charged (Seoul Transportation website, checked 2026-11-25). In Seoul, you get the transfer discount if you board another vehicle within 30 minutes after getting off. If the time you board the next vehicle falls between 21:00 and 7:00 the next morning, the limit is 60 minutes (same source). For buying and topping up a card, see #238 T-money Guide; for the order of steps on your first ride, see #1281 First Time on the Subway.

③ Taxi

Say it Meaning
yeogiro ga juseyo (여기로 가 주세요.) Please take me here. (show the address)
eolmana geollyeoyo (얼마나 걸려요?) How long does it take?
yeogiseo sewo juseyo (여기서 세워 주세요.) Please stop here.
kadeu dwaeyo (카드 돼요?) Can I pay by card?
teureongkeu jom yeoreo juseyo (트렁크 좀 열어 주세요.) Please open the trunk.

What the other person says

  • juso isseoyo (주소 있어요?) — Do you have the address?
  • anjeonbelteu mae juseyo (안전벨트 매 주세요.) — Please fasten your seatbelt.

It is more accurate to show a map app screen or a Hangul address than to say the destination aloud. For how to call one by app, see #1273 Kakao T Taxi.

④ Ordering at a Restaurant

Say it Meaning
du myeongieyo (두 명이에요.) Table for two.
yeongeo menyu isseoyo (영어 메뉴 있어요?) Do you have an English menu?
igeo hana juseyo (이거 하나 주세요.) One of this, please.
an maepkke hae juseyo (안 맵게 해 주세요.) Not spicy, please.
yeogiyo (여기요!) Excuse me! (calling staff)
mureun selpeuyeyo (물은 셀프예요?) Is water self-service?
pojangdwaeyo (포장돼요?) Can I get it to go?

What the other person says

  • myeot ppuniseyo (몇 분이세요?) — How many people?
  • jumuneun kioseukeuro hae juseyo (주문은 키오스크로 해 주세요.) — Please order at the kiosk.
  • iinbunbuteo dwaeyo (2인분부터 돼요.) — The minimum order is two servings.

Some grilled-meat and stew restaurants take orders only from 2 servings ('iinbunbuteo (2인분부터)'). If you are alone, ask before ordering: "irinbun dwaeyo (1인분 돼요?) (Can I order one serving?)". For how to read the kiosk screen, see #1270; for non-spicy dishes, see #1398.

⑤ Cafe

Say it Meaning
aiseu amerikano han jan juseyo (아이스 아메리카노 한 잔 주세요.) One iced Americano, please.
yeogiseo meogeulkkeyo (여기서 먹을게요.) For here.
gajyeogalkkeyo (가져갈게요.) To go.
deol dalge hae juseyo (덜 달게 해 주세요.) Less sweet, please.
waipai bimilbeonho mwoyeyo (와이파이 비밀번호 뭐예요?) What's the Wi-Fi password?

What the other person says

  • deusigo gaseyo (드시고 가세요?) — For here?
  • jindongbel ullimyeon oseyo (진동벨 울리면 오세요.) — Come up when the buzzer goes off.

'deusigo gaseyo (드시고 가세요?)' asks whether you will eat here. If you will drink in the shop, answer "ne (네)" (yes); if you are taking it out, answer "gajyeogalkkeyo (가져갈게요)". For what to do after the buzzer goes off, see #1288.

⑥ Convenience Store

Say it Meaning
bongtu juseyo (봉투 주세요.) A bag, please.
jeotkkarak juseyo (젓가락 주세요.) Chopsticks, please.
jeonjareinji eodi isseoyo (전자레인지 어디 있어요?) Where is the microwave?
gyotongkadeu chungjeonhae juseyo (교통카드 충전해 주세요.) Please top up my transit card.
man won chungjeonhae juseyo (만 원 충전해 주세요.) Please add 10,000 won.

What the other person says

  • bongtu piryohaseyo (봉투 필요하세요?) — Do you need a bag?
  • yeongsujeung deurilkkayo (영수증 드릴까요?) — Would you like a receipt?

⑦ Shopping

Say it Meaning
geunyang boneun geoyeyo (그냥 보는 거예요.) I'm just looking.
dareun saek isseoyo (다른 색 있어요?) Do you have another color?
deo keun saijeu isseoyo (더 큰 사이즈 있어요?) Do you have a bigger size?
ibeo bwado dwaeyo (입어 봐도 돼요?) Can I try it on?
taekseu ripeon dwaeyo (택스 리펀 돼요?) Can I get a tax refund?

What the other person says

  • yeokkwon isseuseyo (여권 있으세요?) — Do you have your passport?
  • pitingnumeun jeojjogieyo (피팅룸은 저쪽이에요.) — The fitting room is over there.

Which refund method suits your shopping is laid out in #1231 Tax Refund Guide.

⑧ Paying

Say it Meaning
eolmayeyo (얼마예요?) How much is it?
gyesaneun eodiseo haeyo (계산은 어디서 해요?) Where do I pay?
kadeuro halkkeyo (카드로 할게요.) I'll pay by card.
ttaro gyesanhalkkeyo (따로 계산할게요.) We'll pay separately.
yeongsujeung juseyo (영수증 주세요.) A receipt, please.

What the other person says

  • ilssibullo hae deurilkkayo (일시불로 해 드릴까요?) — Pay in full (no installments)?
  • kaunteoeseo gyesanhaseyo (카운터에서 계산하세요.) — Please pay at the counter.

When you hand over a card, some shops ask whether it is 'ilssibul (일시불)'. It means paying once in full with no installments, so you can answer "ne (네)" (yes). For where to pay at a restaurant, see #1275 Tipping·Paying Manners.

⑨ Asking Directions

Say it Meaning
hwajangsil eodiyeyo (화장실 어디예요?) Where is the restroom?
gyeongbokkung eotteoke gayo (경복궁 어떻게 가요?) How do I get to Gyeongbokgung?
yeogiseo meoreoyo (여기서 멀어요?) Is it far from here?
georeogal ssu isseoyo (걸어갈 수 있어요?) Can I walk there?
jido jom bwa juseyo (지도 좀 봐 주세요.) Could you look at this map? (show your screen)

What the other person says

  • jjuk gaseo oenjjogiyo (쭉 가서 왼쪽이요.) — Go straight, then left. (오른쪽 oreunjjok = right)
  • gil geonneopyeonieyo (길 건너편이에요.) — It's across the street.

In place of '경복궁', put the name of the place you want to go. For how to read exit numbers in a map app, see #1295.

⑩ Accommodation

Say it Meaning
chekeuinharyeogoyo (체크인하려고요.) I'd like to check in.
jim matkkyeodo dwaeyo (짐 맡겨도 돼요?) Can I leave my luggage here?
sugeon deo juseyo (수건 더 주세요.) More towels, please.
bangi neomu chuwoyo (방이 너무 추워요.) The room is too cold.
waipaiga an dwaeyo (와이파이가 안 돼요.) The Wi-Fi isn't working.

What the other person says

  • yeokkwon boyeo jusigesseoyo (여권 보여 주시겠어요?) — May I see your passport?
  • boilleoneun i beoteunieyo (보일러는 이 버튼이에요.) — This button controls the heating (boiler).

For the floor heating control panel in Korean accommodation and how to throw out trash, see #1380 First Night in Your Accommodation.

⑪ Pharmacy

Say it Meaning
meoriga apayo (머리가 아파요.) I have a headache.
baega apayo (배가 아파요.) I have a stomachache.
yeori nayo (열이 나요.) I have a fever.
gamgiyak isseoyo (감기약 있어요?) Do you have cold medicine?
allereugi isseoyo (알레르기 있어요.) I have an allergy.
harue myeot ppeon meogeoyo (하루에 몇 번 먹어요?) How many times a day do I take it?

What the other person says

  • cheobangjeon isseoyo (처방전 있어요?) — Do you have a prescription?
  • sikue deuseyo (식후에 드세요.) — Take it after meals.

For more sentences by symptom, see #1278 Pharmacy.

⑫ Emergency

Say it Meaning
dowajuseyo (도와주세요!) Help!
gyeongchal bulleo juseyo (경찰 불러 주세요.) Please call the police.
gugeupcha bulleo juseyo (구급차 불러 주세요.) Please call an ambulance.
yeokkwoneul ireobeoryeosseoyo (여권을 잃어버렸어요.) I lost my passport.
gireul ireosseoyo (길을 잃었어요.) I'm lost.
yeongeo haneun saram isseoyo (영어 하는 사람 있어요?) Does anyone speak English?

What the other person says

  • jusoga eodiyeyo (주소가 어디예요?) — What's the address?
  • eodiga apeuseyo (어디가 아프세요?) — Where does it hurt?

Three Numbers

Number When to use Source
112 Police. Crimes such as theft and assault Seoul Tourism Organization (서울관광재단) VisitSeoul safety guide
119 Fire and ambulance. Accidents, ambulance Seoul Tourism Organization (서울관광재단) VisitSeoul safety guide

Besides phone calls, 1330 connects through the messengers KakaoTalk, Facebook, LINE and WhatsApp, and through web chat (Korea Tourism Organization, checked 2026-11-25). 1330 operates 24 hours all year. The Yeongdeungpo-gu (영등포구) guide says Russian, Vietnamese, Thai, Malay and Indonesian are connected only 08:00~19:00. Hours by language can change, so check before calling (checked 2026-11-25). If it is hard to explain the situation by voice, you can report to 119 by text message (SMS·MMS). Attaching a photo or video that shows the address speeds up the connection to the dispatch center (Policy Briefing 2024-06-05). For what each number is used for, see #433 Emergency·Safety Guide and #1299 1330.

⚠ 3 Common Mistakes

  1. Ending with 'jwo (줘)' — The "igeo jwo (이거 줘)" you often hear in dramas is casual speech between friends. In a shop, add 'seyo (-세요)' at the end: "igeo juseyo (이거 주세요)".
  2. Not tapping your card when getting off the bus — If you skip the tap when getting off, the transfer discount on your next ride is lost and you may pay an extra base fare (Seoul Transportation website).
  3. Reporting a crime to 1330 — 1330 is a travel information and interpretation desk. Call 112 for theft and assault, and 119 for accidents and an ambulance (VisitSeoul). If you need interpretation, ask 1330 for help separately from the report.

⚙ FAQ 3

  1. Will they understand me if my pronunciation is off? — The shorter the sentence, the more a few words are enough to get the meaning across. If the sounds do not come out well, show the Hangul sentence on this page on your screen.
  2. Isn't a translation app enough? — Use a translation app for long conversations; for short phrases such as greetings, ordering and paying, saying them yourself is faster. For choosing a translation app, see #432; for ordering manners when using a translation app, see #1339.
  3. Can I speak English in an emergency? — We could not confirm from official sources this time whether you can talk to 119 in English. If you cannot make yourself understood, report to 119 by text message (SMS·MMS) and attach a photo or video that shows the address (Policy Briefing 2024-06-05). 1330 is not an emergency number but a desk that gives travel information and interpretation in several languages (Korea Tourism Organization). Make emergency reports to 112 or 119, and use 1330 separately when you need interpretation help. Shouting "dowajuseyo (도와주세요!)" to people around you is also an option.
