Snow on the Palace — 8 Snowy Spots in the City + Winter Ice Rinks, and How to Wait for the Forecast

Subtitle: You don't need to go all the way to Gangwon-do (강원도) — a snow-viewing window that opens only on the morning it snows in Seoul (Gyeongbokgung, Changdeokgung, Bukchon, Namsan), plus winter activities that are a sure thing even without snow (Seoul Plaza, Lotte World Ice Rink). How to read a snow forecast + the best timing for snow photos + a pre-trip checklist. For a Gangwon-do snow trip, see #747; for night views, see #224.

🎯 Snow in Seoul Isn't Something You "Plan" — It's Something You "Wait For"

A day when snow actually settles on the ground in Seoul is rare enough to count on one hand each winter. Even when it does fall, snow removal crews clear the city center fast, so it's often gone by afternoon. That's why chasing snow scenery isn't something you pencil into an itinerary — it only works if you watch the forecast and move the moment it snows that morning.

💡 The golden window is right after the snow stops through mid-morning. Past noon, Seoul's snow either melts or gets cleared away. If you see a forecast for "snow tomorrow," clearing your schedule for the next day is the core strategy of this guide.

① 8 Snowy Spots in the City

Palaces — The Peak of Snow Scenery

1. Gyeongbokgung (경복궁) — Snow settling on the tiled roof of Geunjeongjeon (근정전) with a whitened Bugaksan (북악산) rising behind it is the signature image of snowy Seoul. Right after the winter opening time of 09:00 (9 AM) is the only window to catch untouched, footprint-free snow. Free entry if you're wearing a rented hanbok (#37).

2. Changdeokgung (창덕궁) — With fewer crowds than Gyeongbokgung, the quiet of a snow-covered garden is still intact here. The Huwon (후원, Secret Garden) requires a separate reservation (#1316).

3. Deoksugung (덕수궁) — The combination of Seokjojeon (석조전, a Western-style building) and snow makes for a completely different picture than the other palaces. With the best access via City Hall Station (시청역), it's the palace you can reach fastest once the snow news breaks.

4. Jongmyo (종묘) — When snow blankets the wide Woldae (월대, stone terrace) in front of the Jeongjeon (정전, main hall), it's minimalism itself. A UNESCO World Heritage Site.

Villages and Hills

5. Bukchon Hanok Village (북촌 한옥마을) — Alleyways where snow settles along the ridgelines of tiled roofs. ⚠ This is a residential area — keep quiet and be mindful of early-morning noise (#38).

6. Namsan and N Seoul Tower (남산) — Ridge walking trails plus a city view, best the day after it snows. Reachable by cable car or the circular bus (#40).

7. Inwangsan (인왕산) Fortress Wall Trail — Snow-covered fortress walls and a city view. ⚠ Ice underfoot — don't attempt this without crampons (아이젠, spiked traction cleats you strap onto your shoes) (#148).

8. Seoul Forest (서울숲) and Hangang Park (한강공원) — Flat, easy-walking snow scenery. A good alternative if you're traveling with kids or want something low on physical effort (#740).

② How to Read the Snow Forecast — When to Move

  • The evening before (D-1): Check the weather app for the "snow" icon plus the expected snow depth (cm of accumulation). Under 1cm often means it won't settle in the city center at all.
  • That day, dawn to morning: Check outside your window — if it's settled, leave immediately. Timing your arrival for the palace's winter opening time (09:00) is ideal.
  • The 2–3 hours right after the snow stops is the peak window for photos. After that, footprints from crowds and snow-removal work break up the surface.
  • There's a backup plan if it doesn't pan out: if it doesn't snow, pivot straight to Part ③, the ice rinks. That's why this guide covers both.

③ Ice Rinks — A Sure Thing for Winter Even Without Snow

Rink Type Details
Seoul Plaza Ice Rink (서울광장 스케이트장) Outdoor, dead center of the city Open only during the winter season (typically December–February); a public facility with very low entry and rental fees. Best access of all, right in front of City Hall — Deoksugung (덕수궁) and the Seoul Metropolitan Library form the backdrop
Lotte World Ice Rink (롯데월드 아이스링크) Indoor, open year-round An indoor rink that runs through all four seasons. Easy to pair with a Jamsil day (Lotte World Tower)
Hotel and department store seasonal rinks Pop-up Small rinks that open for the year-end season only — locations change every year, so check that year's announcement
  • The Seoul Plaza rink customarily runs on a session-based system (entrants are grouped into timed slots and rotated through). Weekend afternoons mean long waits — weekday mornings or early afternoons are better.
  • Gear (skates, helmets) is normally rented on-site, so you can show up empty-handed. Gloves are required (mandatory at most rinks).
  • Operating dates, fees, and booking methods change year to year — check the official announcements from the Seoul city government and Lotte World.

④ Practical Tips for Snow Photos and Safety

  • Ice is the real danger: the stone floors and hill paths inside the palaces get slippery once snow packs down. Deep-tread shoes are a must, and skip Inwangsan (인왕산) and Bugaksan (북악산) entirely if you don't have crampons (아이젠).
  • Camera batteries drain fast in the cold — keep a spare in a pocket close to your body heat.
  • Lens fogging: shooting right after moving from indoors to outdoors will come out hazy. Leave the camera zipped in your bag for 5 minutes to adjust before pulling it out.
  • Hanbok plus snow is a powerful combination, but you'll be exposed to the cold — check whether you're allowed to wear a coat over the rental before you book.
  • On a snowy day at the palace, a hat and hood beat an umbrella (and they don't get in the way of your photos either).
