Getting on Korea's Public Wi-Fi Without the Guesswork — Pick the One With "Secure" in the Name

Subtitle: Seoul public Wi-Fi, fully mapped — telling SSID SEOUL apart from SEOUL_Secure · the exact connection screen flow · city bus Wi-Fi · 3 security rules · cafe password culture · pairing it with eSIM data

① Where Seoul's Public Wi-Fi Actually Works

Seoul has free public Wi-Fi installed across major streets like Gwanghwamun, Myeongdong, and Hongdae, parks including Han River Park, traditional markets like Gwangjang Market, welfare facilities such as community centers and libraries, and even inside Seoul city buses. In other words, you don't need to worry much about "losing internet the moment I step outside my accommodation" — at least within Seoul — as long as you know the exact SSID.

② Two SSIDs — SEOUL vs. SEOUL_Secure, No Need to Overthink It

Open your Wi-Fi list and two similar-looking names show up. A lot of people get stuck here. There's one rule: pick the one with "Secure" in the name.

SSID Type Connecting Notes
SEOUL Open (no password) Requires passing a login page every time you connect Annoying — you have to click through the terms-agreement/login screen again each time
SEOUL_Secure Secured (WPA2) ID seoul · password seoul — enter once Set it up once and it auto-reconnects from then on. The option this piece recommends

SEOUL tends to catch your eye first since the name is more visible, but it makes you pass through the login screen again every single time — a hassle. Lock in SEOUL_Secure once, and it connects automatically anywhere in Seoul without asking again. For a whole trip's worth of convenience, the latter wins.

③ Connection Master Flow — Follow the Screens Exactly

Do this once and you're done for the rest of the trip. Just tap through in this order.

Step What's on screen What to do
1 Your phone's Wi-Fi Settings list Open the list of nearby Wi-Fi networks
2 Both SEOUL and SEOUL_Secure show up in the list Tap SEOUL_Secure (not SEOUL)
3 「Username」 field Enter seoul
4 「Password」 field Enter seoul (same as the ID)
5 The large button at the bottom Tap [ Connect ] or [ Join ]
6 A checkmark appears on the Wi-Fi icon Connected — from here on, any other SEOUL_Secure hotspot around Seoul reconnects automatically

Once you've set this up, you won't need to look at this table again. That's the whole point of picking SEOUL_Secure.

④ City Bus Wi-Fi Is Separate — an Easy Point to Miss

Free Wi-Fi is also installed inside Seoul city buses, but it's a separate connection procedure from SEOUL_Secure above. You'll need to find the SSID printed on the sticker near the ceiling or window and connect to it from your list. Since boarding the bus itself (tagging your card, transferring, ringing the stop bell) is a bigger hurdle than the Wi-Fi, both the bus-riding procedure and the in-bus Wi-Fi connection are covered screen-by-screen in #1271 How to ride city buses. This piece stays focused on "public Wi-Fi on the street and in parks."

⑤ 3 Security Rules — What Not to Do on Public Wi-Fi

Public Wi-Fi is, by definition, an open network shared by whoever happens to be nearby. Convenient, but stick to these three rules.

1. Don't log into banking apps or check account balances on public Wi-Fi — do that on data (eSIM/roaming) instead.

2. Don't open forms asking for personal info like your passport number or card number on public networks.

3. Same goes for online shopping payments — save those for your accommodation's Wi-Fi or mobile data.

Navigation, translation, checking social media — none of that needs to worry you. Just switch to data the moment money or personal info is involved.

⑥ Cafe Wi-Fi Password Culture — Check the Receipt and the Wall

Large chain cafes like Starbucks often connect automatically with no separate login. Independent cafes and bakeries, on the other hand, usually require a password that's printed in small text at the bottom of your receipt, or posted on paper near the register or on the menu board. If you can't find it, just ask the staff, "와이파이 비밀번호 있어요? Wi-Fi password?" — they'll tell you right away. It's not an awkward question to ask.

⑧ 3 Panic Scenarios

1. Connected, but no internet — this happens when you've joined SEOUL (open) and the login page didn't pop up automatically. Open any page in your browser and the login screen should appear. If it still doesn't work, switch to SEOUL_Secure instead.

2. Only SEOUL shows up, no SEOUL_Secure — some spots don't have a SEOUL_Secure hotspot depending on location. Connect to SEOUL for now, then set up SEOUL_Secure once it appears in your area the next time — after that, no more trouble.

3. Weak signal, keeps dropping — public Wi-Fi is designed for outdoor, open-air spaces, so it's normal for it to weaken indoors or underground. If you're inside a cafe or shop, switch to that venue's own Wi-Fi (see ⑥) instead.

⑨ Plan B — When Public Wi-Fi Just Won't Cooperate

  • Switch to eSIM data: public Wi-Fi is a backup at best. If it keeps dropping while you're moving around, it's less stressful to just keep eSIM data on from the start — for install/activation steps, see #1303 eSIM guide.
  • In front of convenience stores: GS25, CU, and other convenience stores often run their own open Wi-Fi out front — handy as a temporary fallback to fill public Wi-Fi's gaps.
