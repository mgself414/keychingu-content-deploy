Seoul Hiking Guide — 8 Best Mountains for Foreign Visitors

Subtitle: City-Entry + Hanyangdoseong History + Serious Peaks + Outer-Ring Hidden Gems — Seoul's Top 8 Hikes Mapped Across 4 Axes (Activity Series #3)

ISSUE 03 · #148 · 2026-05-12

🎯 Why a Seoul Hiking Guide?

Seoul is one of the world's cities where downtown and mountain are closest together — 8+ mountains within 10 km of City Hall. For foreign visitors, hiking is the ultimate Korean experience: the Hanyangdoseong (한양도성) city wall (UNESCO Tentative List), Bukhansan National Park (북한산국립공원), and summit views from Inwangsan (인왕산). "Bukhansan hike" and "Seoul hiking" are among the most frequent keywords on Reddit r/koreatravel, and foreign vlogger videos on YouTube rack up 5M+ views. ⚠ Top 3 Mistakes Foreign Visitors Make

1. Wearing sneakers on serious mountains (Bukhansan, Dobongsan)

Namsan (남산) and Naksan (낙산) are fine in sneakers. Bukhansan (북한산) and Dobongsan (도봉산) require hiking boots — rocky ridges, stream crossings, and wet leaves create serious slip hazards. There are no rentals on-site; hiking boots are available at Decathlon or The North Face stores in Myeongdong (명동) and Itaewon (이태원) from ₩100,000 (~$72 / ~520 RMB / ~10,800 JPY) and up.

2. Not bringing water or snacks

Namsan and Naksan have plenty of snack kiosks along the way. Bukhansan (북한산) and Gwanaksan (관악산) have zero kiosks near the summit — pack at least 1 L of water plus kimbap (김밥, Korean seaweed rice rolls) and chocolate. Seoul hiking culture: kimbap, makgeolli (막걸리, traditional Korean milky rice wine), and boiled eggs are the iconic summit snacks.

3. Not knowing about Bukaksan's entry pass or the Hanyangdoseong city wall

Certain sections of Bukaksan (북악산) behind the former presidential residence require an entry pass — on-site registration is OK, but you must bring your passport. The Hanyangdoseong (한양도성) — a 18.6 km city wall on the UNESCO Tentative List with 600 years of history — has 6 sections where you can collect official stamp certifications; it's hugely popular with international hiking enthusiasts.

Seoul Hiking at a Glance

Why Seoul hiking captivates foreign visitors:

  • Downtown-to-mountain distance < 10 km — start hiking within 30 minutes from the subway
  • Hanyangdoseong (한양도성) — 18.6 km of UNESCO Tentative-listed city walls with 600 years of history
  • Bukhansan National Park (북한산국립공원) — Korea's first urban national park, 837 m summit
  • Safe trails around the clock — some night hiking allowed (Namsan, Naksan); serious mountains 06:00–17:00
  • Iconic summit food — kimbap, makgeolli, boiled eggs, and instant ramen (the essence of Korean hiking culture)
  • Free entry — all mountains, including Bukhansan National Park, charge zero admission

Four visitor intentions:

1. City views + night scenery (Namsan, Naksan) — 1–2 hrs, best for sunset/night

2. History + Hanyangdoseong (Inwangsan, Bukaksan) — 2–3 hrs, the essence of Korea

3. Serious mountain challenge (Bukhansan, Dobongsan) — 4–6 hrs, for hiking enthusiasts

4. Outer-ring value hike (Gwanaksan, Cheonggyesan) — 3–4 hrs, close to Gangnam

① City Entry (2 Mountains)

1. Namsan (남산, 262 m)

The #1 first hike in Korea for foreign visitors — Seoul Tower, cable car, and a full city panorama. Start with a 10-minute walk from Myeongdong (명동), hike to the summit in 1–2 hours via stairs, then enjoy the view from N Seoul Tower (남산타워). The cable car is a great alternative (₩14,000 round trip). Night hiking is fine — excellent lighting and CCTV coverage throughout.

  • Starting point: Line 4 Myeongdong Station (명동역) or Chungmuro Station (충무로역), 10-min walk to north circular trail entrance
  • Distance / Time: ~2 km / approx. 1–2 hrs
  • Difficulty: Beginner (mostly stairs, sneakers OK)
  • Signature: N Seoul Tower + 360° city panorama + cable car
  • Best time: Sunrise 05:00–07:00 (AM) or sunset 18:00–20:00 (PM)
  • Why go: The #1 first hike for foreign visitors. The cable car option removes any fitness barrier.

2. Naksan (낙산, 125 m)

Seoul's lowest mountain — a 1-hour city stroll + the east section of the Hanyangdoseong city wall + night views. Start with a 10-minute walk from Hyehwa Station (혜화역), follow the Hanyangdoseong wall up to Naksan Park (낙산공원) at the summit. The top offers a panoramic view of eastern Seoul — a top night-view spot.

  • Starting point: Line 4 Hyehwa Station (혜화역) Exit 1, 10-min walk
  • Distance / Time: ~1.5 km / approx. 1 hr
  • Difficulty: Beginner (easiest of all 8 — even heels can manage)
  • Signature: East Hanyangdoseong + Naksan Park night view
  • Best time: Sunset + night view 18:00–21:00
  • Why go: Seoul's shortest hike. Perfect for families and couples who want that night-view moment.

② Hanyangdoseong · History (2 Mountains)

3. Inwangsan (인왕산, 338 m)

The essence of the Hanyangdoseong wall + dramatic boulder scenery + views over Gwanghwamun (광화문) and Gyeongbokgung Palace (경복궁). Start from Dongnimmun Station (독립문역) or Gyeongbokgung Station (경복궁역), follow the city wall upward, and take in the iconic rock formations — Seonbawi (선바위) and the summit boulder — at the top. A must for fans of Korean history and nature.

  • Starting point: Line 3 Dongnimmun Station (독립문역) or Gyeongbokgung Station (경복궁역)
  • Distance / Time: ~4 km / approx. 2–3 hrs
  • Difficulty: Intermediate (some bouldering sections; sneakers or hiking boots)
  • Signature: Hanyangdoseong city wall + boulder scenery + Gyeongbokgung view
  • Best time: Morning 09:00–12:00 (sunrise light, fewer crowds)
  • Why go: The most quintessentially Korean mountain. The best section of the UNESCO Hanyangdoseong wall.

4. Bukaksan (북악산, 342 m)

Behind the former Blue House (청와대) · UNESCO Hanyangdoseong · entry pass required for some sections. Start from Jahamun Information Center (자하문 안내소) or Malbawi Information Center (말바위 안내소). The section behind the former presidential residence requires passport registration on-site (available at the entrance). Spectacular scenery along the northern Hanyangdoseong section.

  • Starting point: Jahamun Shelter or Malbawi Shelter (30-min walk from Line 3 Anguk Station (안국역))
  • Distance / Time: ~4.7 km / approx. 2–3 hrs
  • Difficulty: Intermediate
  • Signature: Behind Cheong Wa Dae (청와대) + northern Hanyangdoseong city wall
  • Best time: 09:00–16:00 (official access hours)
  • Why go: Combines the former presidential grounds and the finest section of the UNESCO city wall in a single hike.

③ Serious Peaks (2 Mountains)

5. Bukhansan (북한산, Baegundae Summit 837 m)

Korea's first urban national park — 837 m summit at Baegundae (백운대). Multiple trailheads (Bukhansan Fortress, Ui, Jeongneung, Gupabal, and 10+ more). The rocky Baegundae summit, historic fortress walls, valleys, and Buddhist temples make this the #1 serious hike for foreign visitors. Hiking boots required.

  • Starting point: Bukhansan Fortress entrance (bus from Gupabal Station (구파발역)) or Ui-dong entrance (Ui Sinseol Line (우이신설선))
  • Distance / Time: ~8–10 km round trip / approx. 4–6 hrs
  • Difficulty: Advanced (rocky ridges; hiking boots essential)
  • Signature: Baegundae summit · fortress walls · valley streams
  • Best time: Depart 06:00–12:00 (PM sunrise option available)
  • Why go: The definitive serious hike in an urban national park. #1 choice for international hiking enthusiasts.

6. Dobongsan (도봉산, Jawunbong Summit 740 m)

Next to Bukhansan — dramatic rocky ridges at Jawunbong (자운봉) and Manjangbong (만장봉). Start 5 minutes on foot from Dobongsan Station (도봉산역). Rugged rocky terrain, more technical than Bukhansan. Best for experienced and advanced hikers.

  • Starting point: Line 1 / Line 7 Dobongsan Station (도봉산역)
  • Distance / Time: ~6–8 km round trip / approx. 4–5 hrs
  • Difficulty: Advanced (rocky ridges; hiking boots essential)
  • Signature: Jawunbong (자운봉) and Manjangbong (만장봉) ridgeline
  • Best time: 06:00–12:00
  • Why go: A completely different character from Bukhansan — serious technical ridgeline for rocky-terrain enthusiasts.

④ Outer-Ring Hidden Gems (2 Mountains)

7. Gwanaksan (관악산, Yeonjudae Summit 632 m)

Behind Seoul National University — the dramatic Yeonjudae (연주대) cliff temple at the summit. Start from Seoul National University Station (서울대입구역), hike up to Yeonjudae (a Buddhist hermitage perched on the summit ridge). Peak season: spring azaleas and autumn foliage. Ideal for combining Gangnam (강남) sightseeing with a real mountain hike.

  • Starting point: Line 2 Seoul National University Station (서울대입구역) + short bus ride
  • Distance / Time: ~7–9 km / approx. 3–4 hrs
  • Difficulty: Intermediate–Advanced (some rocky sections)
  • Signature: Yeonjudae + SNU campus + spring azaleas
  • Best time: Spring or autumn mornings
  • Why go: Stunning summit views close to Gangnam — the best combo of city touring and mountain hiking in the south.

8. Cheonggyesan (청계산, 618 m)

Maebong (매봉), Oknyeobong (옥녀봉), and easy access from Gangnam. Start from Yangjae Citizens' Forest Station (양재시민의숲역) and choose from various routes — Maebong (582 m) and Oknyeobong (376 m) — with gentler slopes than Gwanaksan. Family-friendly and beginner-accessible; ideal for pairing with a Gangnam itinerary.

  • Starting point: Sinbundang Line Yangjae Citizens' Forest Station (양재시민의숲역)
  • Distance / Time: ~5–7 km / approx. 3–4 hrs
  • Difficulty: Intermediate (gentle slopes; sneakers or hiking boots)
  • Signature: Maebong (매봉) · Oknyeobong (옥녀봉) · Gangnam panorama
  • Best time: Morning 09:00–12:00
  • Why go: Outer-ring mountain that's family-friendly and beginner-accessible — gentler terrain than Gwanaksan, minutes from Gangnam.

⚙ FAQ — 8 Questions

Q1. Do foreign visitors need ID for hiking?

Bukaksan (북악산) (section behind the former Blue House) — passport required; on-site registration available. Bukhansan National Park (북한산국립공원) and Dobongsan (도봉산) — free entry, no ID needed. All other mountains — no ID required. The Hanyangdoseong stamp certification program at select checkpoints does accept ID for enthusiast hikers.

Q2. Where can I buy or rent hiking shoes and gear?

  • Decathlon (Gangnam (강남), Jamsil (잠실)) — budget hiking boots ₩50,000–₩100,000 (~$36–$72)
  • The North Face · Kolping · Black Yak (Myeongdong (명동), Itaewon (이태원)) — performance hiking boots ₩150,000–₩300,000
  • Foreigner-friendly hotels — some offer hiking shoe rental with room key
  • Daiso (다이소) — budget hiking socks and small daypacks

Q3. Best times of day to hike?

  • Spring & Autumn: 08:00–10:00 AM (sunrise light, fewer crowds)
  • Summer (Jul–Aug): 05:00–07:00 AM (beat the heat)
  • Winter (Dec–Feb): 10:00–14:00 (warmest window, safer trail conditions)
  • Bukaksan (북악산) — strict access hours 09:00–16:00; do not miss the gate time

Q4. What's the deal with Korean summit food culture?

  • Kimbap (김밥) (₩4,000–₩6,000, ~$3–$4) — Korea's #1 hiking food; unwrap at the top
  • Makgeolli (막걸리) + boiled eggs — the essence of Korean hiking culture (one cup of makgeolli at the summit is a must-try ritual)
  • Cup ramen — available at some kiosks near Bukhansan (북한산) ₩3,000–₩4,000
  • Chocolate & bananas — easy energy snacks for the trail

Q5. What if it rains or the weather turns bad?

Namsan (남산) and Naksan (낙산) — an umbrella will do. Inwangsan (인왕산) and Bukaksan (북악산) — an umbrella is possible, but wet rocks become dangerously slippery. Bukhansan (북한산), Dobongsan (도봉산), Gwanaksan (관악산) — absolutely avoid in rain (lightning risk, slippery rocks, wet leaves). Swap the hike for a museum or DDP (동대문디자인플라자) on rainy days.

Q6. Any English-speaking hiking groups for foreign visitors?

Seoul Hash House Harriers (Saturday day hikes) and Korea Hiking Club (Facebook group) — both English-speaking communities that cover serious routes on Bukhansan (북한산) and Dobongsan (도봉산) with group guides.

Q7. Can I hike the full Hanyangdoseong (한양도성) 18.6 km in one go?

The full 18.6 km is best split into 4–6 sections across multiple visits (Bukaksan, Inwangsan, Naksan, Namsan, etc.). Doing it all at once takes 12–14 hours — strictly for serious enthusiasts. Collect official stamp certifications at Bukjeong Village (북정마을) Certification Center and other checkpoints — a beloved challenge among international hiking fans.

Q8. What's the best way to wind down after hiking (food, hot springs, jjimjilbang)?

  • Bukhansan Fortress (북한산성) / Ui-dong (우이동) — several nofo (30+ year traditional restaurants) serving makgeolli (막걸리) and pajeon (파전, savory green-onion pancake)
  • Namsan (남산) — Myeongdong (명동) kalguksu (칼국수, hand-cut noodle soup) and giant dumplings (#134 Dumpling Guide)
  • Inwangsan (인왕산) — Gwanghwamun (광화문) gukbap (국밥, rice-in-soup) (#133 Guide)
  • Jjimjilbang (찜질방) — a Korean-style sauna and communal bathhouse; perfect post-hike recovery in Yangjae (양재), Jamsil (잠실), or Hongdae (홍대) — see #7 Jjimjilbang Guide

🗺 3 Recommended 1-Day Hiking + Sightseeing Itineraries

Route A — "City Entry + Night Views" (Beginner, 5 hrs)

14:00 → Hike Namsan (남산) via stair trail, ~1.5 hrs → Summit views at N Seoul Tower (남산타워) → Cable car down → Dinner in Myeongdong (명동) → If time allows, add Naksan (낙산) for 1 hr of night views (short ride to Hyehwa Station (혜화역))

Route B — "Hanyangdoseong Deep Dive" (History & Hiking Enthusiast, 6 hrs)

08:00 → Inwangsan (인왕산) ~2.5 hrs (start at Dongnimmun (독립문), summit, follow city wall to Jahamun Shelter (자하문 안내소)) → Connect to Bukaksan (북악산) ~2 hrs (section behind former Blue House) → Jahamun (자하문) → Lunch in Gwanghwamun (광화문) → Collect Hanyangdoseong stamp certifications

Route C — "Bukhansan Full Day" (Hiking Enthusiast, full day 8 hrs)

06:00 → Bukhansan Fortress (북한산성) entrance → Bukhansan (북한산) Baegundae summit and back, ~5 hrs (sunrise at the top is an option) → Explore fortress walls + valley → Makgeolli (막걸리) wrap-up in Ui-dong (우이동) → Hotel

🌸 5 Seasonal & Time-of-Day Highlights

1. Spring (Apr–May) — azaleas and cherry blossoms: Inwangsan (인왕산) and Gwanaksan (관악산) peak with azaleas. Best window: late April to early May.

2. Summer (Jul–Aug) — pre-dawn sunrise: Beat the heat and catch sunrise views. Hit Namsan (남산), Inwangsan (인왕산), or Bukhansan (북한산) at 05:00–07:00 AM.

3. Autumn (Oct–Nov) — fall foliage: Bukhansan (북한산), Dobongsan (도봉산), and Cheonggyesan (청계산) ablaze with color. Peak: late October to mid-November. Best photography season.

4. Winter (Dec–Feb) — snow hiking: Snow-dusted peaks on Bukhansan (북한산) and Dobongsan (도봉산) are stunning. Hiking boots and crampons (아이젠) essential.

5. Year-round night views: Namsan (남산) and Naksan (낙산) are lit and monitored 24 hours — safe and beautiful for evening hikers.
