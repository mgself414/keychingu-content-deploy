⚠ 3 mistakes foreigners often make

1. "I look at everything during the day" — Going out at noon on a heat wave day = 1 hour of exhaustion

70%+ of first-time foreign summer travelers to Korea make an unreasonable itinerary that includes visiting Gyeongbokgung Palace, Bukchon, Insa-dong, and Myeong-dong at once between 11 and 14 noon. Result: Dizziness, vomiting, blisters, and risk of heat stroke in just 1 to 2 hours. Rule = Always indoors for 3 hours at noon (COEX 4, Museum 5, Department Store 6). Outdoor excursions are distributed at dawn (axis 1) or after 17:00 in the evening.

2. "I thought I could swim in the main stream of the Han River" — I didn't know that the Han River swimming pool = a pool

Foreigners search SNS for “Hangang River swim” → Attempt to enter the main stream of the river, thinking they are swimming in the river (dangerous and prohibited by law). Actual Han River Swimming Pool = Outdoor pool next to Hangang Park (purified water) — Open for free from July to August. Watering the main stream of the Han River is not recommended for safety reasons. Exact English search = "Hangang outdoor swimming pool". Operating in 5 locations: Banpo, Ttukseom, Yeouido, Jamsil, and Gwangnaru. The exact opening schedule is based on the official guidance of the Han River Business Headquarters (varies from early July to late August every year).

3. "Midnight = Danger" — Korea's 24-hour cafes, saunas, and convenience stores are the safest hours for foreigners

30%+ of foreigners (especially first-time travelers to Korea) stay in hotels after 22:00 and miss the cool night movement on heat wave days. Real Korea = Gangnam, Hongdae, Jongno 24h zone, highest in OECD for nighttime security·Late night cafes, jjimjilbangs, and convenience stores are all safe operation·No. 1 female solo foreigner vlog = 24h sauna night package. Rule = Recharge your cool at the 24h cafe (#005) or sauna (#007) for 1 hour after arriving at the hotel at 23:00.

FAQ 6

Q1. Han River Swimming Pool = A place to swim in the main stream of the Han River?

A. No. Outdoor pool (water treatment) next to Hangang Park. See #2 mistake in this guide. Free from July to August (Banpo, Ttukseom, Yeouido, Jamsil, Gwangnaru). The exact schedule is official from the Han River Project Headquarters.

Q2. Outdoor tours available from 12 noon to 14 p.m.

A. Not recommended. Noon on a heat wave day = feels like 35 to 40 degrees Celsius. 1 hour + outdoors = risk of exhaustion and heat stroke. This guide axis 3 (indoor 11-14) is highly recommended.

Q3. Is it safe for foreigners to hike or bike early in the morning?

A. Safe. Korea ranks high in nighttime and early morning security in OECD countries. Climbing Mt. Inwang and Mt. Bukak in the early morning = many female foreigners with families or solo. Bicycle = Ttareungi app English OK, international card registration OK.

Q4. First attempt by a foreigner to use a 24-hour sauna/jimjilbang?

A. Dragon Hill Spa (Yongsan) = No. 1 foreigner-friendly · English guidance · See #007 full guide. Admission 12,000~16,000 won, night package 18,000~25,000 won (capsule bed included).

Q5. Is the Han River Night Market open in case of rain?

A. Partial operation. Light rain = operation/heavy rainfall = partial cancellation. Check the official SNS (Seoul City/Hangang Business Headquarters) announcement on the day.

Q6. Medical emergency during a heat wave?

A. 119 = English OK·Korean emergency medical care = Free emergency room operation for foreigners (medical insurance separate). See #405 Full Guide to Emergency Medical Services for Foreigners.
