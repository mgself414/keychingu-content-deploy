Korea, the "Republic of Apartments" — Why Most Koreans Live in Near-Identical High-Rise Blocks

> Apartment Republic (아파트 공화국) · Compressed Urbanization (압축 도시화) · Gangnam (강남) Development · Brand Apartments · Jeonse (전세) · Jae-tech (재테크) — A Korean housing culture explainer for foreigners. Not just "Korea has many apartments," but "why, how, and its pros and cons."

From the bus window on the way from Incheon International Airport (인천공항) to Seoul (서울), the first thing foreigners encounter in Korea is massive, identical high-rise apartment complexes. Even when you leave the city and go to the provinces, similar buildings of around 30 stories form danji (단지), or complexes, stretching endlessly, with brand names like Xi (자이), Raemian (래미안), and Hillstate (힐스테이트) emblazoned on their rooftops. Foreigners usually interpret this as "uniform housing," but Korean apartments are not that simple. It's a unique social phenomenon that a French geographer wrote an entire book about. For Koreans, an apartment is not just a place to live, but also their biggest asset and a means of wealth management (재테크). It's a reflection of modern Korean history itself, intertwined with efficiency, convenience, speculation, uniformity, and noise between floors (층간소음). This article doesn't tell you "where to live well," but rather explains why Korea became the 'Republic of Apartments,' its light and shadow, from a foreigner's perspective. This is the 20th installment of the KeyChinGu (키친구) Korean culture explainer series.

> Key Takeaway: In Korea, an apartment is not just a 'home' but an object that combines residence + asset + social status. That's why the entire society is unusually sensitive to apartment prices, to the point of seeming strange to foreigners (see ④·⑥ below).

⚠️ 3 Common Misconceptions Foreigners Often Have

1. "Identical apartments = poor or controlled society" ❌ — In the West, large apartment complexes often carry an image of low-income or public housing, but in Korea, it's the opposite. Luxury brand apartments in Gangnam (강남) are symbols of wealth, and newly built large danji (단지) are the most preferred residences for the middle and upper classes. The Western equation of 'uniform exterior = low status' does not apply in Korea.

2. "Apartments are just places to live" ❌ — For Koreans, apartments are a 'jae-tech (재테크) tool' (wealth management) that accounts for most of their household assets. The neighborhood, brand, pyeong (평) size, and reconstruction (재건축) potential all determine its asset value. This is why real estate news dominates headlines every day.

3. "Korea is small, so apartments are unavoidable" ❌ — This cannot be explained by population density alone. Gelézeau (줄레조) points out that the Netherlands and Belgium, which have higher population densities than Korea, did not opt for large apartment complexes. She analyzes that it's not a lack of land, but a phenomenon created by the choices of the government, chaebol (재벌, large conglomerates), and the middle class (see ①·② below).

① What is the 'Apartment Republic (아파트 공화국)' — Korea as Seen by a French Scholar

The 'Apartment Republic (아파트 공화국)' is not just a nickname but a term derived from academic analysis.

  • Valérie Gelézeau (발레리 줄레조) is a French geographer. She was shocked by Korea, the 'country of apartments,' upon her first visit in 1993. After earning her Ph.D. for her research on Seoul (서울) apartment danji (단지), she published *『The Republic of Apartments (아파트 공화국)』 (original French title: Séoul, ville géante, cités radieuses) (France 2003, Korean edition 2007)*.
  • Her question was incisive — "How did large apartment complexes, considered a 'failed housing model' in France, become the most coveted homes in Korea?"
  • Gelézeau's (줄레조) key answer is the 'three-party alliance.' She argues that apartments were explosively mass-produced as the interests of the government (development-driven), chaebol (재벌) construction companies (mass supply), and the middle class (desire for asset growth) aligned. She also highlighted the socio-economic segregation and aesthetic uniformity resulting from this structure.

> In other words, 'Apartment Republic (아파트 공화국)' is neither praise nor denigration, but an analytical framework that asks "why Korean housing became this way." Koreans themselves frequently use this expression.

② History — From War's Ruins to a Forest of Danji (단지)

Korea's transformation into an Apartment Republic (아파트 공화국) is the result of compressed urbanization (압축 도시화). What other countries achieved over a century, Korea accomplished in a few decades (→ [#1143 Ppalli-Ppalli Culture](korean-ppalli-ppalli-culture)).

  • Starting Point: The Korean War (1950-53) left cities in ruins, and subsequent industrialization drew people to Seoul (서울). In the 1970s, the Korean economy grew at an annual rate of over 10%, leading to an explosion in urban population — there was an absolute shortage of housing.
  • Solution: 'Mass and High-Density': The fastest way to supply the maximum number of households on limited land was high-rise apartment danji (단지). Starting with Mapo Apartment (마포아파트) in 1962, apartments became established as 'modern and convenient new housing.'
  • ★Gangnam (강남) (Yeongdong District, 영동지구) Development: In the 1970s, to decentralize the population concentrated in Gangbuk (강북), the vast empty plains south of the Han River (한강) — called 'Yeongdong (영동)' because it was east of Yeongdeungpo (영등포) — were developed into a new city. As the Gyeongbu Expressway (경부고속도로), Seoul Subway Line 2 (지하철 2호선), and Teheran-ro (테헤란로) were laid out, and large apartment danji (단지) were built in areas like Gaepo (개포) and Dogok (도곡), the population of Gangnam (강남) surged from approximately 320,000 in 1975 to over 800,000 in the 1980s. 'Gangnam Apartments (강남 아파트)' solidified their status as a symbol of wealth and aspiration at this time.

Through this process, apartments gained the image of not just a simple home, but the 'standard housing of a growing nation.'
