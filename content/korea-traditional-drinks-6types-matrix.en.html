Korean Tea & Traditional Drinks 6-Type Matrix — From Green Tea to Sujeonggwa, a Full Non-Alcoholic Set for Foreigners

> Subtitle: 6 Types of Green Tea, Sikhye, Sujeonggwa, Gukhwacha, Omijacha, and Daechucha for Foreign Tourists + Hanok Cafe & Temple Enthusiasts

ISSUE 03 · #840 · 2026-06-09

🎯 Why a Korean Traditional Drinks Matrix?

The pain point for foreign visitors regarding Korean tea and drinks is: "They only know green tea." Korean traditional drinks are non-alcoholic + Sasang Uihak (사상의학, Four Constitutional Medicine, a traditional Korean medical theory) + sachal eumsik (사찰음식, Korean temple food) + the #1 spot for hanok (한옥, traditional Korean house) cafes + highly foreigner-friendly ★ (Halal, Kosher, and pregnant-friendly OK). Even though they're all "Korean tea," Nokcha (녹차) (traditional from Boseong (보성) and Hadong (하동)), Sikhye (식혜) (sweet fermented rice drink), Sujeonggwa (수정과) (cinnamon-ginger punch), Gukhwacha (국화차) (traditional autumn tea), Omijacha (오미자차) (five-flavor berry tea), and Daechucha (대추차) (traditional herbal tea) each have different ingredients, seasons, and benefits. Foreigners (a) only know green tea and haven't experienced other traditional drinks, (b) don't know if Sikhye (식혜) is a drink or a food, (c) don't know about Sujeonggwa (수정과) or Gukhwacha (국화차) for dessert pairing, and (d) it's a perfect fit ★★★★★ for pregnant women, Halal, and Kosher enthusiasts. This guide provides a 6-type matrix + recommendations by hanok (한옥), temple, and MZ (Millennial and Gen Z) cafes + foreigner-friendly entry priorities.

🍵 6 Types Deep Dive

🍵 Nokcha (녹차) — Global #1

  • Signature: Korea's #1 globally recognized tea·The birthplace of Korea's tea industry
  • Types:
  • Boseong Nokcha (보성 녹차) (Boseong (보성), Jeollanam-do (전라남도), Korea's #1 production area) — Traditional + Tea Museum
  • Hadong Nokcha (하동 녹차) (Hadong (하동), Gyeongsangnam-do (경상남도)) — The origin of Korean tea cultivation (始培地, according to Samguk Sagi, Kim Dae-ryeom planted tea seeds in 828 AD during King Heungdeok's 3rd year, marking approximately 1200 years of tea culture)
  • Jeju Nokcha (제주 녹차) (Osulloc (오설록)·Innisfree (이니스프리)) — Traditional from Jeju's (제주) volcanic soil
  • Malcha (말차): Powdered form (MZ (Millennial and Gen Z) trend)
  • Hyeonmi Nokcha (현미녹차): Brown rice + green tea (foreigner-friendly)
  • Foreigner-Friendliness: ★★★★★ (Global recognition)
  • Price: ₩5,000~15,000 (~$3.7-11 / ~27-80 RMB / ~550-1,650 JPY) per cup / ₩10,000~30,000 (~$7.4-22 / ~54-160 RMB / ~1,100-3,300 JPY) per pack
  • Season: Year-round

🍯 Sikhye (식혜) — Foreigner Entry #1

  • Signature: Rice + malted barley fermentation·Sweet·Refreshing = Korea's #1 traditional non-alcoholic drink
  • Types:
  • Traditional Sikhye (전통 식혜): Traditional dessert for jeongsik (정식, Korean set meal)
  • Hobak Sikhye (호박 식혜) (Pumpkin sikhye): Autumn variation
  • Ssal-al Sikhye (쌀 알 식혜) (Rice grain sikhye): With rice grains
  • MZ Sikhye (MZ 식혜): Sikhye (식혜) latte·Sikhye (식혜) bingsu (빙수, shaved ice dessert)
  • Foreigner-Friendliness: ★★★★★ (Sweet·Refreshing·Dessert)
  • Price: ₩3,000~7,000 (~$2.2-5.2 / ~16-38 RMB / ~330-770 JPY) (pyeonuijeom (편의점, convenience store)) / ₩5,000~10,000 (~$3.7-7.4 / ~27-54 RMB / ~550-1,100 JPY) (hanok (한옥) cafe)
  • Season: Summer ★★★★★ (Refreshing)
  • Enthusiasts: Jeongsik (정식) dessert #1·Dessert pairing #1

🌶 Sujeonggwa (수정과) — Traditional

  • Signature: Cinnamon + ginger + gotgam (곶감, dried persimmon) = Traditional royal dessert drink
  • Ingredients: Cinnamon + ginger + gotgam (곶감, dried persimmon) + pine nuts + sugar·honey
  • Foreigner-Friendliness: ★★★★ (Spicy·Sweet enthusiasts)
  • Price: ₩5,000~10,000 (~$3.7-7.4 / ~27-54 RMB / ~550-1,100 JPY) (hanok (한옥) cafe)
  • Season: Winter ★★★★★ (Warm)
  • Enthusiasts: Jeongsik (정식) dessert·Yakgwa (약과, traditional honey cookie) pairing

🌼 Gukhwacha (국화차)

  • Signature: Autumn's #1 traditional tea·Chrysanthemum scent
  • Ingredients: Chrysanthemum flower + honey (optional)
  • Foreigner-Friendliness: ★★★★ (Scent traditional)
  • Price: ₩5,000~10,000 (~$3.7-7.4 / ~27-54 RMB / ~550-1,100 JPY) (hanok (한옥) cafe)
  • Season: Autumn ★★★★★
  • Enthusiasts: Yakgwa (약과) + Gukhwacha (국화차) traditional pairing

🌿 Omijacha (오미자차) — 5-Flavor Balance

  • Signature: 5-flavor (sweet·sour·bitter·spicy·salty) balance·Traditional herbal (hanbang (한방))
  • Ingredients: Omija (오미자) (Schisandra) + honey (optional)
  • Foreigner-Friendliness: ★★★★ (5-flavor enthusiasts)
  • Price: ₩5,000~10,000 (~$3.7-7.4 / ~27-54 RMB / ~550-1,100 JPY)
  • Season: Summer ★★★★ (Refreshing)
  • Enthusiasts: Traditional herbal (hanbang (한방)) cafe

🌰 Daechucha (대추차) — Herbal Restorative

  • Signature: Jujube + ginger + honey = Traditional herbal (hanbang (한방)) restorative
  • Ingredients: Jujube (pitted) + ginger + honey + pine nuts
  • Foreigner-Friendliness: ★★★★★ (Sweet·Herbal·Pregnant-friendly OK)
  • Price: ₩5,000~10,000 (~$3.7-7.4 / ~27-54 RMB / ~550-1,100 JPY)
  • Season: Winter ★★★★★ (Warm)
  • Enthusiasts: Herbal (hanbang (한방)) cafe·Pregnant-friendly caffeine-free OK (check ginger content with the store)

⚠ 5 Common Mistakes by Foreigners

1. "Korean tea = only green tea": There's a full 6-type set + herbal (hanbang (한방)) options. Sikhye (식혜)·Sujeonggwa (수정과)·Gukhwacha (국화차)·Omija (오미자)·Daechu (대추) are also traditional.

2. "Sikhye (식혜) = alcohol": Sikhye (식혜) = non-alcoholic fermented sweet drink (rice + malted barley). Halal·pregnant-friendly·children OK.

3. "Boseong (보성)·Hadong (하동) = same green tea": Boseong (보성) = Korea's #1 production area·Tea Museum / Hadong (하동) = The origin of Korean tea cultivation (始培地, Kim Dae-ryeom planted seeds in 828 AD, marking approximately 1200 years).

4. "Sujeonggwa (수정과) = dessert": Sujeonggwa (수정과) = spicy cinnamon + ginger + balanced sweetness from gotgam (곶감, dried persimmon) drink.

5. "Omija (오미자) = simply sour": Omija (오미자) = 5-flavor balance (sweet·sour·bitter·spicy·salty) = traditional herbal (hanbang (한방)).
