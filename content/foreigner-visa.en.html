Korea Visa & K-ETA Guide — Which Side Is My Passport On: 22 Countries with a Temporary K-ETA Exemption (Through 2026-12-31) · Visa-Free Days by Country · Countries That Need a Visa

Subtitle: K-ETA is not a visa — it is a permit that visa-free travelers get before boarding · The 22 countries on the Ministry of Justice (법무부) list are exempt through 2026-12-31; all other eligible countries pay ₩10,000 for a permit valid 3 years (Ministry of Justice K-ETA) · Visa-free days differ by country — 90 days for the US, Japan and the UK, 6 months for Canada, 90 days within 180 for the Nordic countries, 30 days for Saudi Arabia (Ministry of Foreign Affairs (외교부) table) · Ordinary passports from mainland China, Indonesia, the Philippines, Vietnam and India need a visa · Checked 2026-11-04

🎯 How to Use This Guide

Just above the payment button on your flight booking, a checkbox appears: "Do you have a K-ETA?" Beside it, an ad follows you: "Korea ETA agent, approved in 5 minutes." Many people stall here. This guide answers only that one checkbox — is my passport exempt from K-ETA, do I have to apply, or do I need a visa? Once you land in one of the three tracks, what you have to do next takes two or three lines.

The scope is short visits such as tourism or visiting relatives and friends. Employment, student and working holiday visas are not covered (for people staying while working remotely, see the F-1-D entry in #459 Korea Workation Guide). Customs duty-free limits and tax refunds are in #434 Korea Entry, Customs & Tax Refund Checklist, the screen-by-screen order of the e-Arrival Card (입국신고서) is in #1289, airport automated clearance is in #1412, and medication you bring with you is in #1352.

There are two places to confirm which track your passport belongs to. On the k-eta.go.kr application screen, scan the photo page of your passport; if you are 'exempt from K-ETA', a pop-up appears (K-ETA notice). If your country is not on the list of countries that can apply at all, your passport does not allow visa-free entry, so you get a visa (K-ETA FAQ). The criterion is passport nationality, not where you live or where you depart from (K-ETA FAQ).

🛂 A. Temporary K-ETA Exemption — 22 Countries/Regions, Through 2026-12-31

The Ministry of Justice has extended the temporary K-ETA exemption for tourism promotion by one more year, running from January 1 to December 31, 2026 (K-ETA notice, 2025-12-23). There is no announcement yet for 2027 — if you depart in 2027, check again.

The exempt countries are the 22 countries/regions in the Ministry of Justice notice (the list in force from 2023-04-01, attached to the Consulate General in Atlanta's 2026-01-02 extension notice). The 2026 extension notice says it simply continues the "countries currently exempt."

Region Countries/Regions
Asia (5) Japan · Taiwan · Hong Kong · Macao · Singapore
Americas (2) United States (including Guam) · Canada
Europe (13) United Kingdom · Germany · France · Italy · Netherlands · Spain · Poland · Sweden · Finland · Norway · Belgium · Denmark · Austria
Oceania (2) Australia · New Zealand
  • You can still apply even if exempt. If you want benefits such as skipping the arrival card, you may apply, and a fee applies. A permit you already hold is valid until its expiry date and is not refunded (K-ETA notice).
  • If you are exempt but did not get a K-ETA, you still file the e-Arrival Card. File online from 3 days before arrival, no fee (Ministry of Justice e-Arrival portal). For the steps, see #1289.
  • Country-specific details are separate — Japan #190 · Hong Kong & Macao #335 · Canada #464.

📝 B. Apply for K-ETA — Eligible but Not Exempt

K-ETA (Korea Electronic Travel Authorization, 전자여행허가) is a permit that nationals of countries with visa waiver agreements (67 countries) and countries/regions allowed visa-free entry (45 countries) — 112 countries/regions in total — get before boarding a plane or ship when coming without a visa for purposes such as tourism, visiting relatives and friends, or attending events (K-ETA FAQ, notice). In the Ministry of Justice's own words, "K-ETA is not a visa (VISA), and K-ETA approval does not guarantee entry" — the immigration officer makes the final decision on whether you may enter.

📅 C. How Many Days Visa-Free — Days by Country

"Visa-free 90 days" is not the whole story. Below are the ordinary passport values from the Ministry of Foreign Affairs' table posted by its overseas missions, "Countries Whose Nationals Can Enter Korea Without a Visa" (as of February 2024, revised 2026-01-08), copied as written. '90 days within 180' means 90 days in total within half a year, and '3 months' and '90 days' are shown exactly as the table separates them. New Zealand appears twice in the Ministry of Foreign Affairs table, as 90 days (Asia column) and 3 months (Oceania column), and the K-ETA eligible-country list says 3 months, so 3 months is used here.

🛃 D. Passports That Need a Visa — Mainland China · Indonesia · Philippines · Vietnam · India · Mongolia

In the Ministry of Foreign Affairs table, these countries appear only in the diplomatic and official passport column, not the ordinary passport column (Ministry of Foreign Affairs missions table). If you come as a tourist on an ordinary passport, you get a short-term tourist visa (C-3-9) from a Korean diplomatic mission abroad (embassy, consulate general, visa application center). Documents, fees and processing time vary by mission, so follow that mission's notice. On the Visa Portal (비자포털) visa.go.kr, choose your nationality and purpose in 'VISA NAVIGATOR' and it shows the category that applies to you (visa exemption B-1 · general visa-free B-2-1 · Jeju visa-free B-2-2 · general tourist C-3-9) (Visa Portal).

There are three confirmed exceptions. The first two are time-limited temporary programs.

  • Group visa-free entry does not apply to individual travelers who come alone or with family. Whether your travel agency is a designated travel agency and whether your name is on the list is something to confirm with that agency and the mission's notice.
  • For country-specific procedures, see Mainland China #191 · Indonesia #468 · Philippines #466 (applications go through the KVAC visa application center). For Vietnam, India and Mongolia, check the notices of the Korean mission in that country.
  • To find out whether other nationalities get Jeju visa-free entry, check the visa.go.kr navigator.
  • Visa holders do not apply for a K-ETA (K-ETA FAQ). Individual visa holders file the e-Arrival Card before arrival. ⚠ 5 Common Mistakes

1. "My country is exempt, so I file nothing" — if you did not get a K-ETA, you still file the e-Arrival Card. From 3 days before arrival, free (Ministry of Justice).

2. Paying on an 'agent' site at the top of the search results — the only official channels are k-eta.go.kr and the mobile app, and the fee is ₩10,000 + 3% payment fee. The e-Arrival Card charges nothing (Ministry of Justice). A site that asks for payment details first is not official.

3. Applying two days before departure — review usually takes within 72 hours but can take longer, and without a permit you cannot board. The Ministry of Justice recommends 1~2 months before your planned arrival (K-ETA notice, 2022-04).

4. "Visa-free is 90 days everywhere" — Canada is 6 months, Saudi Arabia, Brunei and Kazakhstan are 30 days, Russia is 60 days, and Sweden, Norway, Denmark and Finland are 90 days within 180 (Ministry of Foreign Affairs table).

5. "I have a K-ETA, so entry is guaranteed" — K-ETA is not a visa and does not guarantee entry (Ministry of Justice). And the temporary exemption ends 2026-12-31 — if you depart in 2027, check the exemption again.

⚙ 4 FAQs

Q1. Do my children and parents need a K-ETA too?

Ages 17 and under and 65 and over (as of the entry date) are not required to (from 2023-07-03). If you want benefits such as skipping the arrival card, you can apply, and a fee applies. Someone who turns 18 on the entry date needs a permit (K-ETA FAQ). Those 17 and under or 65 and over who have no K-ETA also file the e-Arrival Card (Ministry of Justice e-Arrival 'Who Must File' guide).

Q2. I'm only transiting through Incheon.

Transit passengers who do not enter Korea do not apply. It is needed if you go through immigration to collect baggage or change your destination (K-ETA FAQ). For the transit route, see #1015.

Q3. I applied but no result has come.

The K-ETA Center (02-2666-0463) in principle does not give phone or in-person consultations and takes questions through the website's [Inquiries] section (K-ETA application guide). Since you cannot ask by phone, replies may be slow — which is why applying 1~2 months before your planned arrival is recommended (2022-04 notice).

Q4. Who do I ask about stay and immigration once I'm in Korea?

The Ministry of Justice Immigration Contact Center 1345 (외국인종합안내센터) — weekdays 09:00~22:00, after 18:00 in Korean, English and Chinese only, from overseas +82-2-1345 (Ministry of Justice Immigration & Foreign Policy Headquarters). It is not an emergency number. Online, use Hi Korea (하이코리아, hikorea.go.kr). For urgent matters, see #433 Emergency & Safety Guide.
