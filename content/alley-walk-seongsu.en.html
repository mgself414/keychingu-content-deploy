Alley Walk ③ Seongsu (성수) — Why Is This Neighborhood Red? How to Walk the Brick Alleys

Subtitle: Treat Seongsu as just a list of cafés and you miss half of it. This neighborhood's real identity is red brick — it's the only neighborhood in Seoul where a municipal ordinance protects the brick of 1970s–80s factories and warehouses. From the handmade-shoe street by Seongsu Station, through the red-brick alleys and Yeonmujang-gil, to Seoul Forest — about 2.5 hours, walking Seongsu through the lens of architecture and industry. Seoul Alley Walks Pilot 3.

ISSUE · #1342 · 2026-09-02

🎯 The Frame — Brick Protected by Ordinance

Behind the nickname "the Brooklyn of Seoul" is an actual policy. In 2017, Seongdong-gu (성동구) enacted the Red Brick Building Preservation and Support Ordinance, which supports renovating rather than demolishing 1970s–80s brick factories and warehouses and 1980s–90s brick houses (now expanded across all of Seongsu). Many of Seongsu's cafés and boutiques today sit inside those same brick buildings — this route follows the grain of that brick, in order.

  • Total time: about 2.5 hours (3+ hours with galleries and cafés) · Best time: start 1–2 PM, finish at dusk in Seoul Forest (서울숲)
  • Incline: low — this is a flat route, the easiest walk of the three pilots.

🚶 The Route — In Walking Order

① Seongsu Station (성수역) Exit 2 → Handmade Shoe Street (about a 3-minute walk)

  • Shoe shops and workshop signs start right around the station and keep going.
  • 📖 Story 1 — The Shoemakers' Neighborhood: For decades, Seongsu was Korea's cluster for handmade shoes — workshops split up the labor, from cutting leather to building heels, and clustered together in these alleys, leaving traces still visible around the street today. A shoemaking neighborhood layered over a brick-factory neighborhood — that's Seongsu's "made here" DNA.

② Handmade Shoe Street → Red Brick Alley (붉은벽돌 골목) (about a 10-minute walk)

  • Step just one block in from the main road and the density of brick changes. Grid-patterned factory windows, truss roofs on old warehouses, new signage mounted straight onto brick — spotting traces of renovation instead of demolition is the game in this stretch.
  • 📖 Story 2 — Why It Survived: Choosing preservation over redevelopment wasn't only about aesthetics. The ordinance subsidizes repair costs for brick buildings, which is how the neighborhood keeps its face even as rents climb. The best courtesy a visitor can offer is simple: don't lean on the brick walls, and don't tag them.

③ Red Brick Alley → Yeonmujang-gil (연무장길) (about a 10-minute walk)

  • Seongsu's main street. Boutiques (#29), pop-ups, and cafés all lease space inside the brick buildings. Weekends bring serious crowds — if you want to see the alley's actual texture, a weekday is the answer.
  • Lately the brick commercial strip keeps spreading toward Dongyeonmujang-gil (동연무장길) and Bukseongsu (북성수) — the block just outside today's crowd is next year's Seongsu.

④ Yeonmujang-gil → Seoul Forest → Finish (about a 15-minute walk)

  • Out of the straight lines of the factory alleys and into the curves of the forest — that contrast is this route's final move. Finish at dusk on Seoul Forest's Ginkgo Tree Road (은행나무길) (in autumn, see #1314).
  • Ways to end it: extend on to Ttukseom Hangang Park (뚝섬한강공원) (#709), or head back to Seongsu Station for dinner.

🙏 Alley Manners

  • The brick alleys still have working factories and warehouses mixed in — don't photograph through open doors, and give way to forklifts and trucks.
  • Even at brick walls that have become photo spots, let people pass first — no tripods on narrow sidewalks.

💡 Where You Might Get Thrown Off

  • Monday closures are common — many cafés and galleries take Mondays off. Tuesday–Friday afternoons work best for this route.
  • Signless storefronts are trendy in Seongsu — a shop can look closed and still be open. Trust the notice posted at the door more than the hours listed in a map app.
  • Weekend waits on Yeonmujang-gil rank among the longest in Seoul (#1274 Waitlist Manual) — if cafés are your priority, go right at opening.
