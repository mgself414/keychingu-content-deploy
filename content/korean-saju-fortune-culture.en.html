Korean fortune-telling culture — Why do Koreans look at ‘horoscopes’ instead of fortune telling?

Four Fortune-telling, Yin-Yang and Five Elements, Myeongnihak, Fortune-telling Cafe — Explainer of Korean fortune-telling culture for foreigners. Not ‘where to go for fortune telling’, but ‘why, what, and how.’

When you watch a K-drama, there is a scene that always appears. Ahead of marriage, contemplating a change of job, or at the start of a new year — Korean people go somewhere, saying, “I need to see fortune telling.” It also appears when idols watch fortune telling on entertainment shows, when lovers try to match their compatibility, and when parents choose names for their babies. Foreigners usually translate this as “fortune telling,” but the four pillars that Koreans refer to are not just superstition fortune telling. It is a hundreds of years old Oriental philosophy system, and these days, it has been reinterpreted as the MZ generation's Saju Cafe and is emerging as a new Seoul experience for foreign tourists (Seoul Tourism Organization and VisitKorea are covering it as Korean Wave content). This article is not about “where can I see it?” (→ cross-link), but explains the way of thinking, history, and system behind it from a foreigner’s perspective. ⚠️ 3 things foreigners often misunderstand

1. "Foreign fortune = fortune teller superstition" ❌ — The fortune teller is an oriental philosophy system called Saju Myeongrihak(四柱命理學). It is close to a kind of “statistics of fate” that interprets the yin-yang-five elements data of the time of birth. Rather than a fortune teller atmosphere, you often see it with a cup of tea at a neat Saju Cafe.

2. "A person who sees a fortune teller = a shaman" ❌ — Saju fortune (Myeongnihak) is academic analysis learned from books, and Shinjeom (shamanism) is a spiritual fortune that a shaman sees with the power of a divine spirit. The two are completely different (⑤ below).

3. "Foreign fortune = fate is determined (determinism)" ❌ — Most Koreans see fortune telling not as a "determined future" but as "a reference book to understand and prepare for my temperament and flow". Experts also say “There is no such thing as a 100% incompatible couple” — this is advice, not an absolute verdict.

① What are the four pillars and eight letters?

Four pillars literally mean 'four pillars'. The four things year, month, day, and time of birth are each considered a pillar.

  • Each pillar is made up of two characters, top and bottom: Cheongan (天干), and bottom is Jiji (地支).
  • Four pillars × two letters = eight letters, so eight characters(八字). The Korean expression "My Paljaya" comes from here.
  • Among the eight letters, the most important one is Ilil (日干) — the upper letter of the 'day' of birth, which means 'myself' in the fortune telling.

In other words, looking at fortune telling means reading the arrangement of the yin-yang and five elements at the moment of birth and interpreting the flow of one's temperament, relationship, and period.**

② Yin-Yang and Five Elements — The operating system of fortune telling

The foundation of the interpretation of fortune telling is Yin-Yang and Five Elements.

  • Yin Yang (陰陽): Everything in the world is seen as a balance of two energies, yin and yang.
  • Five elements: Five energies: wood, fire, earth, metal, and water. They operate in a relationship of saving (coexistence) and suppressing (opposition) each other.
  • The key to interpretation is reading how and to what extent the eight letters of the fortune teller contain the five elements and how they interact with each other. If you do not know Yin-Yang and the Five Elements, interpreting fortune telling itself is impossible.

This yin-yang and five elements are the basic grammar of Korean and East Asian thinking, which penetrates not only the fortune telling, but also the five directions of oriental medicine, feng shui, and Korean food.

③ History — Tradition treated as ‘study’ rather than superstition

Four Pillars of Myeongri Studies has been treated as a discipline studied and systematized by scholars for a long time in East Asia. Unlike shamans who discuss luck by borrowing supernatural power, Myungri is analysis and interpretation based on the theory of yin-yang and five elements. Even today, there are Myeongri Studies classes in universities and lifelong education centers, and there is even a trend of using Myeongri counseling as psychological counseling.

Note: Fortune telling is not unique to Korea, but is a common tradition in East Asia such as China and Japan (eight letters in Chinese), but the form of fortune telling and popularization through 'Saju Cafe' is a characteristic of Korea.

⑥ Why is it popular ‘now’ — MZ and Saju Cafe

As the traditional fortune telling house was reborn as saju cafe, saju became a part of the MZ generation's daily life and play culture.

  • Saju Cafe = Clean cafe space + reservation system + tea/drinks + counselor who has studied Myeongnihak for many years. Light and trendy instead of the heaviness of old fortune telling stores.
  • K-Content Effect: The number of dramas, entertainment shows, and idols watching fortune telling, compatibility, and tarot is increasing, and the number of foreign fans saying “I want to try it too” has increased significantly (e.g., overseas fans who got started through idol fortune telling videos). It is reported that foreign reservations are made in the following order: USA, Singapore, Canada, and Australia.
  • App/Online: Calculating power app and online fortune-telling service are also popular. The market size also grew significantly.

⑦ Foreigner Experience Guide — Where, How Much, and How

Prices and operations are estimated as of 2026 and vary by store — recommended to check before visiting.

  • Where: Hongdae, Insa-dong, Myeong-dong, Itaewon are crowded with fortune telling and tarot cafes. Stores in tourist attractions display signs in English, Japanese, and Chinese, and have English-speaking consultants or interpreters/translation tools. There are many English sessions, especially in Hongdae and Insa-dong.
  • Cost: Basic 30-40 minute session ₩30,000-50,000 (approximately $22-38). Light taro/sajui including drinks range from $15~30.
  • Reservation: Popular stores/English consultation Advance reservation recommended (Klook, KKday, Naver or store Instagram). Check in advance whether English is available.
  • Materials: Accurate date of birth + time of birth is required (the 'Time' pillar of the fortune telling). If you don't know it, it is an abbreviated form of fortune telling, and can be replaced with tarot.
  • Manner: A respectful attitude through serious academic consultation is good. Rather than receiving the result as a “predetermined fate,” use it for reference/fun.

❓ Foreigners Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

  • If you don't know the time of birth, can't you see the fortune telling? — If the time column is omitted, the accuracy of the interpretation decreases, but it can be abbreviated with just the year, month, and day. If you don't know the poem, you can substitute it with Tarot.
  • Can I view it in English? — Some stores in Hongdae and Insa-dong provide English consultants or interpreter/translation tools. Make sure to check “English OK?” when making a reservation. Tarot is easier to learn in English than Saju.
  • Should we trust the results? — Most Koreans also accept it as self-understanding/reference rather than an “absolute prophecy.” No need to be swayed by negative outcomes — with fun and insight.
  • Saju Cafe vs. Street fortune teller, which is better? — For foreigners, reservation-based Saju Cafe is more convenient (neat, calm, language friendly, clear prices). Traditional fortune telling houses have a deep atmosphere, but the language barrier is significant.
  • Can I watch it even if I have a religion? — Saju is considered not a religion, but oriental philosophy and traditional culture. There are many Koreans who enjoy light cultural experiences.
