⚠ 3 Common Mistakes International Visitors Make

1. Treating Jongno as "palaces and Korean food only" — skipping cafes

The most common mistake on the foreign visitor route. Most travelers see Jongno only as a palace-and-heritage district and miss 19+ cafes in Anguk (안국), Ikseon-dong (익선동), and the Jongno (종로) corridor. In reality, Jongno is the #1 area for Korean cafe culture — hanok (한옥; traditional Korean wooden house) cafes, gallery cafes, modern signature cafes, and bakery cafes are all clustered along a single walkable route. Palace + 1–2 hours at a cafe = the most quintessential Jongno itinerary for international visitors.

2. Assuming Anguk, Ikseon-dong, and Bukchon are separate, inconvenient neighborhoods

Many foreign visitors only go to Gyeongbokgung (경복궁) and look for cafes in Gangnam (강남) or Hongdae (홍대) — but Anguk Station (안국역) + Ikseon-dong (익선동) + Bukchon (북촌) = the #1 cafe route. Over 10 cafes are within a 10-minute walk of Anguk Station. A 30-minute stroll through the Ikseon-dong hanok alley followed by one cafe visit is the best possible combination. No need to go to Gangnam or Hongdae — Jongno has it all.

3. Not checking operating hours and closed days

Popular spots like Onion Anguk (어니언 안국), Abu Coffee (아부커피), and Seoul Coffee Ikseon (서울커피 익선본점) typically have 1–2 hour waits on weekends. Weekday 14:00–17:00 (2–5 PM) is the sweet spot — mornings (while palaces are open) or after 17:00 (5 PM) also work. Some venues are closed Monday and Tuesday — check Instagram or Naver Maps in advance. Hanok cafes have limited seating, so weekday visits are strongly preferred.

The Jongno Cafe Scene: Anguk, Bukchon & Ikseon-dong

Since the mid-2010s, the Jongno (종로) area has established itself as the #1 zone for Korean cafe culture. Where Gangnam (강남) leans trendy-modern, Hongdae (홍대) leans indie-trendy, and Seongsu (성수) leans concept-store-trendy, Jongno stands apart with its hanok (한옥; traditional wooden house), gallery, and quietly atmospheric character.

Anguk (안국) is the top cafe neighborhood in Jongno — over 10 hanok, modern, and bakery cafes are clustered within a 5–10 minute walk of Anguk Station (안국역). Onion Anguk (어니언 안국), Abu Coffee (아부커피), and Amor Napoli Anguk (아모르나폴리 안국점) are the most popular venues for international visitors and Instagram shots.

Ikseon-dong (익선동) is defined by its hanok alley (골목; narrow traditional Korean alleyway) cafes — multiple venues inside the Ikseon-dong hanok village have been renovated from original hanok into cafes. Seoul Coffee Ikseon (서울커피 익선본점), Onjisim (온지심), and others set the standard for hanok atmosphere. This is the top zone for foreign photo moments.

Bukchon (북촌) combines hanok with gallery cafes — Yeongyeongdang (연경당) and other atmospheric venues sit among the hanok village and gallery alleys. A gallery walk + cafe stop is the best Bukchon combination.

Central Jongno (종로) hosts modern and signature cafes — the Jongak (종각) and Jongno 3-ga (종로3가) corridors feature trendy modern spots like Rain Report Rainbow (레인리포트 레인보우), Burnt Seoul (번트서울), and Soha Yeomjeon (소하염전).

⚙ Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

Q1. Which venues have English menus and accept international cards?

A. Onion Anguk (어니언 안국), Abu Coffee (아부커피), Amor Napoli Anguk (아모르나폴리 안국점), Seoul Coffee Ikseon (서울커피 익선본점), Rain Report Rainbow (레인리포트 레인보우), Apipore (아삐뽀레), and Dotori Garden Anguk (도토리가든 안국점) — all 7 venues offer English menus and accept international cards. Since Jongno is the #1 foreign visitor route, most cafes here can accommodate English-speaking guests. Checking Instagram in advance is still recommended.

Q2. What are the opening hours and closed days?

A. Typical hours: Weekdays 10:00–21:00 (10 AM–9 PM), weekends 09:00–22:00 (9 AM–10 PM). Closed days: Some venues close Monday and Tuesday (confirm via Instagram). Best time to visit: Weekdays 14:00–17:00 (2–5 PM) for the quietest experience. Weekend 11:00–14:00 (11 AM–2 PM) commonly sees 1–2 hour waits.

Q3. What are the top foreign Instagram hotspots?

A. Hanok cafes: Onion Anguk (어니언 안국), Seoul Coffee Ikseon (서울커피 익선본점), Onjisim (온지심), Yeongyeongdang (연경당) — these 4 venues produce the most-shared hanok photo moments.

Modern & art: Rain Report Rainbow (레인리포트 레인보우) (★4.92), Abu Coffee (아부커피) (signal 70), Apipore (아삐뽀레).

Garden atmosphere: Dotori Garden Anguk (도토리가든 안국점).

Q4. Weekday vs. weekend — which is better?

A. Weekday (Wed–Fri) is best: 14:00–17:00 (2–5 PM) is quiet, venues are open, and photos turn out great. Weekends: 11:00–14:00 (11 AM–2 PM) draws heavy Korean local traffic — expect 1–2 hour waits. Monday and Tuesday: Some venues are closed. Weekdays strongly recommended.

Q5. How do I combine palace sightseeing with cafe visits?

A. Gyeongbokgung (경복궁) Route: 09:00 Gyeongbokgung → 11:00 Onion Anguk (어니언 안국) morning visit → 12:30 Abu Coffee (아부커피) signature → 14:00 Bukchon (북촌) → 15:30 Yeongyeongdang (연경당) rest stop.

Changdeokgung (창덕궁) Route: 09:00 Changdeokgung → 11:00 Ikseon-dong (익선동) → 12:00 Seoul Coffee Ikseon (서울커피 익선본점) → 14:00 Jongno (종로) stroll.

Bukchon (북촌) Route: 10:00 Bukchon Hanok Village (북촌 한옥마을) → 12:00 Yeongyeongdang (연경당) → 14:00 Amor Napoli Anguk (아모르나폴리 안국점).

Q6. Recommendations by price range?

A. ₩5,000–10,000 (~$4–7 / ~28–55 RMB / ~480–960 JPY) — lighter drinks: Onion Anguk (어니언 안국), Seoul Coffee Ikseon (서울커피 익선본점), Dotori Garden Anguk (도토리가든 안국점), Amor Napoli Anguk (아모르나폴리 안국점).

₩10,000–15,000 (~$7–11 / ~55–82 RMB / ~960–1,450 JPY) — signature + dessert: Abu Coffee (아부커피), Onjisim (온지심), Rain Report Rainbow (레인리포트 레인보우), Apipore (아삐뽀레), Soha Yeomjeon (소하염전).

Q7. Which venues are best for groups?

A. Groups of 4–6: Dotori Garden Anguk (도토리가든 안국점), Amor Napoli Anguk (아모르나폴리 안국점), Yeongyeongdang (연경당) — ample seating.

2–3 people (most venues): Onion Anguk (어니언 안국), Abu Coffee (아부커피), Seoul Coffee Ikseon (서울커피 익선본점) — primarily smaller seating configurations.

Hanok cafes have limited seating — weekday visits strongly preferred.

Q8. Where can I try Korean-style cafe drinks and desserts?

A. Korean-style drinks: Yeongyeongdang (연경당) (traditional Korean tea) · Soha Yeomjeon (소하염전) (Korean signature drinks).

Korean-style desserts: Cafe Sudal (카페 수달) · Apipore (아삐뽀레) · Dotori Garden Anguk (도토리가든 안국점).

Korean specialty coffee: Abu Coffee (아부커피) · Seoul Coffee Ikseon (서울커피 익선본점).

🌸 5 Seasonal & Time-of-Day Tips

Spring (March–May): Bukchon (북촌) + Yeongyeongdang (연경당) + Onion Anguk (어니언 안국) — hanok + cherry blossom + hanok cafe combo. Weekday mornings in April are the best window.

Summer (June–August): Cool cafe interiors + Abu Coffee (아부커피) + Dotori Garden Anguk (도토리가든 안국점) — Anguk cafe hop. Weekday 14:00–17:00 (2–5 PM) is the quietest slot during the hot months.

Autumn (September–November): Ikseon-dong (익선동) + Seoul Coffee Ikseon (서울커피 익선본점) + Onjisim (온지심) — hanok + autumn foliage + cafe. Weekday mornings in October–November are golden.

Winter (December–February): Cozy indoor venues — Rain Report Rainbow (레인리포트 레인보우) + Apipore (아삐뽀레) + Cafe Sudal (카페 수달). Warm interiors are the #1 priority from December to February.

Weekday Golden Hours: Wednesday–Friday, 14:00–17:00 (2–5 PM) — venues are open, crowds are thin, and the light is perfect for photos. Avoid the weekend 1–2 hour waits.
