Korea Banchan (Side Dish) Guide — Refill Rules, 5 Baekban and Taxi-Driver Restaurants, 3 Markets That Sell Banchan, 4 Kimchi Experiences (Official Values, October 2026)

🎯 How to Use This Guide

You order one bowl of rice, small plates are set down one after another, and when you finish the kimchi plate a new one lands silently. This guide is about the banchan (small side dishes served with rice) themselves: how banchan arrive and get refilled, baekban (set-meal) houses and taxi-driver restaurants where banchan fill the table, markets where you buy banchan to take away, and kimchi. Hanjeongsik (full-course Korean meals), Michelin Korean food and temple food are passed on to the #478 Hanjeongsik Guide. Addresses and hours are only values read on VisitKorea, VisitSeoul and UNESCO. It is for people who want to eat banchan properly at a neighborhood eatery instead of an expensive course meal.

The old guide lumped jonggajip (head-family home cooking), banga (noble house), royal court and temple food together as banchan, and wrote "Jingwansa temple food = National Intangible Cultural Property" and "Seonjae sunim (선재스님) appeared on Chef's Table". Both are wrong — what was designated is the Jingwansa Suryukjae (진관사 수륙재, a Buddhist ritual), and the person who appeared on Chef's Table is Jeong Gwan sunim (정관스님) of Baekyangsa (백양사) (the basis for the correction is in #478). Prices such as "Tongin Market lunchbox ₩5,000 (~$3.75)" were also removed because there is no official source.

🍚 How Banchan Arrive — Refill Rules

  • Free refills, no tipping: Korean restaurants do not need tips, and asking for more water or banchan adds no charge (VisitKorea)
  • Self-serve banchan: Self-service, where you fetch your own water and banchan, and restaurants using unmanned ordering kiosks are increasing (VisitKorea)
  • Calling staff: When you want to order more or ask something, call out "Yeogiyo!" ("Over here!") (VisitKorea)
  • For why so many banchan come out, see #1132 Korean Banchan Culture

🥢 Baekban and Taxi-Driver Restaurants — Neighborhood Eateries Where Banchan Fill the Table

  • Gamnamujip Gisasikdang (감나무집기사식당): 25 Yeonnam-ro, Mapo-gu, Seoul. Known for pork bulgogi, with generous banchan. 24 hours, open year-round. 02-325-8727. Close to Gyeongui Line Forest Park (VisitKorea)
  • Bogwang Gisasikdang (보광기사식당): 97 Jangmun-ro 6-gil, Yongsan-gu, Seoul (Dongbinggo-dong). Pork baekban and jeyuk-bokkeum (spicy stir-fried pork). Single orders accepted, water, rice and banchan are self-serve, and leaving food incurs an extra charge. 08:00-22:00, closed Sundays. 0507-1402-8579 (VisitKorea)
  • Dolsan Baekban (돌산백반): 13 Gangnam 8-gil, Yeosu-si, Jeonnam. Dolsan Baekban has 9 different banchan every day, and grilled fish starts at 2 servings. 06:30-20:00 (break 15:00-17:00), closed Sundays. 0507-1333-7374 (VisitKorea)
  • Illo Jangteo Baekban (일로장터백반): 17-12 Sijang-gil, Muan-gun, Jeonnam. One menu item, baekban — Jeolla-style banchan such as jangajji (pickles) and pa-kimchi (green onion kimchi). 09:30-16:00, closed Mondays. 061-282-8074 (VisitKorea)
  • Banchan Sikdang (반찬식당): 484 Bomunsan Park-ro, Jung-gu, Daejeon. Specializes in boribap (barley rice), with vegetables, namul (seasoned greens), kimchi and stew on one table. 07:00-20:00 (weekday break 14:30-16:00), closed Mondays, cards accepted. 042-253-2794 (VisitKorea)
  • None of the five posts prices

🛒 Markets That Sell Banchan — Banchan to Take Away

  • Tongin Market (통인시장): 18 Jahamun-ro 15-gil, Jongno-gu, Seoul. Of about 70 stalls, food businesses such as restaurants and banchan shops are the most numerous. It has run the lunchbox cafe "Tong" (통) since 2012. Stalls 07:00-21:00, lunchbox cafe 11:00-15:00, closing days vary by stall. 0507-1378-0940 (VisitKorea). For the yeopjeon (brass coin) lunchbox route, see the #589 Tongin Market Guide
  • Yeonseo Market (연서시장): 850 Tongil-ro, Eunpyeong-gu, Seoul (near Yeonsinnae Station). Sells produce, seafood, meat and banchan. Hours and closing days vary by stall. 02-355-1272 (VisitKorea)
  • Gangneung Dongbu Market (강릉 동부시장): 48 Okcheon-ro, Gangneung-si. Sells banchan ingredients such as food for ancestral rites, plus jeotgal and kimchi. 06:00-23:00 (varies by stall), cards accepted, 1 hour of free parking. 033-647-7999 (VisitKorea)

🥬 Kimchi — Museums and Kimchi-Making Classes

  • Kimjang: Kimchi is a preserved vegetable dish mixed with seasoning and jeotgal. Kimjang (making and sharing kimchi) was inscribed on the UNESCO Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity in 2013 (UNESCO)
  • Museum Kimchikan (뮤지엄 김치간): Insadong-maru main building, floors 4-6, 35-4 Insadong-gil, Jongno-gu, Seoul. 10:00-18:00 (last entry 17:30), closed Mondays, January 1, the Seollal and Chuseok holidays, and December 25. Paid classes "It's Kimchi", "Kimchi Basic" and "Kimchi Master", and a free Kimchi School, advance booking required, ages 6 and up. 02-2088-8531 (VisitKorea). Check fees on the official site kimchikan.com
  • Kimchi School Busan Branch (김치스쿨 부산지점): 2F, 15 Dongbusan Gwanggwang 9-ro, Gijang-eup, Gijang-gun, Busan. Kimchi-making class and sales. 09:30-16:00, cards accepted (VisitKorea)
  • Jeju Kimchi School (제주김치스쿨): 136 Seohaean-ro, Jeju-si. Kimchi-making class and sales. 08:00-17:00, cards accepted. 064-712-9688 (VisitKorea)
  • Haenam Kimchi Village (해남김치마을): 97 Donghae-gil, Bukpyeong-myeon, Haenam-gun, Jeonnam. A village with seasonal experiences, open at all times and year-round. 0507-1326-1743 (VisitKorea)
  • For kimjang season information, see #751 Kimjang Kimchi Experience

🥗 Dietary Notes

  • Price range: This guide has no officially posted won prices. The price field is empty for the baekban houses, markets and kimchi classes
  • Vegetarian: Kimchi is mixed with jeotgal (UNESCO) — ask before ordering whether banchan kimchi, namul and jangajji contain jeotgal or meat stock. For the meat-free side, use the show-it card in the #1300 Dietary Request Manual
  • Halal: VisitSeoul lists Eid (이드) in Itaewon (15 Usadan-ro 10-gil, Yongsan-gu; daily 12:00-21:00) as one of the few restaurants recognized as halal by the Korea Muslim Federation (KMF). Certification can change, so check before you go. The baekban houses in this guide carry no halal label, and two of them are known for pork bulgogi
  • Allergies: Jeotgal (seafood such as shrimp and anchovy) is used in kimchi (UNESCO). Tell staff in advance about a crustacean allergy

🧰 Banchan Practicalities — Solo, Booking, Payment, Ordering, Getting Around

  • Solo: Bogwang Gisasikdang accepts single orders (VisitKorea). Grilled fish at Dolsan Baekban starts at 2 servings. For restaurants that are comfortable to sit in alone, see #348 Seoul Solo Diner Map
  • Booking: The baekban houses post no booking information. Museum Kimchikan classes require advance booking (VisitKorea)
  • Payment: Banchan Sikdang, Gangneung Dongbu Market and the two Kimchi Schools accept cards (VisitKorea) — for the rest, see #527 Korean Payment Systems
  • Ordering: To get more banchan, call "Yeogiyo!" (VisitKorea). When language gets in the way, see #1339 Ordering Etiquette
  • Getting around: For Yeosu, Muan and Daejeon, see #1297 Express and Intercity Bus Manual; for Seoul, see #1298 Choosing Among 3 Transit Cards

⚠ 4 Common Mistakes

1. "Asking for more banchan costs extra" — extra water and banchan are free (VisitKorea)

2. "Leaving food is fine" — Bogwang Gisasikdang charges extra if you leave food (VisitKorea). With self-serve banchan, take only what you will eat

3. "Leaving a tip" — there is no tipping in Korean restaurants (VisitKorea)

4. "Visiting the kimchi museum on Monday" — Museum Kimchikan is closed on Mondays (VisitKorea)
