Seoul N-S Axis ③ Yangjae (양재), in the Morning — the 0km Point of Gangnam-daero (강남대로), Starting at a Forest and a Flower Market

Subtitle: Gangnam-daero begins at Yeomgok junction. Few travelers know that right beside that 0km point sit, side by side, Seoul's largest wholesale flower market and a downtown forest. This piece is a roughly ninety-minute morning course: get off at Yangjae Citizens' Forest Station, walk through Maeheon Citizens' Forest, pass the memorial hall, cross through the aT Center and the Yangjae Flower Market, and board the first stop of city bus 140. The southern end of Seoul's north-south axis. Seoul N-S Axis, Part 3.

ISSUE · #1355 · 2026-09-07

🎯 The Frame — A Forest and Flowers Where the Road Begins

Gangnam-daero, running north-south through Gangnam, starts at Yeomgok junction (염곡사거리) in Yeomgok-dong, Seocho-gu, and runs 6.9km to the south end of Hannam Bridge (한남대교). Most travelers know only the middle of this road — Gangnam Station. The southern end is different: a wholesale flower market sits right on the avenue, and behind it spreads a flat forest, rare for downtown. It's the stretch with soil and flowers, just before Gangnam's glass towers begin.

This piece walks that southern end in the morning. For the flower market, the hours after the pre-dawn wholesale trading ends and the retail section opens are the right time for a traveler, and the forest has few people around. And at the end of the course sits Line 140's terminus stop — board there and the full traverse from #1353 starts from an empty seat.

  • Total time: about 1 hour 30 minutes (about 30 minutes walking + time in the forest and market)
  • Best time: depart between 9 and 10 AM — timed to the memorial hall's 10 AM opening, the forest → memorial hall → market order flows naturally. Avoid Sunday, when the flower market is closed
  • Incline: none — flat ground the whole way. Fine for strollers or a suitcase too

Why Yangjae — Because It's Where the Axis Starts

On its own, Yangjae has long been a "weak topic." A Yangjaecheon stream (양재천) walk (#43, #697) and a flower-market vendor list (#615) already exist. This piece is different for one reason — it's the southern end of Seoul's north-south axis. Gangnam-daero's 0km point, Line 140's terminus, and the Sinbundang Line's southernmost Gangnam-side station all sit within a 700-meter radius. To read Seoul vertically along the axis, you have to start here.

📖 Story 1 — A Forest That Changed Its Name: The station is still named "Yangjae Citizens' Forest Station (양재시민의숲역)," but the forest's official name changed to Maeheon Citizens' Forest (매헌시민의숲) in October 2022. Maeheon (梅軒) is the pen name of independence activist Yun Bong-gil, who threw a bomb at Japanese military officials at Hongkou Park (훙커우 공원) in Shanghai in 1932. His memorial hall stands inside the forest, and the road out front is also named Maeheon-ro (매헌로). The change went through after 78.6% of citizens agreed in a Seoul city survey to unify the naming — worth a moment's thought for the weight behind that name as you walk the forest.

🚶 The Route — In Walking Order

The starting point is Yangjae Citizens' Forest Station on the Sinbundang Line. Two stops from Gangnam Station on the Sinbundang Line.

① Yangjae Citizens' Forest Station Exit 5 → Maeheon Citizens' Forest (🚶 about 5 minutes)

  • Take Exit 5 and follow Maeheon-ro, and the forest entrance appears shortly. A park of zelkova, pine, and maple trees planted widely across flat ground — the trunks are thick for a park inside Seoul.
  • In the morning, the forest is just local joggers and dog walks. Loop the walking path once (about 20 minutes), or, if you're short on time, cut straight across toward the memorial hall.

② Forest → Maeheon Yun Bong-gil Memorial Hall (매헌 윤봉길 의사 기념관) (🚶 about 5 minutes)

  • The memorial hall sits inside the forest. Opens at 10 AM (summer hours, April–October, 10–18; winter hours, November–March, 10–17; last entry 30 minutes before closing), closed Mondays. Free.
  • Thirty minutes covers the exhibit. It lays out Yun Bong-gil's life and the 1932 Hongkou incident on a fact-by-fact basis. For a foreign visitor, it's a rare space that compresses one scene of Korea's independence movement into a single stop.

③ Memorial Hall → Toward Exit 4 → aT Center (aT센터) (🚶 about 10 minutes)

  • Leave the forest, head back toward the station, and come out onto Gangnam-daero via Exit 4. Across the avenue is the aT Center — an exhibition and convention building the Korea Agro-Fisheries & Food Trade Corporation (aT) opened in November 2002. Gangnam-daero 27, the first address block on this road.
  • 📖 Story 2 — Gangnam-daero's 0km: A little further south from here is Yeomgok junction, Gangnam-daero's starting point. After Hannam Bridge went up in 1969, the road running south of the bridge became Yeongdong 1-ro in 1972, then took the name Gangnam-daero in 1976. That a flower wholesale market and an agricultural-and-fisheries exhibition hall sit where the road ends — or begins — is no accident: it's the typical layout, distribution functions placed at a city's southern edge.

④ aT Center → Yangjae Flower Market (양재꽃시장) (🚶 about 5 minutes)

  • Next to the aT Center is the Yangjae Flower Market (also called the aT flower wholesale market, or F Square). Pre-dawn hours run on wholesale auctions and vendor trading; in the morning, the retail section opens to travelers. It's split into cut-flower, potted-plant, and supply-goods buildings. Hours vary by building and change from time to time, so check aT's official notice before visiting. Closed Sundays.
  • Worth walking even with nothing to buy — the sight of a season's flowers all out at once is the draw. Individual vendor and item guides are in #615. This piece just passes through.

⑤ Flower Market → AT Center·Yangjae Flower Market Stop (AT센터.양재꽃시장) (🚶 about 5 minutes) — Boarding Line 140

  • The stop on Gangnam-daero in front of the market, "AT Center·Yangjae Flower Market," is Line 140's terminus and starting point. Board a bus headed toward Dobongsan and it runs straight through, Gangnam-daero → Hannam Bridge → Namsan 1st Tunnel (남산1호터널) → Jongno.
  • From here the baton passes to #1353's full traverse. Morning forest → flower market → bus traverse → lunch in Jongno fills out a half-day perfectly.
  • If you want to walk Yangjaecheon stream, see #43 and #697 instead of this course — Yangjaecheon isn't covered in this piece.

🙏 Manners — the Core of This Course

  • The flower market's real customers are florists and flower-shop buyers. Wholesale trading still lingers into the morning — don't photograph a counter mid-negotiation, and give way to hand carts and forklifts.
  • If you're just looking, don't step inside the display stalls. The flowers are merchandise — don't touch.
  • The forest is where locals do their morning exercise. Don't hold up the middle of the walking path for a long photo session.
  • The memorial hall is a place of remembrance. Keep quiet, and follow posted signs on whether photographing the exhibits is allowed.

💡 Where You Might Get Thrown Off

  • On Sunday, the course is only half itself — the flower market is closed. Monday, the memorial hall is closed. Tuesday–Saturday mornings are the right answer.
  • The exit number matters: Exit 5 for the forest and memorial hall, Exit 4 for the aT Center, flower market, and Line 140 stop. Take the wrong one and you'll have to cross the avenue.
  • Flower market hours differ by building: the wholesale building wraps up before dawn, and the retail building opens in the morning. Come in the morning expecting the "pre-dawn flower market" scene and you won't find it — this piece is built around retail hours.
  • Bringing luggage is fine, but if you plan to carry it onto the 140 bus, avoid rush hour (see #1353).

6 FAQs

Q1. How far is it from Gangnam Station?

  • Two stops on the Sinbundang Line, about 5 minutes. A good distance for a morning trip from a Gangnam hotel.

Q2. Can I buy flowers? Even as a foreigner?

  • Anyone can buy from the retail building. That said, import rules for fresh flowers vary by country when you fly home, so dried flowers or floral supplies are the safer souvenir choice. See #615 for vendor and item detail.

Q3. Can I watch the pre-dawn auction?

  • The wholesale auction is for vendors, so this piece doesn't recommend it. This course is built around the morning retail hours.

Q4. What happened to Yangjaecheon?

  • This piece covers only the axis's starting point — the forest, memorial hall, aT Center, flower market, and bus terminus. Yangjaecheon has its own pieces at #43 and #697 — from the forest, it's about a 15-minute walk on to Yangjaecheon if you want to continue.

Q5. Can I bring kids?

  • Sure. Flat ground the whole way, the forest has lawns and a play area, and the memorial hall is free. Just watch for hand carts at the flower market.

Q6. What comes after this?

  • Board Line 140 at the stop for #1353's full traverse. Or head back on the Sinbundang Line to Gangnam Station (two stops) or Sinsa Station.
