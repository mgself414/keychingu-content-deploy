Alley Walk ② Haebangchon (해방촌) — Climb the 108 Steps (108계단) and Find a Different Seoul

Subtitle: This is Haebangchon, the hillside neighborhood next to Itaewon, written as a walking sequence. Starting at Noksapyeong Station, the route runs 108 Steps (with an inclined lift) → Sinheung Market → the neighborhood's hottest café alley right now, "Araetmaeul" → a viewpoint on Namsan's Sowol-ro, in about 2 hours. Why is it called "Haebangchon"? Why did the market knit sweaters? A walk that carries the alley's backstory with it. Seoul Alley Walks Pilot 2.

ISSUE · #1341 · 2026-09-02

🎯 The Frame — Even the Name Is History

The name Haebangchon — literally "Liberation Village" — comes from 1945, right after liberation, when people who had come down from the north, returnees from abroad, and refugees from the Korean War gathered at the foot of Namsan (남산) and formed a village. This hillside neighborhood later made its living from the knitwear industry, and today it's one of the most multinational alleys in Seoul. If the existing guide (#79 Itaewon, Hannam & Haebangchon in a Day) answered "where should I go," this one answers "in what order should I walk uphill?"

  • Total time: about 2 hours (2.5 hours with a café break) · Best time: start at 4 PM, finish at dusk on Sowol-ro (소월길). For something more romantic, walk it in reverse — start on Sowol-ro and descend into the market lit up at night
  • Incline: steep — this route is basically one long climb. Comfortable shoes, and water in summer.

🚶 The Route — In Walking Order

① Noksapyeong Station (녹사평역) Exit 2 → Haebangchon Ogeori (해방촌 오거리) (about a 10-minute walk, gentle uphill)

  • The noise of Itaewon (이태원) fades and the texture of the hillside neighborhood begins. The Ogeori is this neighborhood's belly button — a small plaza where five alleys meet.

② Ogeori → the 108 Steps (about a 5-minute walk)

  • 📖 Story 1 — What the Stairs Really Are: This steep 108-step stairway is literally the staircase that once climbed to a Japanese colonial-era Shinto shrine, now repurposed as an alley. In 2018, an inclined lift was installed for residents — the small car that glides up and down a rail beside the steps has become a local landmark in its own right. Take the lift if your knees worry you, the steps if your legs are willing.

③ Top of the 108 Steps → Sinheung Market (신흥시장) (about a 5-minute walk)

  • A market of narrow passageways under an old tin roof — quiet by day, its small shops lighting up in the evening.
  • 📖 Story 2 — The Knitwear Market: Sinheung Market grew alongside the sewing and knitwear industry that was refugees' main livelihood. As knitwear, Haebangchon's core industry, declined, the market faded with it — but urban regeneration has since filled the old structures with cafés, bars, and small shops. It's regeneration layered on top of the old, not a replacement of it, which is why the alley's texture still feels alive.

④ Sinheung Market → Araetmaeul (아랫마을) Alley (about a 10-minute walk, downhill)

  • This is the "Araetmaeul" — literally "lower village" — that let you skip the climb all the way up to Sinheung Market. It's currently the liveliest café-and-bakery alley in Haebangchon. Halfway up the hill, many spots frame Namsan Tower (남산타워) right through the window.
  • Shop names here turn over constantly — walk the alley itself rather than chasing a specific storefront. If you spot a window you like, that's your café for the day.

⑤ Araetmaeul → Sowol-ro Viewpoint → Finish (about a 15-minute walk, uphill)

  • Reach Sowol-ro, which wraps around Namsan's waist, and the view opens toward Yongsan (용산). At dusk, this is the closing shot of the walk.
  • Ways to end it: extend the walk further along Sowol-ro through Namsan, or head down for dinner in Itaewon or Gyeongnidan (경리단) (#79).

🙏 Alley Manners — Enhanced Edition

  • Haebangchon is a dense residential neighborhood. Frame alley photos so they don't capture people or the insides of homes, and keep conversations quiet — especially after early evening.
  • The lift on the 108 Steps exists for residents first. If an elderly resident is waiting, let them go ahead.
  • Carry all your trash back out with you (#1276) — it's normal for these alleys to have no trash cans.

💡 Where You Might Get Thrown Off

  • Map-app walking directions underestimate stairs and inclines — add about 20% extra time to whatever it quotes for the uphill stretches.
  • Sinheung Market can look "closed" during the day — it's an evening kind of alley. If you're walking this in daytime, treat the market as something to look at structurally, and save your spending for Araetmaeul.
  • Some stretches are poorly lit on the way down at night — after dark, stick to the main road, Sinheung-ro (신흥로).
