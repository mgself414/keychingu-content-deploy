🎯 How to Use This Guide

Each museum has its opening hours, closed days, and fees registered, and many are registered as free.

The operating details are copied from values registered in the Korea Tourism Organization tourism data (TourAPI). This guide covers only places registered in the Korea Tourism Organization tourism data.

📍 4 National Museum of Modern and Contemporary Art (MMCA) Branches — Seoul, Gwacheon, Deoksugung, Cheongju

National Museum of Modern and Contemporary Art (MMCA) Seoul (국립현대미술관 서울) (30 Samcheong-ro, Jongno-gu (종로구), ☎ 02-3701-9500) opened in 2013 and focuses on contemporary art. It stands on the site of the Joseon-era Sogyeokseo (소격서), Jongchinbu (종친부), Gyujanggak (규장각), and Saganwon (사간원), and was built around the concept of the 'madang' (마당). It has exhibition halls, a digital information room, a multimedia hall, and a cinema.

  • Opening hours: 10:00~18:00, Wed and Sat 10:00~21:00. Tickets are issued until 1 hour before closing. Closed days: January 1, Seollal, Chuseok.
  • Fee: Permanent exhibition free. Time needed: 2 hours.
  • Parking: 3,000 won for the first 2 hours, 500 won per 30 minutes after that.

National Museum of Modern and Contemporary Art (MMCA) Gwacheon (국립현대미술관 과천) (313 Gwangmyeong-ro, Gwacheon-si (과천시), Makgye-dong (막계동), ☎ 02-2188-6000) opened in 1986. According to the Korea Tourism Organization, it has a building that blends with the scenery of Cheonggyesan (청계산) and an outdoor sculpture park of about 33,000㎡, and you can see works by leading Korean artists such as Nam June Paik (백남준), Lee Jung-seob (이중섭), and Chun Kyung-ja (천경자). At the center of the museum is a work by Nam June Paik made of 1,003 television sets, symbolizing Gaecheonjeol (개천절), October 3. A free art shuttle bus for visitors who want to see all 3 branches (Seoul, Gwacheon, Deoksugung) runs 4 times a day.

  • Opening hours: 10:00~18:00. Closed days: Every Monday, January 1.
  • Fee: 3,000 won. Free on the last Wednesday of each month (Culture Day), for ages 24 and under, ages 65 and over, and others (show ID or supporting documents).
  • Parking: 3,000 won for the first 2 hours, 1,000 won per 30 minutes after that.

National Museum of Modern and Contemporary Art (MMCA) Deoksugung (국립현대미술관 덕수궁) (99 Sejong-daero, Jung-gu (중구), ☎ 02-2022-0600) is a museum specializing in modern art inside Deoksugung Palace (덕수궁), using the west wing of Seokjojeon Hall (석조전), completed in 1938. It is about 70 m from Exit 1 of City Hall Station (시청역) (Lines 1 and 2).

  • Opening hours: Tue, Thu, Fri, Sun 10:00~18:00, Wed and Sat 10:00~21:00. Entry at the main gate of Deoksugung Palace closes at 20:00. Closed days: Every Monday, January 1.
  • Fee: Varies by exhibition, and the Deoksugung Palace admission fee is separate. Free during the Wed and Sat night opening 18:00~21:00 and on the last Wednesday of each month.

National Museum of Modern and Contemporary Art (MMCA) Cheongju (국립현대미술관 청주) (314 Sangdang-ro, Cheongwon-gu (청원구), Cheongju-si (청주시), ☎ 043-261-1400) opened in 2018 in a rebuilt former tobacco factory. According to the MMCA introduction in the Korea Tourism Organization tourism data, it has the first open storage in Korea, based on the museum's collection and the government and Art Bank collections, where you can see the collection in storage.

  • Opening hours: 10:00~18:00 (last entry 17:00). Closed days: Every Monday, January 1.
  • Fee: Floors 1~4 free, 5th-floor special exhibition hall 2,000 won. Free for ages 24 and under, ages 65 and over, and others.

📍 Seoul Museums — Leeum Museum of Art and Seoul Museum of Art (SeMA)

Leeum Museum of Art (리움미술관) (60-16 Itaewon-ro 55-gil, Yongsan-gu (용산구), Hannam-dong (한남동), ☎ 02-2014-6900) consists of MUSEUM 1, which displays traditional Korean art, and MUSEUM 2, which displays Korean and international modern and contemporary art. According to the Korea Tourism Organization, the architecture is the work of the world-famous architects Mario Botta, Jean Nouvel, and Rem Koolhaas, and you can see museum architecture where the individuality of three architects comes together on one site. It sits at the foot of Namsan (남산).

  • Opening hours: 10:00~18:00 (ticket sales end 17:30). Closed days: Every Monday, January 1, the day of Seollal and Chuseok.
  • Fee: Varies by exhibition.

Seoul Museum of Art (SeMA) Seosomun Main Building (서울시립미술관 서소문본관) (61 Deoksugung-gil, Jung-gu (중구), ☎ 02-2124-8800) is a museum along the Deoksugung Stone Wall Road (덕수궁 돌담길), rebuilt with only the front of the former Supreme Court building, built in the 1920s architectural style, preserved as it was. It has 6 exhibition halls, and on the 2nd floor are the permanent exhibition rooms, the Chun Kyung-ja Collection Room (천경자 컬렉션) and the Gana Art Collection Room (가나 아트 컬렉션). According to the Korea Tourism Organization, the bright interior with plenty of natural light also draws attention as a photo location.

  • Opening hours: Weekdays 10:00~20:00. Weekends and public holidays 10:00~19:00 from March to October, 10:00~18:00 from November to February. Entry until 1 hour before closing. Closed days: Every Monday (open as usual if it is a public holiday), January 1.
  • Fee: Free (special exhibitions paid).
  • Parking: 400 won per 5 minutes on weekdays, 300 won per 5 minutes on weekends and public holidays.

📍 Gyeonggi and Busan Museums — Nam June Paik Art Center and Museum of Contemporary Art Busan

Nam June Paik Art Center (백남준아트센터) (10 Baengnamjun-ro, Giheung-gu (기흥구), Yongin-si (용인시), ☎ 031-201-8500) is a Gyeonggi Province museum honoring the art world of the video artist Nam June Paik (1932~2006), and it opened in October 2008. It was designed jointly by the German architects Kirsten Schemel and Marina Stankovic. According to the Korea Tourism Organization, representative works such as "The First Portable TV," "Moon Is the Oldest TV," and "w3" are on display, and Paik defined the mission of this place as "a house where Paik lives long." Anyone can join the exhibition commentary of the regular docent program.

  • Opening hours: 10:00~18:00 (last entry 17:00). Closed days: Every Monday (except public holidays), January 1, the day of Seollal and Chuseok. If a Monday is a public holiday or substitute holiday, it closes on the first weekday after the holiday.
  • Fee: Free. Special exhibitions have different admission fees depending on the exhibition. Time needed: about 1 hour.
  • Parking: Cars 1,000 won for 1 hour (200 won more per 10 minutes), medium and large vehicles 2,000 won for 1 hour (400 won more per 10 minutes). Strollers: Available for rent.

Museum of Contemporary Art Busan (부산현대미술관) (1191 Nakdongnam-ro, Saha-gu (사하구), Busan, ☎ 051-220-7400) is a Busan Metropolitan City public museum on Eulsukdo (을숙도), Natural Monument No. 179, and it opened on June 16, 2018. According to the Korea Tourism Organization, it aims at the sustainability of environmental and ecological values and focuses on contemporary art in various media, including new media.

  • Opening hours: 10:00~18:00 (last entry 17:30). Closed days: Every Monday (closed the next day if it is a public holiday), January 1.
  • Fee: Free. Some exhibitions are paid.

📍 Biennale — 16th Gwangju Biennale

The 16th Gwangju Biennale (제16회 광주비엔날레) (Gwangju Biennale Exhibition Hall (광주비엔날레전시관), 111 Biennale-ro, Buk-gu (북구), Yongbong-dong (용봉동), ☎ 062-608-4500) is registered for September 5~November 15, 2026. According to the Korea Tourism Organization, the title "You Must Change Your Life" comes from the last line of a poem published by Rainer Maria Rilke in 1908.

  • Hours: 10:00~18:00. Visitor age: All ages.
  • Fee: Regular ticket 20,000 won, advance booking 17,000 won, Jeonnam-Gwangju Integration Special Discount 14,000 won.
  • Program: Gwangju Biennale Academy — daily from September 5 to 13, and every Saturday and Sunday from September 19 to November 15, twice a day (11:00 and 14:00). Not held on September 26 and 27 and October 3 and 4. The route is Asia Culture Center (ACC) → Eunam Museum of Art (은암미술관) → Jeonil Building 245 (전일빌딩245) → May 18 Democratization Movement Archives (5·18민주화운동기록관); book in advance on Naver Booking by searching '도시 기억의 변화'.

The Gwangju Biennale Exhibition Hall (광주비엔날레전시관) (☎ 062-608-4114) was founded in 1994. According to the Korea Tourism Organization, it serves as a gateway for Korean artists to go out into the world.

  • Opening hours: 10:00~18:00 (last entry 17:30). Closed days: Every Monday.
  • Fee: Free.

⚠ 3 Things to Know Before You Go

  1. Most close on Mondays — Except for MMCA Seoul, 8 places are registered as closed every Monday. MMCA Seoul closes only on January 1, Seollal, and Chuseok.
  2. There are night openings — MMCA Seoul and Deoksugung are open until 21:00 on Wednesdays and Saturdays, and Seoul Museum of Art (SeMA) is open until 20:00 on weekdays.
  3. Leeum Museum of Art and the Deoksugung branch have fees that vary by exhibition — There is no fixed fee in the Korea Tourism Organization data.

⚙ FAQ 2

Q. Which places can I see for free?

A. According to the Korea Tourism Organization registered data, MMCA Seoul (permanent exhibition) and Cheongju (floors 1~4), Seoul Museum of Art (SeMA), Nam June Paik Art Center, Museum of Contemporary Art Busan, and the Gwangju Biennale Exhibition Hall are free. Special and temporary exhibitions may be paid, and the regular ticket for the 16th Gwangju Biennale is listed separately at 20,000 won.

Q. Can I see several MMCA branches in one day?

A. According to the Korea Tourism Organization, a free art shuttle bus for visitors who want to see all 3 branches (Seoul, Gwacheon, Deoksugung) runs 4 times a day.
