Idol Birthday Cafes for Foreign Fans: How to Find a Saengka, Etiquette, and How to Read Subway Birthday Ads (Checked October 2026)

Subtitle: A birthday cafe is an unofficial event that fans put on by renting a cafe [secondary Korea Herald] · Limited giveaways go first come, first served [secondary Korea Herald] · Ads on Seoul Subway Lines 1-8 are applied for through an ad agency, then reviewed by the Korea Advertising Review Board (한국광고자율심의기구) (Seoul Metro (서울교통공사) official) · 12,433 idol ads ran on the Seoul subway in 2025 [secondary Dailian (데일리안)] — as of 2026-10-21

🎯 How to Use This Guide

You get your drink, and the cup comes wrapped in a paper cup sleeve (컵홀더) printed with an idol's face; a basket by the register is piled with postcards and stickers. That is a birthday cafe, or "saengka" (생카) for short. It is an event where fans rent or book an ordinary cafe to mark an idol's birthday or debut anniversary. It is not an official event run by the agency [secondary Korea Herald 2024-11-18].

This guide is for foreign fans who want to drop by a saengka (생카) while in Korea. Organizers, venues, and dates differ from event to event, and there is no official announcement, so we do not recommend any specific cafe. Instead, using official pages and press reports, we cover how to find a saengka (생카), the manners to follow, what to watch out for on site, and how the subway birthday ads you will see in stations that week get put up. Information from press or fan sources is tagged [secondary].

🎂 What a Saengka (생카) Is: An Event Fans Throw

  • Who runs it: Fans rent or book a cafe. Reportedly you can hold one even if you have no idea how to make merchandise, by ordering goods from an online vendor [secondary Korea Herald 2024-11-18]. Many are run by a "homma" (홈마, a fan photographer who photographs idols and posts the pictures), and one report says they sell photo goods to cover the rental fee [secondary JoongAng Daily 2021-02-02]
  • The cafe's side: Some cafes take bookings for free and some do not. One cafe owner said they charge no rental fee and run on sales of the drinks and desserts that guests buy [secondary Korea Herald]
  • What is there: The host fans make goods such as cup sleeves (컵홀더), keyrings, postcards, and stickers, and sell them on site. Limited-edition giveaways are handed out first come, first served [secondary Korea Herald]. What is sold, what is free, and how many drinks you must buy to get a giveaway all differ by event, so the organizer's post is the reference
  • How many are held: One report says about 50 birthday cafes opened for BTS's V (뷔) on his birthday in 2023, 30 of them in Seoul [secondary Korea Herald]. February and September are packed with idol birthdays, so venues are hard to book [secondary Korea Herald]
  • Does the idol show up: Go assuming they will not. It is a gathering where fans celebrate among themselves, without the birthday star [secondary JoongAng Daily]

🔍 How to Find One: Read the Organizer's Post

There is no official schedule. All the information comes from the host fans' accounts.

  • Search: On X and Instagram, take the artist's Korean or English name and add "saengka (생카)", "birthday cafe (생일카페)", "birthday cafe", or "cup sleeve event". As a birthday nears, fans pool information on who is holding what event at which cafe, and visit several in a day [secondary JoongAng Daily]
  • What to look for in the post: dates and hours, the cafe's name and address, drink-order conditions, the type and quantity of giveaways and any sold-out notice, whether goods are sold and how to pay, and how the line works. The cafe's own business hours can differ from the event hours, so check both
  • Organizing a map: Copy the cafe name from the post in Hangul exactly as written and save it in your map app so you get lost less
  • Saengka (생카) roundup services: Fan-made roundup accounts and map services exist, but we could not open and check them for this guide. Always recheck final details on the organizer's account

🙇 Etiquette: It Is a Working Cafe

  • Drinks first: A cafe may cover the event's costs with the drinks and desserts guests buy [secondary Korea Herald]. Do not just take photos and leave; place an order
  • Lines and order: Line up the way the organizer says. Do not block the cafe entrance or the doorway of the shop next door
  • Giveaways: They are first come, first served and can run out early [secondary Korea Herald]. Do not take it out on cafe staff when they are gone, because staff are not the organizer
  • Seats: Other fans need to get in too. If you plan to stay long, order more or give up your seat
  • Photos: Photo zones and decorations can often be photographed, but frame shots so other guests' faces are not in them. If the post has filming rules, follow them as written

🚇 Subway Birthday Ads: How They Get Put Up

During saengka (생카) week, the digital boards inside stations also carry birthday ads for the same idol. Fans make the rounds photographing public-transit birthday ads too [secondary JoongAng Daily].

  • Application structure: Seoul Metro (서울교통공사) runs ads on Lines 1-8 trains and in station premises through ad agencies chosen by tender. You apply to that ad agency, and the ad is posted after a design review by the outside body, the Korea Advertising Review Board (한국광고자율심의기구). The review takes about 3-5 days, excluding weekends and public holidays (Seoul Metro official)
  • No opinion ads: Reviews of opinion ads, which state the views of individuals or groups, have been suspended since October 21, 2022 (Seoul Metro official). We could not confirm on the Seoul Metro page which category a birthday greeting ad falls under
  • Scale: One report says 12,433 idol ads cleared review and ran on the Seoul subway in 2025, and 8,012 in January-August 2026. The same report says Jung Kook's (정국) birthday ads ran from August 26 to September 1 at 24 stations, on 54 digital ad panels [secondary Dailian (데일리안) 2026-09-25]
  • Viewing tip: Ad locations are also announced on the host fans' accounts. Stations are crowded at rush hour, so do not stand in the middle of a passage filming for long

🧰 Saengka (생카) Practicalities: Going Solo, Booking, Paying, Getting Around

  • Solo: Many fans go alone. Most events let you walk in without a reservation, but if there is a booking or an entry order, it is in the organizer's post
  • Payment: You pay the cafe for drinks, and some events have you pay the organizer separately for goods. Check the goods payment method (cash, bank transfer, and so on) in the post beforehand. For payment in Korea in general, see Foreign Visitors' Guide to Korean Payment Systems (deep dive)
  • Cups: The disposable-cup rules are changing. On November 7, 2023, the Ministry of Environment (환경부) removed disposable paper cups from the list of items restricted for in-store use and extended the grace period for plastic straws (Policy Briefing (정책브리핑) official). In December 2025 a report said a draft would bar giving out plastic disposable cups free and require a minimum charge of ₩100-200 (~$0.08-0.15) [secondary Seoul Shinmun (서울신문) 2025-12-17]. We could not confirm whether or when this takes effect; if a cafe charges separately for the cup, follow its notice
  • Getting around: If you plan to visit several saengka (생카) in a day, get a transit card first. Choosing a Korean Transit Card Without Panic: T-money, WOWPASS, or Climate Card?

⚠ 4 Common Mistakes

1. "It is an official event, so the agency announces it": A saengka (생카) is a fan-run event [secondary Korea Herald]. Check the host fans' account, not the agency's announcements

2. "If I go, I will see the idol": It is a gathering where fans celebrate among themselves, without the birthday star [secondary JoongAng Daily]

3. "Giveaways will still be there if I go late": Limited giveaways are first come, first served [secondary Korea Herald]. Check the sold-out notice before you go

4. "Any content can go up as a subway ad": Ads on Seoul Subway Lines 1-8 pass a design review by the Korea Advertising Review Board (한국광고자율심의기구) (Seoul Metro official)
