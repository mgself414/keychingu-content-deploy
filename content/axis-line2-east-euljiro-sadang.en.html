Seoul E-W Axis ④ Subway Line 2's Eastern Half, Euljiro to Sadang — Crossing the Han River on an Elevated Track, a Window-Seat Course

Subtitle: The north-south axis was bus 140, the east-west axis's first piece was bus 720. This piece has no bus — the only thing that links Euljiro to Seongsu and Konkuk University, crosses the Han River, and continues on through Jamsil, Samseong, Gangnam, Seoul Nat'l Univ. of Education, and Sadang in a single line is Subway Line 2. Seven stations run above ground from Hanyang University to Jamsillaru, crossing the Han River on the Jamsil Railway Bridge — this piece rides the eastern half of the circle line as a window-seat course. About 2 hours, with one stop at Konkuk University. Seoul E-W Axis, Part 4.

ISSUE · #1359 · 2026-09-07

🎯 The Frame — When There's No Bus, the Subway Is the Axis

Seoul's north-south axis (#1353) crossed Gangnam-daero on a single bus, and the east-west axis's first piece (#1356) crossed Jongno the same way. No bus links this piece's anchor points — Euljiro, Seongsu (성수), Konkuk University, Jamsil (잠실), Samseong, Gangnam, Seoul Nat'l Univ. of Education, and Sadang — in a single line. Instead there's Subway Line 2. The eastern half of the circle line that loops all the way around Seoul runs in exactly this order.

And the eastern half of Line 2 is the only stretch on the circle line that climbs above ground and crosses the Han River. Seven stations run above ground in a row from Hanyang University Station (한양대역) to Jamsillaru (잠실나루), and between Gangbyeon Station (강변역) and Jamsillaru, the train crosses the Han River on the Jamsil Railway Bridge (잠실철교). It's a rare stretch where the subway becomes a window-seat course. Seongsu, Jamsil, and Gangnam go by outside the window only. The one stop is at Konkuk University Station (건대입구역) — to point toward the two-stop Line 7 side trip to Gunja (군자), covered in #1360.

  • Total time: about 2 hours (about 45–50 minutes on the train, estimated + 15 minutes off at Konkuk University Station + buffer)
  • Best time: depart on a weekday at 10 AM — the morning rush ends and right-side window seats open up, reaching Sadang before noon. During evening rush (5–7 PM), Line 2's Gangnam stretch is the most crowded in Seoul
  • Incline: none — the only walking segment is 15 minutes on flat ground

Why Line 2's Eastern Half — The Only Place the Circle Line Crosses the Han River

Line 2 started on October 31, 1980 with the Sinseol-dong–Sports Complex Station (종합운동장역) stretch, then extended Sports Complex Station–Seoul Nat'l Univ. of Education in 1982, Euljiro 1-ga Station (을지로입구역)–Seongsu and Seoul Nat'l Univ. of Education–Seoul National University in 1983, and finally, on May 22, 1984, closed the loop with the Seoul National University–Euljiro 1-ga Station stretch, becoming a full circle line. One full loop takes about 1 hour 30 minutes. A train running clockwise is called the inner circle line (내선순환), and the reverse direction the outer circle line (외선순환) — this piece rides the inner circle line.

📖 Story 1 — Why Only This Stretch Runs Above Ground: When the circle line was built, the eastern side, which had to cross the Jungnangcheon (중랑천) and the Han River, was raised onto an elevated track instead of bored underground. That's why Hanyang University, Ttukseom (뚝섬), Seongsu, Konkuk University Station, Guui (구의), Gangbyeon, and Jamsillaru — seven stations in a row — sit above ground, and a six-lane road still runs beneath the elevated track between Seongsu and Guui today. The Jamsil Railway Bridge, between Gangbyeon and Jamsillaru, is Korea's first bridge to carry both a railway and a road together — the road completed in October 1979, the railway in October 1980. Since 2006, part of the road has been converted into a bike and pedestrian path, so people cross the Han River on this bridge too.

🚶 The Route — In the Order It Passes the Window

The starting point is Line 2's Euljiro 1-ga Station. Board at the inner circle line platform (clockwise, toward Seongsu and Jamsil). It's underground as far as Seongsu — the window view starts at Hanyang University Station.

① Euljiro 1-ga → Dongdaemun History & Culture Park (동대문역사문화공원) → Wangsimni → Hanyang University Station (🚇 about 12 minutes · underground → above ground)

  • Passes beneath the wholesale alleys of Euljiro 3-ga and 4-ga (#1346) and the DDP (#1252), then Sindang, Sangwangsimni, Wangsimni (왕십리). The train comes out above ground at Hanyang University Station — the platform toward Seongsu is elevated, so the window brightens from here.
  • The neighborhood guide for this above-ground stretch is covered by the #1250 Wangsimni hub. Here, it's only the moment the dark window turns bright.

② Ttukseom → Seongsu → Get Off at Konkuk University Station (🚇 about 3 minutes · elevated)

  • From the elevated track, Ttukseom and the direction of Seoul Forest appear on the left, and Seongsu-dong's red-brick rooftops (#1342) look down from the right. Seongsu Station is a pass-through — Seongsu has #001, #145, and #1342.
  • Get off at Konkuk University Station. The only stop on this piece.

③ Konkuk University Station Mini-Walk, 15 Minutes — and the Gunja Side Trip (🚶 about 15 minutes)

  • One loop around the station. Just a taste of the daytime alleys beneath the elevated track near Konkuk University — details on Konkuk University and Ttukseom are at #178.
  • Side trip: transfer to Line 7 at the same station, and two stops to Children's Grand Park Station and Gunja Station take you briefly off the axis, into a neighborhood of a free park and a gopchang (grilled beef intestine) alley. That morning course is #1360. To keep riding the axis, come back to Line 2's inner circle line.

④ Guui → Gangbyeon Station → Jamsil Railway Bridge → Jamsillaru (🚇 about 10 minutes · window climax)

  • To the right of Gangbyeon Station, the Dong Seoul Bus Terminal (동서울터미널), built in 1990, and Techno Mart (테크노마트). Once the train clears the station, the buildings disappear and the Han River opens up — you're on the Jamsil Railway Bridge. Upstream to the left, Gwangjin Bridge (광진교) and Achasan (아차산); downstream to the right, Olympic Bridge (올림픽대교); and ahead to the south across the river, Lotte World Tower (롯데월드타워), 555m and opened in 2017, comes into view.
  • 📖 Story 2 — A Subway on a Bridge: The minute-or-so the subway spends running over the river is this piece's peak. The north-south axis crosses the Han River by bus on Hannam Bridge; the east-west axis crosses it by train on the Jamsil Railway Bridge. You can also cross by the pedestrian path beside the bridge — the walking version is left to #1256 and #308.
  • Past Jamsillaru Station, the train goes back underground.

⑤ Jamsil → Jamsilsaenae → Sports Complex Station → Samseong Station (🚇 about 5 minutes · no stop)

  • Jamsil Station: right beneath Lotte World Tower. No stop — Jamsil has #82, #300, and #707. Jamsilsaenae (잠실새내): a station renamed from 'Sincheon' at the end of 2016. Past Sports Complex Station to Samseong Station (삼성역) — exits 5 and 6 lead directly into COEX (코엑스) (#366, #449). Also a pass-through.

⑥ Seolleung (선릉) → Yeoksam (역삼) → Gangnam Station (🚇 about 6 minutes · no stop)

  • Runs beneath Teheran-ro (테헤란로). If it were above ground, it would be an unbroken stretch of glass office towers. Gangnam Station (강남역) — one of the busiest stations in Seoul, and the same intersection the north-south axis (#1353) passed by outside the window. The two axes cross here, forming an X. No stop (#301, #1355).

⑦ Seoul Nat'l Univ. of Education Station → Bangbae → Sadang Station (🚇 about 7 minutes)

  • Above exits 10 and 11 of Seoul Nat'l Univ. of Education Station (교대역), the Seoul Central District Court (서울중앙지방법원) — Seocho-dong's legal district. Past Bangbae (방배) and off at Sadang Station (사당역). The end of Line 2's eastern half.

⑧ Wrap-Up at Sadang Station (🚶 about 3 minutes)

  • 14 exits, where Gwanak, Dongjak, and Seocho districts meet. The evening scene of intercity buses queued up for southern Gyeonggi and the entrance to Gwanaksan are walked separately by #1361. This piece ends here.

🚇 5 Tips for Riding and Seating

1. Ride the inner circle line: at the Euljiro 1-ga Station platform, look for "toward Seongsu and Jamsil" (clockwise). Board the opposite direction (outer circle) and you loop toward City Hall and Hongdae instead.

2. Right-side window seat: sit on the right (south-facing) side in the direction of travel, and the downstream view, Lotte World Tower, and the terminal at Gangbyeon Station come into your window. The left side gets the upstream view and Achasan.

3. No need to worry about a seat until Hanyang University Station: it's an underground stretch. Move to a window seat around Wangsimni.

4. Transfer discount: exit at Konkuk University Station and re-board within 30 minutes, and the transfer fare applies. The Line 7 side trip to Gunja is an in-station transfer, so there's no extra fare.

5. No faster alternative: Line 2 is the fastest way to cover this stretch. Instead, avoid rush hour — go after 10 AM.

🙏 Subway Manners — the Core of This Course

  • Shoot out the window while seated, and keep other passengers out of the frame. Keep phone calls quiet.
  • Leave the pregnancy and priority seats open. Line 2's Gangnam stretch has a lot of standing passengers even during the day.
  • Keep large luggage near the doors — boarding and alighting is especially heavy at Konkuk University Station, Jamsil, and Gangnam.

💡 Where You Might Get Thrown Off

  • Easy to board the wrong direction: both directions on Line 2 are labeled "circle line." Check the sign for "inner circle line, toward Seongsu and Jamsil."
  • The window view runs only from Hanyang University Station to Jamsillaru: it's underground before and after. That roughly 15-minute above-ground stretch is the whole of this piece's view, so don't miss it.
  • The transfer corridor at Konkuk University Station is long: catching the Line 7 side trip means a few minutes' walk following the signs.
  • 14 exits at Sadang Station: pick your exit after you get off — see #1361.

6 FAQs

Q1. Why isn't this piece a bus, like the others?

  • No city bus links these eight anchor points in a single line. Route 360 covers Jamsil → Samseong → Gangnam Station, but then splits off toward Nonhyeon and the Express Bus Terminal. Line 2 is the only axis.

Q2. How is this different from the #1241 Line 2 piece?

  • #1241 is a station-by-station guide covering "what to do once you get off" at 8 key tourist stations. This piece doesn't get off — it passes through in order, watching the window view and the story of the elevated stretch. If you're looking for a place to get off, go to #1241.

Q3. How long is the stretch where you can see the Han River?

  • A minute or so over the Jamsil Railway Bridge itself. Including the elevated stretch before and after it, the window stays bright for about 15 minutes total.

Q4. What if I want to get off at Jamsil or Gangnam?

  • You can. This piece is built on the principle of passing through, so see the individual pieces (#82, #300, #301, #366) for guides to those neighborhoods. Re-board in the same direction within 30 minutes and the transfer discount applies.

Q5. Is it fine with kids or luggage?

  • Yes. The only walking segment is 15 minutes. Just avoid rush hour, and keep suitcases near the doors.

Q6. How does this connect to the north-south axis?

  • The two axes cross at Gangnam Station. Ride this piece all the way to Sadang and loop back on Line 4, or get off at Gangnam Station and transfer to the north-south axis's bus 140 (#1353) — either way, you read Seoul as a cross.
