🎯 How to Use This Guide

This guide gathers three Joseon royal tomb sites and Namhansanseong (남한산성), UNESCO World Heritage sites in Seoul and Gwangju, Gyeonggi. They are Seolleung and Jeongneung in Gangnam-gu (강남구), Taereung and Gangneung in Nowon (노원), Heolleung and Illeung in Seocho (서초), and Sueojangdae and the fortress wall hiking trails at Namhansanseong. All three royal tomb sites are closed on Mondays (Taereung and Gangneung and Heolleung and Illeung open if Monday is a public holiday and close on the first weekday after it), and the admission fee is registered as 1,000 won at all three. Namhansanseong Haenggung (행궁) and the Namhansanseong area are also covered in the #1418 Gwangju, Gyeonggi Day Trip Guide.

The operating details are copied from values registered in the Korea Tourism Organization tourism data (TourAPI). This guide covers only places registered in the Korea Tourism Organization tourism data.

📍 Royal Tombs in Gangnam-gu — Seolleung and Jeongneung

Seoul Seolleung and Jeongneung (서울 선릉과 정릉) [Seolleung-ro 100-gil 1 (선릉로100길 1), Gangnam-gu, ☎ 02-568-1291] is where Seolleung, the tomb of the 9th Joseon king Seongjong (성종) and Queen Jeonghyeon (정현왕후), and Jeongneung, the tomb of the 11th king Jungjong (중종), stand. Seolleung is in the dongwon-igangneung (同原異岡陵) form, with the tombs built on separate hills around one jeongjagak (정자각); seen from in front of the jeongjagak, the left hill holds Seongjong and the right hill holds the tomb of Queen Jeonghyeon. Seongjong's tomb was built in 1495, and Queen Jeonghyeon's tomb was built on the eastern hill in 1530.

Jeongneung was first built elsewhere in 1545, and in 1562 the third queen, Queen Munjeong (문정왕후), moved it to its present site. Queen Munjeong had wished to be buried beside Jungjong, but the moved Jeongneung sat on low ground and often flooded when it rained, so after her death she was buried not at Jeongneung but at Taereung (태릉) in Nowon-gu. Seolleung and Jeongneung suffered damage when the tombs were dug up during the Japanese invasion of 1592 (the Imjin War).

  • Opening hours: 06:00~21:00 (last entry 20:00). Closed days: Every Monday.
  • Fee: Adults and foreigners 1,000 won, local residents 500 won. Free for ages 24 and under, ages 65 and over, people with disabilities and persons of national merit.
  • Parking: Available.

📍 Royal Tombs in Nowon and Seocho — Taereung and Gangneung, Heolleung and Illeung

Seoul Taereung and Gangneung (서울 태릉과 강릉) [Hwarang-ro 681 (화랑로 681), Nowon-gu, ☎ 02-972-0370] are Taereung, the tomb of Queen Munjeong, third queen of Jungjong, and Gangneung (강릉), the tomb of the 13th king Myeongjong (명종) and Queen Insun (인순왕후). Taereung was built in 1565. The burial mound is ringed with folding-screen stones and balustrade stones, and the screen stones are carved with cloud patterns and the twelve zodiac animals. According to the Korea Tourism Organization, the stone figures were set up larger than at other Joseon royal tombs, and it is unusual that the civil and military stone officials have earring holes in their earlobes. The jeongjagak was burned in the Korean War and restored in 1994. Gangneung is a ssangneung (雙陵), with two burial mounds side by side inside one enclosure, and was built on the hill east of Taereung in 1567.

  • Opening hours: Feb~May and Sep~Oct 09:00~18:00 (last entry 17:00), Jun~Aug 09:00~18:30 (last entry 17:30), Nov~Jan 09:00~17:30 (last entry 16:30).
  • Closed days: Every Monday. If Monday is a public holiday, it is open that day and closed on the first weekday after it.
  • Fee: Individuals 1,000 won, groups of 10 or more 800 won. Parking: Available.

Seoul Heolleung and Illeung (서울 헌릉과 인릉) [Heonilleung-gil 34 (헌인릉길 34), Seocho-gu, ☎ 02-445-0347] are Heolleung (헌릉), the tomb of the 3rd Joseon king Taejong (태종) and Queen Wongyeong (원경왕후), and Illeung (인릉), the tomb of the 23rd king Sunjo (순조) and Queen Sunwon (순원황후). Heolleung is a ssangneung form, built with Queen Wongyeong's tomb first in 1420 and Taejong's tomb to its west in 1422. The civil stone officials, military stone officials, stone horses, stone sheep and stone tigers are placed at twice the number of other royal tombs. Illeung is a happjangneung (合葬陵) form, with the king and queen laid together in one mound. It was first built in Gyoha, Paju, and later moved to its present site, and when it was moved the old stone figures of Sejong's former Yeongneung and Queen Janggyeong's former Huireung were taken out and used again.

  • Opening hours: Feb~May and Sep~Oct 09:00~18:00 (last entry 17:00), Jun~Aug 09:00~18:30 (last entry 17:30), Nov~Jan 09:00~17:30 (last entry 16:30).
  • Closed days: Every Monday. If Monday is a public holiday, it is open that day and closed on the first weekday after it.
  • Fee: Individuals 1,000 won, groups of 10 or more 800 won. Parking: Free. Credit cards: Accepted.

📍 The Fortress — Namhansanseong Sueojangdae and Hiking Trails

Namhansanseong Sueojangdae (남한산성 수어장대) [Sanseong-ri (산성리), Namhansanseong-myeon (남한산성면), Gwangju-si, Gyeonggi, ☎ Namhansanseong World Heritage Center (남한산성세계유산센터) 031-8008-5155] is a pavilion built for command and observation. It is the only one left of the five jangdae (장대) that stood at Namhansanseong. It is a 2-story pavilion on the summit of Cheongnyangsan (청량산) (482m above sea level), and was extended to 2 stories in 1751, the 27th year of King Yeongjo (영조). It has a signboard reading "Sueojangdae (守禦將臺)" on the outside and "Mumangnu (無忘樓)" inside.

  • Opening hours: Open at all times. Closed days: Open year-round. Fee: Free.
  • Parking: Two-wheeled vehicles and passenger cars under 1,000cc 1,500 won on weekdays, 2,500 won on weekends and public holidays. Passenger cars of 1,000cc or more, small vans of 15 seats or fewer, and small trucks of 1 ton or less 3,000 won on weekdays, 5,000 won on weekends and public holidays. Medium and large vans and trucks 6,000 won on weekdays, and parking is not allowed on weekends and public holidays.

Namhansanseong Hiking Trails (남한산성 탐방로) [Sanseong-ri (산성리), Namhansanseong-myeon (남한산성면), Gwangju-si, Gyeonggi, ☎ 031-8008-5155] are 5 courses that follow the fortress wall of Namhansanseong.

  • Course 1, Path of the General (장수의 길): About 3.8km, 1 hour 20 minutes. It is described as a gentle path, and passes the North Gate, West Gate, South Gate, secret gates and Sueojangdae.
  • Course 2, Path of the King (국왕의 길): About 3km, 1 hour. It starts at the Haenggung (행궁) and passes Chimgwaejeong (침괘정) and the West Gate.
  • Course 3, Path of the Warrior Monks (승병의 길): About 5.7km, 2 hours. It passes Janggyeongsa (장경사) and Mangwolsa (망월사), and Bongamseong (봉암성), an outer fortress.
  • Course 4, Path of the Ongseong (옹성의 길): About 3.8km, 1 hour 20 minutes. It passes the 1st, 2nd and 3rd South Ongseong (남옹성).
  • Course 5, Path of the Fortress (산성의 길): About 7.7km, 3 hours 20 minutes. It passes the walls, secret gates and chiseong (치성) throughout, and you can see the scenery of Seongnam, Hanam and Gwangju all together.
  • Opening hours: Open at all times. Closed days: Open year-round. Fee: Free. Parking: Available.

⚠ 3 Things to Know Before You Go

  1. The three royal tomb sites are closed on Mondays — Taereung and Gangneung and Heolleung and Illeung open if Monday is a public holiday and close on the first weekday after it.
  2. Seolleung and Jeongneung are open until 21:00 — Taereung and Gangneung and Heolleung and Illeung close at 17:30 in winter (Nov~Jan).
  3. Sueojangdae parking fees are higher on weekends and public holidays — passenger cars of 1,000cc or more pay 3,000 won on weekdays and 5,000 won on weekends and public holidays, and medium and large vans and trucks cannot park on weekends and public holidays.

⚙ FAQ 2

Q. What is the admission fee for foreigners?

A. According to the Korea Tourism Organization registration, Seolleung and Jeongneung is registered as 1,000 won for adults and foreigners, and Taereung and Gangneung and Heolleung and Illeung are registered as "1,000 won for individuals."

Q. Which of the 5 Namhansanseong hiking courses is the shortest?

A. Course 2, Path of the King, is registered as taking 1 hour for about 3km.
