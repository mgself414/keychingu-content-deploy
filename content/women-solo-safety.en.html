Korea Rip-off & Scam Response Guide — Spot Overcharging with the Taxi Fare Table, Keep Evidence, Where to Report (QR Survey · 120 · 1330): For Solo Travelers

Subtitle: Seoul standard taxi base fare ₩4,800 (first 1.6km) · night surcharge 20%/40% · out-of-city surcharge 20% (Seoul Metropolitan Government (서울시)) · The three taxi violations Seoul cracks down on involving foreigners (refusing a ride, soliciting riders at high prices, unfair night fares) and the English/Chinese/Japanese QR survey for reports (Seoul Metropolitan Government 2025-08) · 120 complaints (foreign-language help is option 9, weekdays 09:00~18:00) · 1330 · Reports are said to need the plate number, date/time and place · For crimes call 112; for counseling after violence against women, 1366 (24 hours) · No official warnings exist for street proselytizing, touting or requests to send money, so only behavior rules are given · Checked 2026-11-04

ISSUE 03 · #501 · 2026-05-27

🎯 How to Use This Guide

11 p.m., in front of a hotel in Myeongdong (명동). A taxi driver with the meter off holds up three fingers and says "thirty thousand won." Is this a rip-off, what should you record right now, and where do you report it tomorrow — this guide answers only those three questions. Know the fare table and overcharging becomes visible; keep the plate number, time and place and it becomes a report.

It is written from the viewpoint of someone traveling alone, since solo travelers are easy targets for approaches, but the rules are the same for everyone. For a women's safety checklist covering drinks, bars and online meetups, see #1334; for night-walk services such as the Seoul Safe Return Scout (안심귀가 스카우트) and the Ansim-i app (안심이 앱), see #460. For a night when you miss the last train, see #1395; for the Kakao T hailing screen, #1273; for when a lodging booking asks for payment outside the app, #1414; and for injury or urgent situations, #433.

🚕 Taxi — Know the Fare Table and Overcharging Becomes Visible

These are the standard taxi fares published by the Seoul Metropolitan Government (in effect from 2023-02-01; Seoul transport site updated 2026-10-14). Compare these numbers with the meter.

Time of day Base fare (first 1.6km) Distance fare Time fare (slow/stopped) Surcharge
Daytime (04:00~22:00) ₩4,800 ₩100 per 131m ₩100 per 30 seconds —
22:00~23:00 · 02:00~04:00 ₩5,800 ₩120 per 131m ₩120 per 30 seconds 20%
23:00~02:00 ₩6,700 ₩140 per 131m ₩140 per 30 seconds 40%
  • A total above the base fare is not by itself a rip-off. Past 1.6km, distance and time fares are added, and on trips leaving Seoul (for example to Incheon Airport) a 20% out-of-city surcharge is added (up to 60% when combined with the night surcharge).
  • The three things Seoul cracks down on involving foreigners (2025-08 notice): ① refusing to take a foreigner, saying the distance is short ② soliciting riders by keeping the car parked and quoting a high price ③ unfair fares charged to foreigners returning to their lodging at night. Enforcement focuses on Incheon and Gimpo airports and tourist-heavy areas such as Myeongdong.
  • Before you get in: check that the meter turns on. Do not get into a taxi that stays parked and quotes a price — item ② above is exactly that scene. If you hail by app, the plate number and route are recorded (#1273).
  • After you get in: photograph the plate number. The Seoul 120 notice (posted 2012) says a taxi report is accepted only with the plate number, date/time of the violation and place, plus the reporter's name and contact. We could not confirm the current QR survey or 1330 intake requirements, but having the number is the surest way. Get a receipt when you get out.
  • Reporting: the report channel stated in the original Seoul text is the English/Chinese/Japanese QR survey. QR cards are handed out in business-card form at airport arrival/departure halls and tourist information centers, and in-taxi notice stickers are still at the planning stage (Seoul Metropolitan Government 2025-08). You can also ask about filing through 120 (foreign languages: option 9, weekdays 09:00~18:00) and 1330. 02-2133-2317 is the inquiry number of the Seoul taxi policy team, not a number for receiving reports.
  • Alternative around midnight: Seoul Owl Bus (올빼미버스, N bus), 14 routes, running 23:00~06:00 (first and last departure times vary by route), ₩2,500 (Seoul Metropolitan Government). Routes and how to ride are in #1395.

🛍 Shops & Attractions — Ask the Price First, Get the Receipt After

  • Where there is no price tag, ask the price before you order. One phrase is enough: "얼마예요?" (eol-ma-ye-yo). At markets, ask before the item goes on the scale.
  • Pay by card when you can — it leaves a record. If you paid cash, ask for a receipt.
  • If you were charged an amount different from the price tag, or pressured into a forced purchase, call 1330. Korea Tourism Organization 1330 takes calls by phone, online call, chat and messenger (VisitKorea). We could not find an official source stating the scope of complaints it accepts, so follow the usage laid out in #1299. Having the receipt, photos, date/time and business name makes handling easier (local-government guidance level; the official 1330 standard was not confirmed).
  • Exchange money at banks, airports or through app-based exchange — for exchange rate and fee comparisons, see #431. We found no official warning about private street money changers, so we state only this: "do not exchange money without a receipt."

🙋 People Who Approach You on the Street — No Official Warning, but Three Rules

We found no official warnings or statistics about the approaches below on the websites of the Korean National Police Agency, Korea Tourism Organization or Seoul Metropolitan Government. So we give behavior rules only, without numbers.

1. If someone uses a survey, religion or fortune-telling as a reason to go somewhere else, do not follow. The moment they suggest moving to another place, end the conversation.

2. Do not go into a club or bar when touts lure you with "free entry, free drinks." Many accounts say the price was set only after people went in (not officially confirmed). Rules for drinking situations are in #1334.

3. If anyone asks you to send money on the street, by message, or outside an app, do not send it. What to do when a lodging asks for a bank transfer outside the app is in #1414, with official sources.

If you have already sent money or had it taken, call 112. After that, file a dispute with your card company and contact your embassy.

👩 If You Are a Woman Traveling Alone — Three Things Outside This Guide

  • Night walks: Seoul Safe Return Scout (안심귀가 스카우트; weekday nights, advance application via app) and the Ansim-i app (안심이 앱; supports English/Chinese/Japanese) — participating districts, hours and how to apply are in #460. The original Seoul text does not state whether foreign tourists can use them.
  • Drinking situations, drinks, online meetups: the table in #1334.
  • Counseling after harm: Women's Emergency Hotline 1366 (여성긴급전화; 365 days, 24 hours, Ministry of Gender Equality and Family — supported languages are not in the original). 1577-1366 is the Danuri Call Center (다누리콜센터) for migrant women, reported to offer counseling in 13 languages, but we could not confirm whether tourists can use it. If urgent, 112.

⚠ 5 Common Mistakes

1. Reporting without the plate number — under the Seoul 120 notice, it is hard to get accepted. One photo as soon as you get in.

2. Asking a no-meter taxi "how much will it be?" — once you agree, it is hard to dispute. Ask for the meter to be turned on, and if it is not, do not get in. If you have already set off, ask the driver to stop somewhere busy and safe and get out. If you feel threatened, getting away comes before taking photos; call 112.

3. Treating 1366 and 1577-1366 as the same number — 1366 is the Women's Emergency Hotline (24 hours); 1577-1366 is the Danuri Call Center for migrant women (13 languages, tourist eligibility unconfirmed). If urgent, 112.

4. "Just report it to the Tourist Police" — after the reported abolition, there is no official notice that it is operating. QR survey · 120 · 1330; for crimes, 112.

5. Sending cash on the spot — refuse every request made on the street, by message or outside an app. For lodging, see #1414.

⚙ 4 FAQs

Q1. Can I report even if I do not speak Korean?

At 120 Dasan Call Center, pressing 9 connects you to foreign-language help (English, Chinese, Japanese, Vietnamese, Mongolian), but it takes calls only weekdays 09:00~18:00 (120 Dasan Call Center Foundation). On weekends and at night, use Seoul's QR survey (English/Chinese/Japanese) or 1330. The languages and hours of 1330 are in #433.

Q2. Will I get my money back if I report?

A report is for enforcement and sanctions; we did not find a channel that guarantees a refund. If you paid by card, a dispute with the card company is a separate route.

Q3. At 11 p.m., how much extra does a taxi fare add?

From 23:00~02:00 there is a 40% surcharge, so the base fare is ₩6,700; from 22:00~23:00 and 02:00~04:00 there is a 20% surcharge, ₩5,800 (Seoul Metropolitan Government). On top of that come the distance and time fares, and 20% out-of-city surcharge if leaving Seoul. If the amount was quoted without a meter, or the receipt differs greatly from the calculation, photograph the plate number.

Q4. Who helps after I have been harmed?

For violence, sexual violence or stalking, you can use 132 (Korea Legal Aid Corporation) and 1577-1701 (Ministry of Justice, weekdays 09:00~18:00). Whether taxi, shop or money-transfer scams fall under these contacts, and whether foreign-language counseling is available, is not stated in the original, so call and ask. Also notify your own country's embassy.
