Seoul N-S Axis ① Bus 140, Yangjae (양재) to Jongno (종로) — 50 Years of Seoul Passing by the Window

Subtitle: Switch between subway lines and Seoul looks like a set of separate islands. This piece travels above ground instead — boarding city bus 140 in front of the Yangjae flower market, running 6.9 kilometers up Gangnam-daero, crossing the Han River on Hannam Bridge, and getting off in Jongno after passing through Namsan 1st Tunnel. About two and a half hours on one bus, tracing the north-south line Seoul completed when the bridge went up in December 1969 and the tunnel followed in August 1970. Gangnam, Sinsa, and Jongno all go by outside the window only. Seoul N-S Axis, Part 1.

ISSUE · #1353 · 2026-09-07

🎯 The Frame — Reading Seoul as One Vertical Line

Seoul tourism is mostly a set of points. Myeongdong (명동), Gangnam (강남), Gyeongbokgung (경복궁), Hongdae (홍대) — teleport between them by subway and each neighborhood feels like a separate floating island. What lies between them, and why Gangnam became Gangnam, stays invisible underground.

This course looks at what's in between. One city bus — Line 140 — starts at Yangjae (양재) on Seoul's southern edge, runs north up Gangnam-daero, crosses the Han River, bores through Namsan, and arrives at Jongno, the old city center. Outside the window, Gangnam's glass towers, the width of the Han River, the darkness of Namsan, and Jongno's low rooflines pass by in sequence. There's exactly one stop, at the north end of Hannam Bridge. For the rest, you just sit and watch.

  • Total time: about 2 hours to 2 hours 30 minutes (about 1 hour on the bus + 15 minutes off at Hannam + buffer)
  • Best time: depart on a weekday between 10 and 11 AM — the morning commute rush is over so seats open up, and you reach Jongno around lunchtime. During evening rush (5–7 PM), "even the bus gets stuck" on Gangnam-daero
  • Incline: none — the only walking segment is 15 minutes on flat ground. Fine to bring luggage

Why 140 — One Route That Crosses the Bridge, the Tunnel, and the Road

Among Seoul city buses, only Line 140 runs the full length of Gangnam-daero (강남대로) from its southern end to its northern end, crosses Hannam Bridge (한남대교) and Namsan 1st Tunnel (남산1호터널), and continues on to Jongno (종로) and Daehangno (대학로). It's a trunk route covering a 58km round trip between Dobongsan (도봉산) and the Yangjae Flower Market (양재꽃시장), and while routes 470 and 741 overlap with it on the Namsan 1st Tunnel–Yangjae stretch, Line 140 is the only one that ties the whole line together as far as Jongno and Daehangno.

📖 Story 1 — One Bridge Built Gangnam: On December 25, 1969, Hannam Bridge — then called the 3rd Han River Bridge (제3한강교), about 919 meters long — was built between Hannam-dong (한남동) and Sinsa-dong (신사동). Before that, this stretch south of the Han River (한강) was farmland. Eight months after the bridge opened, on August 15, 1970, Namsan 1st Tunnel was cut through, completing a straight line that ran from the old city center — Jongno and Euljiro (을지로) — through Namsan (남산), across the Han River, and straight south. The road running south of the bridge was designated "Yeongdong 1-ro (영동1로)" in 1972, then took the name "Gangnam-daero" in June 1976. The road this bus runs today is, in that order, the sequence in which Gangnam was born.

🚶 The Route — In the Order It Passes the Window

The starting point is Yangjae Citizens' Forest Station (양재시민의숲역) on the Sinbundang Line (신분당선). Line 140's terminus is the "AT Center·Yangjae Flower Market (AT센터.양재꽃시장)" stop, one stop away from here, so you can board an empty bus right at the start.

① Yangjae Citizens' Forest Station (양재시민의숲역) Exit 4 → AT Center·Yangjae Flower Market (AT센터.양재꽃시장) Stop (🚶 about 7 minutes)

  • Exit 4 puts you right on Gangnam-daero. Walk straight along the main road past the aT Center building to the stop. If you have time, #1355 covers a morning course that walks this area first — the flower market and Maeheon Citizens' Forest (매헌시민의숲).
  • The stop name is "AT Center·Yangjae Flower Market (AT센터.양재꽃시장)" — the terminus and starting point for Line 140. Board a bus headed toward Dobongsan.

② Yeomgok Intersection (염곡사거리)·Yangjae Station (양재역) → Gangnam Station (강남역) Stop (🚌 about 15 minutes · no stop)

  • Right after departure, the bus passes Yeomgok Intersection — the 0km point of Gangnam-daero's 6.9 kilometers. Past Yangjae Station, corporate headquarters and office towers pack in on both sides of the road — the face of Gangnam-daero's southern stretch.
  • Gangnam Station stop sits like an island in the middle of the median bus lane, one of the busiest stops in Seoul. Subway entrances and giant digital billboards pass by outside the window. This piece doesn't get off here — Gangnam Station itself is already well covered by #301, #1220, and others.

③ Sinnonhyeon (신논현)·Nonhyeon (논현) → Sinsa Station (신사역) Stop (🚌 about 8 minutes · no stop)

  • Past Gangnam Station, the street trees thicken and the buildings get a little shorter. One block west of the Sinsa Station stop is Garosu-gil (가로수길) — just note the mouth of the alley from the window (Garosu-gil is covered in #295 and #30).
  • A right-side (east-facing) window seat on this stretch gives you an early look at the Han River approaching.

④ Hannam Bridge (한남대교) (🚌 about 5 minutes · window climax)

  • Just past Sinsa Station, the road lifts slightly, the buildings drop away, and the Han River opens up. From the bridge, about 919 meters long, you can see the skyline toward Dongjak Bridge (동작대교) and Yeouido (여의도) to the west and toward Seongsu (성수) and Jamsil (잠실) to the east, both at once.
  • Gangnam-daero ends here — the south end of Hannam Bridge is its terminus. Cross the bridge and the name changes to Hannam-daero (한남대로).

⑤ Get Off at Soonchunhyang University Hospital (순천향대학병원) Stop → Mini Walk at the North End of Hannam Bridge (🚶 about 15 minutes)

  • Cross the bridge and get off at the first stop, "Soonchunhyang University Hospital (순천향대학병원)" (heading toward Dobongsan; the opposite-direction stop is "Hannam-ogeori (한남오거리)"). This is where Hannam-daero starts climbing toward Namsan.
  • Walk for just 15 minutes: duck into the alley behind the stop and the roar of Hannam-daero suddenly disappears, replaced by low-rise housing and café alleys. That contrast in scale with the glass towers south of the bridge is the whole point of this axis. A proper dusk course through Hannam-dong exists separately at #1354 — this is just a taste.
  • Catch Line 140 again toward Dobongsan from the same stop (every 5–8 minutes on weekdays).

⑥ Namsan 1st Tunnel (남산1호터널) → Downtown (🚌 about 8 minutes · no stop)

  • 📖 Story 2 — The Bus Doesn't Know About the Toll: At the top of Hannam-daero's climb, the bus enters Namsan 1st Tunnel. It was a toll tunnel when it opened in August 1970, and since 1996 it's been a "congestion toll" zone meant to ease downtown traffic — since January 15, 2024, only cars heading into downtown pay 2,000 won. Bus passengers never pay anything. After about 1.5km of darkness, when the light of downtown appears, that's old Seoul.
  • Out of the tunnel, it's toward Myeongdong and Euljiro — Euljiro's day and night are covered in #1346 and #1309.

⑦ Get Off at Jongno 3-ga·Tapgol Park (종로3가.탑골공원) Stop (🚌 about 10 minutes)

  • Pass through the downtown stretch and get off at "Jongno 3-ga·Tapgol Park (종로3가.탑골공원)". Jongno is the northern end of this axis — one bus, all the way from the flower market at the southern end to here.
  • The route continues on to Jongno 4-ga (종로4가) near Jongmyo (종묘), Jongno 5-ga (종로5가) near Gwangjang Market (광장시장), and Daehangno, but this piece's traverse of Seoul ends here.

⑧ Wrap-Up — Connecting to the Jongno 3-ga Hub (🚶 about 5 minutes)

  • Within 5 minutes of the stop you'll find Jongno 3-ga Station (종로3가역) on subway lines 1, 3, and 5, the alleys of Ikseon-dong (익선동), and Tapgol Park. Lunch and onward routes are handed off to #1254, the complete guide to the Jongno 3-ga transfer hub.

🚌 5 Tips for Riding and Seating

1. One card: T-money or a foreign contactless card (whether city buses support it is covered in #1298). Tag on and tag off — don't forget the tag-off, since it's what triggers the transfer discount.

2. Sit toward the front, on the right: heading toward Dobongsan, a right-side (east-facing) window seat puts the Han River and the climb up Namsan straight ahead.

3. Ring the bell one stop early: announcements play in Korean and English. Ring when you hear "Soonchunhyang University Hospital (순천향대학병원)."

4. A faster alternative: if you're short on time, cover Yangjae → Gangnam → Sinsa in 12 minutes on the Sinbundang Line (extended to Sinsa in May 2022) and catch Line 140 at the Sinsa Station stop instead. You'll miss the window view of the Gangnam-daero stretch, though.

5. The reverse direction works too: board in Jongno and you cross Hannam Bridge at night on the way down to Yangjae — a candidate for a series expansion.

🙏 Bus Manners — the Core of This Course

  • Median bus lane stops sit in the middle of the road. Cross only at the crosswalk signal — jaywalking is dangerous and finable.
  • Photos out the window are fine, but keep other passengers out of the frame. Keep phone calls quiet.
  • Some front seats are reserved for the elderly and disabled — leave them open when the bus is crowded. Put large luggage in the space near the rear door.

💡 Where You Might Get Thrown Off

  • Stop names change with direction: the north end of Hannam Bridge is "Soonchunhyang University Hospital (순천향대학병원)" heading toward Dobongsan, and "Hannam-ogeori (한남오거리)" heading toward Yangjae. Check the direction in your app before ringing the bell. The downtown section also runs a different path in each direction — toward Dobongsan the bus climbs Samil-daero (삼일대로) onto Jongno (종로), while toward Yangjae it comes down via Hyehwa-dong rotary (혜화동로터리) and Samil-ro (삼일로). Riding the reverse direction means a different stop, not the same one.
  • Avoid evening rush: between 5 and 7 PM even the bus lane on Gangnam-daero backs up, stretching the course past 3 hours.
  • Signal weakens inside the tunnel: check the next stop in your map app before entering the tunnel.
  • Boarding at the terminus can mean waiting: at the starting stop, a bus waiting to depart sometimes keeps its doors closed — board the one with its doors open.

6 FAQs

Q1. It's a regular city bus, not a tour bus — is that really OK?

  • That's exactly the point of this course. The fare is a regular city bus fare, and you ride the same bus as Seoulites. Unlike a city tour bus, there's no scripted narration — but the view out the window is the narration.

Q2. Why not just take the subway?

  • The subway only runs Yangjae to Sinsa on a single line — north of the Han River, you have to transfer. More importantly, underground you can't see the Han River or Namsan at all. The point of this piece is transit you can actually watch.

Q3. How many stops in total, and how long does it take?

  • From the Yangjae Flower Market to Jongno 3-ga, roughly 50–60 minutes on the bus (an estimate for weekday daytime). Around 20 stops. It varies a lot by time of day.

Q4. What if I want to get off at Gangnam Station or Garosu-gil?

  • You can — but this piece's principle is to pass through, so see the separate pieces (#301, #295) for guides to those neighborhoods. Get off and re-board Line 140 in the same direction within 30 minutes and the transfer discount still applies.

Q5. Is it OK to bring a lot of luggage?

  • Yes. The only walking segment is 15 minutes on flat ground, and there's space for a suitcase by the rear door of the bus. Just avoid rush hour.

Q6. Can I ride it at night?

  • Yes. Hannam Bridge actually looks better at night. But this piece is designed around daylight hours, when Gangnam-daero and the climb up Namsan are visible. A nighttime reverse-direction version is covered in an expansion piece.
