Korean Jjigae and Haejangguk Guide — Kimchi, Doenjang/Tofu, Budae Jjigae and Hangover Soup at 8 Places (Cheongjinok (청진옥), Mugyo-dong Bugeoguk-jip (무교동 북어국집), Odeng Sikdang (오뎅식당), Eunjujeong (은주정)) (Official Figures, October 2026)

Subtitle: Cheongjinok (청진옥) began in 1937 and was named a Seoul Future Heritage site in 2013, open 06:00–22:00 year-round (VisitKorea) · Mugyo-dong Bugeoguk-jip (무교동 북어국집) since 1968, opens at 07:00 on weekdays (VisitKorea) · 12 restaurants on Uijeongbu's Budae Jjigae Street (의정부 부대찌개거리), with a festival every October (VisitKorea) · Hwanggeum Kongbat (황금콩밭) tofu hot pot is a Bib Gourmand (Michelin) — as of 2026-10-21

🎯 How to Use This Guide

When the pot on the tabletop induction burner comes to a boil, ham and minari (미나리, water dropwort) float up through the broth — Daewoo Budae Jjigae (대우부대찌개) in Yeoksam-dong (역삼동) puts an induction burner on every table and starts boiling only once your order goes in (VisitKorea). This guide covers jjigae (찌개, stews boiled in a pot and shared at the table) and morning haejangguk (해장국, hangover soup). There are four branches: kimchi-jjigae (김치찌개), doenjang, cheonggukjang (청국장, strong fermented soybean stew) and tofu, budae-jjigae (부대찌개, army-base stew), and haejangguk. Seolleongtang (설렁탕), gomtang (곰탕) and pork gukbap, where rice is served in the soup, are split off into the #477 Gukbap Guide. We picked only places listed in VisitKorea tourism data and on the official Michelin Guide site. This edition is for anyone who wants to eat the same table spread Koreans eat on a weekday lunch.

The old guide's "Uijeongbu budae-jjigae, 1 hour by KTX" had no basis. The route VisitKorea gives is Exit 2 of Uijeongbu Jungang Station (의정부중앙역) on the Uijeongbu Light Rail. The popularity rankings attached to kimchi-jjigae and cheonggukjang also had no source, so we removed them.

🌶 Kimchi-jjigae — 2 Places in Seoul

  • Eunjujeong (은주정) — 32 Changgyeonggung-ro 8-gil (창경궁로8길), Jung District, Seoul (Bangsan Market (방산시장) by Cheonggyecheon). 11:30–22:00 (last order 21:30), closed every Sunday and over the Seollal (설) and Chuseok (추석) holidays. Signature dish is kimchi-jjigae eaten wrapped in leaves (ssam (쌈)); doenjang-jjigae (된장찌개) and samgyeopsal (삼겹살) are also served. A place that has kept going for over 30 years. Phone 0507-1406-4669, non-smoking throughout (VisitKorea)
  • Guldari Sikdang (굴다리식당) — 8-1 Saechang-ro (새창로), Mapo District (Dohwa-dong (도화동)). 08:00–22:00, open year-round. Kimchi-jjigae is the signature; jeyuk-bokkeum (제육볶음, spicy stir-fried pork) and gyeran-mari (계란말이, rolled omelet) are also available. Gyeongui Line Plaza (경의선광장) and Gyeongui Line Park (경의선공원) are within 1 km. Phone 02-712-0066 (VisitKorea)
  • Price: Neither place has a price posted on VisitKorea — check the menu

🫘 Doenjang, Cheonggukjang and Tofu — 2 Places

  • Boknamine Cheonggukjang (복남이네청국장) — 7-7 Gangchon-ro 26beon-gil (강촌로26번길), Ilsandong District, Goyang, Gyeonggi (Baekseok-dong (백석동)). 11:00–22:00 (last order 20:30), open year-round. Cheonggukjang (청국장) and doenjang-jjigae are made with 100% domestic beans; order cheonggukjang and it comes with barley rice and namul (나물, seasoned vegetables) such as bean sprouts, bracken and saengchae (생채, fresh seasoned vegetables), which you mix in a large metal bowl (yangpun (양푼)). Phone 031-904-6115, non-smoking throughout (VisitKorea)
  • Hwanggeum Kongbat (황금콩밭) — 9 Mapo-daero 16-gil (마포대로16길), Mapo District, Seoul. Mon–Fri 11:30–21:00, Sat–Sun 11:00–21:00. A tofu specialist that makes its own tofu from 100% domestic beans. Dubu-jeongol (두부전골, tofu hot pot), jjabak-dubu (짜박두부, tofu in a thick spicy stew), dubu-jorim (두부조림, braised tofu), dubu duruchigi (두부 두루치기, stir-fried tofu), and takju (탁주, unfiltered rice wine) brewed from rice, nuruk (누룩, fermentation starter) and water. Naver reservations possible, parking available (Michelin page, Bib Gourmand)

🌭 Budae-jjigae — 2 Places

  • Odeng Sikdang (오뎅식당) — 7 Hoguk-ro 1309beon-gil (호국로1309번길), Uijeongbu, Gyeonggi. 08:30–21:30. The first place in Uijeongbu to sell budae-jjigae, now in its third generation. It started as a small street stall and then switched its main dish to budae-jjigae. Budae-jjigae and cheolpan budae-bokkeum (철판부대볶음, griddle-fried budae). Phone 031-842-0423, official site odengsikdang.com (VisitKorea)
  • Uijeongbu Budae Jjigae Street (의정부 부대찌개거리) — A food-themed street where 12 restaurants cluster around Odeng Sikdang (오뎅식당). Odeng Sikdang is the one Uijeongbu city has certified as an original restaurant, and the Uijeongbu Famous Jjigae Food Festival (의정부명물찌개 음식축제) is held every October. Right outside Exit 2 of Uijeongbu Jungang Station on the Uijeongbu Light Rail (VisitKorea)
  • Daewoo Budae Jjigae (대우부대찌개) — 34 Teheran-ro 25-gil (테헤란로25길), Gangnam District, Seoul (Yeoksam-dong (역삼동)). 11:00–21:00, break 15:00–17:00, closed every Saturday. Opened in 1984. Minari (미나리) goes in, so the broth tastes clean. Deungsim budae-jjigae (등심부대찌개, sirloin budae-jjigae), grilled sausage and grilled sirloin; takeout available. Phone 02-552-1663 (VisitKorea)
  • Origin: "Budae" (부대) refers to the US military units stationed in Korea right after the Korean War. The dish comes from people near the bases boiling ham, sausage and bacon with kimchi, gochujang (고추장, chili paste) and tteok (떡, rice cakes) (VisitKorea)

🐟 Haejangguk — 2 Places in Seoul

  • Cheongjinok (청진옥) — 32 Jong-ro 3-gil (종로3길), Jongno District, Seoul (Cheongjin-dong (청진동)). 06:00–22:00 (last order 21:30), open year-round. Started in 1937 in Pimatgol (피맛골) and now in its third generation. The signature is yangseonji haejangguk (양선지 해장국): beef-bone broth with seonji (선지, congealed ox blood), beef offal, ugeoji (우거지, dried cabbage leaves) and bean sprouts; naejang suyuk (내장수육, boiled offal) and modeum suyuk (모듬수육, assorted boiled meat) are also served. Named a Seoul Future Heritage site in 2013. Phone 02-735-1690 (VisitKorea)
  • Mugyo-dong Bugeoguk-jip (무교동 북어국집) — 38 Eulji-ro 1-gil (을지로1길), Jung District, Seoul (Da-dong (다동), behind Seoul City Hall). Weekdays 07:00–20:00 (last order 19:30), weekends 07:00–15:00, open year-round. In the same spot since 1968, with a single menu item, bugeo haejangguk (북어해장국, dried-pollock hangover soup), so it comes out fast and rice and soup are refilled endlessly. Phone 02-777-3891, non-smoking throughout (VisitKorea)
  • For Jeju-style haejangguk, see #1406 Four Jeju-Style Soups

🥗 Dietary Notes

  • Price range: None of the 8 places has a won price posted officially. Hwanggeum Kongbat (황금콩밭) has only the Michelin ₩ symbol. A 2017 VisitKorea article lists Cheongjinok's haejangguk at ₩10,000 (~$7.50), but we could not confirm today's price
  • Vegetarian: Sundubu-jjigae (순두부찌개, soft tofu stew) and dubu-jorim mostly contain saeujeot (새우젓, salted shrimp) (VisitKorea). Kimchi seasoning also mostly contains salted seafood, fish sauce or meat stock (VisitKorea) — for kimchi-jjigae and doenjang-jjigae as well, ask about the broth and salted seafood first. Budae-jjigae contains ham and sausage, and haejangguk contains beef bones, seonji (선지) and offal
  • Halal: VisitSeoul introduces Eid (이드) in Itaewon (15 Usadan-ro 10-gil (우사단로10길), Yongsan District, daily 12:00–21:00) as one of the few restaurants recognized as halal by the Korea Muslim Federation (KMF). None of the jjigae places in this guide carries a halal label, and kimchi-jjigae and budae-jjigae commonly contain pork and ham — Eunjujeong (은주정) also serves samgyeopsal (삼겹살) (VisitKorea)
  • Allergies: Cheonggukjang, doenjang and tofu are all soy. Also check in advance about bugeo (북어, dried pollock), seonji (선지) and offal
  • For a show-it-to-the-staff card, see #1300 Dietary Request Manual

🧰 Jjigae Practicalities — Solo, Reservations, Payment, Ordering, Getting There

  • Solo: Haejangguk (Cheongjinok (청진옥), Mugyo-dong Bugeoguk-jip (무교동 북어국집)) is one bowl per person, so it is easy to go alone. Some places may only take pot jjigae from two servings — hold up one finger as you walk in to ask. For a solo-dining map, see #348 Seoul Solo Diner Map
  • Reservations: Hwanggeum Kongbat (황금콩밭) takes Naver reservations (Michelin page). For the rest, the official pages give no reservation information — queue or call
  • Payment: For cards and mobile payment, see #527 Korea's Payment Systems
  • Ordering: Mugyo-dong Bugeoguk-jip (무교동 북어국집) has one menu item, so once seated you only need to say how many people (VisitKorea). If there is a language barrier, see #1339 Ordering Etiquette
  • Getting there: Uijeongbu and Goyang are outside Seoul — for transit cards, see #1298 Choosing from the 3 Transit Cards

⚠ 4 Common Mistakes

1. "Take the KTX to Uijeongbu" — VisitKorea directs you to Exit 2 of Uijeongbu Jungang Station (의정부중앙역) on the Uijeongbu Light Rail

2. "Daewoo Budae Jjigae on a weekend lunch" — It is closed every Saturday (VisitKorea)

3. "Haejangguk is open all night" — Cheongjinok (청진옥) is open 06:00–22:00, and Mugyo-dong Bugeoguk-jip (무교동 북어국집) closes at 15:00 on weekends (VisitKorea)

4. "Tofu stew is vegetarian" — VisitKorea notes that most sundubu-jjigae and dubu-jorim contain saeujeot (새우젓, salted shrimp)
