🎯 Why "Seoul Solo Diner" Meta?

A full experience for solo foreign diners visiting Korea (#342 Solo meta follow-up). While Korean cuisine traditionally caters to 2-4 people, Korea's infrastructure for solo restaurants, bars, cafes, and shops ranks #2 globally (after Tokyo). Myeongdong (명동), Itaewon (이태원), Hongdae (홍대), and Gangnam (강남) boast clusters of solo-friendly Korean, international, and BBQ restaurants, plus bars, alongside GS25 (GS25) and CU (CU) convenience stores (pyeonuijeom) offering solo lunchboxes, solo coin noraebang (노래방 - karaoke rooms), solo jjimjilbang (찜질방 - Korean bathhouses/spas), and solo cafes.

This guide presents 12 signature solo dining spots across 4 axes. Category Meta Part 7.

⚠ 3 Common Solo Diner Mistakes

1. "Korean Food = 2+ People" — Unaware of Solo Korean Restaurants: It's assumed Korean food is for 2-4 people. In reality, there are Hansot (한솥)·Kimbap Cheonguk (김밥천국)·Saemaeul Sikdang (새마을식당)·Hanam Pig House (하남돼지집) solo sets + hotel solo jeongsik (정식 - Korean set meal) courses + full market solo experiences for ₩5,000.

2. "BBQ = 2+ People" — Unaware of Solo BBQ Restaurants: It's assumed BBQ is for 2+ people. In reality, there are Saemaeul Sikdang (새마을식당)·Hanam Pig House (하남돼지집)·Junggye Gogipati (#179) solo sets for enthusiasts.

3. "Bars·Pochas (포차) = 2+ People" — Unaware of Solo Pocha (포차) Enthusiasts: It's assumed pochas (street food tents/stalls) are for 2+ people. In reality, there are Jongno 3-ga (종로3가) pochas (포차) + Euljiro Nogari Alley (을지로 노가리골목) solo counters + full solo makgeolli (막걸리)·soju (소주)·beer experiences.
