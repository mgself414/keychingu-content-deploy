🎯 How to Use This Guide

You put your card on the convenience store counter and say "Top up, please" (chungjeon-iyo / 충전이요), and the clerk asks back "How much?" (eolmayo? / 얼마요?). How much should you load? Do you tap again when you get off the bus? Do you get leftover money back? This guide covers only the rules from buying one T-money card to getting your money back. Choosing which card to buy is in #1298, subway ticket machines and gate moves are in #1281, the order for boarding a city bus is in #1271, and the full map of transport options is in #430.

The figures in this issue are values seen on 2026-11-05 on the Tmoney user guide and foreign-traveler pages and on the Seoul Metropolitan Government transportation page. The Tmoney guide pages show no effective date, so they are recorded as published that day. Base fares for subways and buses change often and are not listed here — the ticket machine or app screen is the reference.

① Buy — Travel Card 4,000 won, and the Card Price Is Not Refunded

  • The T-money Travel Card for foreign visitors costs 4,000 won, sold at convenience stores and Incheon Airport bus ticket counters. The WOWPASS combo card (TRAVEL CARD+) is 6,000 won per the Tmoney foreign-traveler page, sold at convenience stores; you top it up through the app or an unmanned currency exchange machine, and withdrawing money back to won is also possible. The former KOREA TOUR CARD is no longer sold (Tmoney foreign-traveler page).
  • The price of a card in normal working condition is not refunded (Tmoney). A defective card is the exception if it is within 2 years of the first top-up. If you will only use it for a few days, add the 4,000 won card price to your transport budget — for the comparison of the 3 cards and how to choose, see #1298.
  • If you pay only for the card, you top up separately (for the buy-then-top-up order, see #1298). Go straight to ②.

② Top Up — With Cash, 1,000~90,000 won at a Time

  • Where: convenience stores, subway stations, street stands with a Tmoney sticker, and partner ATMs. Cash is the default (Tmoney top-up guide). At a convenience store you hand over the card and say the amount, and that is all; for the on-screen steps at the station's unmanned top-up machine, see #1298.
  • How much: 1,000~90,000 won per top-up, and the balance the card can hold is up to 500,000 won (Tmoney).
  • Topping up by card works only at Woori Bank ATMs and GS25, and only with Woori credit or debit cards issued by Woori Bank (10,000~50,000 won, Tmoney). The guide mentions no foreign-issued cards, so top up with cash. If you want to fill the card with a card payment and no currency exchange, the WOWPASS combo card is the route (①). The new subway ticket machines accepting foreign cards applies to buying the Climate Card short-term pass, not to T-money top-ups (#1298).
  • Load 1~2 days of fares at a time, based on the fares shown on the ticket machine or app. The more you load, the easier it is to exceed the convenience store limit when you get a refund in ⑥.

③ Tap — When You Board and When You Get Off, Both

The Seoul transfer rules (Seoul transportation, revised 2026-02-02) come down to four lines.

  • Tap on boarding and on exiting. If you do not tap when you get off, the transfer breaks from that vehicle, and at your next boarding the untapped trip is processed as a single ride, so you may pay an extra base fare.
  • Transfer time: boarding the next vehicle within 30 minutes of getting off counts as a transfer; from 21:00 to 07:00 it is 60 minutes. The measure is the boarding time of the next vehicle.
  • Number of transfers: up to 4 per card (5 boardings).
  • Riding the same route or the same vehicle again gets no transfer discount.
  • Fares are distance-based: past the 10km base zone (30km for wide-area buses), 100 won is added every 5km. Riding too long in one trip (5 hours on subways and city buses, 1 hour on village buses) adds the base fare again when you exit (Seoul Metropolitan Government).

For the move of exiting by the back door and touching the reader, and boarding by the front door — #1271. For gate direction and transfer signs, see #1281.

④ Use — Nationwide Subways and Buses Yes, Intercity Buses No

  • Transport: subways in Seoul, Gyeonggi, Incheon, Daejeon, Daegu, Busan, and Gwangju, most city buses nationwide (including Jeju), and express buses are accepted, but intercity buses are not (Tmoney where-to-use page). Taxis work by region, such as major cities and counties in Seoul and Gyeonggi (Tmoney) — for hailing with an app, see #1273. AREX standard trains also run on a transit card (Tmoney where-to-use page · #1249).
  • KTX is not a train you board by tapping a transit card; you need a Korail ticket (#1287). At a Korail ticket window, only a nationwide-compatible card can pay the fare with Tmoney, and a regular T-money card cannot (Tmoney where-to-use page).
  • Outside transport: payments work at convenience stores such as GS25, CU, 7-Eleven, and Emart24, at cafes and bakeries such as Starbucks and Paris Baguette, at subway-station drink vending machines, and at unmanned parcel lockers (Tmoney where-to-use page). At the counter, just say "By T-money" (T-money-ro-yo / 티머니로요). Spending the leftover balance at a convenience store is often faster than ⑥.

⑤ Check — Your Balance at a Convenience Store Counter or in the App

  • When you top up or pay at a convenience store, the balance prints on the receipt or shows on the terminal. You can also say "Check my balance, please" (janaek hwagin-iyo / 잔액 확인이요) at the counter.
  • On an Android phone, in the 'Tmoney Card' menu of the Mobile Tmoney app, hold the physical card to the back of the phone and the balance appears (Tmoney). The Tmoney guide does not say whether sign-up is required.

⑥ Get a Refund — 500 won Fee, and Convenience Stores Have a Limit

The refund rules below are the Tmoney guide for regular cards. There is no notice that the same terms apply to the Travel Card or the WOWPASS combo card, so ask where you bought it, or dial 120, then press 9. For withdrawing from the WOWPASS combo card, see #1298.

  • The balance is returned minus a 500 won fee (Tmoney regular card refund guide). The card price is not refunded.
  • The convenience store limit differs by chain: GS25 under 20,000 won, 7-Eleven 20,000 won or less, CU and Emart24 30,000 won or less (Tmoney). Above that, get it at a subway station customer service center (partial refund of 10,000~50,000 won) or at Tmoney Town (headquarters). Above 50,000 won, Tmoney Town is the safe choice, and a bank ATM refund needs an account in your own name (Tmoney).
  • Tip: if you bring the balance under 20,000 won the day before departure, any convenience store will refund it. Spend the last 1,000~2,000 won at a convenience store, as in ④.
  • For the balance on a broken card, if you hand in a refund envelope by mail or at a station, it is deposited within 10 business days into the account written on the envelope (Tmoney). The guide does not say whether you can receive it in an overseas account, so for travelers the realistic choice is to spend the balance before leaving the country.

⑦ Mobile and Lost Cards

  • Android: the balance of a prepaid Mobile Tmoney is stored on the USIM, and changing the USIM means no refund (Tmoney notice 2026-04-07, LG U+ Android basis). The Tmoney guide does not say whether foreigners can use it with an overseas or temporary USIM.
  • iPhone: the Tmoney transit card in Apple Wallet launched on 2025-07-22. According to press reports, top-ups in Wallet work only with Hyundai Card, while the iOS Tmoney app accepts other cards too (press reports). The Tmoney guide does not say whether it can be topped up with a foreign-issued card — for a short stay, the physical card is simpler.
  • Lost card: a card not pre-registered with the lost/stolen service gets no balance back if you lose it (Tmoney lost-card guide). Registration requires signing up as a website member, and the guide does not say whether the Travel Card is eligible — which is why you should not keep a large amount on it.
  • Inquiries: Tmoney customer center 1644-0088, weekdays 09:00~18:00. For foreign-language help, dial 120 and press 9 (Tmoney guide). For tourism in general, 1330 (#1299).

⚠ 4 Common Mistakes

1. Not tapping when you get off — the transfer breaks and you may pay an extra base fare at the next boarding (③).

2. Loading more than 100,000 won at once — the per-top-up cap is 90,000 won, and loading a lot runs into the convenience store refund limit (20,000~30,000 won) (②·⑥).

3. Trying to board at an intercity bus terminal with T-money — intercity buses are not accepted. Express buses are (④).

4. Expecting the 4,000 won card price back too — the card price is not refunded (①).

⚙ 4 FAQs

Q1. Can I top up with a foreign credit card?

The card top-up in the Tmoney guide is limited to Woori Bank ATMs and GS25 with cards issued by Woori Bank. Foreign card top-ups are not in the guide, so top up with cash. If you want to fill it by card payment, see the WOWPASS combo card (①) or the card comparison issue (#1298).

Q2. Can two people ride on one card?

The transfer discount is based on one person per card (Seoul Metropolitan Government). On buses, if you tell the driver at each boarding that it is a multi-passenger ride, the same group continuing the same trip gets the transfer discount (Seoul Metropolitan Government), but on the subway it is one person per card (#1281). The simplest way is to buy as many cards as there are people.

Q3. I have 70,000 won left and I fly out tomorrow.

That exceeds the convenience store limit. Get a partial refund in 10,000 won increments (10,000~50,000 won) at a subway station customer service center and spend the rest at convenience stores, or go to Tmoney Town (⑥). Next time, bring the balance under 20,000 won on the last day.

Q4. Does the same card work in Busan and Jeju?

Yes. It works on Busan subway Lines 1~4, Jeju buses, and most city buses and subways nationwide (Tmoney where-to-use page). For reading Busan routes, see #1325.
