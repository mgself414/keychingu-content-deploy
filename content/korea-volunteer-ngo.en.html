Three mistakes foreign visitors make

1. "Korea's NGOs are small" — underestimating the scale: in reality, KOICA dispatches 1,000+ volunteers a year, Good Neighbors runs projects in 60 countries, and World Vision Korea has around 700,000 domestic sponsors. Korea is the world's only country to have transitioned from an ODA recipient to an ODA donor — and that infrastructure exists domestically too.

2. "Volunteering in Korea means Korean only" — missing the English channels: KOICA Young Professionals, foreign registration on the 1365 portal, Good Neighbors' English volunteering track, and international work camps (SCI Korea) can all be applied to and joined in English. Korean is not required.

3. "Volunteering here is only short-term" — missing the long-stay options: an F-1-D digital nomad visa (up to 2 years) combined with volunteering, a KOICA Young Professionals internship of 6-12 months, and university exchange volunteer teams by semester are all viable. Visa conditions should always be confirmed individually with KOICA or the immigration office.

Institution-by-institution deep dive

1. KOICA (Korea International Cooperation Agency, 한국국제협력단)

What it does

  • Plans, runs, and evaluates ODA projects for developing countries
  • Supports project planning and research from its domestic headquarters in Seongnam's Sujeong-gu
  • Runs international development cooperation education programs

How foreign visitors can take part

  • KOICA Young Professionals (YP): ages 20-35, preference given to those with a background in international development. English application and interview. 6-12 month internship.
  • KOICA Civil Society Cooperation Office: MOU-based cooperation with foreign NGOs and research institutions — organization-level partnerships possible.
  • Individual volunteering: the official volunteering program mainly targets Korean nationals. Foreign visitors are advised to pursue the Young Professionals route instead.

Cost: Young Professionals — a modest internship stipend is provided (amount varies by cohort; confirm when applying)

How to apply: koica.go.kr, then the recruitment/internship postings, then submit an English application

Access: 825 Daewangpangyo-ro, Sujeong-gu, Seongnam / a 15-minute walk from Moran Station on subway line 8, or by bus

Visa: a short-term tourist visa (within 90 days) is fine for the interview. Once an internship is confirmed, conversion to a D-2 or C-4 visa is required — confirm individually with the immigration office.

2. The UN in Korea — the UN campus in Incheon Songdo (송도)

What it does

  • More than 15 UN and international agencies are clustered in Songdo, Incheon's Yeonsu-gu
  • Key agencies include UNESCAP, UNDP, WFP, UN Women, UNHCR, UNICEF, and WHO's Western Pacific office
  • Regularly runs training, conferences, and fellowship programs

How foreign visitors can take part

  • UN internships: individually posted on each agency's official site. Language: English required, French an advantage at some agencies.
  • Conference attendance: some open events allow foreign participation with advance registration — check UNESCAP's website for its public event schedule.
  • UN Week (around October 24 each year): a public event marking the UN's founding, with free to low-cost participation.

Cost: internships are generally unpaid (some agencies offer a modest stipend). Conference fees are separate.

How to apply: unincheon.org, or each agency's official recruitment portal (such as jobs.undp.org)

Access: Art Center-daero, Yeonsu-gu, Incheon / Incheon Airport Railroad plus subway line 1 to International Business District Station, 10-minute walk

Fit for foreign visitors: an English-first environment with an international network, no Korean required. The #1 pick for #193 European and #194 American enthusiasts.

3. Good Neighbors (굿네이버스)

What it does

  • Domestic social welfare services plus development projects in 60 countries
  • Child sponsorship, education, water, and health programs
  • Domestic volunteering runs through its Seocho-gu, Seoul headquarters and regional branches nationwide

How foreign visitors can take part

  • English-language volunteer applications available (gni.kr, then the Volunteer menu)
  • Short-term volunteering (a day to a few weeks): child education, event support, translation help
  • Long-term volunteering: arranged through direct discussion

Cost: free to volunteer. Overseas dispatch programs carry a separate cost.

Language: English communication available through its international cooperation office. Fluency in Korean widens the range of activities available.

How to apply: search for Good Neighbors programs on gni.kr or the 1365 volunteer portal

Fit for foreign visitors: translation, interpretation, and global education activities are areas where foreign volunteers have a natural advantage.

4. Korea Food for the Hungry International (KFHI) and World Vision Korea

Korea Food for the Hungry International

  • Development programs for children, families, and communities across 43 countries
  • Domestic office: Mapo-gu, Seoul
  • Volunteering: event support, translation, and promotional work

World Vision Korea (월드비전코리아)

  • The Korean chapter of an international NGO (World Vision's global headquarters is in Geneva, Switzerland)
  • Child sponsorship, disaster relief, and advocacy work
  • Domestic volunteering: through its Jongno-gu, Seoul headquarters and regional branches

How foreign visitors can take part

  • Both organizations accept volunteer applications via the 1365 portal or their own websites
  • Confirm English communication availability in advance (email the international team contact)

Cost: free to volunteer

How to apply: kfhi.or.kr / worldvision.or.kr

5. The 1365 Volunteer Portal (1365 자원봉사포털) — foreign registration

Overview

  • A nationwide integrated volunteering platform run by the Ministry of the Interior and Safety
  • Search, apply, and track participation across thousands of programs nationwide

How foreign visitors register

1. Go to 1365.go.kr

2. Sign up using your foreign registration number (an Alien Registration Card is required)

3. Search by region, activity type, and date, then apply

4. Accumulated hours can be turned into an official volunteering certificate

Note: without an Alien Registration Card (held by long-term visa holders such as D-4 or D-2), short-term participation as a tourist varies by program — short-term tourists should contact the host organization directly.

Language: the portal is mostly in Korean, with limited English guidance — direct contact with organizations is recommended alongside it.

6. Temple volunteering — Jingwansa and Haeinsa

Jingwansa (진관사) — Eunpyeong-gu, Seoul

  • A temple within Seoul offering a traditional Korean practice setting
  • Combines a temple stay (templestay, 템플스테이) with volunteering
  • Foreign temple stay booking: templestay.com (English booking available)
  • Volunteer activities: cleaning, meal preparation, and event support — either bundled into the program or applied for separately
  • Cost: a one-night temple stay runs 80,000-100,000 won, meals included
  • Access: subway line 3 to Yeonsinnae Station, then a 20-minute bus transfer

Haeinsa (해인사) — Hapcheon County, South Gyeongsang Province

  • Home to the UNESCO-listed Tripitaka Koreana (Palman Daejanggyeong, 팔만대장경)
  • Runs long-term volunteering and practice programs (one week to several months)
  • Foreign participation: advance application required. Korean and English both available.
  • Cost: free to low-cost (some programs include meals and lodging — confirm individually)
  • Access: from Daegu's Dongbu bus terminal, roughly a 1.5-hour bus ride to Hapcheon

Fit for foreign visitors: English-speaking staff available. Combining traditional Korean culture with volunteering makes this a top pick for #193 and #367 enthusiasts.

7. International work camps — SCI Korea and CCIVS

Overview

  • SCI (Service Civil International) Korea: 2-4 week intensive short-term volunteer camps
  • Multiple programs run each summer and winter season
  • Themes span rural, urban, environmental, and cultural heritage restoration work nationwide

How foreign visitors can take part

  • Search and apply via SCI Korea's official site (scikorea.org) or work camp matching platforms (workcamp.org, workcampingkorea.or.kr)
  • Application language: English. Korean is not required.
  • Lodging: included in most programs, provided by the host organization

Cost: participation fee of 100,000-250,000 won (meals and lodging included). Airfare and transit are separate.

Visa: a short-term tourist visa (within 90 days) works. Confirm the visa fits your intended length of stay.

Fit for foreign visitors: an English-first, multinational environment with lodging included makes this an ideal entry point for foreign volunteers.

8. University volunteer teams — Yonsei, Korea, and Seoul National University

Overview

  • Major Korean universities pair exchange student programs with volunteer teams
  • Yonsei's Office of International Affairs, Korea University's Global Service Learning, and Seoul National University's Volunteer Corps all run their own programs

How foreign visitors can take part

  • Exchange students can join a volunteer team while enrolled
  • Some universities accept non-enrolled foreign volunteers as well — contact the international affairs office by email
  • Activities: multicultural education, environmental work, and community support

Cost: free (some programs partially cover transit costs, which varies by program)

How to apply: each university's international affairs office website or email

Fit for foreign visitors: English-friendly and multinational, based in Seoul, and can run alongside an exchange program.

Practical info — applications, language, visas

Application channels

Visa practicalities

  • Short-term tourist (visa-free, C-3): up to 90 days. Work camps, temple stays, and short-term volunteering are fine. Paid activity is prohibited.
  • D-2 (student visa): for exchange students. University volunteer team activity is allowed.
  • D-4 (language study): language school enrollment plus volunteering can run together.
  • F-1-D (digital nomad): introduced in 2024, up to 2 years. Remote work plus volunteering can run together, but income must come from an overseas employer.
  • Note: the scope of allowed volunteering and any paid-work restrictions vary by visa type — always confirm individually with the Immigration and Korea Immigration Service (1345).

Language preparation

  • English is sufficient for: KOICA Young Professionals, UN internships, SCI work camps, temple volunteering (English-run programs), and Good Neighbors' international team
  • Korean helps for: browsing the 1365 portal, local welfare center volunteering, and elder care fieldwork
  • Translation apps: Papago (strong for Korean) and DeepL used together are recommended

FAQ 5

Q1. Can I volunteer on a tourist visa (visa-free entry)?

Short-term unpaid volunteering is mostly allowed. However, activities involving a paid internship or employment contract require an appropriate work visa. When the line is unclear, confirm with the Immigration and Korea Immigration Service (call 1345).

Q2. Are there programs I can join with no Korean at all?

Yes. KOICA Young Professionals, UN internships, SCI work camps, temple stay volunteering, Good Neighbors' international team, and university exchange volunteer teams all run in English or allow English communication. The 1365 portal is mostly Korean, so pairing it with a direct email to the organization works well.

Q3. Can any nationality apply for KOICA Young Professionals?

In principle there is no nationality restriction, though recruitment conditions can vary by cohort. Always check the eligibility requirements in the posting on koica.go.kr.

Q4. Can I get a certificate proving my volunteer work?

Activities registered on the 1365 portal generate a certificate based on accumulated hours. For volunteering done directly through an organization, request a certificate from that organization. KOICA Young Professionals issues an internship completion certificate at the end.

Q5. Can a 2-4 week short stay still produce meaningful volunteering?

An SCI Korea work camp (2-4 weeks intensive), temple volunteering (3 days to 1 week programs), and event support for Good Neighbors or World Vision (single-day slots) all suit short-term foreign visitors well. Coordinating your schedule with the organization in advance matters most.

Recommended routes

[A] A one-week full package for foreign volunteering enthusiasts (#193, #194, #371)

  • D1: visit KOICA's Seongnam headquarters, gather Young Professionals information
  • D2: UN's Incheon Songdo campus, check UNESCAP's public event schedule
  • D3: Good Neighbors and World Vision Korea's Seoul headquarters, apply to volunteer
  • D4: Jingwansa temple volunteering (doable as a day trip from Seoul, see #463 wellness)
  • D5: visit Yonsei and Korea University's international affairs offices, ask about volunteer teams
  • D6: register on the 1365 portal, apply for an SCI work camp
  • D7: transfer to Incheon Airport

[B] An international organization-focused course (#194, #193)

  • Visit the UN's Songdo campus, target UN Week in October, track internship postings, apply in parallel to KOICA Young Professionals

[C] A short-term work camp introduction (#371, #193)

  • Apply for an SCI Korea summer or winter camp, spend 2-3 weeks volunteering outside Seoul, register the hours on 1365

[D] Temple volunteering plus wellness (#367, #463)

  • Jingwansa (Seoul) plus Haeinsa (Hapcheon, South Gyeongsang Province): a 1-2 week combination of practice and volunteering

Five tips for foreign visitors

1. KOICA is Korea's #1 official volunteering body — Young Professionals delivers 6-12 months of real work experience. Check koica.go.kr's postings regularly.

2. UN Songdo is an English-first international environment — 15+ agencies clustered on a single campus. Search each agency's internship postings individually.

3. SCI work camps are the best short-term entry point for foreigners — English application, lodging included, multinational setting, 2-4 weeks.

4. The 1365 portal covers thousands of programs nationwide — register directly if you hold an Alien Registration Card; otherwise contact organizations by email.

5. Temple volunteering combines culture with service — book in English via templestay.com. Both Jingwansa (Seoul, day trip) and Haeinsa (Hapcheon, overnight or longer) welcome foreign visitors.
