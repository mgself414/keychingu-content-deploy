Korean Hoe (회): The Complete 6-Type Guide — Hwaleo vs Sukseong, Olive Flounder, Yellowtail, Tuna, Salmon, Sea Bream, and Gizzard Shad

Subtitle: A foreign-tourist guide to Korean hoe (raw fish) — hwaleo vs sukseong, the 6-type overview, and how it differs from Japanese sushi

ISSUE 03 · #864 · 2026-06-09

🎯 Why This Guide on Korean Hoe

The #1 pain point for foreign visitors: assuming Korean hoe (회, raw fish) is "the same as Japanese sushi." It isn't. Korean hoe centers on hwaleo (활어, live fish) served fresh-killed, paired with chojang (초장, a chili-vinegar dip) and ssam (쌈, wrapping the fish in leafy greens) — rooted in market culture at Noryangjin (노량진) and Jagalchi (자갈치). Japanese sushi, by contrast, is sukseong (숙성, aged fish) served over rice with soy sauce; Korean hoe pairs hwaleo with chojang and ssam. Under the same "Korean sashimi" label, Gwang-eo (광어, the #1 hwaleo pick), Bang-eo (방어, the winter classic from Jagalchi), Chamchi-Otoro (참치 뱃살, the #1 fatty cut), Yeon-eo (연어, the global favorite), Do-mi (도미, a hanjeongsik classic), and Jeon-eo (전어, the autumn classic) each have their own season and following. Most foreign visitors don't know: (a) the hwaleo vs sukseong difference, (b) chojang vs wasabi (와사비), (c) ssam culture, or (d) the maeuntang (매운탕, spicy fish stew) course that follows. This guide covers the 6-type overview, hwaleo vs sukseong, and a full Korean-style hoe spread.

🐟 Hwaleo vs Sukseong — Korean Hoe vs Japanese Sushi

🔄 Key Differences at a Glance

Axis 🇰🇷 Korean Hoe (Hwaleo) 🇯🇵 Japanese Sushi (Sukseong)
State Hwaleo (live fish, killed just before serving) Sukseong (aged, 1–3 days)
Texture Chewy, firm Soft, rich umami
Seasoning Chojang, ssam, garlic Shoyu (soy sauce), wasabi, ginger
Sides Ssam vegetables, maeuntang, soju (소주, Korean distilled liquor) Sushi rice, miso soup
Atmosphere Traditional market stalls, old-school bars Formal sushi-ya, omakase
Price Market = budget-friendly / hoe restaurant = mid-range Formal omakase = luxury

🎯 Key Points for Foreign Visitors

  • Korean hoe = hwaleo = "chewy texture," served fresh-killed
  • Japanese sushi = sukseong = "umami," aged 1–3 days
  • Tuna and salmon are sukseong (chilled-aged) even in Korea
  • Bang-eo, gwang-eo, do-mi, and jeon-eo are always hwaleo

🐠 Deep Dive: The 6 Types

🐠 Gwang-eo (광어) Hoe — The #1 Starter

  • Signature: Hwaleo classic, available year-round, the #1 pick at hoe restaurants
  • What it is: Live gwang-eo (olive flounder), served with chojang and ssam vegetables
  • Foreign-visitor friendliness: ★★★★★ (#1 starter, globally recognized)
  • Where to try it:
  • Noryangjin Fish Market (노량진수산시장) — year-round, the #1 spot
  • Jagalchi Market (자갈치시장) (Busan) — traditional
  • Hoe restaurant clusters (Gangnam, Hongdae)
  • Season: Peak in January (dead of winter)
  • Price: Market = ₩20,000–40,000 (~$15–30) per kg / hoe restaurant = ₩50,000–120,000 (~$37–90) per person

🐟 Bang-eo (방어) Hoe — The #1 Winter Pick

  • Signature: The #1 winter classic, #1 at Jagalchi and Noryangjin, richly fatty
  • What it is: Live bang-eo (yellowtail), served with chojang and ssam vegetables
  • Foreign-visitor friendliness: ★★★★★ (winter classic)
  • Where to try it:
  • Jagalchi Market (Busan) — the #1 spot
  • Noryangjin Fish Market — traditional
  • Samcheok (삼척) and Guryongpo (구룡포) — East Coast classics
  • Season: Dec–Feb (the #1 winter pick)
  • Price: Market = ₩30,000–60,000 per kg / hoe restaurant = ₩60,000–150,000 per person

🍣 Chamchi-Otoro (참치 뱃살) — The #1 Fatty Cut

  • Signature: The #1 fatty cut, toro grade, MZ-generation (MZ, Korea's Gen Z and Millennials, roughly born 1980s–2000s) Instagram favorite, June peak
  • What it is: Tuna belly (toro) and lean cuts (akami), served with wasabi and soy sauce
  • Foreign-visitor friendliness: ★★★★★ (globally known toro, #1 for Instagram)
  • Where to try it:
  • Tuna specialty restaurants (Gangnam, Apgujeong, Hongdae) — traditional
  • All-you-can-eat tuna — budget-friendly
  • Noryangjin Fish Market — pre-dawn wholesale
  • Season: Peak in June, available year-round (frozen)
  • Price: Formal tuna restaurant = ₩80,000–200,000 per person / all-you-can-eat = ₩30,000–50,000

🟠 Yeon-eo (연어) Hoe — The Global Favorite

  • Signature: Globally familiar, MZ-generation Instagram favorite, available year-round
  • What it is: Salmon (imported from Norway or Chile), served with wasabi and soy sauce
  • Foreign-visitor friendliness: ★★★★ (the #1 global favorite)
  • Where to try it:
  • All-you-can-eat salmon (Hongdae, Gangnam, Sinchon) — the #1 budget option
  • Salmon specialty restaurants — traditional
  • Season: Year-round (sukseong-aged)
  • Price: All-you-can-eat = ₩25,000–40,000 per person / formal = ₩40,000–80,000

🐡 Do-mi (도미) Hoe — The Hanjeongsik Classic

  • Signature: A hanjeongsik (한정식, Korean set-course meal) course classic, peak in January and December
  • What it is: Live do-mi (sea bream), served with chojang and ssam vegetables
  • Foreign-visitor friendliness: ★★★★ (a classic hanjeongsik course dish)
  • Where to try it: Hanjeongsik course restaurants (see the related hanjeongsik guide), Gangnam hoe restaurants, Jagalchi Market
  • Season: Spring (April), winter (January, December)
  • Price: ₩50,000–120,000 per person

🐠 Jeon-eo (전어) Hoe — The Autumn Classic

  • Signature: "Even a daughter-in-law who left home comes back for the smell of autumn jeon-eo grilling" — peak Sep–Oct
  • What it is: Live jeon-eo (gizzard shad), served with chojang and ssam vegetables
  • Foreign-visitor friendliness: ★★★★ (autumn classic)
  • Where to try it:
  • Ganghwa Jeon-eo Village (강화 전어 마을) — the #1 autumn spot
  • Sorae Port (소래포구) (Incheon) — autumn favorite
  • Jagalchi Market — traditional
  • Season: Sep–Oct (the #1 autumn pick)
  • Price: Market = ₩15,000–30,000 per kg / hoe restaurant = ₩30,000–60,000 per person

🍽 A Full Korean-Style Hoe Spread

The Complete Korean Hoe Spread

1. Hoe (회) — the main dish (gwang-eo, bang-eo, do-mi, etc.)

2. Chojang (초장) — gochujang (고추장, chili paste) mixed with vinegar, the traditional Korean dip

3. Wasabi (와사비) — a Japanese-style option

4. Ssam vegetables — lettuce, perilla leaves, napa cabbage (for wrapping the fish)

5. Garlic, chili, and doenjang (된장, soybean paste) — side accompaniments

6. Maeuntang (매운탕) — a spicy fish stew served last

7. Soju, beer, or somaek (소맥, a soju-beer mix) — optional drink pairing

8. Steamed rice — to finish the meal

The Formal Hanjeongsik Hoe Course

  • The 6-course hanjeongsik meal (see the related hanjeongsik guide) includes hoe in its first or second course
  • Try it at The Shilla Hotel's La Yeon (신라호텔 라연) (Michelin 3-star), Samcheonggak (삼청각), or Insadong (인사동) hanjeongsik restaurants

⚠ 5 Common Mistakes Foreign Visitors Make

1. "Korean hoe = Japanese sushi": Korean hoe is hwaleo-based, served with chojang and ssam; Japanese sushi is sukseong-based, served with soy sauce and rice. They're different dishes.

2. "Hwaleo just means fresher": Hwaleo delivers a chewy texture from fresh-killed fish; sukseong delivers umami from aging.

3. "Bang-eo is available year-round": Bang-eo peaks in winter, Dec–Feb — outside that window it's typically farmed or frozen.

4. "Jeon-eo is just an ordinary hoe": Jeon-eo peaks in autumn, Sep–Oct (hence the Korean saying about its grilling smell).

5. "Hoe is the whole meal": The Korean way pairs hoe with a maeuntang course — a spicy fish stew to finish.

⚙ 8 FAQs

1. Best for first-timers: Gwang-eo and yeon-eo (globally familiar) → bang-eo (winter) → tuna (for fatty-cut fans).

2. Best for Instagram/vlogging: Bang-eo at Jagalchi, dawn at Noryangjin, tuna toro.

3. MZ-generation trends: All-you-can-eat salmon, tuna toro, MZ-favorite hoe restaurants.

4. Winter favorites: Bang-eo (#1), plus gwang-eo and do-mi.

5. Autumn favorites: Jeon-eo (#1), at Ganghwa and Sorae Port.

6. Allergies: Ask staff in advance about fish and shellfish cross-contamination.

7. Payment and tipping: Markets take cash and card / hoe restaurants take card / no tipping.

8. VAT refund: Food and drink generally aren't eligible for VAT refund.

⚠ Medical, Safety, and Dietary Disclaimer

Hoe is raw, uncooked fish — take food-safety precautions. Pregnant travelers should avoid hoe (per CDC guidance). Children should have only cooked fish (age 5+). Disclose allergies (fish, shellfish, and soy-based gluten in sauces) in advance. If you suspect food poisoning, call 1339 (medical hotline, English available). This guide is general information, not medical advice.

Dietary accommodation notes:

  • Halal / Kosher: All 6 fish types are generally considered halal (opinions vary by authority — confirm with the restaurant); for kosher, all 6 (gwang-eo, bang-eo, do-mi, salmon, tuna, jeon-eo) have fins and scales, so they qualify.
  • Vegetarian / Vegan: None of the 6 types are vegetarian; try bibimbap or temple food instead.
  • Gluten-free: Hoe itself is gluten-free; soy sauce contains gluten (ask for tamari as a substitute).
