① Why Kakao T Is the Default

In Korea's big cities, flagging down an empty taxi on the street is fading fast, and ride-hailing apps have become the standard. Kakao T has by far the largest user base and vehicle pool among Koreans, so it gets you matched the fastest. The problem is payment — the whole structure is built around Korean phone numbers and Korean-issued cards, so foreign tourists can usually sign up fine but often get stuck at card registration. The core of this edition is confronting that exact point head-on.

③ The Real Trap — "My Card Won't Register" → Pay the Driver Directly

Kakao T's default payment method is auto-pay: the card you registered in the app is automatically charged when you get off. To register auto-pay, the payment screen requires another round of identity verification (SMS), and as of April 2026 that verification is designed around numbers activated in Korea. Even if you succeeded in signing up with an overseas number back in ②, getting stuck here again is completely normal — it's not your card's fault, and you didn't do anything wrong.

The standard workaround: pay the driver directly (on-site payment)

  • On the "Register payment method (결제수단 등록)" screen, don't keep trying to add a card — move on by tapping [ Later (나중에) ].
  • If you leave the account with zero cards registered, requesting a ride automatically switches to "pay the driver directly" mode.
  • When you get off, pay directly on the driver's card terminal or pay cash — terminal payment is covered in ⑥.
  • Some reviews mention getting a card to register by linking an overseas Kakao Pay account, but this isn't reliably reproducible. This guide recommends not wasting time on retries and instead treating direct payment as the standard path.

⑥ Payment on Arrival, Car Types, and Late-Night Surcharges

Overseas cards at the terminal

  • Most taxi card terminals accept overseas cards like Visa and Mastercard — outright card rejection is rare.
  • Overseas-issued cards usually have a usage cap: around 500,000 won per month and 200,000 won per transaction (varies by issuer and product). Long-distance fares like airport-to-downtown can get close to that per-transaction cap, so checking your card issuer's limits before you leave is a safe move.
  • If a card is declined: retry with a different card → if that still fails, pay cash.

Car types

Car type Character
Regular Standard mid-size taxi
Blue Mid-size, Kakao-affiliated, higher matching priority
Black Large premium sedan
Venti Large van — for lots of luggage or passengers

The exact fare multiplier changes by time period and region. Trusting the estimated fare shown on screen as-is is the safest bet.

Late-night surcharge: A surcharge is automatically added during late-night hours (the exact time window and surcharge rate can be adjusted by municipality and time period). It's already factored into the estimated fare shown in the app, so you don't need to calculate it yourself — the number on screen is what you'll actually pay.

⑦ How to Get There Without Saying a Word

Your destination was already sent to the driver's screen through the app during the ④ request step. You don't need to say it again after boarding.

  • One greeting when you board ("annyeonghaseyo (안녕하세요)" — hello) is enough — the driver's navigation handles the rest.
  • If you need to confirm something, showing a screenshot can stand in for speaking.
  • You can watch the route in real time on the app's map, so you can check it without a word.

⑨ 2 Panic Scenarios — What Actually Happens, and How to Cope

1. No driver gets matched, no matter how long you wait — Try switching the car type from Regular to Blue, move the pickup pin to a bigger road, or if it's peak time (commute hours, late night) wait a few minutes and retry. If that still doesn't work, switch to Plan B in ⑩.

2. It feels like the route is taking a strange detour — The real-time route is shown right there on the app's map, so you can just quietly check it. If the driver is clearly about to drop you somewhere else, point at the destination on screen and say "here," or file a report through the app's inquiry/report feature after you get off.

⑩ Plan B — When the App Just Doesn't Work

  • Empty taxis on the street: If the roof light is on (the color varies by car type and region), it's empty. You can flag it down by raising your hand — write your destination on paper or show it on a translation app screen.
  • International taxi: Major airports like Incheon have an international taxi service built around advance booking and English-speaking drivers. A good option when you want something reliably arranged in advance, like an airport transfer.
