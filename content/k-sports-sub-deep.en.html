🎯 How to Use This Guide

This guide gathers 3 baseball stadiums, 3 football stadiums and museums, and 2 taekwondo facilities in Korea where you can watch a pro game or look around a sports venue. For the baseball stadiums, usage hours are registered as "varies by game"; for the football stadiums, museum and taekwondo facilities, opening hours are registered.

The operating details are copied from values registered in the Korea Tourism Organization tourism data (TourAPI). Game schedules and ticket prices are not in the Korea Tourism Organization data, so they are not included. The esports arena LoL Park is in the #613 Korea Game and Esports Guide.

📍 Baseball Stadiums — Jamsil Baseball Stadium, Sajik Baseball Stadium, Gocheok Sky Dome

Jamsil Baseball Stadium (19-2 Olympic-ro, Songpa-gu, Jamsil-dong, ☎ 02-2202-3834) opened in 1982 and is the home ground of the LG Twins and the Doosan Bears. According to the Korea Tourism Organization, it is Korea's representative baseball stadium and has also hosted Games 5, 6 and 7 of the Korean Series (한국시리즈). It is at Exits 5 and 6 of Sports Complex Station (종합운동장역) on Line 2.

  • Usage hours: Vary by game. Closed days: Off-season period.
  • Parking: On ordinary days 200 won per 5 minutes for small vehicles and 400 won per 5 minutes for large vehicles (free if you leave within 15 minutes); on pro baseball game days and pre-arranged large event days 6,000 won for small and 12,000 won for large (prepaid).

Sajik Baseball Stadium (45 Sajik-ro, Dongnae-gu, Busan, Sajik-dong, ☎ 051-590-9000) is a baseball stadium inside Sajik Sports Complex (사직종합운동장). It was completed in October 1985 and has been the home ground of the Lotte Giants since 1986. According to the Korea Tourism Organization, it was first built as a multipurpose stadium that could also host football and rugby, but it is now used only for baseball, and Lotte has managed it under contract since 2008. A Giants store is always open to the right of the central entrance and at the plaza parking lot, and on home game days one more store opens.

  • Usage hours and closed days: Vary by game.

Gocheok Sky Dome (430 Gyeongin-ro, Guro-gu, Gocheok-dong, ☎ 1666-3102) is a domed baseball stadium completed in 2015 and the home ground of the Kiwoom Heroes. According to the Korea Tourism Organization, it is Korea's first domed baseball stadium, and in 2017 it hosted the WBC (World Baseball Classic) in Korea for the first time. It has 2 basement floors and 4 floors above ground, with 16,783 seats, and it connects to a baseball-themed street.

  • Usage hours, fees and closed days: Vary by game or event.

📍 Football Stadiums — Seoul World Cup Stadium, Suwon World Cup Stadium, Football Museum

Seoul World Cup Stadium (240 World Cup-ro, Mapo-gu, Seongsan-dong, ☎ 02-2128-2002) is a multipurpose stadium built for the 2002 FIFA World Cup. According to the Korea Tourism Organization, it is an iconic place for Korean football where national team home matches and K League games are often held, and there are a park and cultural facilities around the stadium.

  • Seats: 66,704 in the main stadium, 1,012 in the auxiliary stadium.
  • Usage hours: 09:00~18:00. Closed days: Open all year.
  • Parking: 150 won per 5 minutes for small vehicles, 300 won per 5 minutes for medium vehicles, 450 won per 5 minutes for large vehicles (stadium parking lot, west side basis).

Suwon World Cup Stadium (310 World Cup-ro, Paldal-gu, Suwon-si, ☎ 031-202-2002) is a football-only stadium with 43,959 seats, built for the 2002 FIFA World Cup, and it is the home ground of the K League's Suwon Samsung Bluewings. According to the Korea Tourism Organization, it got the nickname "Big Bird" (빅 버드) from the large wing-shaped roof over the east and west stands and from the home team's nickname.

  • Usage hours: 09:00~18:00. Closed days: Open all year.
  • Parking: Paid.

Suwon World Cup Stadium Football Museum (310 World Cup-ro, Paldal-gu, Suwon-si, Uman-dong, ☎ 031-259-2070) is the World Cup Memorial Hall, opened to mark the hosting of the 2002 Korea-Japan World Cup, reopened as a football museum. According to the Korea Tourism Organization, you can see the history of Korean football and of North Korean and world football, from Korea's first football boots in 1882 to the Park Ji-sung (박지성) memorial corner.

  • Opening hours: 10:00~17:00. Closed days: Every Monday, January 1, the day of Seollal and Chuseok.
  • Fee: Free.
  • Parking (passenger cars): 600 won for the first 30 minutes, 300 won for each additional 10 minutes, 7,000 won per day.

📍 Taekwondo — Kukkiwon, Taekwondowon

Kukkiwon (32 Teheran-ro 7-gil, Gangnam-gu, Yeoksam-dong, ☎ 02-567-1058) is the World Taekwondo Headquarters, which opened on November 30, 1972. According to the Korea Tourism Organization, "guk-ki" means taekwondo as a country's own distinctive martial art, and "won" means house. The adjacent Kukkiwon Memorial Hall opened on November 30, 1991 and displays relics of taekwondo history. Kukkiwon is about 500m from Exit 12 of Gangnam Station (강남역).

  • Opening hours: Kukkiwon Mon~Thu 09:00~18:00, Fri 09:00~16:00. Memorial Hall 09:00~17:30 (lunch break 11:30~13:00).
  • Closed days: Weekends, public holidays.
  • Fee and parking: Free.

Taekwondowon (1482 Museol-ro, Seolcheon-myeon, Muju-gun, ☎ 063-320-0114) is a taekwondo-specialized space with a stadium, performance hall and training facilities, plus lodging and dining facilities. According to the Korea Tourism Organization, it is the first place for a single Olympic sport where you can compete, experience, train, study, research and exchange all in one spot. If you ride the monorail up to the observatory, you can look down over the whole of Taekwondowon.

  • Opening hours: 10:00~18:00 (last entry 17:00). Monorail 10:00~17:00 (last boarding 16:30).
  • Closed days: Every Monday, January 1, the day of Seollal and Chuseok and the day before. If a Monday or consecutive weekdays are public holidays, it closes on the next weekday.
  • Fee: Free admission. Yap! Experience Center (체험관 Yap!) taekwondo experience ticket 4,000 won, VR·AR experience ticket 6,000 won, combined ticket 8,000 won. Monorail adults (ages 19 and over) 4,000 won, children and teens (ages 3~18) 2,000 won.
  • Parking: Free.

⚠ 3 Things to Know Before You Go

  1. Baseball stadium hours are registered as varying by game — Jamsil Baseball Stadium closes during the off-season, and Sajik Baseball Stadium and Gocheok Sky Dome have usage hours registered as varying by game.
  2. Kukkiwon closes on weekends — it is registered as closed on weekends and public holidays. On Fridays it closes at 16:00.
  3. The museum and Taekwondowon close on Mondays — the Suwon Football Museum and Taekwondowon are closed every Monday.

⚙ FAQ 2

Q. Which places can I look around for free?

A. According to the Korea Tourism Organization's registered data, the Suwon World Cup Stadium Football Museum and Kukkiwon are free, and Taekwondowon has free admission (the Experience Center and monorail are paid).

Q. Which places are easy to reach by subway?

A. According to the Korea Tourism Organization's registered data, Jamsil Baseball Stadium is at Exits 5 and 6 of Sports Complex Station on Line 2, and Kukkiwon is about 500m from Exit 12 of Gangnam Station.
