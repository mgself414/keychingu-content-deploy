Korea City Bus, the Zero-Panic Manual — From the Bus-Stop Display to the Stop-Request Button

🎯 How to Read This Manual — Screen Notation Rules

The bus edition uses the same notation rules as #1270 (the kiosk edition). Rather than telling you "what to do," it writes down, exactly, "what appears at the stop and inside the bus, and in what order you move."

  • Bus-stop display (BIT) text and in-bus announcements appear in quotes: "3 minutes until arrival (도착예정 3분)"
  • Physical buttons you press appear in brackets: [ Stop Request (하차벨) ]
  • Actions you do by hand are bold: "Touch your transit card to the reader, lightly"

① Why You Need Buses, Not Just the Subway

Seoul's subway (#237) has dense coverage, but the gaps between stations, tucked-away alleys, and low-lying neighborhoods south of the Han River are often areas where the bus is the only public transit option. Fares are cheaper than or about the same as the subway, and with a transfer you can effectively reach your destination on a single fare. There's just one catch — stops are brief, and announcements are mostly Korean-only. This manual covers exactly what you need to avoid getting lost in that brief window.

② Reading the Bus-Stop Display (BIT) — A Screen Walkthrough

The bus-stop display (BIT) looks dense with information, but there are really only 5 elements you need to read.

Screen Element Example Meaning = What to Check
Large number (bus number) "143" Whether it matches your bus number down to every digit (bus 43 and bus 143 are different buses)
Direction label next to the number "Toward Uidong (우이동 방면)" The terminus direction — if it's the opposite direction, you need to board from the stop across the street
Estimated arrival time/distance "3 min" or "2 stops away" Time remaining — if it says "Arriving soon (곧 도착)," head out and wait right away
Congestion color (green/yellow/red) Red = crowded For reference only — you can still board even if it's red
Wheelchair icon Marks a low-floor bus If you have a stroller or large suitcase, prioritize boarding a bus with this icon

Some rural and small-town stops don't have a display. In that case, check the route map and interval schedule posted on the stop's pole instead.

③ The Bus Color System — Trunk, Branch, Wide-Area, Village

Korean buses can be told apart by body color alone. Even if you can't read the number, the color gives you a first-pass classification.

Type Color Where it runs Fare (with transit card)
Trunk (ganseon beoseu, 간선버스) Blue Major arterial roads in the city, relatively long routes About 1,500 won
Branch (jiseon beoseu, 지선버스) Green Connects trunk lines to villages, reaching deep into neighborhoods About 1,500 won (same as trunk)
Wide-area (gwangyeok beoseu, 광역버스) Red Long-distance Seoul↔Greater Seoul routes, often reserved-seating About 3,000 won
Village (maeul beoseu, 마을버스) Green (small vehicle) Every corner of the neighborhood; a district name precedes the number (e.g., Mapo02 (마포02)) About 1,200–1,500 won
  • Buses with a letter or district name before the number (village-bus type) are smaller with shorter routes — for short trips, these are often actually the better choice.
  • The exact fare varies by card type, age, and time of day, so use the table above only to get a rough sense.

④ Master Flow — From Boarding to Getting Off

Buses don't have separate brands, but no route strays from the sequence below.

Step Situation What to do Trap
1 Arrive at the stop; the display shows numbers and minutes Check your bus number and direction using the table in ② Even the same number, if it's the opposite direction, you need to board from a different stop
2 Bus arrives Board through the front door, tap your transit card on the reader Boarding through the back door is not allowed — your card won't register and you could be mistaken for fare evasion
3 While riding Sit or hold a handrail, and check your stop in advance Many routes only announce stops in Korean — checking a map app yourself is recommended
4 Approaching your stop Find the button and press [ Stop Request (하차벨) ] ahead of time If you don't press the button, the bus may pass your stop without stopping
5 Getting off Exit through the back door, tap your card on the reader again If you skip the exit tag, the loss structure in ⑤ applies

⑤ Why You Tag Twice — the Real Reason Skipping Costs You

Korean bus fares are based on distance traveled. You have to tag once when boarding and once when getting off so the system can calculate how many stops you rode and apply the correct fare and transfer discount.

  • Transfer discount condition: If you board another bus or the subway within 30 minutes of your exit tag, the transfer is free or discounted (up to 4 consecutive transfers).
  • If you don't tag when getting off: this ride gets charged at the maximum-distance fare, and your next ride starts fresh with the full base fare and no transfer discount. In other words, simply forgetting to tag actually costs you real money.
  • So this edition boils down to one rule — listen for the "beep" both when you board and when you get off, every time.

⑥ Cashless Buses — Plan B When You Don't Have a Card

Since a pilot launch in 2021, most Seoul city bus routes now effectively run as cashless buses. In other words, assuming "I have cash, so I'll be fine" right before boarding often doesn't work.

Situation What to do
You don't have a transit card yet Buy one immediately at a convenience store (CU, GS25, 7-Eleven, emart24) — card cost plus top-up, done in 3 minutes (see #238 for detailed steps)
You need a foreigner-specific card WOWPASS kiosks (Myeongdong, Incheon Airport, Hongdae) — handle currency exchange and card issuance in one step
You're wondering if contactless credit card payment works Some routes accept international Visa/Mastercard contactless tags, but this varies by route and is hard to confirm in advance — preparing a transit card ahead of time is the safer bet
You truly only have cash Show your situation to the driver and ask — cash acceptance varies by route and time of day, so buying a card at a convenience store is much faster and more reliable

On-screen Korean Meaning = What to Check
jeongnyujang (정류장) Bus stop
hachabel (하차벨) Stop request button — press it to make the bus stop
gyotongkadeu (교통카드) Transit card (T-money, etc.)
taegeu (태그) Tap the card reader
hwanseung (환승) Transfer — re-tag within 30 minutes
daeum jeongnyujang (다음 정류장) Next stop
jeosang beoseu (저상버스) Low-floor bus — wheelchair/stroller accessible
baecha ganggyeok (배차간격) Interval between buses
makcha (막차) Last bus of the day
cheotcha (첫차) First bus of the day
jongjeom (종점) Terminus — the route's final stop
geupjeongcha (급정차) Sudden braking — hold the handrail

Tip: of the 13, the three that come up most in practice are hachabel (하차벨) — stop request, hwanseung (환승) — the 30-minute rule, and jeosang beoseu (저상버스) — accessibility.

⑧ 3 Panic Scenarios — What Actually Happens, and How to Cope

1. The bus looks packed and you can't tell if you'll fit — Crowding is normal during rush hours (7–9 AM, 6–8 PM). As long as the doors haven't closed, you can still board and stand — standing is the default even for Koreans. If it feels like too much, wait for the next one — unless the route has a long interval, it'll come soon.

2. You pressed the stop button but couldn't get off — Even if the doors already closed or you missed the timing, it's not a big deal. Get off at the next stop and walk to the stop on the opposite side. Thanks to the transfer rule in ⑤, if you re-board within 30 minutes there's almost no extra fare.

3. You boarded the wrong number or direction and you're headed somewhere else — Tag off (back door) at the next stop, and check your original destination's direction again with a map app. Here too, the 30-minute transfer rule applies, so the extra cost stays minimal.

⑨ Plan B — If You're Still Stuck

  • Ask the driver for help: Show your map app screen with the destination name, and the one phrase "yeogiseo naeryeoyo? (여기서 내려요?)" — "do I get off here?" — is enough. Drivers often tell you directly when to get off.
  • Check real-time location: Tracking the bus's real-time location and remaining stops on Kakao Map or Naver Map greatly reduces the odds of missing your stop-button timing — for app setup, see #1230.
  • Practice with a companion: Have one person in your group guide using the ④ table on this page while the others try it for real — this is the least stressful way to handle your first ride.
