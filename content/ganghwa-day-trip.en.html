A Day Trip from Seoul to Ganghwa Island (강화도) — Dolmens, Jeondeungsa (전등사) and the Folk Market, by Bus 3000 and Local County Buses

Subtitle: About 2 hours on express bus 3000 from Sinchon and Hongdae to Ganghwa Terminal (강화터미널) · Ganghwa History Museum (강화역사박물관) and the dolmens (UNESCO World Heritage) are closed Mondays · Jeondeungsa (전등사) is ₩4,000 (~$3.00) for adults · Ganghwa Folk Market (강화풍물시장) market days fall on the 2nd and 7th · County buses run only 7~16 times a day, so the timetable is everything · 6 spots for jeotguk-galbi (pork ribs in salted-shrimp broth), mugwort pot rice and banjaengi (confirmed through Naver reviews) — as of 2026-10-19

🎯 How to Do Ganghwa

Ganghwa (강화) is an island at the western edge of Seoul's region, part of Incheon (it is joined to the mainland by bridges, so you go by bus). Korea's Bronze Age dolmens (UNESCO World Heritage), the mountain-fortress temple Jeondeungsa (전등사) and the Ganghwa Folk Market (강화풍물시장), where salted shrimp and ginseng are sold, are all on the one island. Even on Reddit, people ask whether Ganghwa works as a day trip from Seoul.

The bus is the catch. Express bus 3000 between Seoul and Ganghwa runs often, but the county buses on the island run only 7~16 times a day per route. This guide builds a route that goes out and back from the terminal.

Travel times with no official timetable are marked [Est.]. Before you set out, check again on a map app (Naver Map or KakaoMap).

⚠ Avoid Mondays. The Ganghwa History Museum is closed Mondays, and the folk market is closed on the third Monday of each month.

① Ganghwa History Museum (강화역사박물관) and the Dolmens — Big Stones of the Bronze Age

  • The Ganghwa dolmens were listed as UNESCO World Heritage in 2000 as the "Gochang, Hwasun and Ganghwa Dolmen Sites" [Secondary — we could not open the UNESCO original this time]. The largest, the Bugeun-ri dolmen (부근리 지석묘) (Hajeom-myeon, Historic Site No. 137), stands in Dolmen Park next to the museum
  • Ganghwa History Museum (강화역사박물관): 09:00~18:00 (ticket sales until 17:30), closed Mondays, January 1, and on Seollal and Chuseok days, adults ₩3,000, children and teens ₩2,000, free for ages 65+ and 6 and under, combined ticket with the Natural History Museum (Ganghwa County)
  • Getting there: from Ganghwa Terminal, county bus 27 (7 times a day) and 30 (16 times a day) pass the museum and Hajeom (Ganghwa County)
  • The dolmen site itself has no admission line in the official fee table (we could not find wording that says it is free)

② Jeondeungsa (전등사) — An Old Temple Inside the Fortress

  • Adults ₩4,000, teens ₩3,000, children ₩1,500, free for ages 65+, summer 08:00~18:30, winter 08:30~18:00 (Incheon Tourism Organization, English site, updated 2025-11). An old Ganghwa County fee table still shows ₩2,000, so confirm the fee at the gate
  • Getting there: take bus 70 and get off at "Jeondeungsa East Gate / South Gate (전등사 동문/남문)", then walk about 10 min (Jeondeungsa official). From the terminal, loop buses 03 and 04 also pass by (Ganghwa County)

③ Ganghwa Folk Market (강화풍물시장) — Turnips, Ginseng, Salted Shrimp

  • Market days the 2nd, 7th, 12th, 17th, 22nd and 27th (a 5-day market), closed the third Monday of each month, a few steps from Ganghwa Terminal (Ganghwa County)
  • Ganghwa's local specialties are turnips (turnip kimchi), ginseng and salted shrimp [Secondary]. Market days are crowded

④ Plan B — Peace Observatory + Gyodongdo (교동도) (Tight by Public Transit)

Gyodongdo (교동도) Daeryong Market (대룡시장) · Hwagae Garden (화개정원) Hwagae Garden daily 08:00~20:00, ₩5,000 · monorail round trip ₩14,000 County bus 18 (11 times a day, timetable unconfirmed) Official (Incheon Tourism Organization · Ganghwa County)

⚠ At Gyodongdo you get an electronic entry pass by QR code at the checkpoint before the bridge (Ganghwa County, introduced 2022-11). The Incheon Tourism Organization's English notice only mentions "photo ID", and we could not officially confirm whether a foreign passport works — check with the Ganghwa County Tourism Division (032-930-3124) before you go. Bring your passport to the Peace Observatory too (sources differ on ID requirements).

It is tight to see both Gyodongdo and the Peace Observatory in one day by county bus. Pick one.

The second floor of the folk market is lined with banjaengi (a small herring-family fish) restaurants. Eat dinner here after the market and catch bus 3000 without breaking your route.

⚠ 5 Traps You Will Actually Hit

1. Ignoring the county bus timetable — Each route runs only 7~16 times a day. The wait for the next bus can be over an hour. As soon as you get off, check the return time.

2. Visiting on a Monday — The museum is closed, and two of the restaurants (Wangjajeong and Solbapjip) are also closed Mondays.

3. Going to Gyodongdo with only a passport — Whether a foreign passport is accepted has not been officially confirmed. Call ahead to check.

4. Conflicting Jeondeungsa fee sources — ₩4,000 (Incheon English site, 2025-11) and ₩2,000 (old county table) are both out there.

5. Heading straight to Incheon Airport — The official route list has no direct route from Ganghwa to Incheon Airport. It is safer to take bus 3000 into Seoul and then the Airport Railroad.

⚙ FAQ 3

1. Can I do it in a day without a car? — Plan A (museum, dolmens, Jeondeungsa, market) works as a round trip from the terminal. For the Peace Observatory or Gyodongdo, pick only one per day.

2. How much is the transport? — Bus 3000 is about ₩3,000 by card (press report 2025-07 [Secondary]). County buses take a transit card (#1298).

3. When is the best time to go? — Time it for a market day (the 2nd or 7th) and the market is liveliest. Avoid Mondays.
