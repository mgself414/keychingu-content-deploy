🏪 Gwangjang Market (광장시장) — Jongno's #1 Food Destination

The most-searched food keyword in Jongno-gu (종로구) among foreign visitors — Gwangjang Market (광장시장). Opened in 1905 as Seoul's first modern market, this two-floor indoor venue holds textile & hanbok (한복) shops + a dedicated street food alley. Half of all visitors come purely for the food.

Location: Yejidong (예지동), Jongno-gu (종로구) → Line 1 Jongno 5-ga Station (종로5가역) Exit 8, 2-min walk / Lines 2 & 5 Euljiro 4-ga Station (을지로4가역) Exit 4, 5-min walk

Hours: Daily 09:00–23:00 (food alley partially open 24 hrs) · Some vendors closed Sunday

Practical Tips

  • Cash is essential: Most Gwangjang Market (광장시장) vendors are cash-only — bring ₩50,000–70,000
  • Best combo: 1 Bindaetteok (빈대떡) + 1 roll Mayak Gimbap (마약김밥) + 1 plate Yukhoe (육회) = ₩25,000–35,000 total
  • Photo spot: Ground-floor central food alley entrance — grandmother (할머니) vendors cooking on the griddle
  • Best time: Mornings 10:00–11:30 or after 14:00 are quieter; avoid the 12:00–14:00 lunch peak
  • No bargaining at food stalls (fixed prices). Bargaining is OK in the textile/clothing section only.
  • Mayak Q: "마약 (mayak)" is Korean slang for "addictive" — zero controlled substances, no customs issue

⚠ 3 Common Mistakes First-Time International Visitors Make

1. "Jongno = Gyeongbokgung only" — Missing the neighborhood breakdown

Most visitors picture Jongno (종로) as just Gyeongbokgung Palace (경복궁) and Cheonggyecheon (청계천). In reality, Jongno covers five distinct sub-areas: Gwanghwamun (광화문, downtown) + Insadong (인사동, traditional) + Ikseondong (익선동, hanok alleys) + Bukchon (북촌, hanok village) + Jonggak (종각, commercial). All are walkable within Seoul Metro Lines 1 and 3 — but without knowing the sub-area breakdown, you'll only scratch the surface.

2. Mixing up Insadong (인사동) and Ikseondong (익선동)

Both have a hanok (한옥) vibe, but they're very different: Insadong (인사동) centers on traditional food, culture, and souvenirs, while Ikseondong (익선동) is a regenerated MZ-generation hotspot known for cafés, bars, and Instagram-worthy spots. A practical approach: start at Insadong and walk ~7 minutes to Ikseondong.

3. Searching for samgyetang (삼계탕) or traditional food only in Gwanghwamun (광화문)

The 60-year-old institution Jangsu Samgyetang (장수삼계탕) is near Gwanghwamun, but Insadong and Ikseondong have their own popular modern Korean restaurants (Doma (도마) and Solsot (솔솥)). The food vibe differs by sub-area — decide what you want to eat first, then pick your sub-area.

Jongno-gu (종로구) at a Glance — International Visitors' #1 Area

Jongno-gu (종로구) is home to Korea's government buildings and royal palaces (Gyeongbokgung (경복궁), Changdeokgung (창덕궁)), making it the #1 destination for first-time visitors to Korea. Gwanghwamun (광화문, downtown), Insadong (인사동, traditional), Ikseondong (익선동, hanok MZ alleys), Bukchon (북촌, hanok village), and Jonggak (종각, commercial) are all within walking distance. ① Gwanghwamun (광화문) — 2 Spots

Quill Bros Gwanghwamun (질할브로스 광화문점)

A Western food hotspot in the Gwanghwamun (광화문) downtown area. ★5.0 from international reviewers. A 5-minute walk from Gwanghwamun Square (광화문 광장). English menus and foreigner-friendly staff. Great for both lunch and dinner. Price range: ₩15,000–30,000.

  • Address: Jongno-gu (종로구), Gwanghwamun (광화문) area
  • Hours: Daily 11:30–22:00
  • Menu: Pasta, steak, wine
  • Signature: Gwanghwamun downtown Western food hotspot
  • Why visit: Best lunch or dinner spot after touring Gyeongbokgung (경복궁). Top marks for foreigner-friendliness.

Standard Bun Starfield Grand Seoul (스탠다드번 스타필드 그랑서울점)

A café and bakery chain located inside Starfield Grand Seoul (스타필드 그랑서울) at Gwanghwamun Station (광화문역). ★4.6 rating. Perfect for a rest or light bite during your Gwanghwamun sightseeing route.

  • Address: 33 Jongno (종로), Jongno-gu (종로구), Starfield Grand Seoul (스타필드 그랑서울)
  • Hours: Daily 10:00–22:00
  • Menu: Bakery items, sandwiches, coffee
  • Signature: Gwanghwamun downtown café & bakery
  • Why visit: A convenient rest stop along the Gwanghwamun walking route. Café + bakery combo for a mid-route break.

② Insadong (인사동) — 2 Spots

Hangwa Mi-Uisik Annyeong Insadong (한과미의식 안녕인사동점)

A curated traditional hangwa (한과, Korean traditional confectionery) shop in the Insadong (인사동) area. Features authentic hangwa (한과), yakgwa (약과, honey cookies), and dasik (다식, pressed tea confections) with modern plating. The best introduction to Korean traditional desserts for international visitors.

  • Address: Insadong (인사동) area, Jongno-gu (종로구)
  • Hours: Daily 11:00–21:00
  • Menu: Hangwa (한과) set ₩15,000–30,000, tea from ₩7,000
  • Signature: Traditional hangwa (한과) dessert curation
  • Why visit: Perfect on an Insadong (인사동) itinerary and as a gift or souvenir for family. Best first stop for hangwa (한과) newcomers.

Doma Insadong (도마 인사동점)

An authentic Korean restaurant in Insadong (인사동). ★5.0 rating. Traditional Korean food with foreigner-friendly service. Open for lunch and dinner. Price range: ₩20,000–40,000.

  • Address: Insadong (인사동) area, Jongno-gu (종로구)
  • Hours: Daily 11:30–22:00
  • Menu: Korean set meals (한식 정식), jjigae (찌개, Korean stew), grilled dishes
  • Signature: Authentic Korean food in the heart of Insadong (인사동)
  • Why visit: Genuine Korean cuisine right in Insadong. The best choice for a first full Korean meal.

A signature hanok (한옥) + café experience in the hanok alleyways of Ikseondong (익선동). ★5.0 rating. Authentic hanok (한옥) atmosphere paired with a modern café menu. The #1 Instagram photo spot in the area. A true Ikseondong (익선동) hotspot.

  • Why visit: The perfect introduction to Ikseondong (익선동). Ticks both the Instagram and hanok (한옥) atmosphere boxes.

Solsot Ikseondong (솔솥 익선점)

A stone-pot Korean food restaurant in Ikseondong (익선동). ★5.0 rating. Specializes in sotbap (솥밥, stone-pot rice), jeon (전, savory pancakes), and gukbap (국밥, rice soup) set meals. Hanok (한옥) atmosphere combined with traditional Korean cuisine. The best lunch or dinner stop on an Ikseondong (익선동) itinerary.

  • Address: Ikseondong (익선동) area, Jongno-gu (종로구)
  • Hours: Daily 11:30–22:00
  • Menu: Sotbap (솥밥) set meal ₩15,000–25,000
  • Signature: Stone-pot Korean food + Ikseondong (익선동) hanok (한옥) atmosphere
  • Why visit: The #1 Korean food pick in Ikseondong. Experience hanok (한옥) and traditional sotbap (솥밥) all at once.

④ Bukchon·Jonggak (북촌·종각) — 2 Spots

Ho-Sunsaeng Bar (호선생전)

A Korean pub-style dining bar in the Ikseondong (익선동) and Jongno (종로) area with signature anju (안주, bar snacks). ★4.4 rating. A foreigner-friendly pojangmacha-style venue serving classic Korean anju (안주) with makgeolli (막걸리, Korean rice wine) and soju (소주). Best for evenings and late nights.

  • Address: Jongno-gu (종로구) area
  • Hours: Daily 17:00–01:00
  • Menu: Anju (안주) ₩15,000–25,000 / makgeolli (막걸리), soju (소주)
  • Signature: Signature anju (안주) + Korean pub-style dining
  • Why visit: The best place for international visitors to experience Korean drinking culture with authentic anju (안주). Great night-cap after an Ikseondong (익선동) day.

Jangsu Samgyetang (장수삼계탕)

A 60-year-old legacy samgyetang (삼계탕, ginseng chicken soup) institution near Gwanghwamun (광화문) in Jongno-gu (종로구). ★5.0 rating. Consistently praised in foreign-language reviews — the #1 pick for traditional Korean samgyetang (삼계탕). Recommended for lunch.

  • Address: Near Gwanghwamun (광화문), Jongno-gu (종로구)
  • Hours: Daily 11:00–22:00
  • Menu: Samgyetang (삼계탕) ₩18,000–25,000
  • Signature: 60-year legacy authentic samgyetang (삼계탕)
  • Why visit: The best lunch spot after exploring Gwanghwamun (광화문) and Gyeongbokgung (경복궁). The #1 first-time samgyetang (삼계탕) experience for international visitors.

⚙ FAQ — 8 Questions

Q1. Recommended 1-day itinerary for a first visit to Jongno-gu (종로구)?

Gyeongbokgung (경복궁) at 10:00 → Jangsu Samgyetang (장수삼계탕) for lunch (12:00) → Gwanghwamun Square (광화문 광장) stroll → Standard Bun (스탠다드번) coffee break → Hangwa Mi-Uisik (한과미의식) + Doma (도마) Insadong (인사동) route (15:00) → Walk to Ikseondong (익선동) → Hanok Ransom (한옥랑솜) + Solsot (솔솥) for dinner → Ho-Sunsaeng Bar (호선생전) nightcap

Q2. Insadong (인사동) vs. Ikseondong (익선동) — which one?

Insadong (인사동): Traditional food, souvenirs, and authentic Korean cuisine (Hangwa Mi-Uisik (한과미의식), Doma (도마)). Ikseondong (익선동): MZ cafés, bars, and hanok (한옥) Instagram spots (Hanok Ransom (한옥랑솜), Solsot (솔솥), Ho-Sunsaeng Bar (호선생전)). Choose based on your vibe — traditional = Insadong / MZ + Instagram = Ikseondong.

Q3. Are English menus and service available?

Quill Bros (질할브로스) / Standard Bun (스탠다드번) / Hangwa Mi-Uisik (한과미의식) / Doma (도마) — high English-friendliness. Solsot (솔솥) / Ho-Sunsaeng Bar (호선생전) — partial English. Jangsu Samgyetang (장수삼계탕) — legacy restaurant, primarily Korean-language service.

Q4. Legacy/traditional vs. modern Korean food?

Legacy traditional: Jangsu Samgyetang (장수삼계탕) (60 years) / Doma Insadong (도마 인사동) (traditional Korean). Modern Korean: Solsot Ikseondong (솔솥 익선) (stone-pot) / Hangwa Mi-Uisik (한과미의식) (modern plating). Modern works well as a first introduction; legacy spots appeal more to enthusiasts.

Q5. Best time to visit Ikseondong (익선동) hanok (한옥) cafés?

Weekday mornings (11:00–13:00) offer the most space. On weekends from 14:00–17:00, expect 30+ minute waits. For the best natural light photos in the hanok (한옥) setting, visit between 13:00–15:00.

Q6. What are the price ranges?

Western food (Quill Bros (질할브로스)) ₩15,000–30,000 / Korean food (Doma (도마), Solsot (솔솥)) ₩15,000–25,000 / Café & dessert ₩7,000–12,000 / Hangwa (한과) ₩15,000–30,000 / Samgyetang (삼계탕) ₩18,000–25,000.

Q7. Any closures or hours to watch out for?

Most venues are open daily. Jangsu Samgyetang (장수삼계탕) closes on public holidays. Ho-Sunsaeng Bar (호선생전) may close some Mondays. Hangwa Mi-Uisik (한과미의식) hours vary by season. Always check before visiting.

Q8. Wheelchair and stroller accessibility?

Quill Bros (질할브로스) / Standard Bun (스탠다드번) / Doma (도마) — flat, accessible routes. Hanok Ransom (한옥랑솜), Solsot (솔솥), Ho-Sunsaeng Bar (호선생전), and the Ikseondong (익선동) alleys generally have narrow paths, steps, and raised doorways.

Q9. What is Gwangjang Market (광장시장) famous for?

Bindaetteok (빈대떡)·Mayak Gimbap (마약김밥)·Yukhoe (육회) are the top 3 picks for foreign visitors. The griddle-fresh mung-bean pancakes and thumb-size mini gimbap (김밥) have gone viral on social media. Seoul's first modern market (opened 1905) with an iconic indoor food alley.

Q10. Is Gwangjang Market (광장시장) worth visiting?

Strongly recommended. With ₩30,000–40,000 cash you can try bindaetteok (빈대떡), mayak gimbap (마약김밥), and yukhoe (육회) all in one visit. Allow about 2 hours. A 2-minute walk from Jongno 5-ga Station (종로5가역).

Q11. When should I visit Gwangjang Market (광장시장) on a Jongno (종로) day trip?

Lunch slot (11:30–13:00) works best — morning tour of Gyeongbokgung (경복궁) or Insadong (인사동) → Gwangjang Market (광장시장) lunch → Ikseondong (익선동) afternoon is the optimal Jongno (종로) 1-day flow.

🌸 5 Seasonal & Timing Tips

1. Spring (Mar–May) — Cherry blossoms at Gyeongbokgung (경복궁): Pair the palace with Jangsu Samgyetang (장수삼계탕) and an Insadong (인사동) stroll — the essence of Korean spring.

2. Summer (Jun–Aug) — Sambok (삼복) season: Peak time for Jangsu Samgyetang (장수삼계탕). International visitors can experience traditional Korean restorative food (보양식) first-hand.

3. Autumn (Sep–Nov) — Fall foliage in Ikseondong (익선동): The hanok (한옥) alleys around Hanok Ransom (한옥랑솜) and Solsot Ikseondong (솔솥 익선) are stunning during leaf-change season.

4. Winter (Dec–Feb) — Ho-Sunsaeng Bar (호선생전) warm-up: A cozy pub-dining session with makgeolli (막걸리) is perfect on a cold evening.

5. Early morning (08:00–10:00): Gwanghwamun (광화문) and Gyeongbokgung (경복궁) are quietest. Jangsu Samgyetang (장수삼계탕) opens at 11:00, so arriving early keeps lunch stress-free.
