The Complete Korean Convenience Store Drink Map — 9 Categories and 18 Picks, from Banana Milk to Hangover Cures

> Subtitle: Hundreds of drinks on a single wall of fridges — dairy, coffee, tea, soda, ion drinks, energy, zero, ice cups, and hot cabinets: the one guide you need to read a Korean convenience store drink aisle

ISSUE 03 · #1269 · 2026-07-24

🎯 Why a Standalone Drink Guide

The drink fridge in a Korean convenience store packs hundreds of SKUs onto a single wall — practically a miniature museum of the Korean beverage industry. Tourists stand in front of this fridge at least once a day, yet the only thing most of them know is banana milk. In the convenience store cluster (#26 intro, #1261 four-chain comparison, #1262 food roundup), drinks got only three quick mentions — this issue is a standalone deep dive into the entire drink aisle, organized into 9 categories. The core hooks are the things foreigners don't even know exist: hangover-cure drinks, ice cup mixology, and the winter hot cabinet. For after-meal drinks at restaurants see #924, traditional drinks #1192, and drink chains #1266 — here, it's the convenience store fridge only.

⚠️ Data note: New drink releases have short life cycles — availability and prices of individual products depend on the shelf at the time of your visit. The 18 classics have been steady sellers for decades.

⚠ 3 Common Mistakes Foreigners Make

1. "Sikhye (식혜) is juice": A traditional sweet rice drink with rice grains floating in it — take a sip expecting juice and the rice will catch you off guard. Sikhye and Sujeonggwa (수정과) follow the dessert drink grammar (#1192 traditional drinks). A rice illustration on the label means it belongs to this family.

2. "Not knowing the ice cup system": You buy a cup of ice separately from the freezer, then pour in a pouch drink or can — a self-assembly system where the cup and the drink are each rung up. It's the standard Korean summer routine, but foreigners often end up standing there puzzled, holding just the cup.

3. "Not knowing hangover-cure drinks exist": Korea keeps drinkable hangover cures (Condition (컨디션), Morning Care (모닝케어)) and chewable pills (Sangkwaehwan (상쾌환)) in a dedicated corner next to the register — and the Korean way is to take them before drinking. Learn this as a set with soju (소주) culture (#26) and your next morning will be a different story.

① Dairy — The Land of Banana Milk

1. Banana Milk (바나나맛우유) ★ The National Photo-Op Item

  • Born in 1974 with its jar-shaped (pot-shaped) container — from bathhouse milk culture to national icon
  • Why it's famous: the jar-plus-straw photo has become a rite of passage for Korea trips — it even made it into airport duty-free shops
  • Tip: straws are next to the register — the moment you poke it in is photo time. Strawberry and melon sister flavors exist

2. Yakult Grand (야쿠르트 그랜드)

  • The jumbo version of the finger-sized Yakult — a legacy of the childhood "Yakult lady" delivery culture
  • Why it's famous: the wit of chugging what used to be a tiny drink — connected to the Fredit (프레딧) delivery culture

② Coffee — The World of 1,000-Won Caffeine

3. The Two Giants of Canned Coffee — Cantata & T.O.P

  • Cantata by Lotte (롯데), T.O.P by Maxim (맥심), plus Georgia (조지아) — a whole fridge row of canned and cup coffee
  • Why it's famous: a quarter the price of a cafe Americano — morning fuel for Korean office workers
  • Tip: sweetness is on the label — look for 'black' or 'unsweetened' markings. The in-store fresh-brew machine serves a bean-based Americano for around 1,000 won

③ Tea Drinks — Tea Instead of Water

4. Corn Silk Tea, Heotgae-cha & Barley Tea (보리차)

  • Drinking unsweetened grain and herbal teas from PET bottles like mineral water is a Korean habit — a whole fridge shelf is the tea zone
  • Why it's famous: the answer between "water is boring and soda is sweet" — corn silk tea rode V-line face marketing, while Heotgae-cha is the national tea for the morning after a company dinner
  • Tip: leaf or grain illustrations on the label = unsweetened tea family. The traditional sweet rice drink Sikhye (#1192) is separate — a sweet dessert drink

④ Soda — The Land of Cider

5. Chilsung Cider (칠성사이다) ★ The National Soda, Born 1950

  • In Korea, 'cider' means a clear lemon-lime soda (not alcoholic) — the original that became the national drink before cola did
  • Why it's famous: the brand even created an idiom — "that's so cider" means refreshingly satisfying, the opposite of frustrating

6. Milkis & McCol — Korea-Only Soda Genres

  • Milkis: the soda + milk combo — shocking at first, addictive by the second sip. A steady seller that went viral in Russia
  • McCol: barley soda, a genre virtually unique in the world — a favorite love-it-or-hate-it challenge
  • Tip: grape Fanta (환타) is also a steady Korean convenience store favorite — fun to compare with the Fanta back home

⑤ Ion & Sports — The Heatwave Survival Zone

7. Pocari Sweat, Gatorade & Toreta (토레타)

  • Survival gear for summer heatwaves (#905 cool routes), hiking, and Hangang (한강) runs — the large-bottle zone at the bottom of the fridge
  • Why it's famous: in Korea's humid summer heat, ion drinks beat plain water — and convenience stores, not pharmacies, are the supply base
  • Tip: 2+1 deals are frequent in this category — large PET bottles are the better value

⑥ Energy & Hangover Cures — The Culture Shock Category ★

8. Bacchus (박카스) ★ The National Fatigue-Recovery Tonic, Born 1961

  • Korea's original take on the energy drink — started in pharmacies, became the national convenience store tonic
  • Why it's famous: loved across generations, from exam students to overtime workers to seniors — rediscovered by Gen Z as an ice cup mixology ingredient (the 'Eolbaksa (얼박사)' family)

9. Hot6 & Korea-Exclusive Monster Flavors

  • Hot6 is Korea's flagship energy drink — Korea-exclusive Monster flavors are collector items
  • Why it's famous: a set with nightlife culture (#1172 the 24-hour city) — the bestseller of late-night convenience stores

10. Condition & Sangkwaehwan ★ The Land of Hangover Cures

  • Condition (drink type), Morning Care (모닝케어), and Sangkwaehwan (pill type) — the always-stocked corner next to the register
  • Why it's famous: company dinner culture built one of the world's largest hangover-cure markets — taking it before drinking is the Korean grammar
  • Tip: that corner the clerk glances at when foreigners buy soju and beer — it makes the next morning a different story

⑦ Zero & Health — The Fridge's Rising Stars

11. Zero Sodas, Protein Drinks & Kombucha

  • Cola, cider, even Milkis — the entire lineup has gone zero — an era where the 'ZERO' label is a default option
  • Why it's famous: the fitness and running boom — protein drinks claimed a whole fridge shelf, with kombucha and caffeine-free lines expanding
  • Tip: check the nutrition label for 'sugars 0g' — some drinks are 'low-sugar' rather than zero, so read the label

⑧ Ice Cup Mixology — The Standard Summer Ritual ★

12. Cup Ice + Pouch = Self-Assembly

  • Buy an ice cup (a cup filled with just ice) from the freezer, pour in a pouch drink or can, and it's done — the cup and the drink are rung up separately
  • Why it's famous: the combining itself is the fun — SNS recipes like Bacchus + tonic (the 'Eolbaksa' family) keep spawning new variants
  • 3 recommended combos: pouch Americano (the basic) / peach iced tea + espresso ('Ah-sha-chu (아샷추)') / Bacchus + tonic water
  • Tip: assemble at the dine-in table — peel off the lid seal, pour, and you're done

⑨ Hot Cabinet — Winter's Plot Twist ★

13. The World of Warm Cans — Yulmu-cha, Honey Water & Canned Coffee

  • In winter, convenience stores roll out the hot cabinet (a heated drink display) — the cans double as hand warmers
  • Why it's famous: Yulmu-cha (Job's tears tea), honey water, ginger tea, and warm canned coffee — winter saviors on outdoor sightseeing routes (palaces, night views)
  • Tip: the display glowing orange next to the fridge is the hot cabinet — a winter-only system that's a tourist attraction in itself

⚙ 6 FAQs

1. Which bottled water should I buy?: Samdasoo (삼다수) (Jeju (제주) volcanic bedrock water) is the national default — Icis (아이시스) and Baeksansu (백산수) are also steady picks. Around 1,000 won for 500ml.

2. How do I check caffeine?: The 'high caffeine' warning is a legally required label — check the bottom of energy and coffee drink labels. Decaf is marked 'DECAF'.

3. How do I tell zero from low-sugar?: Sugars 0g on the nutrition label = zero. 'Low-sugar' or 'light' still contains sugar — spotting the ZERO logo is the fastest check.

4. How do 1+1 / 2+1 deals work?: Look for the yellow sticker on the price tag — grab that many of the same product and it applies automatically at the register. Drinks are the category with the most deals (#1261).

5. Where do bottles and cans go?: Recycling bins in front of the store or next to the dine-in table — sorted into cans, PET, and general waste. The deposit system covers only certain items, so as a tourist, sorting correctly is all you need to do.

6. Do convenience stores carry traditional drinks?: Canned Sikhye and Sujeonggwa are steady sellers — for the deep dive, see #1192 (traditional drink culture) and #924 (after-meal drinks).

🗺 3 Field Courses

A. Summer Ice Cup 3-Way Tasting (30 min)

  • One convenience store: 3 ice cups + pouch Americano + peach iced tea & espresso + Bacchus & tonic → assemble and compare at the dine-in table

B. Winter Hot Cabinet Tour (Paired with a Night-View Route)

  • Before palace or Namsan (남산) night views, pick up Yulmu-cha and honey water from the hot cabinet → hand warmer and warm-up in one → finish with cup ramyeon (컵라면) after the views (#26)

C. Hangang Picnic Drink Basket (Pairs with #06)

  • Large ion drink + Milkis & McCol challenge + zero soda + 2 ice cups = a picnic drink set for around 10,000 won

🌸 5 Seasons

Summer Jun–Aug is peak season for ice cups and ion drinks / Winter Dec–Feb is hot cabinet season (Yulmu-cha, honey water) / Spring and fall bring concentrated new drink launches / The year-end company dinner season peaks the hangover-cure corner / Year-round, drinks lead all categories in 1+1 deals
