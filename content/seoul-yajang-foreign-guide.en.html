🎯 Why Seoul Night Market Guide?

Yajang = Korea's long-established outdoor casual nighttime food culture. Pain point for foreign tourists = (a) "Yajang = street food" Wrong perception — Yajang = Outdoor casual seating at a formal restaurant + regular menu + ordering possible (b) "Korean street food at outdoor benches" Reddit·Quora Foreigner search multiple — Key Friend's full guide, part 0 (c) May to October night market season = Coincident with the peak season for foreigners traveling to Korea (d) Korean nightlife = Club·Bar + Night market ordinary = 2-track. ⚠ 3 common mistakes foreigners make

1. "Yajang = street food?": Yajang = Outdoor flat seating in a formal restaurant + Set menu + Can be ordered + Payment can be made. Different from street stalls (food stalls, street food). Yajang = restaurant outdoor seating, not street food.

2. "Do I have to take off my shoes?": Plain = Sitting with shoes off (1·2·5·7) or chair with shoes on (3·4·6·8) Available on both sides. Varies by store. There are many shoe rack and bench information signs at the entrance. Sitting flat = Cross-legged, legs spread OK.

3. "Also OK for foreigners?": Ikseon·Jongno 5-ga·Hongdae·Sinchon Yajang = Foreigners welcome + English menu available. Menu = Many photos. Insa·Plaza = Some places are Korean only — ordering is OK with photos. Sharing table = Yajang culture (Korean Yajang etiquette, although it may be awkward at first for foreigners).

⚙ 4 FAQs

1. When to go: May to October field season. Best from July to September. November~April = Many nightspots are closed (indoor seating only). Foreign travel peak in Korea (May, June, September, October) = coincides with the night market season.

2. Reservation available?: Ikseon Goundon·Insa Hanok Yajang = Partial reservations available (catch table·Naver reservation). Gwangjang, Euljiro, Sinchon, Hongdae, Cheonggye = walk-in majority. Weekends 7-10 p.m. = Usually full. Recommended = Arrive between 7-9pm on weekdays or 5-6pm on weekends.

3. Can I go alone?: Sinchon, Hongdae, Jongno 5-ga = OK alone + Shared seating possible. Ikseon Goundon = Recommended for 2 or more people. Gwangjang·Cheonggye = Market Pyeongsang = OK alone + fit for foreign enthusiasts.

4. Foreigners’ night jang etiquette: (a) Noise allowed (loud voices are natural on other tables) (b) Shared seating = night market culture (if there are not enough seats, join on other tables) (c) Clinking glasses = toast = Korean night jang etiquette (d) Take off your shoes and sit on the table = cross your legs or spread your legs (e) Payment = card and cash are OK in most cases.
