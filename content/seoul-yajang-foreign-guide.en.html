🎯 Why a Seoul Yajang Guide?

Yajang (야장) = Korea's nopo (노포, 30+ year traditional restaurant) outdoor pyeongsan (평상, flat bench) dining culture at night. Foreign tourist pain points: (a) The misconception that "Yajang (야장) = nojeom (노점, street food stalls)" — Yajang (야장) = outdoor pyeongsan (평상) seating of a proper restaurant + full menu + ordering available. (b) Many foreigners search Reddit/Quora for "Korean street food at outdoor benches" — KeyChinGu Full Guide Issue 0. (c) The Yajang (야장) season (May-October) coincides with the peak season for foreign travel to Korea. (d) Korean nightlife = clubs/bars + Yajang (야장) pyeongsan (평상) = a 2-track experience. Clubs/bars are covered in [#450 Seoul Nightlife Map](450_seoul_nightlife_map.md) and [#454 Foreigner's Guide to Seoul Clubs](454_foreigner_seoul_clubs.md) — Yajang (야장) = KeyChinGu Enthusiast Matrix #1 (unfamiliar to foreigners + most Korean). (e) Yajang (야장) crosses 8 Seoul neighborhoods (Ikseon (익선), Gwangjang (광장), Euljiro (을지로), Sinchon (신촌), Insa (인사), Hongdae (홍대), Jongno (종로), Cheonggye (청계)).

This guide provides foreigner-friendly Yajang (야장) etiquette + an 8-spot matrix + season/time recommendations + itinerary suggestions + a full enthusiast's guide.

⚠ 3 Common Mistakes by Foreigners

1. "Yajang (야장) = Nojeom (노점, street food stalls)?": Yajang (야장) = outdoor pyeongsan (평상) seating of a proper restaurant + full menu + ordering available + payment possible. It is different from nojeom (노점, street food stalls) or pojangmacha (포장마차, street food carts/tent bars). Yajang (야장) = restaurant outdoor seating, not street food.

2. "Do I need to take off my shoes?": Pyeongsan (평상) seating can be either shoes off for floor seating (1, 2, 5, 7) or shoes on for chair-style seating (3, 4, 6, 8). It varies by establishment. You'll often find shoe racks or pyeongsan (평상) guide signs at the entrance. For floor seating pyeongsan (평상), sitting cross-legged or with legs stretched out is fine.

3. "Are foreigners welcome?": Yajang (야장) spots in Ikseon (익선), Jongno 5-ga (종로5가), Hongdae (홍대), and Sinchon (신촌) are foreigner-friendly + some English menus are available. Menus often have many pictures. In Insadong (인사동) and Gwangjang (광장), some places might be Korean-only — but ordering with pictures is fine. Hap-seok (합석, sharing a table) is part of Yajang (야장) culture (it might feel awkward for foreigners at first, but it's Korean Yajang (야장) etiquette).

⚙ 4 FAQs

1. When to go: Yajang (야장) season is May-October. July-September is best. Most Yajang (야장) spots close from November-April (indoor seating only). The peak season for foreign travel to Korea (May, June, September, October) coincides with the Yajang (야장) season.

2. Is reservation possible?: Some Yajang (야장) spots like Ikseon Goundon (익선 고운돈) and Insadong Hanok Yajang (인사동 한옥 야장) allow reservations (via Catch Table or Naver Reservation). Most places in Gwangjang (광장), Euljiro (을지로), Sinchon (신촌), Hongdae (홍대), and Cheonggye (청계) are walk-in. Pyeongsan (평상) seating is often full on weekends from 19:00-22:00 (7-10 PM). Recommended: Arrive on weekdays from 19:00-21:00 (7-9 PM) or weekends from 17:00-18:00 (5-6 PM).

3. Can I go alone?: Sinchon (신촌), Hongdae (홍대), and Jongno 5-ga (종로5가) are solo-friendly + hap-seok (합석) is possible. Ikseon Goundon (익선 고운돈) is recommended for 2 or more people. Gwangjang (광장) and Cheonggye (청계) market pyeongsan (평상) spots are solo-friendly + fit for foreign enthusiasts.

4. Foreigner's Yajang (야장) etiquette: (a) Loudness is acceptable (loud conversations from other pyeongsan (평상) are natural). (b) Hap-seok (합석) = Yajang (야장) culture (share a pyeongsan (평상) with others if seats are scarce). (c) Clinking glasses = Geonbae (건배, cheers!) = Korean Yajang (야장) etiquette. (d) For shoes-off floor seating pyeongsan (평상), sit cross-legged or with legs stretched out. (e) Payment = most places accept cards and cash.
