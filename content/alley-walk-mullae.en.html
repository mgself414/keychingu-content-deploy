Alley Walk ④ Mullae (문래) — The Neighborhood Where the Sound of Machines and the Work of Artists Share the Same Alley

Subtitle: By day, the alleys ring with the sound of metal being cut; around the corner, sculptures and murals wait. Mullae is Seoul's only neighborhood where ironworks and artists have shared the same alleys for decades. From Mullae Station through Ironworks Alley and the Art Village alleys to evening cafés and bars, about 2 hours — walked alongside the story of how a spinning wheel became iron, and iron became art. Seoul Alley Walks Expansion 1.

ISSUE · #1343 · 2026-09-02

🎯 The Frame — Iron and Art, Living Side by Side

If the other neighborhoods in this series are places where "the present sits layered on top of the past," Mullae (문래) is a neighborhood where past and present are still running at the same time, right now. In the morning alleys, metal is actually being cut and welded, while artists work in the studio next door. That coexistence is Mullae's identity — which is exactly why the time of day you walk it matters.

  • Total time: about 2 hours (2.5 hours with cafés and galleries) · Best time: start on a weekday around 3–4 PM — to see the ironworks alleys still running, then catch the turn as the Art Village's lights come on at dusk
  • Incline: low — flat ground. A route that loops through three branching alleys.

🚶 The Route — In Walking Order

① Mullae Station (문래역) Exit 7 → Ironworks Alley (철공골목) (about a 5-minute walk)

  • The sound guides you from the mouth of the alley — grinders, welding torches, metal being hammered.
  • 📖 Story 1 — From Spinning Wheel to Iron: The most popular theory holds that the name "Mullae" comes from the spinning wheels (mulle) of textile mills that once stood in this area during the Japanese colonial era. After liberation, steel dealers and small machine-metal factories moved into the neighborhood's worker housing blocks, turning it into an ironworks alley — a steel district that once ran around the clock, until it fell quiet through the IMF crisis of the late 1990s.

② Ironworks Alley → Mullae Art Village (문래창작촌) Alleys (about a 10-minute walk)

  • Between shuttered factories and ones still running, studios, galleries, and sculptures are tucked into the gaps. Murals painted on steel doors, sculptures welded together from bolts — art made from the ironworks' own materials is this alley's signature.
  • 📖 Story 2 — Art That Bloomed in the Gaps: Starting around 2003, artists searching for cheap workspace began moving into the emptying second floors and storerooms above the ironworks. They didn't arrive by pushing anyone out through redevelopment — they seeped in, filling the gaps left behind. That's why ironworks and art studios are still neighbors today.

③ Art Village Alleys → A Loop of Sculpture and Mural Spots (about a 20-minute walk)

  • Having no fixed viewing route is this neighborhood's charm — sweep the alleys in a grid and discover steel sculptures and murals like a treasure hunt.
  • As dusk falls, the shuttered ironworks alley lights up with cafés, bars, and restaurants — the same alley's second face.

④ Evening Finish in the Alley (open-ended)

  • Finish at a café or pub tucked among the ironworks. Head toward Yeongdeungpo (영등포) to connect with the major shopping district (#449 Seoul Shopping Mall Map).

🙏 Alley Manners — Essential for This Neighborhood

  • A working ironworks is someone's workplace — no photographing the interiors, don't block entrances to take photos, and give way to forklifts and motorcycles.
  • Studios and galleries are for viewing only when open — don't knock on closed doors.
  • Don't touch the sculptures (welded edges can be sharp, too).

💡 Where You Might Get Thrown Off

  • Weekend daytime is quiet, the opposite of weekdays — the ironworks are closed, so you'll miss the "alley at work." Come on a weekday to see iron and art coexisting; weekends are fine if cafés and an evening out are your goal.
  • The grid layout makes map-app pins vague — better to set "Mullae Art Village" as your center pin and walk the radius around it.
  • Handle restroom needs at a café (#1277) — public facilities are rare in the alleys.
