Templestay Beginner's Guide — Full Guide for International Tourists

Subtitle: A 1-night-2-day stay at a Korean Buddhist temple + booking, etiquette, and use tips + 8 foreigner-friendly temples

ISSUE 02 · #36 · 2026-05-15 (v2 Full Update)

🎯 Why Templestay Deserves Its Own Guide

Templestay is the #1 way to experience Korean culture and spirituality — a 1-night-2-day stay at a Korean Buddhist temple combining meditation, dawn worship (예불), and balwoo gongyang (발우공양, formal monastic dining). It's a course where international visitors encounter a different side of Korea — history, religion, and peace. It's been featured repeatedly on Netflix, BBC, and CNN, driving rising awareness among foreign travelers.

⚠ 3 Common Mistakes Foreigners Make

1. Underestimating the early-morning schedule

The templestay schedule starts at 4–5am — dawn worship and meditation. Combined with jet lag, the early start is tough for foreigners. Get your body ready beforehand and cut caffeine.

2. Ignoring the dress code

No exposed skin inside temple grounds — long pants and long sleeves recommended. Some temples provide hanbok-style robes. Check the venue's dress guide beforehand.

3. Photos and phone use

Some temple areas prohibit photography (main hall, worship spaces). Keep your phone on silent and don't use it during meditation time. Follow the guide's instructions.

⚙ FAQ

Q1. Is English support / booking available?

A. International Seon Center, Jogyesa, and Bongeunsa all offer full English guides. Advance English-language booking is available via Klook and Trip.com. Temples' official websites also offer English booking.

Q2. Are atheists or people of other faiths welcome?

A. Yes — no religious affiliation is required to join. The focus is on experiencing Korean culture, not converting anyone. There's no pressure to bow or worship. That said, temple rules (quiet conduct, dress code) must be followed.

Q3. Recommendations by price range?

A. Budget ₩50K–70K: a 1-night-2-day city temple. Standard ₩70K–100K: mountain or UNESCO temples. Premium ₩100K+: full English guide + interpretation.

Q4. Day Program vs. 1-night-2-days?

A. Day Program: ₩30K–50K — 4–6 hours of meditation, tea talk, and balwoo gongyang. 1 Night 2 Days: ₩50K–120K — the full set of dawn worship, walks, and meditation.

Q5. Meals and balwoo gongyang?

A. Temple food is 100% vegetarian. Balwoo gongyang is the essence of a Korean temple stay — food is portioned evenly across 4 bowls, nothing is left over, and each bowl is left spotless.
