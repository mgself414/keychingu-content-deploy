36_Templestay Beginner's Guide

One-Line Intro

Templestay: sleep in a Buddhist temple, wake to dawn bells, and find calm in Seoul's chaos.

A templestay is an overnight experience at a Buddhist temple — wake to morning bell sounds, meditate, and sip tea.

Who This Is For

  • Travelers looking to escape Seoul's hustle and seek a quiet, meaningful experience
  • Anyone interested in Buddhist culture or meditation

Templestay Program Schedule

Time Activity Description
16:00 Check-in + Orientation Temple rules & etiquette briefing, put on temple robes (법복)
17:00 108 Prostrations or Meditation Bowing practice to cleanse body and mind
18:00 Evening Gongyang (공양) — Dinner Vegetarian temple cuisine, eaten in silence
19:00 Tea Talk or Conversation with a Monk Discussion on Buddhist culture and Korean mindfulness
21:00 Bedtime Shared sleeping quarters (bedding on ondol (온돌) heated floors)
04:00 Dawn Ceremony (새벽 예불) Morning bell, chanting alongside resident monks
06:00 Morning Gongyang (공양) — Breakfast Temple-style morning meal
07:00 Free Time + Check-out

Tips for International Visitors

  • English Support: Most urban temple templestay programs provide English guidance. When booking, specifically request an "English program."
  • What to Bring
  • Comfortable inner clothing (temple robes are provided, but wear something easy underneath)
  • Toiletries (bring your own toothbrush and toothpaste)
  • Smartphone (allowed, but consider putting it away to fully enjoy the experience)
  • Cash (some temples sell souvenirs in cash only)
  • Cultural Etiquette
  • Alcohol and smoking are strictly prohibited on temple grounds
  • Avoid shorts and sleeveless tops
  • Remove shoes before entering the main hall (법당)
  • Speak quietly and do not run
  • How to Book: English-language reservations are available at templestay.com — the official site of the Korean Buddhist Cultural Service Organization (한국불교문화사업단).

Simple English Summary

Templestay programs offer an authentic immersion into Korean Buddhist culture. The overnight experience (₩70,000–120,000) includes evening meditation, a vegetarian dinner eaten in silence, a 4am dawn ceremony, and morning rice porridge. Most programs at urban temples like Jogyesa (조계사) and Bongeunsa (봉은사) offer English-language guidance. The experience is designed for all faiths — no Buddhist background required. Book at templestay.com at least 1–2 weeks in advance as spots fill quickly, especially on weekends.

Estimated Total Cost

  • Budget: ₩70,000 (day program, meals included)
  • Standard: ₩100,000 (1 night 2 days templestay, transportation not included)
  • Premium: ₩150,000 (1 night 2 days + private tea talk with a monk + souvenirs)
