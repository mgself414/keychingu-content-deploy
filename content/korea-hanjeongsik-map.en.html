Korean Hanjeongsik Guide — 3 Michelin-Starred Korean Course Restaurants in Seoul (Mingles, La Yeon, Onjium), Jeonju Hanjeongsik, Andong Heotjesabap, and Temple Food (Official Data, October 2026)

Subtitle: Based on the Michelin Guide Seoul 2026: Mingles 3 stars, La Yeon 2 stars, Onjium 1 star · Jeonju became a UNESCO City of Gastronomy in 2012, with 3 hanjeongsik restaurants (VisitKorea) · 2 Andong heotjesabap restaurants open 11:00-20:00 (VisitKorea) · Temple food = Venerable Jeong Kwan of Baekyangsa (Chef's Table, 2017) — as of 2026-10-20

ISSUE 03 · #478 · 2026-05-27

🎯 How to Use This Guide

Small dishes land on the table one by one, and before you know it the table is full to the edges — in Jeonju, a single table can carry 30 or so side dishes (VisitKorea). This guide splits hanjeongsik (한정식), the Korean full-course meal, into four branches: three Michelin-starred Korean course restaurants in Seoul (Mingles, La Yeon, Onjium), Jeonju hanjeongsik, Andong heotjesabap, and temple food. Ratings, addresses, and hours are only what we read on the official pages of the Michelin Guide Seoul & Busan 2026, VisitKorea, the Jeonju city tourism site, and the Korea Heritage Service. Where a price or opening hours are not posted, we say so. It is made for anyone who wants to eat a proper Korean course meal at least once, and for anyone willing to give a day to Jeonju or Andong outside Seoul.

The old version of this guide had no sources for "Korea #1", its price ranges (La Yeon at 300,000-500,000 won per person, etc.), or the word "month". "Venerable Seonjae = Chef's Table" was wrong (the featured monk is Venerable Jeong Kwan of Baekyangsa, VisitKorea), and what is designated as National Intangible Heritage is not Jingwansa Temple's temple food but the Suryukjae of Jingwansa Temple (a Buddhist ritual, designated in 2013, Korea Heritage Service).

🍽 Seoul — 3 Michelin 2026 Korean Course Restaurants

Values from the official Michelin Guide Seoul & Busan 2026 pages (viewed 2026-10-07). All 16 restaurants are in #1109 Seoul Michelin Guide 2026.

  • Mingles (밍글스) — 3 stars (2 years in a row, the only one in Seoul). 19 Dosan-daero 67-gil, Gangnam-gu, Seoul, 2nd floor. Chef Kang Min-goo. Lunch Tue-Sat 12:00-15:00, closed Mon and Sun (Michelin page; check the official site for dinner). Reserve on the official site restaurant-mingles.com or by phone +82-2-515-7306. Cards accepted
  • La Yeon (라연) — 2 stars. 249 Dongho-ro, Jung-gu, Seoul, 23rd floor of the Shilla Hotel. Lunch daily 12:00-14:30 (Michelin page). Reserve on the official Shilla Hotel site, via Naver Reservation, or by phone +82-2-2230-3367. Wheelchair accessible
  • Onjium (온지음) — 1 star. 49 Hyoja-ro, Jongno-gu, Seoul, 4th floor. Reinterprets Joseon royal court cuisine with traditional and modern techniques. Lunch Tue-Fri 12:00-15:00 (Michelin page). Reserve by phone +82-2-6952-0024. The Michelin page says "cash only"
  • For all three, the only price information is the Michelin symbol ₩₩₩₩ — ask for the won amount when you book

🍚 Jeonju Hanjeongsik

  • What it is: One table carries 30 or so side dishes, and it took hold as the "baekban keunsang" (a big table of rice and sides) enjoyed by Jeonju's local officials became commercialized (VisitKorea)
  • UNESCO: Jeonju joined the UNESCO Creative Cities Network in the gastronomy field in 2012 (unesco.org)
  • Three hanjeongsik restaurants (listed in the VisitKorea article): Hanbyeokru (한벽루) 063-280-7003 (Jeonjucheon-dong-ro, Wansan-gu) · Gung (궁) 063-227-0844 (Eunhaeng-ro, Wansan-gu) · Jeolla Hoegwan (전라회관) 063-228-3033 (Anhaeng 4-gil, Wansan-gu). The article gives no detailed address, hours, or prices — call to check
  • Bibimbap (listed on the official Jeonju city tourism site): Gyodong Seokgalbi (26 Taejo-ro) · Manwol (3 Choi Myeong-hui-gil, yukhoe bibimbap with raw beef) · Pungnammun Bibimbap (53 Pungnammun 2-gil) · Keungama (46 Nosong Gwangjang-ro). Hours and prices not posted
  • Getting there: We could not open an official travel time for the Seoul-Jeonju KTX — check the Korail app. For a one-day Jeonju route, see #384 Jeonju Travel Map

🐟 Andong Heotjesabap

  • What it is: A local Andong dish inspired by ancestral ritual food. Andong people named it "fake ritual food" as an excuse to eat something delicious. It comes with rice, radish and tofu and beef soup, grilled mackerel, and more (VisitKorea)
  • Heotjesabap at Kkachigumeong-jip (까치구멍집): 203 Seokju-ro, Andong-si, Gyeongbuk, 11:00-20:00, closed on Seollal and Chuseok day (VisitKorea). Price not posted
  • Heotjesabap at Mat 50 Nyeon (맛50년): 201 Seokju-ro, Andong-si, Gyeongbuk, 11:00-20:00, closed on Seollal and Chuseok day (VisitKorea). Price not posted
  • Nearby: Hahoe and Yangdong historic villages were inscribed as UNESCO World Heritage in 2010 (Korea Heritage Service English page)

🪷 Temple Food

  • What it is: A food tradition handed down from Buddhist temples (VisitKorea). Venerable Jeong Kwan of Baekyangsa Temple (백양사) is a temple food master who received a James Beard Award and appeared on Netflix's "Chef's Table" (2017) (VisitKorea)
  • Korea Temple Food Center (한국사찰음식센터): VisitKorea lists 2nd floor, Ankuk Building, 39 Yulgok-ro, Jongno-gu, while the center's official site (koreatemplefood.com) lists 56 Ujeongguk-ro, Jongno-gu, so the addresses differ — confirm by phone at 02-733-4650 before you go. General opening hours are not posted, and programs are by reservation, session by session
  • Jingwansa Temple (진관사): 73 Jingwan-gil, Eunpyeong-gu, Seoul, 09:00-18:00, open year-round (VisitKorea). What is designated as National Intangible Heritage is the "Suryukjae of Jingwansa" (a Buddhist ritual, designated 2013-12-31, Korea Heritage Service)

🥗 Dietary Needs

  • Price range: This guide has no officially posted won prices. The 3 Michelin restaurants show only the ₩₩₩₩ symbol, and the Jeonju and Andong listing pages have no price field
  • Vegetarian: Most napa cabbage kimchi seasoning contains salted fermented seafood or fish sauce (VisitKorea) — ask about the kimchi and namul (seasoned vegetables) on a hanjeongsik table before you order. Confirm temple food ingredients when you reserve
  • Halal: VisitSeoul lists Eid (이드) in Itaewon (15 Usadan-ro 10-gil, Yongsan-gu; daily 12:00-21:00) as one of the few restaurants recognized as halal by the Korea Muslim Federation (KMF). Certification can change, so check before you go. None of the hanjeongsik restaurants in this guide carry a halal label
  • For a show-and-point card, see #1300 Dietary Request Manual

🧰 Hanjeongsik Practicalities — Solo, Reservations, Payment, Ordering, Getting Around

  • Solo: Some hanjeongsik places may only serve tables of 2 or more — call and ask first. For a map of restaurants that are comfortable for solo diners, see #348 Seoul Solo Diner Map
  • Reservations: From overseas, a phone call or a hotel concierge is the sure route. Catch Table is a Korean app, and we could not confirm whether you can sign up with a foreign phone number (#1109)
  • Payment: Onjium is "cash only" on its Michelin page, and Mingles accepts cards — for cards and mobile pay, see #527 Korea Payment Systems and #431 Payment and SIM Map
  • Ordering: Extra side dishes and water are free, and there is no tipping (VisitKorea) — if you cannot get across in words, see #1339 Ordering Etiquette
  • Getting around: For Jeonju and Andong, also read #1297 Express and Intercity Bus Manual. For transit cards, see #1298 Choosing Among 3 Transit Cards

⚠ 4 Common Mistakes

1. "Venerable Seonjae appeared on Chef's Table" — The featured monk is Venerable Jeong Kwan of Baekyangsa (VisitKorea). Venerable Seonjae is a different person

2. "Jingwansa's temple food is a National Intangible Cultural Property" — What is designated is the Suryukjae of Jingwansa, a Buddhist ritual (Korea Heritage Service)

3. "Michelin Korean for Monday lunch" — Mingles is closed Mon and Sun, and Onjium lunch is Tue-Fri. On Monday, look at La Yeon, which serves lunch every day

4. "Michelin Korean restaurant prices are public" — The Michelin pages show only the ₩₩₩₩ symbol. Ask for the amount when you reserve
