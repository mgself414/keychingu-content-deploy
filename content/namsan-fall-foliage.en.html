🎯 How to Use This Guide

This guide gathers places to visit on Namsan, in the middle of Seoul. They are Namsan Park, N Seoul Tower and Palgakjeong Pavilion at the summit, two walking trails, the outdoor botanical garden, Namsangol Hanok Village on the northern slope, and the Namsan beacon fire event. Namsangol Hanok Village closes on Mondays, and the park, tower, trails and botanical garden are registered as open year-round.

The operating details are copied from values registered in the Korea Tourism Organization tourism data (TourAPI). This guide covers only places registered in the Korea Tourism Organization tourism data.

📍 Namsan Park, N Seoul Tower and Namsan Palgakjeong

Namsan Park (남산공원) [Samil-daero 231 (삼일대로 231), Jung-gu, ☎ 02-3783-5900] is officially named Namsan Urban Nature Park (남산도시자연공원). Its original name was Ingyeongsan (인경산), and after King Taejo (태조) of Joseon moved the capital from Gaeseong (개성) to Seoul, it was called Namsan, meaning the mountain to the south. Through the 'Restoring Namsan's Original Appearance' (남산제모습 가꾸기) project that began in 1991 and ran for eight years, 89 buildings in the park were moved and the outdoor botanical garden was created. Since May 1, 2005, ordinary cars and taxis have not been allowed in the park. To reach N Seoul Tower, you take the circulation bus or Namsan Cable Car (남산케이블카) from Myeong-dong (명동), Chungmuro (충무로) or Dongguk Univ. Station (동대입구역), or climb on foot.

  • Opening hours: Always open. Closed days: Open year-round. Parking: Available.

N Seoul Tower (남산서울타워) [Namsan Gongwon-gil 105 (남산공원길 105), Yongsan-gu, ☎ 02-756-2486] is a tower that serves both broadcast signal transmission and sightseeing views. According to the Korea Tourism Organization, it is the first integrated radio wave tower in Korea. It is also known as a filming location for variety shows and dramas. 'Locks of love' hang along the way up to the tower observatory.

  • Opening hours: Weekdays 10:00~22:30, weekends 10:00~23:00. Closed days: Open year-round.
  • Parking: Available.

Namsan Palgakjeong (남산 팔각정) [Yejang-dong 8-1 (예장동 8-1), Jung-gu, ☎ Namsan Park 02-3783-5900] is a pavilion at the summit of Namsan with views over the city and its night scenery. You can climb to it on foot, by Namsan circulation bus or by Namsan Cable Car.

  • Opening hours: Always open. Closed days: Open year-round.

📍 Walking Trails — Namsan Dullegil and Namsan Gukchigil

Namsan Dullegil (남산둘레길) [Namsan Gongwon-gil 609 (남산공원길 609), Jung-gu, ☎ Namsan Park 02-3783-5900] is a trail that circles Namsan starting from Seoul Tower, 7.3km in total and about 2 hours 30 minutes long. It connects the North Circular Road (북측순환로), the History and Culture Trail (역사문화길), the Nature and Ecology Trail (자연생태길), the Wildflower Garden Trail (야생화원길) and the Forest Trail (산림숲길).

  • North Circular Road (북측순환로): 3.4km from the Namsan Cable Car entrance to the National Theater of Korea (국립극장). Cars and bicycles are not allowed, and there are tactile blocks down the middle of the path. It was selected as a barrier-free walking trail that is easy for wheelchair and stroller users and visually impaired people.
  • Sights along the way: An Jung-geun Memorial Hall (안중근 의사 기념관), Jamdubong Photo Island (잠두봉 포토아일랜드), the locks of love, and N Seoul Tower.
  • Opening hours: Always open. Closed days: Open year-round.

Namsan Gukchigil (남산국치길) [Juja-dong (주자동), Jung-gu, ☎ Myeong-dong Tourist Information Center (명동관광안내소) 02-774-3238] is a historical walking trail of about 1.7km along Namsan Yejang-jarak (예장자락). The Seoul Metropolitan Government created it to remember the sites where national sovereignty was lost, including the national humiliation of 1910. It starts at Namsan Yejang Park (남산예장공원), where the Residence of the Resident-General of Korea (한국통감관저) stood and the Japan-Korea Annexation Treaty was signed, and follows the logo shaped like the Korean letter giyeok (ㄱ) on the ground past the site of the Residency-General of Korea (한국통감부), the site of Nogi Shrine (노기신사), the Gabo Yeok Monument (갑오역기념비) erected by Imperial Japan after its victory in the First Sino-Japanese War, and the site of Gyeongseong Shrine (경성신사), leading to the site of Joseon Shrine (조선신궁). At the end of the trail stands the Seoul Memorial to the Victims of the Japanese Military 'Comfort Women' (서울 일본군 '위안부' 피해자 기림비).

  • Opening hours: Always open. Closed days: Open year-round.

📍 Namsan Outdoor Botanical Garden and Namsangol Hanok Village

Namsan Outdoor Botanical Garden (남산 야외식물원) [Itaewon-dong (이태원동), Yongsan-gu, ☎ 02-798-3771] was built on the site of foreigners' houses in Hannam-dong (한남동) that were demolished under the 'Restoring Namsan's Original Appearance' project, and opened on February 18, 1997. Along the walking paths you can see flowers and trees that change with the seasons.

  • Opening hours: Always open. Closed days: Open year-round. Parking: Available.

Namsangol Hanok Village (남산골한옥마을) [Toegye-ro 34-gil 28 (퇴계로34길 28), Jung-gu, ☎ Traditional House Operations Office (전통가옥 운영사무실) 02-6358-5533, cultural tourism guide 02-2133-6269] was built in 1998 on 79,934㎡ at the former Capital Defense Command (수도방위사령부) site on the north side of Namsan. Five hanok, moved or restored from around Seoul, stand here, along with the Traditional Crafts Hall (전통공예관), Cheonugak (천우각), a traditional garden, Seoul Namsan Gugakdang (서울남산국악당) and the Seoul Millennium Time Capsule (서울천년타임캡슐) plaza.

  • Experiences: Trying on hanbok (한복), folding hanji (한지), writing Hangeul (한글), drinking traditional tea, a traditional etiquette school and herbal medicine experiences. On Saturdays and Sundays real traditional weddings take place and a traditional wedding experience is offered. In the yard of Pigeumjeong (피금정) you can play jegichagi (제기차기), yutnori (윷놀이) and biseokchigi (비석치기), and traditional cultural heritage interpreters are on duty.
  • Opening hours: Apr~Oct 09:00~21:00, Nov~Mar 09:00~20:00.
  • Closed days: Every Monday. If Monday is a public holiday, it closes the next day.
  • Parking: Available. Stroller rental: Available.

📍 Event — Namsan Bongsu Ceremony and Other Traditional Culture Events

Namsan Bongsu Ceremony and other traditional culture events (남산봉수의식 등 전통문화행사) [Namsan Beacon Fire Station (남산 봉수대) and Bosingak (보신각), ☎ event site 02-319-1220, operator 02-737-6444] is a Seoul Metropolitan Government event that recreates bongsu (봉수), the beacon fire communication method of the Joseon era. Namsan Bongsudae was where beacon fires from beacon stations across the country were gathered and reported to the capital. In 2026 it runs from January 1 to December 31, 11:00~15:40, and admission is free.

  • Programs: A beacon fire ceremony and historical commentary at Namsan Bongsudae, the daily noon public bell-ringing event at Bosingak, open to citizens and visitors, a samulnori (사물놀이) performance and traditional martial arts demonstration at the Namsan Tower Palgakjeong plaza, and a one-day bongsu keeper (봉수꾼) experience.

⚠ 3 Things to Know Before You Go

  1. Ordinary cars and taxis cannot enter Namsan Park — You climb by circulation bus, Namsan Cable Car or on foot.
  2. Namsangol Hanok Village closes on Mondays — From November to March it closes at 20:00.
  3. The registered period for the Namsan Bongsu Ceremony runs until December 31, 2026 — It is 11:00~15:40 and free.

⚙ FAQ 2

Q. How do you get up to N Seoul Tower?

A. According to the Korea Tourism Organization registered information, you take the circulation bus or Namsan Cable Car from Myeong-dong, Chungmuro or Dongguk Univ. Station, or climb on foot. Ordinary cars and taxis cannot enter the park.

Q. Is there a trail that people who find walking hard can use?

A. The North Circular Road of Namsan Dullegil (Namsan Cable Car entrance to the National Theater of Korea, 3.4km). It was selected as a barrier-free walking trail, and cars and bicycles are not allowed.
