Hong Kong and Macau to Korea Travel Guide: 90 Days Visa-Free, Checking K-ETA, Automated Immigration, Direct Flights (Official Values, October 2026)

🎯 How to Use This Guide

The landing announcement plays, the meal trays are cleared, and it is time to take your passport out of the front pocket of your bag. With a Hong Kong or Macau passport, you are among the first who can use the automated gates in Korea's arrival line. This guide lists what travelers coming from Hong Kong and Macau should check before departure, using only values from official pages: entry documents (visa-free days, K-ETA, e-Arrival), the airport immigration line (automated clearance), payment, flights, and, if you are traveling for food, where to look.

The old guide's "90 days visa-free between Korea and Hong Kong" matches the Ministry of Foreign Affairs list, so it stays. "400,000 visitors in 2024", "average spend of 1,800 USD", "100% independent travel", "8+ daily Incheon–Hong Kong flights", and the restaurant and hotel rankings and star ratings were removed because we could not confirm them officially this time.

🛂 Entry Documents: 90 Days Visa-Free, K-ETA, e-Arrival Card

  • Visa-free stay: the Ministry of Foreign Affairs list shows Hong Kong 90 days and Macau 90 days (both reciprocity) (Ministry of Foreign Affairs official list "Countries whose nationals may enter Korea without a visa", as of February 2024, revised 2026-01-08). How other passport types such as the Hong Kong BN(O) are treated is not in this list; check with the embassy
  • Temporary K-ETA exemption: it runs from January 1 to December 31, 2026 (K-ETA official notice, posted 2025-12-23). The notice does not list the eligible countries, so we could not confirm from the notice whether Hong Kong and Macau are included. Scan your passport on the k-eta.go.kr application screen and it will tell you whether you are "exempt". Whether it will be extended into 2027 has not been decided
  • e-Arrival Card: if you did not actually obtain a K-ETA, submit the e-Arrival Card. It is free at e-arrivalcard.go.kr from 3 days before arrival, and a paper form is also accepted (Ministry of Justice official site, #1289 e-Arrival manual)
  • Companions traveling on mainland Chinese passports face entirely different conditions: Mainland China to Korea Travel Guide: Visa for Independent Travel, 15-Day Visa-Free for Groups (Through 2026-12-31), 30 Days on Jeju, Payments

🚪 The Airport Immigration Line: Automated Clearance

  • Automated immigration clearance for foreigners started on 2025-11-03 with 4 targets (Germany, Taiwan, Hong Kong, Macau) and expanded to 42 countries and regions on 2026-03-16 (Ministry of Justice official). It applies to visitors aged 17 or older entering visa-free
  • On your first entry, register your passport scan, fingerprints and face at the registration line, and from that day you can use the automated gates. We could not confirm the registration counter's opening hours from official documents; for the steps, see #1412 automated clearance manual
  • On departure, short-term visitors aged 17 or older who gave fingerprints and a face scan on entry can use the automated gates (Ministry of Justice immigration official guidance)

👥 Traveling Companions

  • Under-17s cannot use automated immigration clearance (Ministry of Justice). If you travel with a child, it is easier to queue together in the regular line
  • A party mixing Hong Kong and mainland Chinese passports faces different entry conditions: mainland passports are not on the visa-free list (Ministry of Foreign Affairs list)

⚠ 4 Common Mistakes

1. "I'm visa-free, so I don't have to submit anything": if you did not obtain a K-ETA, you must still submit the e-Arrival Card (Ministry of Justice)

2. "The K-ETA exemption will continue": the announced temporary exemption lasts only through 2026-12-31 (K-ETA notice). What comes after is undecided

3. "I have a Hong Kong passport, so my mainland friend traveling with me is visa-free too": mainland Chinese passports are not on the visa-free list (Ministry of Foreign Affairs list)

4. "The whole family can use the automated line": under-17s cannot use automated clearance (Ministry of Justice)
