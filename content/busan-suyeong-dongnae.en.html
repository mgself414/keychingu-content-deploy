Busan's Point of Origin — Suyeong (수영) and Dongnae (동래), Behind Gwangalli and Joseon-Era Busan

Subtitle: If Haeundae and Gwangalli are "tourist Busan," two neighborhoods behind them are where the city actually began — one the Joseon-era administrative seat and birthplace of the local pancake, the other home to an old naval fortress now flanked by a converted-factory arts complex and a rising cafe alley. A single subway line connects both for a one-day trip into the "original file" of Busan.

ISSUE · #1329 · 2026-08-24

🎯 The Frame — Busan Before Haeundae

Busan grew into a port city after its 1876 opening, but before that, the region's center wasn't the sea — it was inland Dongnae (동래). The traces remain: Dongnae Eupseong (동래읍성), the fortress town that saw the first battle of the 1592 Japanese invasions; Gaya-era burial mounds; and Gyeongsang Jwasuyeong (경상좌수영), the Joseon navy's southern command post (the origin of today's Suyeong-gu district name). If you've already seen the Gwangan Bridge night view (#1326), spend the next day walking these two neighborhoods, half a day each — the route that opens the "original file" of tourist Busan.

  • Transit backbone: Subway Lines 2 and 3. Suyeong is served by Suyeong Station (수영역) on Lines 2/3; Dongnae is served by the Dongnae (동래역) and Suan (수안역) station area on Lines 1/4 — Line 3 connects both directly (see #1325 for transit details).

① Suyeong, Half a Day — Market, Fortress, F1963, and Mangmi-dangil (Walking Route)

A vetted route also featured as an official City of Busan walking course. Total walking time is about 2.5–3 hours, plus however long you linger.

1. Suyeong Station (수영역), Exit 5 → Suyeong Paldo Market (수영팔도시장) (about 3 min. on foot) — an arcade market whose name literally means "goods from all eight provinces gather here," a nod to its Joseon-era naval days. It's a neighborhood market, not a tourist one, so fish-cake and street-food prices stay low

2. Market → Suyeong Historic Park (수영사적공원) (about 5 min. on foot) — the site of the old Jwasuyeong naval fortress. Centuries-old black pine trees and traces of the fortress gate remain, and the Suyeongseong Village Museum (수영성마을박물관) sits right beside it

3. Historic Park → F1963 (about 15 min. on foot) — a 1963 wire factory built by Kiswire (고려제강), converted whole into a cultural complex. A bookstore, exhibition hall, cafes, and a garden all sit inside the factory's original bones — Busan's answer to Seoul's Seongsu-dong-style factory renovations, at Busan scale

4. F1963 → Mangmi-dangil (망미단길) (about 5 min. on foot) — a rising alley in the Mangmi-dong (망미동) residential district, where cafes, flower shops, and small eateries have quietly moved in. It reads more like a neighborhood than a commercial strip — keeping your voice down is the local etiquette

Gwangalli Beach (#243, #1023) is a 15-minute walk from Suyeong Station — walk this route in the afternoon, then close the day with the Gwangalli sunset and the Gwangan Bridge night view.

② Dongnae, Half a Day — Market, Pajeon, Fortress Town, and Burial Mounds (Walking Route)

1. Suan Station (수안역), Exit 5 → Dongnae Market (동래시장) (about 5 min. on foot) — a traditional market loaded with jokbal (pig's trotters), kalguksu (knife-cut noodles), and fish cakes. But there's really one reason to come: Dongnae pajeon (동래파전)

2. What makes Dongnae pajeon different: unlike ordinary scallion pancakes, the batter is rice flour mixed with anchovy stock, giving a crisp exterior and a dense, moist inside. Local lore holds that the Dongnae magistrate once presented it to the king in the Joseon era — the dish is this neighborhood's signature, servable both in the market's pancake alley and at a specialty old-guard restaurant behind the district office (one now in its fourth generation is especially well known)

3. Market → Dongnae Eupseong North Gate and History Hall (동래읍성 북문·역사관) (an uphill walk of about 20 min.) — the site of the first battle of the 1592 Japanese invasions. A maintained trekking trail runs along the fortress wall, and the North Gate area overlooks the city

4. Fortress town → Bokcheon-dong Burial Mounds and Bokcheon Museum (복천동 고분군·복천박물관) (about 15 min. on foot) — Gaya-era burial mounds run along a ridge like a chain of grassy hills. The museum is free, and the hillside itself doubles as a walking path

If your legs are done, finish with a soak at Dongnae Hot Springs (동래온천) (#269) — it's one subway stop from the market.

③ Both Neighborhoods in One Day — Suggested Schedule

Time Route
10:00 Suan Station → early pajeon lunch at Dongnae Market
12:00 Dongnae Eupseong → Bokcheon Museum
15:00 Line 3 transfer (about 20 min.) → Suyeong Paldo Market → Suyeong Historic Park
16:30 F1963 → coffee in Mangmi-dangil
18:30 Walk or bus to Gwangalli — sunset and the Gwangan Bridge night view (#1326)
  • To ease the physical load, split this into two half-days over two days. In summer, it's better to schedule the Dongnae Eupseong climb in the morning.

💡 Points of Confusion

  • Dongnae Eupseong isn't "a single standing fortress" — it's a cluster of ruins scattered across the hillside. Setting the North Gate and History Hall as your map pin is the accurate move.
  • F1963's indoor facilities (exhibitions, etc.) may run on different schedules by day of the week — check official notices before visiting.
  • Mangmi-dangil and the Suyeong alleys are residential areas. Take photos inside shops, and keep your voice down in the alleys — that's the local etiquette.
