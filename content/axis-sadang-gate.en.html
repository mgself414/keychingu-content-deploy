Seoul E-W Axis ⑥ Sadang, in the Evening — The Gate Out of Seoul: Restaurant Alley, Transfer Stop, and the Gwanaksan Trailhead

Subtitle: The western end of the Line 2 eastern semicircle (#1359). Sadang Station has fourteen exits, and three districts — Gwanak, Dongjak, and Seocho — meet at its intersection, where express buses queue every evening bound for southern Gyeonggi Province. This piece walks that evening scene — the restaurant alley around the exits, the transfer stop, the road that continues past Namtaeryeong into Gyeonggi, and on to the Gwanaksan trailhead, about an hour and a half in all. No hiking involved. Seoul E-W Axis, Part 6.

ISSUE · #1361 · 2026-09-07

🎯 The Frame — The Gate Out of Seoul

Seoul's gateways aren't only the airport and Seoul Station. Sadang Station (사당역) is the gate that tens of thousands pass through every evening on their way out of the city. Line 2 and Line 4 meet here, and express buses toward Gwacheon (과천), Anyang (안양), Suwon (수원), Gunpo (군포), and Hwaseong (화성) load up at the stop out front and carry people over Namtaeryeong (남태령) pass. If the N-S Axis (#1353) entered downtown through the Namsan tunnel, this piece stands at the point where the city ends.

Sadang is also where three districts meet. Around the intersection, Exits 4, 5, and 6 sit in Gwanak-gu (관악구), Exits 7 through 10 in Dongjak-gu (동작구), and the rest in Seocho-gu (서초구). And from Exit 4, it's 15 minutes to the start of the Gwanaksan (관악산) trail. This piece walks that boundary in the evening — the transfer stop packed with commuters, the lit-up restaurant alley, and on to the dark at the mountain's entrance.

  • Total time: about 1 hour 30 minutes (about 40 minutes walking + time at the stop and alley)
  • Best time: depart between 5:30 and 6 PM — this carries you through the transfer stop at its busiest and puts you at the Gwanaksan entrance right at dusk. Since you won't enter the trail itself, it's fine if it's dark by then
  • Incline: gentle to moderate — only the stretch toward the Gwanaksan entrance climbs gently

🚶 The Route — In the Order You Walk It

The starting point is Sadang Station, the terminus of the Line 2 eastern semicircle (#1359) and a Line 4 transfer station.

① Sadang Station → Picking an Exit → the Restaurant Alley (🚶 about 5 minutes)

  • There are fourteen exits. Eight opened with Line 2 originally, and six more came with Line 4. The evening course starts toward Exits 4, 5, and 6, on the Gwanak-gu side — the alley behind the station is packed tight with restaurants and bars. No specific alley name is used here (the official name is unconfirmed). Walk through it once: this is when after-work customers start claiming their seats.
  • 📖 Story 1 — The Intersection Where Three Districts Meet: The Sadang intersection is the boundary of Gwanak-gu, Dongjak-gu, and Seocho-gu. A station whose exits straddle three districts is rare even in Seoul. A station sitting on a boundary belongs to the center of no neighborhood — instead, it became "a place you pass through." That's this station's character.

② The Restaurant Alley → the Express Bus Transfer Stop (🚶 about 10 minutes)

  • Leave the alley for the Sadang intersection. At the roadside stops in front of the station, express buses toward Gwacheon, Anyang, Suwon, Gunpo, Hwaseong, and Pyeongtaek (평택) line up. Around 6 PM, the sight of the line growing longer is the center of this piece — the scene of Seoulites leaving Seoul.
  • 📖 Story 2 — Namtaeryeong: These buses head south over Namtaeryeong pass, 183m above sea level. It's the boundary between Seoul's Gwanak-gu and Seocho-gu and Gyeonggi's Gwacheon. Since 2017, inbound express buses have stopped at Line 4's Namtaeryeong Station (남태령역), letting riders transfer to the subway. A single pass divides Seoul from Gyeonggi — the reason this piece calls Sadang a "gate."

③ The Transfer Stop → Toward the Gwanaksan Entrance (🚶 about 15 minutes · gentle uphill)

  • Head back toward Exit 4 and go south. Turn off the main road onto the alley toward Gwaneumsa (관음사), and the residential blocks soon end where the mountain begins. This is the Gwanaksan trailhead — a serious trail that takes two and a half to three hours to the summit, Yeonjudae (연주대) (#148, #738).
  • This piece goes only as far as the entrance. Turn around at the entrance at dusk and you'll see the lights of the Sadang intersection and the city beyond.

④ The Entrance → Kkachisan Neighborhood Park or Back to Sadang Station (🚶 about 10 minutes)

  • Optional: coming down through Kkachisan Neighborhood Park (까치산근린공원) on the Sadang-dong side (connected to Gwanaksan by an eco-bridge since late 2006), the walking path above the residential blocks opens onto a neighborhood night view (confirm the location and route with on-site signage). If you're short on time, retrace your steps to Sadang Station.
  • Wrap up at Sadang Station. Head back on Line 4 toward Isu Station (이수역) — officially Chongshin University Station (총신대입구역) — or Dongjak, or on Line 2 toward Gangnam or Jamsil.

🙏 Manners — the Core of This Course

  • The transfer stop is commuters heading home. Don't cut across the line or linger taking photos in front of the stop.
  • The restaurant alley is just opening for the evening — don't photograph shop displays or tables out front.
  • Past the Gwanaksan entrance is the trail itself. Don't enter it after dark.
  • Behind the alley is a residential area. Keep your voice down.

💡 Where You Might Get Thrown Off

  • 14 exits: decide on Exit 4 (toward Gwanaksan and the alley) before you get off the train, and follow the signage. Come out the opposite side, toward Seocho-gu, and you'll have to cross the intersection twice.
  • The stops are split up: express bus stops are scattered by direction and route. If you're just here to watch, one stop near the intersection is enough.
  • Don't climb Gwanaksan at night: this piece ends at the entrance. Save the hike for morning (#148).
  • Evening rush: Sadang Station's transfer corridors are packed from 5:30 to 7 PM. Avoid it if you're carrying bulky bags.

6 FAQs

Q1. Is there a reason for tourists to go to Sadang?

  • You go to see "where Seoul ends." The line of express buses, Namtaeryeong pass, and the mountain beyond it. The point is a scene, not a landmark.

Q2. What's the restaurant alley called?

  • No name is used here because the official name couldn't be confirmed. The alley behind Exits 4, 5, and 6 is all you need.

Q3. How do I climb Gwanaksan?

  • From Sadang Station Exit 4, take the trail toward Gwaneumsa. Two and a half to three hours to the summit. See the hiking pieces #148, #738, #594.

Q4. Are Isu Station and Sadang Station different stations?

  • They're one Line 4 stop apart. Isu Station was renamed Chongshin University Station (Isu) just six days after it opened in 1985. Some of the evening crowd gets off here, the stop right after Sadang.

Q5. How does this connect with the overview (#1359)?

  • Ride the Line 2 inner loop from Euljiro to Sadang and get off, and this piece picks up right where it left off. The overview in the morning, this piece in the evening — that makes for one complete day.

Q6. What if I want to go past Namtaeryeong?

  • Take Line 4 one stop toward Namtaeryeong and Gwacheon, and you're in Gyeonggi Province. For Gwacheon's Seoul Grand Park (서울대공원) and racecourse park, see #451 and #553.
