Seoul's 12 Sunset Spots — The 45 Minutes Before Sunset: A Golden-Hour Guide

Subtitle: A different hour of Seoul than night view (#224) — 12 golden-hour spots you can only catch by arriving 45 minutes before sunset + a pick-by-type matrix + a monthly sunset-time table + a route that flows straight into night view (links to the western Han River #305 · Eungbongsan #051)

ISSUE 04 · #1308 · 2026-08-19

🎯 Why Sunset Gets Its Own Guide — a Different Angle from the Night-View Guide

Seoul content has already covered night view a lot (#002 Free Night Views · #224 Night View Full Guide). But sunset is a completely different game from night view.

Category Sunset (this guide) Night View (#224)
Golden window 45 min before sunset to 20 min after — just 1 hour a day after sunset to late night, plenty of time
Direction Only spots with an open western sky work direction doesn't matter, light density is what counts
Failure mode Arrive late = the day is over. No retry until tomorrow almost none
Best season Sep–Nov (less fine dust · cool evening air) year-round

In a travel itinerary, sunset is free content that fits exactly into the "one hour before dinner" slot. All 12 spots in this guide are free to enter, chosen for public-transit access.

① 3 Mistakes Foreigners Often Make

1. Timing Your Arrival to the Sunset "Moment"

The peak of a sunset isn't the official sunset time — it's 15–30 minutes before sunset (the window when the sky spreads from orange to pink). If you arrive at sunset time, it's already over. Arriving 45 minutes early is the formula.

2. Chasing Height Without Checking Direction

Namsan Tower (남산타워) is tall, but buildings overlap the western view. A sunset isn't decided by height — it's decided by how open the western horizon is. That's why the top picks in this guide cluster along the western Han River and west-facing hills.

3. Watching the Sunset and Heading Straight Back Down

Half of these sunset spots transform into night-view spots 20 minutes later. Eungbongsan (응봉산, #051) and Maebongsan (매봉산) are the prime examples — watching sunset flow straight into night view from the same spot is the most efficient route.

③ The 12 Spots in Detail

Han River Water-Reflection Type

1. Seonyudo Park (선유도공원) — An island park in the middle of the Yanghwa (양화) stretch of the Han River. The sun setting between the old water-treatment plant's structures makes the most "un-Seoul-like" picture in this guide. 10-min walk from Seonyudo Station (선유도역), Line 9. (Western Han River detail: #305)

2. Nodeul Island (노들섬) — A culture island on the Hangang Bridge (한강대교). The best day is one where a sunset toward Yeouido (여의도) from the western lawn overlaps with a weekend-evening live performance. 10-min walk from Nodeul Station (노들역), Line 9.

3. Banpo Hangang Park (반포 한강공원) — The sun sets behind Some Sevit (세빛섬). Once sunset ends, it flows naturally into the Moonlight Rainbow Fountain (달빛무지개분수) (Mar 16–Oct 31, 2026). 15-min walk from Express Bus Terminal Station (고속터미널역).

4. Mangwon Hangang Park (망원 한강공원) — The most local-heavy sunset. A shrine to the convenience store (편의점, Korea's ubiquitous 24-hour corner store) instant-noodles-plus-picnic-mat combo. 12-min walk from Mangwon Station (망원역). (Han River culture full guide: #209·#700)

Hillside Panorama Type

5. Haneul Park (하늘공원) — A silver-grass hill inside World Cup Park (월드컵공원). The combination of October silver-grass season + sunset ranks #1 in all of Seoul. 291 steps, or the Maengkkongi electric car (맹꽁이차, paid). 20+ min walk from World Cup Stadium Station (월드컵경기장역).

6. Eungbongsan Pavilion (응봉산 팔각정) — The only steep hill that looks west over the eastern Han River. The sunset-to-night-view transition (#051) is the most dramatic here. 15-min stair walk from Eungbong Station (응봉역).

7. Maebongsan Pavilion (매봉산 팔각정) — A hidden hill behind Hannam (한남) and Beotigogae (버티고개). A composition that frames Namsan Tower (남산타워) and the Han River together while catching the western sunset. 15-min walk from Beotigogae Station (버티고개역). Almost no tourists.

8. Yongyangbongjeojeong Park (용양봉저정공원) — From a hill in Noryangjin (노량진), the sun sets beyond the Hangang Railroad Bridge (한강철교) and the Yeouido skyline. 15-min walk from Nodeul Station or Noryangjin Station (노량진역). A local evening-stroll course.

Fortress Wall + Skyline Silhouette Type

9. Inwangsan Beombawi (인왕산 범바위) — A rock lookout partway up the fortress-wall trail. The sun sets to the west as you look down over the Gyeongbokgung (경복궁)·Gwanghwamun (광화문) axis — the only composition that captures downtown and sunset together. 40-min hike from Dongnimmun Station (독립문역). (Hiking basics: #148)

10. Naksan Park (낙산공원) — A hill above Dongdaemun (동대문). The downtown silhouette beyond the fortress wall sinks into backlight. 15-min walk from Hyehwa Station (혜화역) — a great-value course for fortress-wall sunset without the hike.

11. Bugak Pavilion (북악 팔각정) — A lookout reachable by car or taxi. An alternative for groups who struggle to walk (family·senior companions). A full view of Seoul plus sunset from the city's north side.

After-Work Value Type

12. Yeouido Mulbit Square (여의도 물빛광장) — Right in front of Yeouinaru Station (여의나루역). #1 for accessibility among all 12 spots. Watching it mixed in with the after-work crowd (퇴근길, the homeward commute from Korea's financial district) is Seoul's weekday evening itself.

🍂 Oct–Nov Autumn Sunset Notes (2026 Update)

Autumn is the season of the clearest sunsets, but a route built for summer will miss the mark in some places.

  • Arrival slides into the 4 PM hour — Late-October sunset is around 17:30 (5:30 PM) and November's is about 17:20 (5:20 PM), so arriving 45 minutes early means 16:30–16:45 (4:30–4:45 PM). Finish museum and shopping plans before 4 PM.
  • Banpo Hangang Park: a 2-hour gap between sunset and the fountain — The Moonlight Rainbow Fountain runs 3/16–10/31 in 2026, and the October night shows are at 19:30·20:00·20:30·21:00 (20 min each). The sun sets around 17:30 (5:30 PM), so to link "sunset → fountain," the realistic plan is to eat dinner near Some Sevit and wait. From November there is no fountain — at Banpo, watch only the sunset and then move on to another night view.
  • Haneul Park: during the silver-grass festival, the glow stays after sunset — The 25th Seoul Silver Grass Festival (서울억새축제) (10/17–23, 10:00–21:00) turns on night lighting, so you get backlit silver grass at sunset → the lit-up silver-grass field in one go. The full festival schedule is in #1404, the silver-grass season guide in #1313.
  • Indoor sunset for rain or cold: Seoul City Hall Sky Observatory (서울시청 하늘전망대) — A free viewing space on the 8th–9th floors of the west wing of City Hall's main building (no reservation). It looks over downtown toward Deoksugung (덕수궁) and Gwanghwamun (광화문). It's open until 18:00 on weekdays (Mon–Thu) and until 22:00 on Fridays and Saturdays (21:00 from November to February). October sunsets come before 18:00, so you can catch the sunset even on a weekday. Take the dedicated elevator next to the Robot Café on the 1st floor. Right outside City Hall Station.
  • Where autumn leaves meet sunset: two fortress-wall spots — From late October to mid-November, the leaves around Naksan Park's fortress wall trail and Inwangsan Beombawi catch the backlight of the sunset. For downtown palace and fortress-wall foliage routes, see #273.

⑤ Field Tips

  • Lighting for the way down: The stairs at Eungbongsan, Inwangsan, and Maebongsan go dark after sunset. Your phone light is enough, but flip-flops are a hard no.
  • Tripod: The hillside pavilions have railings, so a mini tripod is plenty. For the Han River type, a low angle from a picnic mat actually works better.
  • Food route: Mangwon (망원 → Mangwon Market (망원시장) food) · Eungbongsan (응봉산 → Seongsu (성수) dinner) · Naksan (낙산 → Daehangno (대학로)) — these 3 spots, with a dinner neighborhood right next door after sunset, are the most efficient for first-timers.
  • All of them are free. The only things that cost money are the Haneul Park Maengkkongi car and your dinner.
