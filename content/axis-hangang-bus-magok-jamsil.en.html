Seoul E-W Axis ⑦ Hangang Bus, from Magok to Jamsil — Reading Seoul Sideways from the Water

Subtitle: After a 50-year-old road (Bus 140), a 600-year-old road (Bus 720), and a loop line (Line 2), the third stroke is the river. Board the Hangang Bus at Magok pier, transfer once at Yeouido, and ride past Apgujeong or Oksu, Seoul Forest, and Ttukseom to Jamsil — about three and a half hours on the water. Seoul E-W Axis, Part 7.

ISSUE · #1362 · 2026-09-07

🎯 The Frame — The Third Stroke, the River

The bus that reads Seoul north to south (#1353), the bus that reads it east to west (#1356), and the eastern half of the loop line (#1359) — this series has so far ridden only roads on land. This piece goes down onto the water. The Han River (한강) is the oldest straight line running east-west through Seoul, and since September 18, 2025, the Hangang Bus (한강버스) has run on it as public transit. It isn't a sightseeing cruise. It's a boat you tap a transit card to board, get off at the next pier, and pay about 3,000 won one way.

Eight piers run west to east — Magok pier (마곡 선착장), Mangwon (망원), Yeouido (여의도), Apgujeong (압구정), Oksu (옥수), Seoul Forest (서울숲), Ttukseom (뚝섬), and Jamsil (잠실). The route splits at Yeouido into a western leg and an eastern leg, so getting from Magok to Jamsil means getting off once at Yeouido and transferring. This piece uses that structure as its course exactly: board at the western end, Magok, transfer at Yeouido, and get off at the eastern end, Jamsil. Every neighborhood around the piers is a pass-through — the protagonist of this piece is the river itself.

  • Total time: about 3 hours 30 minutes (about 2 hours on the water + waiting for the Yeouido transfer + about 30 minutes walking between stations and piers — varies a lot by schedule)
  • Best time: depart on a weekday morning — check the schedule first. Departures run about an hour apart, so if you don't time your start, the wait gets long. You can also work backward to catch sunset on the water by timing the eastern leg for late afternoon (afternoon and evening runs have increased since August 2026)
  • Incline: none — the walking stretches, station to pier, are flat

Why the Hangang Bus — Seoul's Newest Public Transit

The Hangang Bus ran a public trial from July 2025, then began official service on September 18, 2025. A mechanical fault stopped part of the route over the winter; full service resumed on March 1, 2026, and ridership climbed quickly afterward — 62,000 in March, 76,000 in April, and 91,000 in May, each a new monthly high, and cumulative ridership passed 500,000 as of August 13, 2026 (per the Seoul city government). A user survey (3,569 respondents, June–July 2026) found 97% satisfaction. Going by card-payment ratios at onboard facilities, the operator estimates that about 35% of riders are foreign — the piers where foreign visitors board most are Yeouido and Ttukseom.

📖 Story 1 — The River Was There First: The N-S Axis's bridge (1969) and tunnel (1970), the E-W Axis's 600-year-old Jongno (종로) and loop line (1984) — all of these are lines people laid down. The Han River was there before any of them. This straight line dividing Seoul east and west was a route for ferry crossings and grain shipping in the Joseon era, became scenery passing under bridges in the 20th century, and turned into a route again in 2025. In effect, Seoul's newest public transit runs along its oldest axis.

🚶 The Route — In the Order of the Water

The starting point is Yangcheon Hyanggyo Station (양천향교역) on Line 9 (9호선). It's about a 12-minute walk (0.8 km) to Magok pier. The shuttle bus was discontinued in August 2026, so you walk.

① Yangcheon Hyanggyo Station → Magok Pier → Buying a Ticket (🚶 about 12 minutes)

  • From the station, head straight toward the river. Buy your ticket at the ticket machine in the pier building — card payment only, no advance booking; a transit card also works, such as T-money (티머니). Adults 3,000 won, teens 1,800 won, children 1,100 won (official as of September 2026, subject to change). Each boat holds 199 passengers, so on weekends you may get bumped to the next departure.
  • Check the schedule again here. The western leg (Magok–Yeouido) and eastern leg (Yeouido–Jamsil) run on separate schedules.

② Magok → Mangwon (🚢 about 25 minutes · pass-through)

  • As the boat pulls out to mid-river, western Seoul opens up. On the left (north) bank, apartment blocks around Magok and Gayang; to the right, the direction of Banghwa Bridge and Haengju Fortress. Every bridge you pass under adds another name to Seoul's list of bridges.
  • Mangwon pier — you don't get off. Mangwon Market (망원시장) and the Mangwon neighborhood (#489, #25) are up across the river from here.

③ Mangwon → Yeouido — Transfer (🚢 about 20 minutes · disembark)

  • Past Yanghwa Bridge (양화대교), Yeouido's glass towers and the 63 Building appear on the left. Everyone gets off at Yeouido pier — the western and eastern legs both end and begin here. To keep going the same direction, you disembark and board again on the eastern (Jamsil-bound) leg. Follow the on-site staff for transfer fares and procedure.
  • 📖 Story 2 — Transferring at Yeouido: The route splitting in two at Yeouido follows Seoul's own geography. Yeouido is the hinge between western Seoul and eastern Seoul, and even on the river, this is where people change boats. If the transfer wait is long, you can step out to the river-view café on the third floor of the pier building — Starbucks Yeouido Hangang Park (스타벅스 여의도한강공원점), one representative example — or to the riverside park toward Yeouinaru Station (여의나루역) on Line 5, about a 4-minute walk.

④ Yeouido → Apgujeong or Oksu (🚢 about 30 minutes · pass-through)

  • The eastern leg passes under Mapo Bridge, Hangang Bridge, Dongjak Bridge, and Banpo Bridge (반포대교) in turn. To the right (south), Yongsan and Ichon; to the left, Banpo and Sebitseom (세빛섬). Hannam Bridge (한남대교), which the N-S Axis (#1353) crossed by bus, appears straight ahead — this time you pass beneath it.
  • The next stop is either Apgujeong or Oksu, alternating by run. You pass through both regardless. Apgujeong is more than a 20-minute walk from Apgujeong Station, while Oksu is 5 minutes from Exit 3 of Oksu Station (옥수역) — the Oksu neighborhood is the eastern neighbor of Hannam (한남) from N-S Axis #1354.

⑤ → Seoul Forest → Ttukseom (🚢 about 15 minutes · pass-through)

  • Past Seongsu Bridge (성수대교), Seoul Forest appears on the left. The Seoul Forest pier operates only from June 8 to late October 2026 (tied to a garden fair), so it may not be a stop afterward. Next is Ttukseom pier — the pier where foreign passengers get off second-most after Yeouido. This piece passes through both (Seongsu and Seoul Forest are covered in #1342, #701, #709).

⑥ Ttukseom → Jamsil — Closing (🚢 about 25 minutes)

  • Past Cheongdam and Jamsil bridges, Lotte World Tower (롯데월드타워, 555 m) draws closer beyond the right bank. Get off at Jamsil pier, the eastern end.
  • Closing: Jamsil pier is far from the subway — about a 15-minute walk toward Jamsilsaenae Station (잠실새내역) (per on-site signage). The Jamsil neighborhood is covered in #82, #300, #707. This piece ends right beneath the spot where the Line 2 (2호선) eastern semicircle (#1359) crossed the river above on Jamsil Railway Bridge (잠실철교).

🚢 5 Ways to Ride It

1. Check the schedule first: departures run about an hour apart. Check the day's schedule at hgbus.co.kr or at the pier's ticket machine, and fix your departure time.

2. No advance booking, card only: pay by card (including transit cards) at the on-site ticket machine. No cash (#1301). Capacity: 199 passengers.

3. You must get off at Yeouido: the western and eastern routes are separate. Disembark, then re-board on the eastern leg.

4. Seat choice — upper deck or window: the upper deck is best for passing under bridges; if it's windy, sit by an indoor window. On the eastern leg, the right (south) side has the Gangnam skyline.

5. Check for service suspensions: service stops when Paldang Dam (팔당댐) discharge exceeds 3,000 m³/s, Jamsu Bridge (잠수교) water level exceeds its threshold, or weather turns severe. During monsoon and typhoon season, check the notice before you leave — alternatives are #1256 (the subway's Han River line) and #1248 (Gyeongui-Jungang Line car windows).

🙏 Manners on Board — the Core of This Course

  • Don't lean on or climb the deck railing. Follow crew instructions.
  • Don't shout when passing under bridges — it takes the sound of the river away from other passengers.
  • The pier buildings and riverside parks belong to residents. Don't spend a long time photographing in the ticket line.
  • Put trash in the sorted bins on board. Throwing anything into the river goes without saying.

💡 Where Visitors Get Thrown Off

  • The shuttle bus is gone: the free shuttles at Magok, Apgujeong, and Jamsil were discontinued in August 2026. You walk from the station or take a city bus — the reason this piece routes Magok through a walk from Yangcheon Hyanggyo Station and Jamsil through a walk from Jamsilsaenae Station.
  • Apgujeong and Oksu alternate: only one stops per run. This piece passes through either way, so it doesn't matter here — but if you plan to get off, check which run you're on.
  • The Seoul Forest pier is temporary: it runs only until late October 2026. It may drop off the schedule after that.
  • Fares may change: differential weekend/holiday pricing is under review (undecided as of September 2026). Whatever the ticket machine shows on the day is the answer.
  • The transfer wait can be long: you may wait more than 30 minutes at Yeouido for the next eastern-leg departure. The third-floor café or the riverside park serves as your waiting room.

FAQ 6

Q1. How is it different from a sightseeing cruise?

  • The sightseeing cruise (#704) is a round-trip tourist product; the Hangang Bus is one-way public transit. You board with a transit card, get off at the next pier, and transfer to the subway. The fare is also in the 3,000-won range.

Q2. Can you ride one boat straight from Magok to Jamsil?

  • No. You must get off at Yeouido and transfer to the eastern leg. That's the structure of this course.

Q3. How long does it take?

  • The western leg to Yeouido takes about 45 minutes; the eastern leg from Yeouido to Jamsil takes roughly 1 hour 20 to 30 minutes (varies by which stops your run makes). Add the transfer wait and it comes to about three and a half hours.

Q4. Is it easy for foreign visitors to ride?

  • The ticket machine takes cards, transit cards work, and English signage is available; the operator estimates that about 35% of riders are foreign. That said, you have to check the schedule yourself.

Q5. What if it rains?

  • Service is suspended in severe weather or during heavy Paldang Dam discharge. Check the day's notice, and if it's suspended, switch to the subway's Han River line (#1256).

Q6. How does this connect to the N-S and E-W Axes?

  • This boat passes under Hannam Bridge from the N-S Axis (#1353), and this piece ends beneath Jamsil Railway Bridge from E-W Axis Part 4 (#1359). All three strokes meet at the river.
