Seoul Hospitals for Foreign Patients — International Healthcare Centers Are Daytime Only, Nights and Weekends Mean the ER: Contacts, Languages and Hours for 8 Hospitals

Subtitle: Contacts and interpreter languages of the international healthcare centers at Seoul National University Hospital, Severance, Gangnam Severance, Samsung Medical Center, Asan Medical Center and Korea University Anam Hospital · International healthcare centers are daytime only (Seoul National University and Asan weekdays 09:00·08:30~17:30, Anam 08:30~17:30) · 24-hour ER wording confirmed at: Seoul National University, Gangnam Severance, Anam, Soonchunhyang and National Medical Center · Checked on each hospital's website 2026-11-03

ISSUE 03 · #405 · 2026-05-26

🎯 How to use this guide

On the third afternoon of your trip, you twist your ankle and it throbs with every step. There are plenty of hospitals, but you do not know which one treats you in English, or when they will take you. This guide answers only those two questions — which hospitals have a counter that treats patients in a foreign language, and where to go at this hour.

One thing to remember first. The big hospitals' international healthcare centers are open in the daytime only — Seoul National University and Asan on weekdays only. At night, on weekends and holidays, or if it is urgent, go to the ER, and if you cannot breathe, are losing consciousness or cannot stop the bleeding, call 119 before you choose a hospital. The hospital contacts and hours here are only the values read on each hospital's website on 2026-11-03.

🧰 Where to go now — steps by situation

1. You cannot breathe, are losing consciousness, cannot stop the bleeding, or are badly hurt: Do not choose a hospital — call 119. Say your location and symptoms briefly, and do not hang up.

2. Night, weekend or holiday, and you can walk: Go to a nearby ER. Before leaving, check the ER's operating status and phone number on the National Emergency Medical Center's E-Gen (중앙응급의료센터). We could not confirm on the hospital websites whether the ERs have foreign-language interpretation — if you cannot communicate, use a translation app.

3. Weekday daytime, not urgent: Book first with the International Healthcare Center in the table above by phone or email. For a first visit to Asan, booking on the website is recommended.

4. A child has a fever at night (no urgent symptoms): Find a Moonlight Children's Hospital on E-Gen → #433 Emergency and Safety Guide.

5. Mild symptoms: A local clinic or pharmacy is faster → #1307 Hospital Visit Procedure · #1278 Buying Medicine at a Pharmacy.

📸 Save the International Healthcare Center number and the ER number of one hospital near where you are staying in your phone. Bring your passport.

⚠ 4 common mistakes

1. "The international healthcare center is open at night too" — The Seoul National University and Asan centers are open on weekday daytimes only, and Anam only 08:30~17:30. Nights and weekends mean the ER.

2. "All ERs are 24 hours, the same everywhere" — Operating status can differ by hospital and by day. Check E-Gen before you leave.

3. "Every hospital has the same interpreter languages" — It differs by hospital: Asan has 7, Severance 6 and Seoul National University 5 languages, and some hospitals have a separate phone number for each language.

4. "In an emergency, find a hospital that speaks English first" — In an emergency, 119 comes before a hospital search.

⚙ FAQ 4

Q1. Can I be treated in English?

The international healthcare centers at Seoul National University, Severance and Asan offer interpretation (languages in the table above). Seoul National University and Asan have a separate phone number for each language. The centers are open only in the daytime, so book ahead by phone or email.

Q2. Where do I go if I get sick at night?

If it is urgent, call 119; if you can walk, go to a nearby ER. Seoul National University, Gangnam Severance, Korea University Anam, Soonchunhyang and National Medical Center state 24-hour emergency care on their websites. Check the operating status on E-Gen before you leave. We could not confirm ER interpretation, so have a translation app ready.

Q3. What should I bring?

Your passport, a card that works for overseas payments, and your insurance details if you have travel insurance. The registration and payment order at the hospital is laid out as it is in #1307 Hospital Visit Procedure.

Q4. What if we cannot understand each other?

Ask the International Healthcare Center for an interpreter first, and if it is not an emergency and you do not know where to go, ask the tourist interpretation line 1330 → #1299 How to Use 1330.
