Foreigner Hongdae Bakery deep dive

> Indie·Specialty·Dessert·MZ 4 types — 15 bakeries in Hongdae, Sangsu, Hapjeong, and Yeonnam matrix

Bakeries in the Hongdae area are not just bakeries. Four types coexist within a narrow radius: a sourdough specialty store, a one-chef indie store, a donut dessert cafe, and a variation of traditional Korean bread. For foreigners, this density is a competitive advantage — it's not as crowded with foreigners as Itaewon, but quality is concentrated within walking distance.

3 mistakes foreigners often make

1. Misconception that “specialty bread = you can buy it if you go early”

Sourdough specialty stores like Milgwa People operate based on baking time, not opening time. Even if you go at 10 a.m., the first lot of the day may still be in the oven. It is more accurate to check the “In stock today” notice in the Naver Map review or Instagram story, or to call the store directly and ask, “When will the salt bread/sourdough come out?” Some specialty stores operate a SNS reservation open system.

2. “Cafe Noted = Only Sells Donuts” Misconception

NOTTED is known as a donut brand, but the Hongdae branch is a cafe format with brunch menu, drinks, and cake. This is why foreign visitors are embarrassed by the “30-minute wait when they go just to buy donuts” — it is because local demand for cafe seats is mixed. If you just want takeout, you can use the takeout counter line without waiting.

3. Misconception that “Yeonnam and Hongdae indie bakeries appear on Google”

Many of the indie bakeries in Hongdae do not have Google Maps registration information or the information is outdated. To find stores that are actually open, it is more accurate to use Naver Map + Instagram hashtags. If you check posts within the last 1-2 weeks on the #연남베이커리, #홍대빵집, and #합정카페 hashtags, information on closures, renewals, and new menus will be posted in real time. Items marked as clusters in this guide should be approached in this manner.

1. Specialty·Sourdough (Sangsu·Hapjeong)

1. Milgwa People (3-minute walk from Sangsu Station)

  • Category: Sourdough specialty — Korea’s first generation specialty bakery
  • Location: Sangsu-dong, Mapo-gu, Seoul (3-minute walk from Sangsu Station Exit 3)
  • Signature: Naturally fermented sourdough bread, campagne, bagel / Seasonal limited-time red bean and multigrain version
  • Price: ₩5,000~₩12,000 (based on ₩8,000~₩10,000 per loaf of bread)
  • Foreign fit: ★★★ — Many regular foreign sourdough enthusiasts. Many Google Maps English reviews say “best sourdough in Seoul”
  • English communication: No English menu, photo menu available. You can order by telling the staff “sourdough bread” or pointing to the bread.
  • Recommended timing: 10:00 to 12:00 AM (1st lot fresh) or 2nd lot after 2 PM. No additional charges if stock runs out on the day
  • Packaging: All items can be packaged (paper bag provided). No seats or very few seats
  • Tip: If this is your first visit, buy a sourdough bread and a bagel set together and compare them. The price is reasonable for this quality in Korea.

2. Bakersfield (Hapjeong + Insa-dong branch)

  • Category: Sourdough/French Bakery
  • Location: Hapjeong-dong, Mapo-gu, Seoul (5-7 minutes walk from Hapjeong Station, separate Insa-dong branch)
  • Signature: Sourdough, croissant, baguette / seasonal fruit tart
  • Price: ₩4,500~₩13,000
  • Foreigners fit: ★★ — Fits European tourists who prefer French style. Partial English menu
  • English Communication: English part possible. You can order by pointing to the menu photo.
  • Recommended timing: Weekdays from AM to 2 PM. Waiting available on weekend afternoons
  • Tip: Hapjeong branch is accessible on foot from Hapjeong Station, so it is good to combine it with Cafe Onion Hapjeong branch on the same day — The pattern of purchasing bread and then spending it at a cafe is common even among locals.

3. Hapjeong specialty bakery cluster

There are 3 to 5 small specialty bakeries within a 10-minute walk from Hapjeong Station. Features of this cluster:

  • Common features: Specializing in a single or small number of items, operated directly by the owner-chef, and promoting primarily through Instagram
  • How to find: Search for “Hapjeong Bakery” on Naver Map and filter for stores within 3 months of the most recent review. Or check the posts of the last week on Instagram #합정베이커리
  • What to expect: Unlike large chains, the menu is narrow with 3 to 6 items per day. It is best to visit in the morning as it sells out quickly. Check first at the entrance whether card payment is possible.

2. Indie·MZ Design (Yeonnam·Hongdae)

4. Yeonnam dessert cafe/bakery cluster

Yeonnam-dong is a 10-15 minute walk from Hongik University Station or an alley toward Gajwa Station on the Gyeongui-Jungang Line. Characteristics of this cluster:

  • Size: 5 to 7 locations distributed around the main alley of Yeonnam-dong (near Dongjin Market) and Gyeongui Line Forest Road.
  • Foreigner fit: ★★ — MZ trend/Insta photo fit. Foreigners often visit in the context of “walking around Yeonnam-dong” rather than recognizing individual stores.
  • How to find: #연남동카페, #연남디저트 Instagram hashtags. Naver Map "Yeonnam-dong" radius filter gives priority to stores with many recent visits
  • What to expect: Focus on visual desserts such as cakes, macarons, and tarts. Consumption pattern for beverage + dessert sets rather than bread. As there are many stores with limited seating, it is recommended to take out and then consume on the Gyeongui Line Forest Trail.
  • Recommended timing: Weekdays 2-5 PM (waiting time is 15-30 minutes on weekend afternoons)

5. Hongdae main indie bakery cluster

Small indie bakeries are scattered in the alleys between the main gate of Hongik University and Hongik University Station:

  • Features: One-person or small-scale operation. Trendy visual and social media viral items (salt cream bread, crople, croissant variations, etc.) updated every season
  • Foreigner Approach: Google Maps is not trustworthy. Check current business status in real time with Naver Map + #홍대빵집 Instagram. Language barriers are low — point to bread on display and say “Igeo hana” (Igeo hana) or indicate quantity with your finger.
  • Budget: ₩3,500~₩7,000 for 1 item. Even if you buy 3-4 pieces, it costs ₩15,000~₩25,000

6. Hapjeong Indie Designer Bakery

Small stores run by solo chefs around Hapjeong Station:

  • Features: A small menu of 3 to 8 items, which the owner cooks and sells himself. Recipes change depending on the season and supply of ingredients.
  • Foreigner fit: There is a language barrier, but ordering itself is not difficult because you choose by looking at the display case.
  • How to find: #합정카페, #합정베이커리 Instagram postings within 1 week are the most reliable

3. Dessert·Cake (Hongdae·Hapjeong)

7. Cafe Notted Hongdae (NOTTED Hongdae)

  • Category: Donuts + Cafe (Korean signature dessert brand)
  • Location: Hongdae area, Mapo-gu, Seoul (about 5 minutes walk from Hongik University Station)
  • Signature: Glazed donut, cream field donut (original of Korean SNS virality), drink, cake
  • Price: 1 donut ₩3,800~₩5,500 / drink ₩6,000~₩8,000
  • Foreigner fit: ★★★ — High recognition among foreigners. K-beauty·K-food frequently appears in SNS content. Instagram visual optimization
  • English communication: English menu available. Staff available in English
  • Operating hours: 09:00~22:00 (Daily, including public holidays — recommended to check before visiting)
  • Waiting: 30 to 60 minutes on weekend afternoons. There is a separate line for takeout, so only use takeout to avoid waiting for seats.
  • Recommended timing: After 2:00 PM on weekdays (wait times peak during weekend lunch)
  • Related content: See Foreigner Seoul Café Meta (#1116)

8. Cafe Onion Hapjeong Branch/Hongdae Branch (ONION)

  • Category: Bread + Cafe (Interior Signature Brand)
  • Location: Hapjeong Branch — Hapjeong-dong, Mapo-gu, Seoul (7-minute walk from Hapjeong Station) / Separate Hongdae branch
  • Signature: Beef butter croissant, bread + reserve coffee / interior structure background photo
  • Price: Bread ₩4,500~₩9,000 / Drinks ₩6,500~₩9,000
  • Foreigner fit: ★★★ — The interior itself is a shooting point. A store that often appears on foreign travel blogs. A regular on the “Seoul cafe must-visit” list
  • English communication: English menu available. Staff English proficiency is good
  • Operating Hours: 09:00~22:00 (Daily)
  • Recommended timing: Weekday mornings (no lines, plenty of time to take photos). Competition for seats is fierce on weekend afternoons.
  • Tip: After purchasing bread, many visitors aim to take pictures in the store's second floor and rooftop space. It's OK to just buy bread and eat it at a nearby park.
  • Related content: See Foreigner Seoul Café Meta (#1116)

9. Hongdae dessert cafe cluster

  • Size: 5 to 7 locations distributed between Hongik University Station and Sangsu Station
  • Items: Cake, Tart, Macaroon, Shaved Ice (summer only), Crepe
  • Foreigner approach: Visit after identifying currently trending items using K-food hashtags (#hongdaecafe, #홍대디저트). Low language barrier due to visual consumption pattern

10. Hapjeong cake brand cluster

  • Feature: MZ Target cake specialty store (lettering cake, mini cake, sculpture cake)
  • Reservation Availability: Reservations can be made on the same day at popular stores. Naver reservation or Instagram DM reservation system operation
  • Foreigner Approach: Seating Cake Cafe has a low language barrier. Custom cakes (custom) require communication in Korean — Foreigners are encouraged to purchase piece cakes on the same day.

4. Korean Signature Bread/Old Store (Hongdae)

11. Hongdae salt bread cluster

  • What: Salt bread (Sogeum-ppang) is a Korean variant of a combination of croissant dough + butter + salt. After being introduced from Japan, it evolved independently in Korea. Currently, most major bakeries in the Hongdae area offer salt bread.
  • Foreigner fit: ★★★ — The name is simple ("salt bread") so it's easy to explain, and the buttery flavor is familiar, so there's less resistance.
  • Where to find it: Sold at most indie bakeries and cafes in the Hongdae area. However, there is a large difference in quality - it tastes best when eaten freshly baked (with butter running down), so visiting between morning and lunch is recommended.
  • Price: ₩2,500~₩4,500 (1 unit)
  • Related content: Foreigners refer to Seoul Bakery Meta (#1119)

12. Hapjeong Angbeoteo cluster

  • What is: Ang-butter = A sandwich made with sweet red beans (bean paste) + butter cream on bread, croissants, or bagels. One of the most original inventions of Korean cafes
  • Foreigner fit: ★★★ — A combination of sweet and salty, sweet red beans are familiar to travelers from Japan and East Asia. It can be easily explained to American and European travelers as “peanut butter-like, but different.”
  • What to expect: There is both a cold version (cream cheese butter combination) and a warm version. Most cafes and bakeries near Hapjeong have at least one bean butter menu item.

13. Hongdae Injeolmi Toast

  • What is: Korean fusion toast with injeolmi (glutinous rice cake powder) sprinkled on bread or filled with filling
  • Foreigner fit: ★★ — The unique savory taste of soybean powder may be unfamiliar, but it is highly accepted when described as “nutty powdered toast.”
  • Where to find it: Sold at snack and toast restaurants and some cafes in the Hongdae area. Appears more often as a side menu in cafes than in bakery stores.

14. Yeonnam No-Ho Bakery

  • Features: A local bakery that has been in operation since before the 1990s. We sell classic Korean bread (red bean bread, cream bread, soboro) that has nothing to do with MZ trends.
  • Foreigners fit: ★ — No English guidance, not targeted at foreigners. However, it is meaningful for travelers who want to experience the "original Korean bakery"
  • Price: ₩1,500~₩3,500 (single item). The cheapest way to taste Korean bread in Korea
  • How to find: Search for “Yeonnam-dong Bakery” on Naver Map + old stores with few reviews

15. SPC Paris Baguette Hongdae (global brand)

  • Location: Multiple locations near Hongik University Station
  • Foreigner fit: ★★★ — Global brand awareness + English sign + 100% credit card payment + Open from 7 a.m. to 11 p.m.
  • Role: For early morning or late evening when specialty/indie stores are closed, or for a quick breakfast. Since it is a chain throughout Korea, it is also OK for beginners to Korean bread.
  • Price: ₩2,000~₩6,000
  • 팁: 여기서 한국 빵 기초(소금빵·앙버터·크림빵)를 맛본 뒤 인디 매장 버전과 비교하면 차이가 명확하다

5. Standard daily route (3 patterns)

Pattern A: Specialty·Sourdough Mania

  • 10:00 Milgwa People (Sangsu Station) — Purchase 1-2 sourdoughs
  • 12:00 Bakersfield (Hapjeong) — Croissant·Baguette + Coffee
  • 14:00 Cafe Onion Hapjeong Branch — Beef Butter Croissant + Cafe Tour
  • 16:00 Cafe Noted Hongdae Branch — Donut Takeout + Photos
  • Total budget: ₩35,000~₩55,000 per person / Total travel within 30 minutes on foot

Pattern B: MZ·Insta·Design

  • 11:00 Yeonnam dessert cafe hop (2~3 locations) — Visual desserts and photos
  • 14:00 Hongdae Indie Bakery — Salt bread, trendy items
  • 16:00 Hapjeong Cake Cafe — Piece of cake + coffee
  • Total Budget: ₩40,000~₩70,000 per person

Pattern C: Introduction to Korean Signature Bread

  • 11:00 Hongdae Salt Bread and Ang Butter Store (near Indie or Noted)
  • 13:00 Injeolmi Toast (Hongdae Toast Specialty Store)
  • 15:00 Tour a long-established bakery in Yeonnam + Compare Korean chain bread with Paris Baguette
  • Total Budget: ₩20,000~₩40,000 per person

6. Practical information for foreigners

Transportation/Route

  • Hongik University Station: Line 2, Airport Railroad, and Gyeongui-Jungang Line intersect. Approximately 43 minutes directly from Incheon Airport by Airport Railroad
  • Movement within the area: Hapjeong Station~Sangsu Station~Hongik University Station, Line 6, 2nd stop. It is a 15-20 minute walk, so the pattern of exploring bakeries while walking is popular with foreigners.
  • Access to Yeonnam-dong: 10-minute walk from Exit 6 of Hongik University Station or walk along Gyeongui Line Forest Trail (photo spot)

Payment

  • Card: Large brands (Noted, Onion, Paris Baguette) — Overseas cards Visa/Mastercard 100% OK
  • Small indie store: Cards accepted, but cash is recommended. Bring ₩30,000~₩50,000 emergency cash.
  • Easy payment: Kakao Pay and Samsung Pay require an account in Korea. International cards are given priority for foreigners

Language/Order

  • Store with English menu: Cafe Noted, Cafe Onion, SPC Paris Baguette
  • Point to photo OK: Milgwa People, Bakersfield, most indie stores
  • 한국어 위주: 연남 노포·합정 소규모 인디 cluster — "이거 하나 주세요"(Igeo hana juseyo) 또는 손가락 주문으로 대부분 해결
  • Translation app: Take a photo of the menu using Papago or Google Translate camera function → Instant translation

Packaging/In-store consumption

  • Store Seating: Cafe Onion · Cafe Noted — Seating available (waiting required on weekends)
  • Takeout-focused: Most of Milgwa People, Bakersfield, and Indie stores focus on takeout. Consume at Gyeongui Line Forest Road, Yeonnam-dong alley, and Hongdae Park.
  • Dietary restrictions: Most bakeries contain gluten and dairy products. Vegan options vary by store (we recommend inquiring via Instagram DM before visiting)

7. FAQ

Q1. Hongdae Bakery and Itaewon/Gangnam Bakery — What’s the difference?

The Hongdae, Hapjeong, and Yeonnam areas have different densities. Itaewon is centered on restaurants targeting foreigners, so it has a higher proportion of restaurants than bakeries, while Gangnam focuses on single premium brands. In the Hongdae area, four types of cafes, from specialty sour dough to MZ design cafes, coexist within a 20-minute walk radius — perfect for travelers who want to compare several styles in one visit.

Q2. Salt bread, bean butter, and injeolmi — I want to eat all three, but what is the budget?

If you buy one of each of the three, it costs ₩8,000~₩14,000. This is the best cost-effective introductory course to Korean bread. You can get them at any Paris Baguette or Hongdae indie bakery. The key to freshly baked salt bread is that it is recommended to buy it last.

Q3. Can I buy Cafe Noted donuts without waiting in line?

If you use the takeout-only counter, you can purchase donuts without waiting for a cafe seat. However, popular types (glazed and cream) may be sold out after 3 p.m. Weekday mornings are the easiest with no waiting.

Q4. How do I order from a store that doesn't speak any English?

Most bakeries in Hongdae have a display case approach — you point to the bread you want to choose and indicate the quantity with your finger. The calculation is completed by handing over the card as the amount is displayed on the screen. If you take a picture of the menu using the Papago app camera function, it will be translated in real time.

Q5. I want to buy bread and eat it at the hotel the next day. How do I store it?

Sourdough and bread can be stored in a paper bag → room temperature for 1 day (if refrigerated, it becomes dry). Beef butter and cream are recommended to be consumed within 4 hours after refrigeration. Donuts (noted) are to be consumed on the same day - they have a cream filling so they spread the next day. Specialty sourdough is unsliced ​​and packaged in paper → kept at room temperature for 1-2 days.
