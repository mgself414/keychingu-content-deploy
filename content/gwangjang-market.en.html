🎯 Why a standalone Gwangjang Market guide

Gwangjang Market (광장시장, opened in 1905) is Seoul's oldest and largest traditional market — the #1 destination on any visitor's route for experiencing Korean market food culture. It has appeared repeatedly on global media like Netflix's Street Food: Asia, making it the most recognized traditional market among foreign visitors.

Bindaetteok, mayak gimbap, yukhoe, sundae, hanbok fabric, general goods, and vintage items can all be experienced in one space. It's where visitors can experience Korean food culture and market browsing in a single stop. ⚠ 3 common mistakes visitors make

1. Bringing only a card — payment failures

More than 60% of Gwangjang Market stalls are cash only. A growing number take cards, but reliability is low. Withdraw KRW 30,000–50,000 in cash beforehand — there's an ATM at the market entrance or at banks near Jongno 5-ga Station.

2. Ordering yukhoe without checking hygiene standards

Yukhoe uses raw Korean beef, so freshness is critical. Choose a busy stall with a steady line of customers. Visitors wary of hygiene should stick to cooked dishes (bindaetteok, sundae, mandu) instead. Be especially cautious with yukhoe in summer (July–August).

3. Visiting during the weekend peak (12–2pm)

Weekends 12–2pm see the heaviest crowds — both visitors and Korean families — leading to a shortage of seats and 30+ minute waits. Weekday mornings 11am–12pm or afternoons 4–6pm are much quieter and the best times to visit.

The Gwangjang Market landscape

Gwangjang Market opened in 1905 as Korea's first permanent market — founded independently by Korean merchants in response to Japanese-run markets during the colonial period. Located in Yeji-dong and Jongno 5-ga in Jongno-gu, it packs 5,000+ stalls into one space.

The 1st floor is the food street, with bindaetteok, mayak gimbap, yukhoe, and sundae stalls lining the central aisle. The 2nd floor has hanbok fabric, butcher and skewer stalls, and clothing. The basement has vintage, secondhand, and general goods.

Gwangjang Market and Bindaetteok Alley have appeared repeatedly in *Episode 1 (Korea) of Netflix's Street Food: Asia*** — making it the most recognized traditional market among visitors. It's also been named to "Asia's 50 Best Street Food" lists on a recurring basis.

Transit: 3-minute walk from Exit 8, Jongno 5-ga Station (Line 1). Transfer between Lines 1 and 5 is also possible.

Deep dive into the 8 categories

1. Bindaetteok Alley (Bindaetteok Alley) ★ Gwangjang Market's #1 spot

  • Location: Central, 1st floor
  • Signature: Freshly cooked mung bean pancake on the spot. The essence of Korean market food — the #1 Instagram shot for visitors.
  • Price: ₩4,000–6,000 per piece
  • Recommended: 1 bindaetteok + 1 glass of makgeolli (₩8,000) = the classic Korean pairing
  • Tip: The live-cooking scene makes a great Instagram shot. Drinking makgeolli straight from the bowl is part of the market culture.

2. Mayak Gimbap Alley (Mayak Gimbap Alley)

  • Location: 2nd floor area
  • Signature: Finger-width mini gimbap rolls. Served with a mustard-soy sauce — the name "mayak" ("drug") refers to how addictive they are. Featured on Netflix's Street Food.
  • Price: ₩3,000–4,000 per roll
  • Recommended: 2–3 rolls with mustard sauce, good for sharing in a group
  • Tip: The #1 stop on most visitors' routes. Quieter on weekday mornings.

3. Yukhoe specialty corner (Yukhoe / Korean Beef Tartare)

  • Location: Central aisle
  • Signature: Authentic yukhoe made with fresh Korean beef, served with sesame oil, garlic, pear, and egg yolk.
  • Price: ₩15,000–25,000 per person
  • Recommended: A yukhoe serving with a bottle of soju
  • Tip: Hygiene is critical — choose a busy stall with a line. Be especially cautious in summer.

4. Sundae Alley (Sundae Alley)

  • Location: Near the entrance
  • Signature: Glass-noodle sundae plus offal and an assorted plate. Korea's version of blood sausage.
  • Price: ₩5,000–8,000 per plate
  • Recommended: Sundae + assorted offal, paired with salt and pepper

5. Tteokbokki Alley (Tteokbokki Alley)

  • Location: East side, 1st floor
  • Signature: Freshly made tteokbokki, fish cake, and fried snacks. The essence of Korean market-style tteokbokki.
  • Price: ₩4,000–6,000 per person

6. Hanbok fabric market (Hanbok Fabric Market)

  • Location: 2nd floor
  • Signature: Hanbok fabric and finished garments for sale. The #1 hanbok souvenir spot for visitors.
  • Price: Varies (fabric from ₩10,000/meter, finished hanbok from ₩200K)
  • Tip: Some shops offer English service. Measurements and custom tailoring are available.

7. Vintage & general goods corner (Vintage & Goods)

  • Location: Basement
  • Signature: Secondhand clothing, household items, and general goods. A top pick for visitors who love vintage finds.
  • Price: ₩5,000–50,000

8. Majang-dong butcher & skewer corner (Butcher & Skewer)

  • Location: West side, 2nd floor
  • Signature: Freshly grilled meat and skewers. Fresh Korean beef and pork grilled on the spot.
  • Price: ₩10,000–20,000 per person

⚙ Frequently asked questions (FAQ)

Q1. Are there English menus and card payment?

A. Almost no English menus. Pointing at photos or the display works fine. Payment is about 70% cash, 30% card — withdrawing KRW 30,000–50,000 beforehand is a must.

Q2. What Korean phrases are essential?

A. "Igeo juseyo" (This, please — point with your finger) / "Mayak gimbap du jul juseyo" (Two rolls of mayak gimbap, please) / "Makgeolli han jan juseyo" (One glass of makgeolli, please) / "Pojanghaejuseyo" (To go, please).

Q3. Any hygiene and health tips?

A. Yukhoe: choose a busy stall (high turnover means fresher meat), and be cautious in summer. All food: hand-washing facilities are limited — bringing wet wipes is recommended. Food allergies: check directly with staff in English.

Q4. What's a recommended one-day course?

A. 11:00 Exit 8, Jongno 5-ga Station → 11:10 mayak gimbap (2 rolls) → 11:30 bindaetteok + makgeolli → 12:00 yukhoe → 12:30 sundae + browsing the market → 13:00 basement goods shopping → 13:30 combine with a walk around Jongno.

Q5. What about buying hanbok fabric?

A. The hanbok fabric market is on the 2nd floor. Some shops offer English service. Measurements and custom tailoring run ₩200K+, fabric from ₩10K/meter. Some shops ship to the US and Europe.

Q6. Can you haggle on price?

A. Food is fixed price — haggling is considered rude. General goods, fabric, and vintage items allow some haggling (mostly in the basement stalls). Large discounts are unlikely, but bulk purchases can get you ₩1,000–5,000 off.

Q7. How does it compare to other markets?

A. Gwangjang Market (#24): opened in 1905, Seoul's oldest and largest market, the most recognized among visitors.

Mangwon Market (망원시장, #25): a local neighborhood market, fewer tourists, no overcharging.

Namdaemun Market: mainly clothing and general goods.

Dongdaemun Market: clothing plus late-night shopping.

Q8. How's the transit and accessibility?

A. Subway: 3-minute walk from Exit 8, Jongno 5-ga Station (Line 1) / also Jongno 4-ga Station (Line 5). Taxi: drop-off possible at the Jongno 5-ga entrance. Wheelchair access: the 1st floor entrance is fine, but the 2nd floor and basement involve stairs, making access difficult.

🌸 5 seasonal and time-of-day tips

Spring (March–May): quiet on weekday mornings, 11am–noon. Hanbok fabric season is in full swing.

Summer (June–August): bindaetteok, makgeolli, and cold drinks. Be cautious with yukhoe.

Fall (September–November): the best season overall — good any day, weekday or weekend.

Winter (December–February): warm sundae and gukbap. Best atmosphere at the night stalls.

Weekday afternoon lull: 4–6pm — quiet after the lunch rush. The best time for photos and browsing.
