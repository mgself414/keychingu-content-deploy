Busan Shopping Guide — Between the World's Largest Department Store and a War-Refugee Market Alley

Subtitle: Busan links the Guinness-certified world's largest department store to an alley market that began as refugee stalls during the Korean War — just a few subway stops apart. That range is the whole appeal of shopping here. Three axes — mega malls, markets, and concept shops — plus real-world tax-refund tips: the grammar of Busan shopping to read before you open your wallet.

ISSUE · #1327 · 2026-08-23

🎯 The Spectrum of Busan Shopping

If Seoul's shopping scene has split by taste into Myeongdong, Seongsu, and Hannam, Busan is a city where two extremes sit close together. From the mega department stores of Centum City (센텀시티), it's just a few subway stops to the alleys of Gukje Market (국제시장), which trace back to the Korean War refugee era. Walking both in a single day is a shopping route only Busan can pull off.

① The Mega Axis — Centum City and Gwangbok-ro

  • Shinsegae Centum City (신세계 센텀시티) — certified by Guinness World Records in 2009 as the world's largest department store (bigger than Macy's in New York). It's not just shopping — Spa Land (two types of onsite hot-spring water, 22 baths, 13 jjimjilbang rooms) is inside the same building, so shopping and a Korean-style sauna experience finish in one stop. For foreign visitors, that combination is a stronger hook than the department store itself.
  • Lotte Department Store (롯데백화점) and Busan Cinema Center (영화의전당, #247) sit in the same Centum district, which makes this pocket of Centum the anchor for an indoor itinerary on a rainy day.
  • Gwangbok-ro (광복로), in Nampo (남포) — a fashion street plus Lotte Gwangbok (롯데 광복점). It connects on foot to Gukje Market (#244), making it the fastest place to pivot from "department store" to "market."

② The Market Axis — the Grammar of Gukje and Kkangtong Markets

  • Gukje Market (국제시장, #244) — ground zero of Busan commerce, born from the stalls of Korean War refugees. It's split into secondhand, kitchenware, and lighting alleys, so wandering without a destination is the standard way to see it.
  • Bupyeong Kkangtong Market (부평깡통시장) — fish cakes and dried seafood by day, a night market (#544, Busan's entry in the nationwide night-market roundup) after dark. This is home base for food shopping — think fish-cake gift sets.
  • Bosu-dong Book Street (보수동 책방골목, #1024) — a row of used bookstores. The final boss of markets where walking, not buying, is the point.
  • Three lines of market etiquette: ① more shops take cards these days, but small cash still helps ② haggle only on big-ticket items — food is fixed-price ③ it's fine to accept a free sample without feeling obligated to buy.

③ The Concept-Shop Axis — Finding K-Retro in Jeonpo

  • Jeonpo (전포, #246) concept-shop strip — the gaps between the cafés form a belt of concept shops. The standout case: a shop specializing in classic Korean stationery — Secret Friend (비밀친구), in Busanjin-gu (부산진구) — is buzzing in local curation. It sells the stationery, stickers, and character goods Korean kids born in the 1990s grew up with — a K-retro genre that becomes a souvenir of "a Korean childhood" for foreign visitors.
  • The difference from Seoul's concept shops (#202): lower rent means more single-owner shops with sharper, more personal taste.
  • For general souvenir advice (Olive Young, Daiso, traditional gifts), see the nationwide piece #052 — this piece focuses only on what you can't get anywhere but Busan.

💳 Tax Refund, in Practice

1. Show your passport at stores marked Tax Free — purchases over 30,000 won qualify for an instant refund (the post-sale duty-free scheme).

2. Department stores have a refund desk on every floor; most markets and concept shops don't offer refunds at all — refunds belong to the mega axis, feeling belongs to the market axis.

3. At Gimhae Airport (김해공항), the refund counter exists both before and after international departure security, so leave extra time if you're claiming a large refund.
