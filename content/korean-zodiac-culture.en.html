What's Your Zodiac Animal? — The Meaning Behind the Animal of the Year You Were Born

> sibiji (십이지, the 12 Earthly Branches) · tti (띠, zodiac animal) · yuksipgapja (육십갑자, the 60-year cycle) · gunghap (궁합, compatibility) · samjae (삼재, the 3-year unlucky cycle) — a Korean zodiac-culture explainer for foreigners. Not "fortune-telling," but "why, what, and how it's used."

In Korea, when asking someone's age directly feels awkward, people ask this instead: "Museun tti-seyo? (무슨 띠세요?, What's your zodiac animal?)" If the other person says "I'm a Rabbit," a Korean mentally counts the 12-year cycle and figures, "Ah, so you were probably born in '89 or in 2011," gauging their age. This is exactly what tti (띠, zodiac animal) is — the old Korean and East Asian counting system that marks the year you were born with one of 12 animals. At New Year people read their tojeongbigyeol (토정비결, an annual fortune almanac), saying "this year is the year of such-and-such animal," and before a wedding both families check gunghap (궁합, compatibility). Foreigners often call it the "Chinese zodiac," but in Korea the tti is an everyday tool for asking age and a cultural code. And — ★most importantly — the tti is different from saju (사주, the Four Pillars), which Koreans consult before big decisions (see ⑤ below). This article isn't about "where to go," but unpacks the meaning, uses, and distinctions of the tti at a foreigner's eye level. Korean culture explainer series, Part 59.

⚠️ 3 things foreigners often misunderstand

1. "tti = saju (사주)" ❌ — the biggest misconception. The tti looks at the birth 'year' only. Saju (사주, the Four Pillars) looks at all four — the year, month, day, and hour of birth. The tti is just one small piece that makes up the saju. (★For details, see ⑤ below — and the saju episode #1133 of this series.)

2. "the zodiac animal determines your personality" ❌ — there are tti-by-tti personality readings like "Dragons are ambitious, Goats are gentle," but most Koreans treat this as fun and conversation material. How much people believe it varies wildly — some take it seriously, others laugh it off.

3. "the Korean zodiac = exactly the same as China's and Japan's" ❌ — they're nearly identical, but each country has small differences. Most notably, Vietnam has a Cat instead of a Rabbit (see ⑦). So if you lump it all together as the "Chinese zodiac," a Vietnamese friend might correct you.

① What the tti is — 'the animal of the year you were born'

The tti (띠, zodiac animal) is the animal assigned to the year you were born. The 12 animals are assigned one per year, and after 12 years it returns to the start — hence a 12-year cycle.

  • The basis for these 12 animals is the sibiji (십이지, 十二支, the Twelve Earthly Branches) — the twelve Earthly Branches (jiji, 지지, 地支) that counted time, direction, and years in the traditional East Asian calendar. Each branch was paired with one animal. So the tti is less a "fortune-telling" and more a traditional counting method that puts a name tag on the year — a way of marking time that Korea, China, Japan, and Vietnam have shared for over a thousand years.
  • In the Goryeo (고려, Goryeo Dynasty) and Joseon (조선, Joseon Dynasty) eras too, the sibiji was used in official calendars, court rituals, and administrative records (the National Folk Museum holds many sibiji folktales and artifacts). The sibijisinsang (십이지신상, 十二支神像, the Twelve Guardian Deity statues) carvings that guard royal tombs come from here too.

② The 12 animals and their order — why is the rat number one?

The order is rat (자, 子) → ox (축, 丑) → tiger (인, 寅) → rabbit (묘, 卯) → dragon (진, 辰) → snake (사, 巳) → horse (오, 午) → goat (미, 未) → monkey (신, 申) → rooster (유, 酉) → dog (술, 戌) → pig (해, 亥).

According to a widely known folktale (passed down via the National Folk Museum and others), long ago the Lord of Heaven (or the Buddha) told the animals he would "set the 12 branches in the order they arrived first on New Year's Day." The diligent ox took the lead, but the rat, who had secretly climbed onto the ox's back, jumped off just before the finish line and seized first place — which is why the rat is number one and the ox number two. There's also a version where the slow-footed cat, tricked by the rat's lie, overslept and was left out of the 12 branches (in Vietnam the cat is included — see ⑦).

> True or not, this tale is a device that helps you fondly remember each animal's character (the clever rat, the earnest ox, the brave tiger…). It's a staple subject of children's picture books, folk paintings, and museum exhibits.

③ Gauging age by the tti — the Korean way of 'asking around the bush'

The tti's most practical use is estimating age.

  • In Korea, when asking age directly feels awkward, "Museun tti-seyo? (무슨 띠세요?, What's your zodiac animal?)" becomes a gentle alternative. From the answer, you roughly know someone's age bracket via the 12-year cycle.
  • That said, since people of the same tti can be 12, 24, or 36 years apart, you guess along with their appearance. If they're a "Rabbit," you narrow it down by context — whether they were born in 1999 (late 20s) or 1987 (late 30s).
  • Korea is a culture where speech style (jondaenmal/banmal, 존댓말/반말, formal/casual speech) and forms of address change by age, so gauging someone's age bracket by the tti isn't mere curiosity — it's also a clue for setting up the relationship.

> Foreigner tip: if a Korean friend asks "Museun tti-yeyo? (무슨 띠예요?, What's your zodiac animal?)," they're not prying into your age but signaling they want to get closer. If you don't know your own tti, just say your birth year.

④ Uses of the tti — gunghap, samjae, tojeongbigyeol

This is how the tti is used in daily life. The key point: many people take all of these as reference and fun, not "absolute truth."

(1) tti gunghap (궁합, 宮合, zodiac compatibility) — checking whether two people's tti match well. Traditionally, if they're in a samhap (삼합, 三合, a harmonious trine) or yukhap (육합, 六合, a harmonious pair) relationship they're considered a good match, while a chung (충, 沖, head-on opposition) relationship is said to clash (e.g., rat-ox-dragon-monkey get along easily, while rat↔horse is a chung). In the old days, both families weighed compatibility before a marriage. But even myeongri (명리, fortune-study) experts say, "Even if the zodiac compatibility is bad, a perfectly good married life is possible if the two are considerate of each other" — it's advice, not an absolute verdict.

(2) samjae (삼재, 三災, the 3-year unlucky cycle) — the folk belief that "3 of every 12 years carry misfortune." The samjae years are set by the group your own tti belongs to (e.g., tiger·horse·dog tti → samjae in the monkey·rooster·dog years). The 3 years are called deulsamjae (들삼재, the entering year), nulsamjae (눌삼재, the middle year), and nalsamjae (날삼재, the leaving year). In samjae years some people postpone big decisions or keep a charm, but these days more people treat it lightly, as just "a signal to be careful."

(3) tojeongbigyeol (토정비결, 土亭祕訣, an annual fortune almanac) — a book said to have been written by Tojeong Yi Ji-ham (토정 이지함) in the Joseon era, used to read one's fortune for the coming year. Around the first lunar month (near Seollal, 설날, Lunar New Year) you enter your birth date to interpret the year's fortune. Since the tti and birth year are the core inputs, it travels bundled with tti culture. It's closer to a New Year seasonal custom.

> ⚠️ Striking a balance: not every Korean believes in samjae or tojeongbigyeol. "I look at it for fun / I don't look at it / I take it seriously" all coexist. Differences by generation and individual are large.

⑤ ★saju (사주, the Four Pillars) vs. tti — the most important distinction

This is the point foreigners find most confusing, and the one thing to take away from this article. The tti and the saju are not the same thing.

tti (띠) saju (사주)
What it looks at one thing: the birth 'year' four things: the birth year, month, day, and hour
Amount of info 1 animal (one of 12) eight characters (paljja, 팔자, 八字) — far more precise
Use gauging age, simple compatibility, fun interpreting one's overall life, temperament, and flow by period
Who reads it anyone (common knowledge, conversation) a myeongri (명리, fortune-study) counselor (saju cafes, etc.)
Analogy a single word on a name tag a four-line profile
  • The crux: the tti is one piece of the saju. The Earthly Branch of the saju's 'year pillar' is exactly that person's tti. So even people of the "same tti" have completely different saju if their month, day, and hour differ.
  • That's why myeongri counselors say, "Fortune is too coarse to read by tti alone." The tti divides people into 1-of-12, but the saju multiplies year·month·day·hour to read far more finely. In modern myeongri there's even a trend of weighting the birth 'day' (ilgan, 일간, 日干) more than the tti (year).
  • ★ What the saju is, and how to try a saju-cafe experience, is covered in depth in this series' episode #1133, Korean Saju & Fortune-Telling Culture.

> One-line summary: "What's your tti?" is a light age-and-conversation code, while "I'm going to get my saju read" is a deep look into one's life. Mix the two and a Korean will tilt their head.

⑥ When does the tti change — Seollal? Ipchun? (★check recommended)

Surprisingly, this is a point even Koreans get confused about. "I was born on January 1st — what's my tti?" is a perennial question. Three standards coexist.

1. January 1 on the solar calendar (Sinjeong, 신정, New Year's Day) — the simplest. The view that the tti changes on the first day of the new year, in line with modern people who use the Gregorian calendar. Many people comfortably count it this way in daily life.

2. Seollal on the lunar calendar (Gujeong, 구정, Lunar New Year) — the traditional-holiday standard. The view that "the lunar new year is the real new year." Since Seollal usually falls between late January and mid-February, people born between January 1 and Seollal end up with an ambiguous tti.

3. Ipchun (입춘, 立春, around February 4 on the solar calendar) — ★the official standard of saju myeongrihak (fortune-study). The view that the sexagenary cycle (ganji, 간지, 干支) changes at Ipchun, the start of spring among the 24 solar terms. If you get a myeongri reading, the tti is usually set by this standard.

> So those born early in the year (January to early February) can have a different tti depending on the standard. Example: someone born on February 5, 1997 is an Ox by the myeongri standard because it's after Ipchun (Feb 4), but is sometimes counted as a Rat by the folk standard because it's before Seollal (Feb 7). If you're curious about your exact tti, it's recommended to check with a myeongri counselor or a manseryeok (만세력, perpetual-calendar) app.

⑦ Comparing Asian zodiacs — the same yet different 12 animals

The tti isn't Korea's alone but a shared culture across East and Southeast Asia. Still, one or two animals differ by country.

Slot Korea China Japan Vietnam
#2 so (소, Ox) niu (牛, ox) ushi (うし, ox) Water Buffalo
#4 tokki (토끼, Rabbit) tu (兔, rabbit) usagi (うさぎ, rabbit) Cat
#8 yang (양, Goat) yang (羊, goat) hitsuji (ひつじ, goat) Goat
  • Vietnam's Cat tti is the most famous difference. One theory says the Earthly Branch for the rabbit, 'myo (묘, 卯, mão)', sounds similar to the Vietnamese word for cat (mèo), so it changed; another cultural explanation notes that in a rice-farming country, the cat that catches rats was more familiar. So when you say "I'm a Rabbit," a Vietnamese friend may reply, "We're Cats!"
  • Japan has the same animal lineup as Korea and China, but the flavor leans toward enjoying the tti through New Year greeting cards and ganji (간지, 干支, sexagenary) notation.

> Note: behind the 12 animals lies a larger structure called the yuksipgapja (육십갑자, 六十甲子, the sexagenary cycle). Combining the 10 Heavenly Stems (cheongan, 천간, 天干) with the 12 Earthly Branches (the sibiji) makes a 60-year cycle, which is why a person's 60th birthday (hwangap·hoegap, 환갑·회갑) is celebrated grandly as "having gone all the way around once." ❓ Foreigners' Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

  • How do I find out my tti? — find your birth year in the table above. But those born in January to early February can differ depending on the standard (Seollal·Ipchun), so checking with a manseryeok app or a myeongri reading is recommended (⑥).
  • Is the tti the same as the Chinese zodiac? — the roots are the same. But in Korea it's called the tti (띠), and since animals differ slightly by country (Vietnam's cat, etc.), "Chinese zodiac" isn't always accurate.
  • What's the difference between the tti and saju (사주)? — ★ the tti is the birth 'year' only, while the saju is all four — year, month, day, and hour (⑤, #1133).
  • Should I believe in tti personality and compatibility readings? — even Koreans believe to varying degrees. Most enjoy it as fun and conversation material. Just enjoy it without pressure.
  • If a foreigner is asked "What's your tti?", how should I answer? — just say your birth year and a Korean will calculate the tti for you. It's not prying into your age but a signal of wanting to get closer (③).
