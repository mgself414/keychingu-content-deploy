Korea's 3 Transit Cards, No-Panic Manual — Pick the Card That Fits Your Itinerary

  • Hand actions appear bold: "insert bills into the slot one at a time"

① What This Piece Covers — Why There Are 3 Cards

Search for Korea transit cards and nine times out of ten you'll land on a T-money-only piece (details in #238). But over the last 2–3 years, a foreigner-exclusive card (WOWPASS) and a Seoul-focused unlimited pass for foreign visitors (Climate Card foreigner short-term pass) have joined the field — so "just get a T-money card" is no longer the automatic answer.

The three cards exist for different purposes.

  • T-money — a general-purpose transit card that works nationwide
  • WOWPASS — a foreigner-only card that handles currency exchange and payment in one
  • Climate Card foreigner short-term pass — a fixed-period unlimited pass for people riding Seoul's subway and buses a lot

Use the table below to pick the card that matches your itinerary first.

② Decision Table — Which Card Fits Your Itinerary

Your route / purpose Recommended card Why
Nationwide travel (Seoul + Busan, Jeju, etc.) or you'll ride taxis often T-money Works as-is on nationwide public transit and taxis — details in #238
Shopping-focused, want to skip cash and handle currency exchange in one step WOWPASS It's a prepaid debit card, so it doubles as a payment card, and you can top it up right at the exchange kiosk
Staying within Seoul only, riding the subway 3+ times a day (1–7 day stay) Climate Card foreigner short-term pass Buy it once for unlimited bus + subway rides — no per-tap fare math
A mix of the above (most travelers) T-money + Climate Card combo See ⑨ Plan B combo strategy

The three cards aren't mutually exclusive. T-money in your wallet, plus a Climate Card for the Seoul-heavy stretch of your trip, is the most common combination.

③ T-money — Nationwide, Buy & Charge Procedure

Where it works: nationwide public transit in Seoul, Busan, Daegu, Incheon, and more, Jeju buses, taxis, even payment at some convenience stores — the only one of the three that works nationwide.

Buying it: pay the card fee (about ₩4,000) at the register of CU, GS25, 7-Eleven, Emart24, and similar convenience stores, and it's issued on the spot. No application form or passport needed.

Charging it — at a convenience store:

1. Hand the card to the cashier and say "Chungjeon-hae juseyo" (충전해 주세요) — "please charge this"

2. Hand over cash for the amount you want (round to ₩10,000 units is easiest)

3. The clerk charges it on the terminal → you get the card back and you're done (no fee)

Charging it — station self-service charger screen flow (when no convenience store is in sight):

1. On the ticket machine's first screen, tap [ Transit Card Top-up (교통카드 충전) ] — #1281's single-ride ticket machine is the same machine, a different menu

2. Place the card on the reader at the bottom (tap, don't insert)

3. On the 「chungjeon geumaek seontaek (충전 금액 선택) — Select charge amount」 screen, tap a preset amount or enter one manually

4. Insert cash into the bill slot — older machines take cash only; if the machine won't take your card, head to a convenience store instead

5. Confirm the 「chungjeon wanryo (충전 완료) — Charge complete」 screen and the balance shown, then take the card back

Transfers: free transfers within 30 minutes of getting off (up to 4 times, including bus↔subway).

④ WOWPASS — Foreigner-only, Exchange + Payment in One

WOWPASS is a foreigner-only prepaid debit card. It has a T-money chip built in, so the moment it's issued it also works as a transit card. The decisive difference from T-money: at any of the roughly 300 dedicated kiosks nationwide, issuing the card and exchanging currency (topping it up) happen on the same machine, in one go.

Exchange kiosk screen flow:

1. First screen 「Select Language」 → tap [ English ]

2. Choose 「Get New Card」 vs. [ Top Up ] (charge an existing card)

3. For a new card, scan your passport on the scanner

4. On the 「Insert Your Currency」 screen → insert your home-currency bills into the slot one at a time

5. The KRW amount, converted at the real-time exchange rate, appears on screen → [ Confirm ]

6. The card is dispensed (new card) or the screen ends on a 「Top Up Complete」 balance-updated screen

Once issued, it works as a debit card not just for transit but for everyday payments at convenience stores, cafes, and more. Kiosks cluster at the airport, major stations, and Myeongdong, so it's easy to slot into your first-day route alongside the arrival-day set (#398).

⑤ Climate Card Foreigner Short-term Pass — Seoul-focused, Facing the "Seoul-only" Trap Head-on

A foreigner-only fixed-period pass for unlimited rides on Seoul buses and subway (including the Gimpo Gold Line) for a set number of days. You prepay for the number of days you'll use, so no fare is deducted per tap.

Period Price
1 day ₩5,000
2 days ₩8,000
3 days ₩10,000
5 days ₩15,000
7 days ₩20,000

The "Seoul-only" trap — you need to know this: exactly as the name says, it only covers the Seoul segment. Two situations trip people up in particular.

  • No AREX express train between Incheon Airport and Seoul Station — even though it's the airport railway, the express (non-stop) service runs on a separate fare structure and doesn't accept the Climate Card. Don't confuse it with the regular (all-stop) train.
  • No segments that cross out of Seoul — sections where a line runs beyond Seoul's border, like Line 1 toward Suwon or the Gyeongchun Line toward Gapyeong, stop being covered the moment you cross the Seoul city limit.

In other words, you get full value out of it only on an itinerary where you're moving around entirely within Seoul. If your trip includes airport transfers or a side trip outside the city, keep separate T-money balance ready for just that stretch.

Buying it: sold as a physical card. It isn't a rechargeable card — it's a fixed-period pass you prepay for by the number of days. No passport needed to buy. Confirm exact sales locations and current stock at the on-site signage or station counter when you visit.

⑥ Refund Basics

Card Refund method
T-money Refundable instantly at convenience stores — but there's a cap on the balance(high balances can't be refunded instantly). Small amounts go through convenience stores; anything over the cap goes through a station counter or customer service. Confirm the exact cap on-screen or with staff at refund time
WOWPASS You can withdraw the balance back to cash at a kiosk (a fee applies) — check the real-time amount on screen before proceeding
Climate Card short-term pass Refundable only before you tag (use) it. Once you've passed through a gate even once, it's non-refundable — think carefully unless you're using it immediately after buying

⑧ 3 Panic Scenarios

1. You rode the AREX express with a Climate Card and got stopped at the gate — don't panic, walk over to the regular (all-stop) train platform, or buy a separate ticket at the express-only counter. Your remaining Climate Card days stay valid — the only loss is the fare for that one segment.

2. A warning beep at the gate (insufficient balance) — happens with both T-money and WOWPASS. Charge it immediately at a nearby convenience store or via the station charger screen flow in ③, then re-tap to get through.

3. The charger only takes cash and you have none — older station chargers often can't take card payment. Don't waste time — it's faster to head to the nearest convenience store and ask them to charge the card instead.

⑨ Plan B — Combo Strategy

  • For most travelers, the answer is "T-money + Climate Card combined." Get a T-money card on day one and it works like insurance everywhere — airport transfers, side trips, taxis, convenience stores — and you just add a Climate Card short-term pass for the Seoul-heavy stretch of your trip.
  • Little downside if your plans change. T-money balance carries over to your next city or next visit, but the Climate Card is a fixed-period pass — its remaining value disappears once the period ends. It's better to buy exactly the days you need, not extra "just in case."
  • If currency exchange worries you, make WOWPASS your wallet's main card. If you'd rather consolidate transit + payment + exchange into one card, you can start with just WOWPASS instead of T-money.
