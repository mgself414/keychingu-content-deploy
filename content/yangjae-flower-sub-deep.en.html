Why Yangjae Flower Market Sub Matters Now — Korea's No. 1 Flower Market, 1991, F-Square

Yangjae Flower Market (양재 꽃시장) is Korea's No. 1 flower market, established in 1991, plus F-Square (F스퀘어) — officially the aT Flower Market — and the full set of cut flowers, potted plants, trees, wreaths, materials, and artificial flowers. What sets it apart from the general market series is a single, sharp focus: an enthusiast cluster built around flowers, plants, floriculture, and the seasons.

Plenty of places in Seoul sell flowers, but a large share of the country's flower distribution passes through this one site. For foreign travelers, three things make it special.

1. Direct access to the wholesale floor: Wholesale trading opens at 23:30. Regular shoppers who show up after retail hours can still buy at close to wholesale prices.

2. A look inside the aT Flower Market (F-Square): The only facility in the country where you can watch a live flower auction and distribution operation. A rare experience for flower lovers.

3. Scale: Cut flowers, potted plants, trees, wreaths, materials, and artificial flowers are all visible in one zone. Half a day is enough to see the entire set.

⚠️ 3 Common Mistakes Foreign Visitors Make

Mistake 1: Mixing up wholesale and retail hours

The biggest quirk of Yangjae Flower Market is that wholesale (23:30–10:00) and retail (10:00–21:00) run on separate clocks. Arrive too early and wholesale is already winding down while retail hasn't opened yet. If you're here to shop retail, visiting after 10 a.m. is the safest bet.

Mistake 2: Trying to buy small quantities in the wholesale zone

Some wholesale stalls operate on a box-only, bulk-purchase basis. Buying a single stem or two is far easier at a retail-hour stall. Trying it in the wholesale zone can get you turned away, or you'll pay a different price.

Mistake 3: Not bringing cash

Many cut flower and potted plant wholesale stalls prefer cash, and some are cash-only. Plenty of retail stalls take cards, but during wholesale hours, or at materials/artificial flower stalls, cash is the default. Bring at least ₩20,000–30,000 in cash before you go.

12 Picks Across 4 Axes

Axis 1: Cut Flower Stalls (3 picks) — 23:30–10:00 wholesale, retail after

1. The main cut flower hall — Korea's No. 1 for cut stems

This zone represents the heart of Yangjae Flower Market. It covers the country's major commercial cut flowers — roses, carnations, tulips, chrysanthemums, lisianthus, and more. It isn't a single shop but an entire zone packed with dozens of stalls. First-time visitors are often overwhelmed by the scale.

  • Hours: 10:00–21:00 for retail (wholesale runs 23:30–10:00, for early-morning visitors only)
  • Prices (2026): A bundle of 10 roses ₩3,000–6,000 (wholesale hours), retail bouquets ₩10,000–30,000
  • Foreigner fit: There's a language barrier. A translation app (Papago) is essential. Numbers can be communicated with fingers.
  • Access: 5-minute walk from Exit 5, Yangjae Citizens' Forest Station (양재시민의숲역) (Line 3)
  • English: Only a few stalls have basic English. Korean dominates overall.
  • Timing: 10 a.m.–noon retail is best for freshness and stock. Some items may sell out by late afternoon.

2. Cut flower wholesale, 23:30–10:00 — the pre-dawn enthusiast run

The main users here are flower professionals, florists, and event planners. It's not especially practical for foreign tourists, but for flower lovers and traveling florists, it's a singular experience. The scene right after the 2–3 a.m. flower auction is hard to find anywhere else in the world.

  • Hours: 23:30–10:00
  • Prices (2026): Box-unit trading is the default. Small purchases are difficult or come at a higher per-unit price.
  • Foreigner fit: ★★★ — high experiential value, but limited practical shopping
  • Timing: 1–4 a.m. sees peak volume. The pre-dawn atmosphere is distinctive.
  • Caution: The wholesale zone has complex traffic flow and forklifts moving through it. Watch your step.

3. Cut flower retail, 10:00–21:00 — the general enthusiast's hours

Most foreign tourists come during this window. Small or single-stem purchases are both possible, and many stalls take cards, making it easy to access. Plenty of stalls will also make up a bouquet for you on the spot.

  • Hours: 10:00–21:00
  • Prices (2026): Small bouquets ₩10,000–20,000, large ones ₩30,000–50,000. Prices can rise during seasonal peaks (spring tulips, autumn chrysanthemums).
  • Payment: Many stalls take cards (still worth confirming)
  • English: A handful of stalls can handle basic communication. Showing a photo on Google Translate or Papago covers most situations.

Axis 2: Potted Plants and Trees (3 picks)

4. Potted plant stalls, 07:00–19:00 — pots and foliage

Potted flowers and foliage plants are sold here, ranging from small cacti and succulents to large indoor plants. For foreign tourists, the key question is usually whether the plant can be taken home on the flight (see the practical info section below).

  • Hours: 07:00–19:00
  • Prices (2026): Small succulents/cacti ₩5,000–15,000, medium foliage plants ₩20,000–50,000
  • Foreigner fit: ★★★★ — strong value for gift or decorative purposes, but check export rules first.
  • Timing: 7–9 a.m. is right after fresh stock arrives. After 3 p.m., plants tend to look good since they've just been watered.

5. The tree market, 07:00–21:00 — open every day of the year

Landscape trees, shrubs, and small garden trees are sold here. Visitors tend to be landscapers and interior decorators more than tourists. What's unusual is that it's open 365 days a year — even when the rest of Yangjae Flower Market takes a holiday, the tree market stays open.

  • Hours: 07:00–21:00 (open year-round)
  • Prices (2026): Small shrubs ₩10,000–30,000, medium landscape trees from ₩50,000
  • Foreigner fit: ★★★ — interesting to browse, but low practical value for purchase (many items can't be exported)

6. Wreath stalls, 07:00–21:00 — closed alternate Sundays

The main products here are wreaths for openings, funerals, and events. Foreign tourists rarely buy directly, but it's a chance to see Korean wreath culture up close.

  • Hours: 07:00–21:00 (closed every other Sunday — check ahead)
  • Foreigner fit: ★★ — mainly for viewing and cultural interest

Axis 3: F-Square and Materials (3 picks)

7. aT Flower Market (F-Square)

Run by the Korea Agro-Fisheries and Food Trade Corporation (aT), this is the official flower market. Official information is available at flower.at.or.kr. This is where auctions and wholesale distribution actually take place, and for foreign tourists it's less about buying and more about watching the operation in action.

  • Operator: Korea Agro-Fisheries and Food Trade Corporation (aT)
  • Official site: flower.at.or.kr
  • Foreigner fit: ★★★★ — a flower auction is a rare sight globally. Some visitors say it recalls the Aalsmeer flower auction in the Netherlands.
  • Note: Whether you can enter the market floor or watch an auction depends on the season and day of the week. Call ahead or check the website before visiting.

8. Materials and artificial flower stalls, 23:30–15:00

Ribbon, wrapping paper, artificial flowers, vases, floral foam, and other supplies are sold here. Since this zone runs alongside wholesale hours, it closes early.

  • Hours: 23:30–15:00 (be sure to arrive before 3 p.m.)
  • Prices (2026): Small amounts of ribbon/wrapping paper ₩1,000–3,000, small artificial flowers ₩5,000–20,000
  • Foreigner fit: ★★★ — popular for cheap decor items and gift-wrapping materials
  • Cash recommended

9. The Yangjae flower culture space

A space for flower-related exhibitions and cultural events. It's often not open on a fixed schedule, so check the calendar before visiting. Seasonal flower exhibitions and floral arrangement demonstrations happen here.

  • Foreigner fit: ★★★ — a special experience if your visit lines up with a seasonal event
  • Note: Not always open. Check ahead of your visit.

Axis 4: Foreign Enthusiasts (3 picks)

10. Foreign floriculture enthusiasts and hobbyists

This course is especially suited to foreign visitors with a floral arrangement or floristry hobby. At retail stalls, you can pick the flowers you want and ask them to wrap a bouquet on the spot. Showing a photo of the color palette or flower type you're after usually gets the message across.

  • Recommended time: Weekday mornings, 10 a.m.–1 p.m. (quiet, easy to get help)
  • What to bring: A smartphone translation app, a reference photo of the bouquet you want

11. Seasonal enthusiasts (cherry blossoms, roses, chrysanthemums)

This is where the country's largest volume of each season's signature flower arrives.

  • Prices rise and stock disappears fast at the height of each season. If there's a specific flower you want, an early-morning visit works in your favor.

12. The nearby Yangjae fabric wholesale cluster (cross-ref)

Near Yangjae Flower Market there's a cluster of fabric and textile wholesalers. It's an easy add-on by foot or car after the flower market. For visitors interested in fabric interiors or DIY projects, it makes for an extra half-day course.

  • Cross-ref: fabric wholesale district
  • Access: within walking distance of Yangjae Citizens' Forest Station

Practical Info

Transport

  • Subway: Line 3, Yangjae Citizens' Forest Station, Exit 5 → 5-minute walk
  • Bus: Get off at the Yangjae Flower Market stop (served by many Seoul city bus routes)
  • Parking: There's a paid lot inside the market. Crowded on weekends and during seasonal peaks.
  • Taxi: Search "Yangjae Flower Market" in the KakaoTaxi (카카오택시) app to call one directly

Language

  • Korean dominates overall. English signage and English-speaking staff are limited.
  • Using the Papago (파파고) app or Google Translate's photo translation feature is recommended.
  • Numbers can be shown with fingers, and showing a photo of the flower you want usually works for most transactions.
  • Tourist interpretation help: ☎ 1330 (Korea Tourism Organization, supports English/Chinese/Japanese/Russian and more)

Bringing flowers home

If you're a foreign tourist buying flowers or plants to take home, you must check quarantine and customs regulations before packing them.

  • Cut flowers (bouquets): Whether they can be imported varies by country. The US, EU, Japan, China, and others each have their own quarantine standards, and entry can be refused.
  • Potted plants with soil: Banned in most countries, or requiring separate quarantine clearance.
  • Dried or pressed flowers: Rules tend to be more relaxed, but still need country-specific confirmation.
  • Practical advice: The safest approach is to enjoy your flowers at your accommodation and leave them out of your luggage home. Dried materials and seedless artificial flowers tend to be easier to bring back.

FAQ 5

Q1. When and how should a first-time visitor go?

Visit between 10 a.m. and noon and take the 5-minute walk from Exit 5, Yangjae Citizens' Forest Station (Line 3). This is right after retail opens, so both freshness and stock are good, and it's easy to browse without crowds. Weekdays are even quieter.

Q2. How cheap is it really?

Even at retail hours, prices are often 30–50% below Seoul city florists. A bundle of 10 roses runs about ₩3,000–6,000 (retail hours). Wholesale hours (23:30–10:00) are cheaper still, but small purchases are harder to make.

Q3. Can I get by in English?

Korean dominates overall. Bring the Papago or Google Translate app. Showing a photo of the flower you want gets you past the language barrier for most purchases. Call 1330 if you need tourist interpretation.

Q4. Any good combined course with other series?

A flower market morning plus the Yangjae fabric wholesale district in the afternoon makes a solid half-day. Pair a full day with Garak Market (가락시장) for a route covering two of Seoul's major wholesale markets.

Q5. Can I bring the bouquet I buy home on the flight?

It can't be guaranteed, since quarantine rules vary by country. The realistic options are enjoying it at your accommodation and leaving it behind, or choosing dried flowers or artificial flower items that are cleared for export.

3 Full-Day Courses

1. Full cut-flower course: Yangjae Citizens' Forest Station → cut flower retail zone (from 10 a.m.) → tour the aT Flower Market → potted plant stalls → materials/artificial flowers (before 3 p.m.) → head back

2. Full market course: Yangjae Flower Market in the morning → cross over to Garak Market in the afternoon (about a 20-minute drive)

3. Outskirts cluster course: Yangjae Flower Market in the morning → Seorae Village (서래마을) café and stroll in the afternoon

5 Tips for Foreign Enthusiasts

1. Exit 5, Yangjae Citizens' Forest Station (Line 3) — a 5-minute walk gets you the best access

2. Wholesale 23:30–10:00 vs. retail 10:00–21:00 — pick the time slot that matches your goal

3. Bring at least ₩20,000–30,000 in cash — wholesale and materials zones favor cash

4. Open year-round (the tree market) — stays open even on the market's citywide holidays

5. Papago/Google Translate plus a photo of the flower you want — the key fix for the language barrier
