🎯 How to Use This Guide

Daegu has several food alleys where shops all selling one kind of food gather together. This guide covers three alleys introduced by the tourism organization (Dongin-dong Jjimgalbi (동인동 찜갈비, braised beef ribs), Anjirang Gopchang (안지랑 곱창, seasoned gopchang (small intestine)), Pyeonghwa Market Dakttongjip (평화시장 닭똥집, chicken gizzards)) and two long-established shops (Gukil Ttaro Gukbap (국일따로국밥), Miseongdang Napjak Mandu (미성당납작만두)). The Seomun Market Night Market gets only its days of operation, and the details are left to #641 Daegu Seomun Market, Day and Night.

The operating details are copied from values registered in the Korea Tourism Organization tourism data (TourAPI). Menu prices are values registered on Diningcode (secondary source, checked 2026-11-09), so they may change.

🍲 Dongin-dong Jjimgalbi Alley — Spicy Braised Ribs in an Aluminum Pot

Dongin-dong Jjimgalbi Alley (Dongin-dong 1-ga, Jung-gu, Daegu (대구 중구 동인동1가), ☎ Daegu Tourist Information Center 053-627-8906) is an alley that formed on both sides of a road about 100 meters long, after the braised ribs people had been eating in the Dongin-dong residential area since the 1970s became well known and restaurants opened. Beef ribs are slow-cooked in a dented aluminum pot, with hot chili powder and minced garlic as the main seasonings. Unlike galbi-jjim flavored only with soy sauce, it is spicy enough to make your tongue tingle, yet also slightly sweet. After eating, you mix rice into the leftover sauce.

  • Opening hours and closed days: They differ by shop.
  • Famous Dongin-dong jjimgalbi house — Nakyeong Jjimgalbi main branch (9-17 Dongdeok-ro 36-gil, Dongin-dong 1-ga, Jung-gu (중구 동덕로36길 9-17), ☎ 053-423-3330): It started in 1972 as a bar and has been passed down through the generations. 10:00~21:00 (last order 20:30), open year-round, cards accepted, free parking, takeout available for some menu items.
  • Prices (Diningcode, secondary source): Jjimgalbi 22,000 won, Hanwoo (한우) jjimgalbi 30,000 won, beef stew (쇠고기찌개) 9,000 won.

🔥 Anjirang Gopchang Alley — Seasoned Gopchang on the Way to Apsan

Anjirang Gopchang Alley (67 Anjirang-ro 16-gil, Nam-gu (남구 안지랑로16길 67), ☎ information 053-664-2763) is located midway on the road from Anjirang Five-Way Intersection (안지랑오거리) to Anjirang Valley (안지랑골) in Apsan (앞산). It began around 1979 with Chungbuk Sikdang (충북식당), and the number of shops grew after the 1998 IMF crisis. Seasoned gopchang restaurants stand in a row, and recently it has developed into a street led by young people. In 2012 it was chosen as one of the 5 Nationwide Food Theme Streets, organized by the Ministry of Culture, Sports and Tourism.

  • Opening hours and closed days: They differ by shop. Parking available.
  • Gopchang restaurant registered with the tourism organization — Anji Gopchang Makchang (63 Daemyeong-ro 36-gil, Nam-gu (남구 대명로36길 63), ☎ 0507-1310-3086): It uses a handmade sauce and also serves boiled makchang (막창), heart (염통), spicy chicken feet (불닭발), and kimchi stew (김치찌개). 09:00~02:00, closed Mondays, cards accepted, takeout available. If you use the nearby Anjirang No. 2 Public Parking Lot, 1 hour of parking fees is covered.
  • Prices (Diningcode, secondary source): Gopchang Hanbagaji 16,000 won, makchang + gopchang small 29,000 won, medium 37,000 won, makchang + gopchang + heart large 44,000 won.

🍗 Pyeonghwa Market Dakttongjip Alley — Started in 1972 as a Makgeolli Side Dish

Daegu Pyeonghwa Market Dakttongjip Alley (6 Ayang-ro 9-gil, Dong-gu (동구 아양로9길 6), ☎ Daegu Tourist Information Center 053-627-8906) is, according to the tourism organization's description, the only dakttongjip alley in the country. In 1972, Sama Tongdak (삼아통닭) fried leftover chicken gizzards for laborers at the day-labor market and served them as a side dish for makgeolli, and that was the beginning. In the 2000s, soy sauce gizzards and garlic gizzards appeared, and menu items such as squid gizzards and shrimp gizzards were added.

  • Opening hours and closed days: They differ by shop. Parking available.
  • Nearby: It is close to Dongdaegu Station, and Dongchon Amusement Park (동촌유원지) is nearby along the Geumhogang River.
  • Daegu chicken and the Chimaek Festival are covered in #483 Daegu Chimaek Guide.

🥣 Gukil Ttaro Gukbap — Since 1946, Rice and Soup Served Separately

Gukil Ttaro Gukbap (571 Gukchaebosang-ro, Jung-gu (중구 국채보상로 571), ☎ 053-253-7623) has been run for 3 generations since 1946, and is designated a Daegu Metropolitan City local traditional restaurant. Ttaro gukbap got its name because the rice and the soup are served separately (ttaro). Beef, congealed blood (선지), and seasoning go into a beef-bone broth.

  • Opening hours and closed days: Listed as 'regular business' with no set hours, open year-round.
  • Facilities: Cards accepted, takeout available, parking available.
  • Prices (Diningcode, secondary source): Ttaro gukbap 11,000 won, special ttaro gukbap (특따로국밥) 12,000 won, boxed lunch ttaro (도시락 따로) 11,000 won.

🥟 Miseongdang Napjak Mandu — Three Fillings: Scallion, Chives, Glass Noodles

Miseongdang Napjak Mandu (93 Myeongdeok-ro, Jung-gu (중구 명덕로 93), ☎ 053-255-0742) was started by the father in 1963 and carried on by the son, and according to the tourism organization's description it is the napjak mandu specialty shop with the deepest tradition in Daegu. The napjak mandu filling has only three things, scallion, chives, and glass noodles (당면), and you eat it with soy sauce poured on top.

  • Opening hours: Weekdays 10:30~21:00, weekends 10:30~20:00.
  • Closed days: Every Monday, and on the days of Seollal and Chuseok. No parking.
  • Prices (Diningcode, secondary source): Napjak mandu 4,500 won, wang mandu (왕만두) 5,000 won, jjolmyeon (쫄면) 6,000 won.

🌙 Seomun Market Night Market — Friday, Saturday, Sunday Evenings Only

Seomun Market Night Market (45 Keunjang-ro 26-gil, Jung-gu (중구 큰장로26길 45)) opens Fri·Sat 19:00~23:30 and Sun 19:00~22:30, and is closed Mon~Thu. The daytime market is open 09:00~18:00 and closed on the first and third Sundays of each month. For the daytime market, Chilseong Night Market (칠성야시장), and the Keunjang-gil Bedding Famous Street, see #641 Daegu Seomun Market, Day and Night.

🥗 If You Have Dietary Restrictions

  • If you avoid pork: Jjimgalbi and ttaro gukbap are beef, and dakttongjip is chicken. The gopchang restaurants also sell pork belly and pork duruchigi, so it is hard to choose. No place is marked halal-certified in KTO data.
  • If you are vegetarian: No place is marked as having vegetarian menu items. According to the tourism organization's description, the filling of Miseongdang napjak mandu has only scallion, chives, and glass noodles.
  • If you have trouble with spicy food: Dongin-dong jjimgalbi is spicy enough to make your tongue tingle (according to the tourism organization's description). Napjak mandu is eaten with soy sauce poured on top.

⚠ 3 Common Mistakes

  1. Thinking jjimgalbi is Seoul-style galbi-jjim — Dongin-dong jjimgalbi is not soy-sauce galbi-jjim but a spicy jjimgalbi seasoned with chili powder and garlic.
  2. Going for napjak mandu on a Monday — Miseongdang Napjak Mandu is closed every Monday, and Anji Gopchang Makchang is also closed on Mondays.
  3. Looking for the Seomun Market Night Market on a Mon~Thu evening — The night market opens only Fri·Sat·Sun. On Mon~Thu evenings, pick an alley restaurant.

⚙ FAQ 2

Q. Where can I eat late at night?

A. According to the tourism organization's information, Anji Gopchang Makchang is open until 02:00, and Gukil Ttaro Gukbap is listed as 'regular business' with no set hours. Other shops in the alleys differ by shop.

Q. Can I pay by card?

A. According to the tourism organization's information, cards are accepted at Nakyeong Jjimgalbi, Anji Gopchang Makchang, and Gukil Ttaro Gukbap. No card information is registered for Miseongdang Napjak Mandu or the other shops in the alleys.
