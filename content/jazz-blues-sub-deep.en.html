Seoul's jazz scene has a distinctly Korean structure. Itaewon, Hongdae, and Cheongdam each occupy a different tier, and each cluster's foreign-visitor friendliness, price point, and performance style are clearly distinct. First-time foreign visitors who assume "any jazz bar will do" tend to be disappointed — the matrix below is built to match each enthusiast segment to the right venue.

12 picks, 4 axes — the deep dive

Axis 1: Itaewon's traditional jazz scene (3 picks)

1. All That Jazz (Itaewon, since 1976 — Korea's #1 traditional jazz club)

Seoul's oldest dedicated jazz club. Since opening in 1976, it has stood as a symbol of Korea's jazz scene. It sits in a basement space just off Itaewon's main strip, with a regular crowd made up of long-term foreign residents and JP enthusiasts.

Location: Itaewon-ro, Itaewon-dong, Yongsan-gu, Seoul (5-minute walk from Itaewon Station exit 1)

Cover charge: none. A minimum of one drink order (roughly 12,000-15,000 won).

Showtime: Mon-Fri starts at 21:00, Sat starts at 20:30. Closed Sundays (recommended check).

Performance style: standard jazz, Latin jazz, and vocal jazz, built around a house band with rotating guest artists across sets.

Fit for foreign visitors: *****. English communication is available and the regular crowd includes a high share of foreign visitors. Dress code is smart casual — jeans and a shirt are fine.

Access: from Itaewon Station exit 1, walk 5 minutes along Itaewon-ro, then into the alley and down the basement stairs. Search "All That Jazz Itaewon" on Naver Map.

Reservation: no formal booking system. On weekend evenings, arriving 20-30 minutes before opening gets you in. Weekdays are relaxed.

Timing: arriving 30 minutes before showtime is the key to a good seat. Entry mid-show is possible but seating options shrink.

2. Onefive Jazz Bar (Cheongdam)

A luxury jazz bar in Cheongdam-dong (Cheongdam-dong, 청담동). Both the interior and the sound system are high-end. Its clientele is largely foreign residents based in Cheongdam, JP and EN businesspeople, and couples.

Location: Cheongdam-dong, Gangnam-gu, Seoul — 10-15 minutes on foot from Apgujeong Rodeo Station (압구정로데오역), or an 8-minute walk from Cheongdam Station

Cover charge: 20,000-30,000 won, typically structured to include one drink (confirm before visiting).

Showtime: Fri-Sat starts at 21:00. Confirm weekday operating hours separately.

Fit for foreign visitors: ****. An English menu is available, and the volume stays low enough for conversation.

Reservation: recommended for weekends, via Naver Reservation or phone.

Timing: October-November fall shows on Fri-Sat tend to have the best atmosphere.

3. Club Evans (Itaewon)

An established club within the Itaewon jazz cluster. It skews younger than All That Jazz, with a performance style centered on modern and fusion jazz.

Location: Itaewon-dong, Yongsan-gu, Seoul (near Itaewon Station, recommended check on Naver Map)

Cover charge: none (drink order required).

Showtime: Wed-Sun starts at 21:00.

Fit for foreign visitors: ****. A foreigner-friendly atmosphere where talking about the music is welcomed.

Access: roughly 5-10 minutes on foot from Itaewon Station exit 1.

Reservation: not needed. Arrive early on weekend evenings.

Axis 2: Cheongdam luxury (3 picks)

4. The Cheongdam luxury jazz bar cluster

Cheongdam and Apgujeong host several standalone luxury jazz lounges. It's more practical to know the character of the area than to fix on a single confirmed name.

Features: intimate seating (20-40 seats), a strong focus on sound quality, drinks running 20,000-40,000 won.

Fit for foreign visitors: ****. Many venues offer English communication. Dress code: smart casual or above.

Practical tip: before visiting, search "Cheongdam jazz bar" on Google Maps and check recent reviews. The same search works on Naver's English mode or Kakao Map.

5. The Apgujeong jazz lounge area

Around Apgujeong Rodeo Street, in a restaurant-meets-live-lounge format.

Features: a dinner-plus-jazz package format, 50,000-80,000 won per person including a meal.

Fit for foreign visitors: ****. Some venues take English reservations.

Reservation: advance booking required.

6. 5-star hotel live sets (Cheongdam/Gangnam area, cross with #577)

Hotels such as Signiel Seoul (Signiel Seoul, 시그니엘 서울) and InterContinental Gangnam run live jazz sets on Friday and Saturday nights in their bars and lounges. One of the most accessible options for foreign travelers.

Cover charge: a minimum one-drink charge of 25,000-40,000 won, or a separate admission fee of 50,000-100,000+ won. Varies by hotel.

Showtime: typically Fri-Sat starting at 20:00.

Fit for foreign visitors: *****. English-speaking staff are always on hand, and reservations can be made in English.

Reservation: book directly with the hotel restaurant 1-2 weeks ahead. OpenTable or the hotel's official site also work.

Axis 3: Hongdae indie jazz (3 picks)

7. Boogie Woogie (Hongdae, #455 pick #11)

The flagship club of Hongdae's (Hongdae, 홍대) indie jazz scene. A small space (50 seats or fewer) that puts performers and the audience close together.

Location: near Hongik University Station in Mapo-gu, Seoul (roughly a 10-15 minute walk from exit 2, recommended check on Naver Map)

Cover charge: 10,000-15,000 won (sometimes includes a drink).

Showtime: Thu-Sun starts at 20:30.

Fit for foreign visitors: ****. English communication is available, and the vibe centers on the music itself.

Access: from Hongik University Station exit 2, walk through the Hongdae front alleys.

Reservation: not needed. The small capacity rewards arriving early.

8. EBS Space Gonggam (Mapo/Sangam — public taping)

A public music-taping program run by EBS. It covers jazz, world music, indie, and more, and lets visitors watch actual broadcast recordings for free.

Location: EBS headquarters in Sangam-dong, Mapo-gu, Seoul (10-minute walk from Digital Media City Station)

Admission: free (advance application to attend the taping required).

Showtime: varies by broadcast schedule.

Fit for foreign visitors: ***. Guidance is mostly in Korean, though the music itself needs no translation. The application page is in Korean.

How to apply: through EBS's official site, under the public taping application section. Confirm the application deadline in advance.

Timing: spring and fall lineups tend to be the most varied.

9. The Hongdae indie jazz spot cluster

Hongdae has a number of small jazz spaces that run as pop-ups or recurring shows rather than under a single fixed name.

Features: show announcements posted on social media the same day, admission of 5,000-10,000 won, small capacity.

Practical tip: search "Hongdae jazz show" or "Hongdae jazz live" on Naver or Google Maps and check that month's schedule. The Instagram hashtag "#hongdaejazz" also works.

Axis 4: The foreign enthusiast segment matrix

10. The JP enthusiast segment (traditional jazz)

Japanese travelers tend to have a sharp ear for jazz quality. All That Jazz (traditional, since 1976) and Evans (modern) rank #1 and #2. Looking up the performing artists' names beforehand gives you something to talk about. There's no Japanese-language menu, but English plus body language covers it.

Recommended route: Itaewon Station, then All That Jazz, then a move to Evans (same area).

11. The #1 pick for EN expat jazz enthusiasts

Within Itaewon's foreign resident community, All That Jazz is known as the "official jazz spot." It ranks #1 in recommendations from Trazy and expat Facebook groups. Visit on a weekend evening and you'll easily find an English-speaking conversation partner.

Budget: roughly 25,000-35,000 won per person for two drinks.

12. CN luxury jazz plus Cheongdam

Among Chinese-speaking travelers, the luxury segment tends to favor Cheongdam and 5-star hotel live sets. Confirm whether English- or Chinese-speaking staff are available when booking the hotel in advance.

Recommended: start at Signiel Seoul's live bar, then continue to Onefive.

Three big mistakes foreign visitors make

Mistake 1 — showing up on a weekend with no plan

All That Jazz has no formal reservation system, but a line forms 30 minutes before the 21:00 weekend show. If you're not prepared to wait in line, a reservation-based club (Onefive, a 5-star hotel) is the safer choice.

Mistake 2 — overlooking the dress code

Smart casual is the baseline at Cheongdam and 5-star hotel venues. Showing up in flip-flops or shorts has led to turned-away cases. Itaewon clubs are more relaxed, but neat clothing still fits the mood.

Mistake 3 — underestimating the drink budget

Even with no cover charge, a minimum drink order is standard. Jazz club drinks run 5-10 times convenience-store prices. Budget 30,000-60,000 won per visit for two people to enjoy it properly.

Practical info: transit, booking, language

Transit

  • Itaewon clubs: Itaewon Station (line 6), exit 1. Buses run late at night (check real-time routes on Naver Map).
  • Hongdae clubs: Hongik University Station (line 2, Airport Railroad), exit 2.
  • Cheongdam/Gangnam: Apgujeong Rodeo Station (Suin-Bundang Line) or Cheongdam Station (line 7), then walk.
  • Getting home late: subway service ends around 1am. Install the Kakao T or Uber app in advance.

Booking and reservations

  • All That Jazz, Evans, Boogie Woogie: no advance booking — first-come, first-served.
  • Onefive and 5-star hotels: Naver Reservation, each hotel's official site, or OpenTable.
  • EBS Space Gonggam: apply for the public taping on EBS's website (Korean-language page).

Language

  • All That Jazz and Evans: English communication available (staff have basic English).
  • Cheongdam and 5-star hotels: full English support.
  • Small Hongdae clubs: mostly Korean — bring a translation app (Papago, Google Translate).
  • MC banter during shows is in Korean, but the music itself needs no translation.

FAQ

Q1. If I can only visit one jazz venue in Seoul, which one?

All That Jazz (Itaewon, since 1976). Korea's oldest traditional jazz club, with a strong foreign regular crowd, no reservation needed, and entry with just a drink order. The default choice when you don't have another reason to pick elsewhere.

Q2. What kind of budget should I plan for?

At Itaewon clubs, budget 30,000-60,000 won for two people per visit (2-3 drinks). Cheongdam and 5-star hotels run 100,000-200,000+ won (including a meal package). Remember that no cover charge doesn't mean zero budget.

Q3. If I only want venues where English works?

All That Jazz, then 5-star hotel live sets, then Onefive, in that order. Small Hongdae clubs are manageable with a translation app.

Q4. Is there a dress code?

Itaewon calls for smart casual (jeans and a shirt is fine). Cheongdam and 5-star hotels recommend business casual or above. There are cases of flip-flops and shorts being turned away at Cheongdam clubs.

Q5. How can I find out about Korean jazz show schedules in advance?

  • All That Jazz's official social media (search Instagram, Facebook)
  • Interpark or YES24 performance search ("jazz")
  • Search "Itaewon jazz show" on Naver for recent blog and cafe posts
  • For Hongdae's indie schedule: the Instagram hashtags "#hongdaejazz #hongdaeshow"

Three full-day courses

Course A — the full traditional Itaewon jazz set

Dinner in Itaewon (rooftop dining or Korean food), then All That Jazz at 21:00, then move on to Evans in the same area. Total budget: 60,000-100,000 won per person.

Course B — a Cheongdam luxury jazz night

Shopping and cafes in Cheongdam, then Onefive at 21:00 or a 5-star hotel live set at 20:00. Dress code preparation essential. Total budget: 100,000-200,000 won per person.

Course C — a Hongdae indie jazz crawl

Hongdae street walk, then dinner, then Boogie Woogie at 20:30. Total budget: 40,000-70,000 won per person.

Five tips for foreign enthusiasts

1. All That Jazz = traditional since 1976 — the obvious starting point for anyone new to Korea's jazz scene.

2. The #1 segment for JP and EN enthusiasts — the Itaewon cluster is accessible with no reservation required.

3. Book ahead for Cheongdam and 5-star venues — 1-2 weeks out on weekends; for Itaewon, arriving early substitutes for a reservation.

4. Dress smart casual or above — especially at Cheongdam and Gangnam lounges.

5. Combine #455, #605, #142, and #138 — covering all three areas: Itaewon, Cheongdam, and Hongdae.
