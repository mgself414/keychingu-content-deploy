Why Guri Agricultural & Fisheries Market, Right Now — Korea's #2 Wholesale Market, a Northeast Seoul Hub

The Guri Agricultural & Fisheries Wholesale Market (구리 농수산물 도매시장) is Korea's #2 agricultural and fisheries wholesale market. If Garak Market (가락시장) is centered on southern Seoul's Gangnam, Guri is the logistics hub for northeast Seoul and northern Gyeonggi. It's a 10-minute walk from Guri Station, reachable with a single ride on the Gyeongui–Jungang Line (경의중앙선).

Why enthusiasts pick Guri over Garak: it's smaller, but there are fewer crowds, direct-purchase counters at wholesale prices stay open, and auctions run from 2 a.m. Travelers who got overwhelmed and exhausted at Garak often find they can dig even deeper once they come to Guri.

Located in Injang-dong (인창동), Guri, the market is operated by Gamaco Co., Ltd. (주식회사 가락). It's divided into a seafood building, a produce building, a parking building, and more, with a continuous 24-hour flow from dawn auctions to retail direct sales.

⚠️ 3 Common Mistakes Foreign Visitors Make

Mistake 1: Showing Up at Midday and Wondering "Why Is It So Empty?"

Guri is a pre-dawn market. Seafood auctions peak between 2 and 5 a.m., produce auctions between 4 and 8 a.m. Arrive after 10 a.m. and the auctions are over, with only half the volume left. The key is to plan your visit for 3 to 7 a.m.

Mistake 2: Wandering Into the Auction Area Uninvited

The auction floor is restricted to registered wholesale dealers only. Visitors can only watch from designated walkways or viewing routes. Getting in the way of working auctioneers or forklifts is dangerous. Follow the signage and safety lines.

Mistake 3: Assuming "Wholesale Price" Just Means "Cheap"

Wholesale trading is by the box or kilogram as a baseline. Buying just one item or a small quantity has to happen at a retail counter or a direct-sales corner inside the market. Ask a wholesale stall for just one piece and you'll get an awkward look. Figure out which building has direct retail sales before you head in.

12 Picks Across 4 Axes, In Depth

Axis 1: Seafood Wholesale (3 Picks)

1. The Seafood Building (Main Seafood Wholesale Hall)

The heart of the Guri market. Fish, shellfish, and crustaceans trade in wholesale lots. Once the 2–5 a.m. auction wraps up, wholesale dealers spend the next stretch sorting their purchases at individual stalls. Foreign visitors can watch the live-fish tanks between 5 and 7 a.m., right after the auction, and buy small quantities at the retail direct-sales corner.

  • Hours: Auction 02:00–05:00 / retail direct sales from 06:00
  • Prices (2026 reference): Flounder (gwanghwaeo), 1 fish, ₩15,000–30,000 / rockfish (ureok), 1 fish, ₩8,000–15,000 (varies by season and size)
  • Foreign-visitor fit: The live-fish tanks are a sight in themselves / use the direct-sales corner for small purchases
  • English: Limited — a translation app is essential
  • Pre-dawn timing: 03:00–06:00 recommended (right after the auction peak)

2. The Live and Fresh Fish Stalls

Rows of live-fish tanks line this section inside the seafood building. Each tank holds a different species — flounder, rockfish, red seabream, long-arm octopus, and more. Fresh (non-live) chilled fish are stacked by the box. For a solo foreign visitor buying a small amount, the direct-sales corner is the better bet — this section is worth it mainly for the scale and atmosphere.

  • Hours: Peaks 06:00–11:00 after the dawn auction / quiet after 2 p.m.
  • Prices (2026 reference): Long-arm octopus (nakji), 1kg, ₩20,000–35,000 / blue crab (kkotge), 1kg, ₩15,000–25,000 (varies widely by season)
  • Foreign-visitor fit: A great photo spot / a chance to see the range of Korean seafood
  • Access: Straight in from the seafood building's entrance

3. The Frozen & Dried Seafood Stalls

Frozen shrimp, squid, mackerel, and pollock, plus dried goods (anchovies, kelp, dried pollock) are traded by the box. Restaurant owners are the main customers, but some counters sell small quantities directly. This section is the realistic choice for foreign travelers looking to bring home a food souvenir from Guri. Vacuum-sealed dried goods make it easier to take items home.

  • Hours: 06:00–14:00 (mostly cold-storage stalls — afternoon visits work)
  • Prices (2026 reference): Dried anchovies, 500g, ₩5,000–9,000 / dried squid, 5 pieces, ₩8,000–14,000
  • Foreign-visitor fit: Vacuum-sealed dried goods may be takeable abroad (check your home country's customs rules separately)
  • Access: Behind the seafood building or in a separate cold-storage building (check on-site signage)

Axis 2: Produce Wholesale (3 Picks)

4. The Produce Building (Main Produce Wholesale Hall)

The main hall for wholesale fruit and vegetable trading. Auctions run from 4 to 8 a.m. After the auction, individual counters sell small quantities — a box of apples, a head of napa cabbage. Visit at dawn and the forklifts weaving through, stacking boxes, are a sight on their own.

  • Hours: Auction 04:00–08:00 / retail direct sales 08:00–14:00
  • Prices (2026 reference): Apples, 10kg box, ₩25,000–45,000 / cherry tomatoes, 2kg, ₩6,000–10,000
  • Foreign-visitor fit: Watching the produce auction (stay behind the safety line) / buying small amounts of fresh fruit
  • English: Almost none — bring a translation app and point
  • Pre-dawn timing: 05:00–08:00 recommended

5. The Seasonal Produce Section (Strawberries, Peaches, Tangerines)

This section concentrates on seasonal fruit. Strawberries peak December–May, peaches July–September, tangerines October–January. Prices often run 30–40% below supermarkets — but wholesale units (2kg or 4kg trays) are the baseline. If you want a small amount, ask the direct-sales counter first whether individual pieces are available.

  • Seasonal prices (2026 reference): Strawberries, 2kg, ₩10,000–18,000 / peaches, 4kg, ₩15,000–28,000 / tangerines, 5kg, ₩8,000–15,000
  • Foreign-visitor fit: Experience Korean seasonal fruit at true wholesale prices — strawberry season (winter–spring) gets the best reaction from foreign visitors
  • Access: The seasonal corner inside the produce building (check the entrance signage)

6. The General Vegetables & Produce Section

This section handles bulk shipments of basic cooking ingredients — chili peppers, garlic, onions, potatoes, carrots. Restaurant owners are the main customers. A solo foreign visitor is realistically here to take in the scale, and the entrance-side direct-sales stand is the practical choice for a small purchase. For travelers encountering the range of Korean ingredients for the first time, the visual experience is content in itself.

  • Hours: 06:00–14:00 (concentrated in the morning, given how vegetables move)
  • Foreign-visitor fit: Explore the variety of Korean ingredients / buy small amounts of garlic, chili peppers, and other seasonings

Axis 3: Dawn Auctions (3 Picks)

7. The Seafood Auction (02:00–08:00)

The auction begins at 2 a.m. An auctioneer calls out prices, and wholesale dealers raise numbered paddles to bid. It's smaller in scale than the seafood auction at Garak, but with fewer crowds, you can actually see the action better. Foreign visitors can only watch from the designated viewing route. Between 2 and 4 a.m. is the auction's peak, when the energy on the floor is highest.

  • Can visitors watch?: There's an outside viewing walkway (check with market staff to be sure)
  • Pre-dawn timing: 02:00–04:00 auction peak / 04:00–06:00 sorting and settlement
  • Caution: Stay behind the safety line / work vehicles (forklifts, carts) are moving

8. The Produce Auction (04:00–08:00)

It starts a bit later than the seafood auction. Between 4 and 6 a.m., you'll see stock being distributed out to individual counters right after the auction. Given the nature of produce auctions, the piles of fruit and vegetables stacking up make a great photo. This is the most accessible entry point for foreign visitors to experience Korea's dawn-market culture.

  • Pre-dawn timing: 05:00–07:00 (peak movement of stock right after the auction)
  • Foreign-visitor fit: Photo and video — Guri's produce auction has fewer crowds, leaving plenty of room to shoot

9. The 24-Hour Wholesale Section

Some counters stay open 24 hours, even after the auctions end — mainly the frozen, dried-goods, and processed-food counters. Even if you miss the dawn auction, the whole market is still lively through the morning. It quiets down in the afternoon and evening.

  • Hours: Some counters run 24h (varies by item — check on site)
  • Foreign-visitor fit: If matching the auction time is hard, a mid-morning visit is a reasonable compromise

Axis 4: Nearby Cross-References (3 Picks)

10. Gyeongui–Jungang Line, Guri Station Cross-Reference

It's about a 10-minute walk from Guri Station to the market. By bus, you can get off right at the Guri Agricultural & Fisheries Market stop. The Gyeongui–Jungang Line connects to downtown Seoul via Seoul Station, Yongsan Station, and Wangsimni Station. A route that starts with late-night shopping at Dongdaemun (동대문) (#583) and moves on to Guri before dawn has become an established route among foreign enthusiasts.

  • Transfer: Board the Gyeongui–Jungang Line at Wangsimni Station → get off at Guri Station
  • Fare: About ₩1,300 with a transit card (as of 2026 — check current fare)
  • Travel time: Wangsimni to Guri, about 10–12 minutes

11. #587 Noryangjin, Garak, and Majang Cross-Reference

A full-set route across Seoul and Gyeonggi's wholesale markets, Guri included. Combine it with #587 — Noryangjin (노량진) and Garak at dawn — and #592 — Majang (마장) livestock market — and you can experience seafood, livestock, and produce wholesale all in one go. Fitting it all into a single day is exhausting — planning a 2-day, 1-night dawn route is the realistic approach.

  • Suggested route: Guri at dawn (03:00–07:00) → Majang (#592) in the morning → rest that evening → Noryangjin and Garak (#587) the next day

12. Restaurants and Food Near Guri

There are hangover-soup restaurants inside the market or near the entrance that open before dawn. Between 5 and 7 a.m., right after the dawn auction wraps up, is when the market workers eat. Korean hangover-soup dishes — sundaeguk (순댓국, blood sausage soup), bugeoguk (북엇국, dried pollock soup), kongnamul-gukbap (콩나물국밥, bean-sprout rice soup) — dominate the menu. Individual restaurant names and hours change, so it's safer to just find whichever spot is open when you're there.

  • Pre-dawn restaurant hours: Concentrated 05:00–10:00 (some run 24h)
  • Price range: ₩6,000–9,000 per person (for hangover soup)
  • Foreign-visitor fit: Experience Korea's dawn-market hangover-soup culture — high satisfaction among foreign visitors who enjoy broth-based dishes

Payment

Wholesale counters run on cash or direct bank transfer to trading partners as a baseline. Some retail direct-sales corners accept cards, but check first. It's safest for foreign visitors to bring ₩100,000–300,000 in cash before visiting.

Language

English signage and English-speaking staff are extremely limited. Bring a translation app like Google Translate or Papago. Writing down numbers and pointing covers a good deal of communication. Calling 1330 (24-hour tourist interpretation, Korean/English) can also connect you to an interpreter.

Hygiene & Safety

Pre-dawn markets have wet floors with lots of water and ice around. Wear slip-resistant shoes. Forklifts and carts move through the auction area, so don't stand in their path.

FAQ

Q1. Guri vs. Garak (#587) — Which Should I Visit First?

For a first visit, Garak is more accessible (central Seoul). If you've already done Garak and want a wholesale experience without the crowds, that's Guri. Guri is the northeast Seoul hub, and it pairs naturally with a Dongdaemun–Wangsimni route.

Q2. What's the Best Time to Go?

Seafood peaks 3–5 a.m., produce peaks 5–7 a.m. To see both, entering at 3 a.m. and leaving at 7 a.m. is ideal. If that's too much, just timing your visit for the produce auction peak at 5–7 a.m. is enough.

Q3. Can I Buy Small Quantities?

Wholesale counters trade by the box or kilogram as a baseline. Small quantities are available at the retail direct-sales corners, often near the entrance of the seafood and produce buildings. Ask "hana man" (just one) before you go in.

Q4. Is It Safe for a Foreign Visitor to Go Alone?

It's a pre-dawn market, but it's safe — full of workers and well lit. Slippery floors and work-vehicle paths are the main things to watch for. As long as you avoid wandering into the auction area, going alone is fine.

Q5. What's the Best Season to Visit?

Seafood volume is richest in autumn and winter (September–February). Produce peaks in spring and summer (March–August) with strawberries, peaches, watermelon, and other seasonal fruit. Strawberry season (December–April) gets an especially good reaction from foreign visitors at the produce building.

5 Tips for Foreign Visitors

1. Gyeongui–Jungang Line, Guri Station → 10-min walk (public transit is enough)

2. Time it for 3–7 a.m. to catch both the auctions and peak volume

3. Bring ₩100,000–300,000 in cash — cash is standard at wholesale counters

4. Buy small quantities at the direct-sales corners — don't ask a wholesale counter for just one piece

5. Plan the #587 full set — combining Guri with Majang and Noryangjin packs in more than visiting Guri alone
