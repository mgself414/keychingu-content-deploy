Korea Guide for Malaysian Travelers — Checking K-ETA, the 4 Halal Restaurant Categories, Prayer Rooms, Direct Flights (Official Values, October 2026)

🎯 How to Use This Guide

On a Friday afternoon, walk about 5 minutes uphill from Itaewon Station and a white dome and minaret come into view — that is the Seoul Central Mosque. This guide covers the four things that trip up travelers from Malaysia before they leave, using only values from official pages: entry paperwork (K-ETA, e-Arrival), how to choose halal restaurants (the Korea Tourism Organization's 4 categories), where to pray (Seoul Central Mosque and the Incheon Airport prayer rooms), and flights. Anything that could not be confirmed on an official page is stated as unconfirmed. This edition is for Muslim travelers visiting Korea for the first time, and for anyone traveling with family or friends whose dietary needs differ.

The old guide's figures — "400,000 visitors a year," "800,000+ BTS Malaysia ARMY," "65% independent travelers, $1,200 spend per traveler," "30+ KMF halal certifications," and "Korea #1 for multi-faith friendliness" — were removed because no official source could be found.

🛂 Entry Paperwork — K-ETA and the e-Arrival Card

  • Temporary K-ETA exemption: It runs from January 1 to December 31, 2026 (official K-ETA notice). The notice does not list eligible countries, so we could not confirm whether Malaysia is included — scan your passport on the k-eta.go.kr application screen and it will show whether you are "exempt"
  • Embassy guidance: The Korean Embassy in Malaysia posting says Malaysian passport holders should in principle apply for K-ETA at least 3 weeks before arrival (fee KRW 10,000, valid for 3 years — the posting reflects the situation as of July 2023, last edited 2025-09-29)
  • Visa-free length of stay: Up to 3 months for Malaysian passports (K-ETA official eligible-country list · MOFA mission visa-free table, https://www.k-eta.go.kr/portal/guide/viewetaalification.do?locale=EN)
  • e-Arrival Card: If you did not actually obtain a K-ETA, submit an e-Arrival Card. It is free at e-arrivalcard.go.kr from 3 days before arrival, and a paper form can also be used (Ministry of Justice, #1396 Pre-Departure Checklist)

🍽 Halal Restaurants — The Korea Tourism Organization's 4 Categories

Category Meaning (Korea Tourism Organization) Alcohol
Halal Certified Certified by a recognized body such as the Korea Muslim Federation (한국이슬람교중앙회) (KMF) —
Self-certified A Muslim owner declares the restaurant halal; every menu item is halal —
Muslim Friendly Only some menu items are halal May be sold
Pork free No halal menu, but no pork is used May be sold
  • Seoul has 1 KMF-certified Korean restaurant (Eid (이드), VisitSeoul). The total number of KMF-certified businesses could not be opened on an official page
  • Ingredients to watch for in Korean food: the seasoning for cabbage kimchi almost always contains salted fermented seafood or fish sauce (VisitKorea). Ask about meat marinades and broths before ordering — for a show-to-the-server card, see #1300 Dietary Request Manual

🕌 Where to Pray

  • Seoul Central Mosque (서울중앙성원): 39 Usadan-ro 10-gil, Yongsan-gu, Seoul, about a 5-minute walk from Itaewon Station (listing information on VisitKorea — the detail page's text did not open, so this was confirmed from search results). For the surrounding streets, see #144 Itaewon & Hannam Guide
  • Incheon Airport Terminal 1 prayer rooms (two): near Gate 24 on the 3rd floor, and in the west transit lounge on the 4th floor. Open 24 hours; inquiries 1577-2600 (Incheon Airport official). They are not labeled as Muslim-only
  • The Korea Tourism Organization encourages tourist sites and local governments to provide prayer spaces, and has distributed prayer mats and qibla compasses to some hotels — ask your hotel in advance

✈ Flights

  • Malaysia Airlines: Its official site lists a direct flight between Seoul (Incheon) and Kuala Lumpur. Operating days and times are not on that page
  • AirAsia X and Korean Air routes, and direct flights from Kota Kinabalu or Penang, could not be confirmed on official pages — search by date on a booking site

👥 Traveling with a Group — Dietary Needs May Differ

According to Malaysia's 2020 census (Department of Statistics Malaysia), the ethnic breakdown is Bumiputera 69.4%, Chinese 23.2%, and Indian 6.7%, and the population by religion is 20.6 million Muslims, 6.1 million Buddhists, 2.9 million Christians, and 2.0 million Hindus. Even within one group, halal, vegetarian, and pork preferences can differ, so choose restaurants using the 4 categories above, matching the strictest person.

⚠ 4 Common Mistakes

1. "It's an exempt country, so I don't need to submit anything" — If you did not obtain a K-ETA, you must still submit the e-Arrival Card (Ministry of Justice)

2. "Malaysia is obviously K-ETA exempt" — The 2026 notice does not list eligible countries. Scan your passport on the application screen to check

3. "Muslim Friendly = halal restaurant" — Only some menu items are halal, and alcohol may be sold. Restaurants with a fully halal menu are Halal Certified and Self-certified (Korea Tourism Organization)

4. "Kimchi is a vegetable side dish" — The seasoning for cabbage kimchi almost always contains salted fermented seafood or fish sauce (VisitKorea)
