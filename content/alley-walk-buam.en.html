Alley Walk ⑤ Buam-dong (부암동) — The First Neighborhood Outside the City Gate, and a Secret Valley at the End of the Alley

Subtitle: Ten minutes by bus from Gyeongbokgung Palace, Seoul suddenly turns into a mountain village. From the Yun Dong-ju Literature Museum past Changuimun Gate into the alleys of Buam-dong, and on to the hidden Baeksasil Valley at the alley's end — about 3 hours, walked alongside the reason a valley where salamanders live has survived right next to downtown. Seoul Alley Walks Expansion 2.

ISSUE · #1344 · 2026-09-02

🎯 The Frame — The First Neighborhood Outside the City Wall

Buam-dong (부암동) is the first neighborhood just outside Changuimun (창의문), the northwestern gate of the Hanyangdoseong (한양도성) fortress wall. Cross a single gate and the bustle inside the wall — Gyeongbokgung Palace (경복궁), Seochon (서촌) — gives way to alleys and mountain slopes. And at the end of those alleys, a valley hides the remains of a Joseon-era noble's retreat. This route is the most "nature-adjacent alley" in the series.

  • Total time: about 3 hours (with the museum and cafés) · Best time: start 10–11 AM — to walk the valley stretch in daylight, finishing at a café in the afternoon
  • Incline: moderate to steep — a mountainside neighborhood plus a downhill valley stretch. Sneakers are a must; the valley path gets slippery after rain.
  • Access: there's no subway station here — the standard route in is Gyeongbokgung Station (경복궁역) Exit 3 → bus toward Jahamun Pass (자하문고개), about 10 minutes.

🚶 The Route — In Walking Order

① Gyeongbokgung Station Exit 3 → Bus → Jahamun Pass Stop (about 10 minutes)

  • The route begins the moment the bus starts climbing the pass — the view outside the window shifts from downtown to a mountain village.

② Jahamun Pass → Yun Dong-ju Literature Museum (윤동주문학관) (about a 2-minute walk)

  • A small literature museum converted from a former water-pumping station. Manuscripts by the poet Yun Dong-ju and an exhibition space built around a well motif — small in size, dense in substance.

③ The Museum → Changuimun Gate (about a 3-minute walk)

  • 📖 Story 1 — The Boundary of a Single Gate: Changuimun is the northwestern gate among the four minor gates of the Hanyangdoseong fortress wall. Inside this gate was the king's city; outside was Buam-dong — being the first neighborhood outside the wall is exactly what has preserved this neighborhood's quiet for centuries. You can walk directly beneath the gate tower.

④ Changuimun Gate → Buam-dong Alleys (about a 15-minute walk)

  • Cafés and small shops sit scattered along the mountainside alleys — Bugaksan (북악산) and Inwangsan (인왕산) mountains take turns appearing over the walls. Since the alleys climb a hill, the view changes every few steps — that's the fun of this stretch.

⑤ Buam-dong Alleys → Baeksasil Valley (백사실계곡, also called Baekseokdongcheon 백석동천) (about a 15-minute walk, downhill)

  • Right where the residential alleys end, a forest and valley suddenly open up.
  • 📖 Story 2 — What This Secret Valley Really Is: Baekseokdongcheon is a designated Scenic Site preserving the remains of a Joseon-era noble's private retreat, and also one of Seoul's Ecological and Landscape Conservation Areas — a rare place where first-class habitat for salamanders and crayfish survives right next to downtown. Stand on the old pond site and the foundation stones, and for a moment it's hard to believe you're still "inside Seoul."

⑥ The Valley → Descend Toward Segeomjeong (세검정) → Finish (about a 15-minute walk)

  • Follow the valley down and you'll reach the Segeomjeong pavilion — the route ends beside the waters of Hongjecheon (홍제천) stream. Take the bus back to Gyeongbokgung Station, or keep walking into Seochon (#014).

🙏 Alley and Valley Manners

  • Baeksasil Valley is a protected area — don't wade into the water, don't flip over rocks (salamanders live under them), and no eating or littering.
  • Buam-dong's alleys are a quiet residential area — don't photograph inside walled properties, and keep your voice down.
  • Don't walk the valley stretch after dark — there's no lighting.

💡 Where You Might Get Thrown Off

  • There's no subway station — even if a map app suggests a 30-minute walking route, the bus is the right answer. Get off at the "Jahamun Pass · Yun Dong-ju Literature Museum" stop.
  • The valley entrance sits tucked between houses, so it's easy to miss at first — follow the arrows on the Baeksasil Valley signposts.
  • Restaurants and convenience stores are scarce — get water and snacks before you start.
