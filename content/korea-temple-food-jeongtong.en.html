Seoul Temple Food and Templestay Guide: Korean Temple Food Culture Experience Center, Templestay Information Center, Jingwansa, Geumseonsa, Gilsangsa and Bongeunsa

🎯 How to Use This Guide

This guide gathers places in Seoul where you can learn about and experience temple food and Templestay. They are an experience center where monks teach temple cooking, an information center that offers Templestay guidance and activities, and four temples in Seoul that run Templestay or education programs. The Korean Temple Food Culture Experience Center is by advance reservation only, and it is closed on Mondays and public holidays.

📍 Learn and Find Out — Korean Temple Food Culture Experience Center and Templestay Information Center

Korean Temple Food Culture Experience Center (한국사찰음식문화체험관) [Yulgok-ro 39 (율곡로 39), Jongno-gu (종로구), ☎ 02-733-4650] is a cultural center that introduces how temple food is cooked. According to the Korea Tourism Organization, it is the first cultural center in Korea specializing in temple food. Monks currently serving as instructors teach temple cooking made with seasonal ingredients and without artificial seasonings. There are programs such as a one-day class where you press dasik (다식) with traditional patterns by hand. All experiences are registered as requiring advance reservation on the official website.

  • Opening hours: 09:00~18:00. Preparation time 11:30~13:00.
  • Closed days: Every Monday, public holidays.
  • Fee: One-day experience 10,000 won~30,000 won per person. Regular courses vary by program.
  • Parking: 1 hour free during classes at the experience center, and the fee after that is registered as "1,500 won per 10."

Templestay Information Center (템플스테이 홍보관) [Ujeongguk-ro 56 (우정국로 56), Jongno-gu, ☎ 02-2031-2000] is a place that gives guidance on Templestay and temple food. It runs activities such as tea talks with monks, coloring traditional patterns and making lotus-flower paper flowers, and introduces Korean culture to foreigners visiting Korea. Templestay is a program where you experience the daily life of a practitioner in a mountain temple, and it began with the 2002 Korea-Japan World Cup. According to the Korea Tourism Organization, about 2 million people have taken part so far.

  • Opening hours: 09:00~18:00. Closed days: New Year's Day, Seollal and Chuseok holidays, statutory public holidays.
  • Parking: Available. Pets: Not allowed.

📍 Temples in Seoul — Jingwansa, Geumseonsa, Gilsangsa and Bongeunsa

Jingwansa (진관사) [Jingwan-gil 73 (진관길 73), Eunpyeong-gu (은평구), ☎ 02-359-8410] is a temple directly administered by the Jogye Order of Korean Buddhism (대한불교조계종). According to the Korea Tourism Organization, together with Buramsa (불암사), Sammaksa (삼막사) and Seunggasa (승가사), it has long been counted as one of the four great temples near Seoul. It was founded by King Hyeonjong (현종) of Goryeo (고려) in 1011 for the Great Master Jingwan (진관대사), and is said to have been reduced to ruins by bombing during the Korean War and then restored. In 2009, in the course of dismantling Chilseonggak (칠성각), more than 20 relics related to the independence movement came to light, including an independence newspaper wrapped in a Taegukgi (태극기). Templestay is registered.

  • Opening hours: 09:00~18:00. Closed days: Open year-round. Fee: Free. Parking: Available.

Geumseonsa (금선사) [Bibong-gil 137 (비봉길 137), Jongno-gu, ☎ 02-395-9911] is a temple in Gugi-dong (구기동), Jongno-gu, on the way up the Bibong course of Bukhansan National Park (북한산국립공원). Past Banyajeon (반야전) there is a pine tree more than 200 years old, and climbing the 108 steps brings you to Daejeokgwangjeon (대적광전), which enshrines the main Buddha. It runs Templestay (reservation information for Korean citizens ☎ 02-395-9955), and it is registered that it can be conducted in English if you apply in advance.

  • Opening hours: Open at all times. Closed days: Open year-round. Fee: Free. Parking: Available.

Gilsangsa (길상사) [Seonjam-ro 5-gil 68 (선잠로5길 68), Seongbuk-gu (성북구), ☎ 02-3672-5945] is a temple at the foot of Samgaksan (삼각산) in Seongbuk-dong (성북동). It began in 1987 when Kim Yeong-han (김영한) was moved by the "Muso-yu" (무소유) thought of Venerable Beopjeong (법정스님) and donated more than 7,000 pyeong of land and more than 40 buildings, and it was founded as Gilsangsa on December 14, 1997. It has Geungnakjeon (극락전), Seolbeopjeon (설법전), Jijangjeon (지장전) and Beomjonggak (범종각), and a statue of Avalokitesvara (관세음보살) by the sculptor Choi Jong-tae (최종태). In 2013 it opened Jinyeonggak (진영각), which displays the belongings and writings of Venerable Beopjeong. It runs education programs at Seolbeopjeon such as a Buddhist college, yoga, sutra copying and Seon meditation.

  • Opening hours: 09:00~18:00. Closed days: Open year-round. Parking: Available.

Bongeunsa (봉은사) [Bongeunsa-ro 531 (봉은사로 531), Gangnam-gu (강남구), ☎ 02-3218-4800] is a temple in Samseong-dong (삼성동), Gangnam-gu. It was founded in 794 by Yeonhwa Guksa (연화국사) under the name Gyeonseongsa (견성사), and in 1498 Queen Jeonghyeon (정현왕후), the later queen consort of Seongjong (성종), greatly rebuilt it and changed its name to Bongeunsa. It runs programs such as Templestay, a Buddhist college and a sutra school.

  • Opening hours: 09:00~18:00. Closed days: Open year-round. Fee: Free. Parking: Available.

⚠ 3 Things to Know Before You Go

  1. The Korean Temple Food Culture Experience Center is by advance reservation only — it is closed on Mondays and public holidays, and 11:30~13:00 is preparation time.
  2. The Templestay Information Center is closed on statutory public holidays — it is also closed on New Year's Day and the Seollal and Chuseok holidays.
  3. Geumseonsa Templestay in English is applied for in advance — the phone number for Templestay reservation information for Korean citizens is registered as 02-395-9955.

⚙ FAQ 2

Q. Can I make temple food myself?

A. According to the Korea Tourism Organization registration, the Korean Temple Food Culture Experience Center has temple cooking taught by monks and a dasik one-day class, and the one-day experience is 10,000 won~30,000 won per person. It is by advance reservation.

Q. Can I do Templestay in a foreign language?

A. Geumseonsa is registered as able to conduct Templestay in English if you apply in advance.
