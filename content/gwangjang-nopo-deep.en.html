Gwangjang Market Guide: Gwangjang Market and Its Food Stalls, the Hanbok Shops, the 365 Market, Dongdaemun Dak-hanmari Alley and Bangsan General Market

Subtitle: Gwangjang Market (a permanent market opened in 1905, varies by shop) · Gwangjang Market Hanbok Shops (09:00~18:00) · Gwangjang Market 365 Market (10:00~19:00, open year-round) · Seoul Dongdaemun Dak-hanmari Alley (Dongdaemun Station Exits 8 and 9) · Bangsan General Market (printing, packaging and craft supplies) — confirmed via Korea Tourism Organization tourism data (TourAPI)

🎯 How to Use This Guide

This guide gathers Gwangjang Market (광장시장) in Jongno, Seoul, along with the alleys and markets registered with it. It covers Gwangjang Market with its food stalls, the hanbok shops and the 365 Market food shop inside it, Dak-hanmari Alley (닭한마리 골목) that used to feed market workers, and Bangsan General Market (방산 종합시장) that sells packaging and craft supplies. 📍 The Market — Gwangjang Market

Gwangjang Market (광장시장) [Changgyeonggung-ro 88 (창경궁로 88), Jongno-gu (종로구), ☎ 02-2267-0291] is a market that opened in 1905. According to the Korea Tourism Organization, it is the first permanent market in Korea. It is described as a market newly opened in Jongno (종로) as an economic breakthrough after Japan took control of the management of Namdaemun Market (남대문시장) following the Eulsa Treaty (을사조약) of 1905.

The name has a story. Originally the plan was to cover the stretch between Gwanggyo (광교) and Janggyo (장교) bridges and make a market there, so it was named Gwangjang (廣長) Market, "wide and long," from the first syllables of the two bridge names. Then, when the site moved to Baeogae (배오개), it kept the same Korean sound but became Gwangjang (廣藏) Market, meaning "gather widely and keep."

At first it mainly traded farm and fishery products and firewood. Now it is a general market that trades silk fabrics, fabrics for hanbok, textiles, women's clothing, curtain fabric, bedding, needlework, najeon-chilgi (mother-of-pearl lacquerware, 나전칠기), kitchenware, fruit and vegetables, dried seafood, fish, meat and vegetables. According to the Korea Tourism Organization, the liveliest spot is the food stall area on the street where East Gate (동문), North Gate 2 (북2문) and South Gate 1 (남1문) meet.

  • Opening hours and closed days: Vary by shop. Parking: Available.

📍 Inside the Market — Hanbok Shops and the 365 Market

Gwangjang Market Hanbok Shops (광장시장 한복매장) [Changgyeonggung-ro 88 (창경궁로 88), Jongno-gu (종로구), ☎ Gwangjang Market Merchants' Federation (광장시장 상인총연합회) 02-2272-0967] deal in traditional hanbok. There are hanbok in many designs and colors, from made-to-order hanbok to everyday hanbok, and they also make hanbok to order.

  • Opening hours: 09:00~18:00. Closed days: Open year-round. Both may differ by shop.
  • Parking: Available. Credit cards: Accepted.
  • Opening date: The registered value is 1904, which conflicts with the description that Gwangjang Market opened in 1905.

Gwangjang Market 365 Market (광장시장 365일장) [Jongno 32-gil 21 (종로32길 21), 1st floor, Jongno-gu (종로구), ☎ 0507-1418-0469] is a food shop made inside the market. It sells local ingredients from across the country, traditional liquor and its own products, and you can pick out ready meals, side dishes for drinking and liquor together. Traditional liquor, local design products and Gwangjang Market goods are registered as items for sale.

  • Opening hours: 10:00~19:00 (varies by store). Closed days: Open year-round. Parking: Not available.

📍 Alley and Market — Dongdaemun Dak-hanmari Alley and Bangsan General Market

Seoul Dongdaemun Dak-hanmari Alley (서울 동대문 닭한마리 골목) [Jongno 5-ga (종로5가), Jongno-gu (종로구), ☎ Dongdaemun Tourist Information Center (동대문관광안내소) 02-2236-9135] is an alley that fed the workers of the Dongdaemun fabric market and Pyeonghwa Market (평화시장). Come out of Exit 8 or 9 of Dongdaemun Station (동대문역) on Subway Line 4, go straight, and turn left past the Industrial Bank of Korea (기업은행) building to reach a one-way street. About halfway along it, the dak-hanmari restaurants are in the alley on the right.

The dish of this alley began with dak-kalguksu (닭칼국수), chicken noodle soup. At first they sold noodle soup with chicken, and the word dak-kalguksu still remains on the signs and windows. According to the Korea Tourism Organization, the oldest of the remaining restaurants is Jin Okhwa Dak-hanmari (진옥화 닭한마리), which was rebuilt in February 2009 after a fire.

Here is how you eat it. Broth is poured into a large bowl (yangpun, 양푼) and a whole chicken is boiled in it. The chicken comes out lightly boiled, but it is described as better to boil it longer on the table. After you pick out the meat and side ingredients such as potatoes and tteok (떡), you put kalguksu noodles into the remaining broth and boil them. What differs from restaurant to restaurant is the dipping sauce and the broth.

  • Opening hours and closed days: Vary by shop. Parking: Not available.

Bangsan General Market (방산 종합시장) [Dongho-ro 37-gil 20 (동호로37길 20), Jung-gu (중구), ☎ Bangsan Market Merchants' Association (방산시장 상인연합회) 02-2266-8765] is a market specializing in industrial supplies. It sells printing, packaging, bookbinding and craft supplies, along with stationery and fancy goods. Small shops are gathered together, so it is described as easy to compare prices.

  • Opening hours and closed days: Vary by store. Parking: Available.

⚠ 3 Things to Know Before You Go

  1. Opening hours differ by shop at Gwangjang Market — no common business hours are registered. The hanbok shops are registered as 09:00~18:00 and the 365 Market as 10:00~19:00.
  2. Parking is not available at the 365 Market and Dak-hanmari Alley — Gwangjang Market, the hanbok shops and Bangsan General Market are registered as having parking.
  3. For dak-hanmari, you put in the kalguksu noodles last — you pick out the meat and side ingredients first.

⚙ FAQ 2

Q. Can I have hanbok made at Gwangjang Market?

A. According to the Korea Tourism Organization registration, the Gwangjang Market Hanbok Shops make hanbok to order, and they open 09:00~18:00 (varies by shop).

Q. How do I get to Dak-hanmari Alley?

A. It is described as follows: come out of Exit 8 or 9 of Dongdaemun Station on Subway Line 4, go straight, turn left past the Industrial Bank of Korea building, and then enter the alley on the right about halfway along that street.
