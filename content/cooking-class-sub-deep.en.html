Why cooking class sub now — Foreign Mania #1 Korean Food Experience

The most sought-after experience by foreigners when traveling to Korea is “cooking your own food.” When eating and making are combined, it becomes memorable. The experience of making kimchi, assembling bibimbap, and stir-frying tteokbokki sauce is a rare category of tourism consumption in that it can be replicated after returning home.

Cooking classes for foreigners in Seoul are largely divided into two types.

  • Fixed location: Recipe-centered progress in studio/research center. Fully equipped with an English guide, completion within 1-2 hours.
  • Market tour linked type: Tour Gwangjang Market, Tongin Market, Bangsan Market, etc. and purchase ingredients and then cook. It takes 3-4 hours and has a high sense of local connection.

The 3 biggest mistakes foreigners make

Mistake 1: Trying to make a same-day reservation

Most cooking classes for foreigners in Seoul are small-scale (4-12 people). During peak season (cherry blossoms in April-May and autumn leaves in September-November), closings are common 1-2 weeks in advance. Book at least 3-5 days in advance, or 2 weeks in advance during peak season.

Mistake 2: Not disclosing allergies or dietary restrictions

Kimchi contains fish sauce (anchovies, shrimp). Whether it is vegan, halal, or gluten-free varies depending on the class. Must be specified when making a reservation. For I Love Hansik, NOW COOKING, and byFood partner classes, vegetarian menu alternatives may be available upon prior request.

Mistake 3: Double booking after booking on the platform

There are cases where the same class is listed repeatedly in Klook, Viator, byFood, and My Real Trip. When making a reservation on each platform, check if it is the same date and the same company. Although the price including platform fees may be 5-15% higher than booking directly with the company, it has the advantage of a clear cancellation and refund policy.

Axis 1: Insadong-based class (3 lines)

1. I Love Hansik (Insadong)

Address: Insa-dong area, Jongno-gu (detailed address provided after reservation)

Menu composition: You can choose between bibimbap, japchae, and pancakes (green onion pancakes or kimchi pancakes). Kimjang Kimchi (November) and Sikhye/Sujeonggwa (December-February) are sometimes added to the seasonal menu.

Cost: ₩65,000~85,000 (including material cost, recipe card, and apron). Insadong hanbok rental package is separate.

Time required: 3 hours (40 minutes market tour + 90 minutes cooking + 30 minutes tasting)

Taught in English: All courses taught in English. Japanese and Chinese can be requested separately (confirm in advance).

Fit for foreigners: The location of Insa-dong itself is included in the tourist route, so travel efficiency is high. It is conducted in small groups (maximum of 8 people), making it closer to 1:1 teaching.

Reservation: Book directly on ilovehansik.com or search on Klook (“I Love Hansik”). Full refund if canceled 48 hours in advance.

Access: 5-minute walk from Exit 6 of Anguk Station, Line 3.

2. Yam Lab / CuisineSeoul

Menu composition: Bibimbap, tteokbokki, green onion pancake. Recipe card (English) provided, level of difficulty that can be reproduced at home after returning home.

Cost: Weekdays ₩45,000 / Weekends/Holidays ₩60,000. Including material costs and cooking utensils.

Time required: 2~2.5 hours (focused on cooking, no market tour)

Taught in English: English-only class.

Foreigner fit: The content is good for the price. “First time cooking class” An introductory level of difficulty suitable for foreigners. Accessible to central Seoul.

Reservation: cuisineseoul.com. Reservation recommended at least 24 hours in advance.

Access: Share your exact location when confirming your reservation on the website. Subway available (recommended to check).

3. Insa-dong intensive 1-day full-day package

In addition to the I Love Hansik above, there are several small-scale Korean food experience spaces in the Insa-dong area. Practical information

Reservation Channel Priority

1. Direct reservation with the company: Best price, cancellation policy negotiable directly. I Love Hansik·CuisineSeoul·NOW COOKING.

2. Klook: Instant confirmation, English customer support, clear cancellation and refund. Smooth overseas card payments.

3. byFood: When you need Japanese language support or when exploring advanced partner classes.

4. My Real Trip: Domestic platform. Class diversity is high, but beware of English filters.

5. Airbnb Experiences: When you want a small private class.

Transportation

  • Insadong-based class: Anguk Station Line 3, Exit 6, 5-10 minute walk.
  • Seodaemun-based class: Seodaemun Station on Line 5 or Ewha Womans University Station on Line 2.
  • Tongin Market: Line 3 Gyeongbokgung Station, Exit 2, 10-minute walk.
  • Other: After confirmation of reservation, the company provides subway station and walking information.

Language support

  • English: 9 out of 12 options available (apply in person or request an interpreter).
  • Japanese: byFood partner, I Love Hansik (request in advance).
  • Chinese: Varies by class. Confirmation required upon reservation.
  • If the language is unclear, classes listed on platforms such as Klook and Viator will clearly indicate whether they are in English.

Payment

  • Most card payments (Visa/Mastercard) are accepted.
  • You can select KRW or USD when making a reservation on the platform (Klook·Airbnb).
  • There are cases where cash is requested when making a reservation directly from the company. Check in advance.

FAQ

Q1. This is my first cooking class. Where should I start?

CuisineSeoul (Yam Lab) weekday classes (₩45,000) are suitable for beginners in terms of price, English, and level of difficulty. If you want to go around Insa-dong first, I Love Hansik.

Q2. Can I learn how to make kimchi separately?

possible. NOW COOKING Studio runs exclusive kimchi making sessions. If you search for “kimchi making class Seoul” on Klook, you will get multiple options. November is kimchi season, so there are more special classes.

Q3. Are there any vegan/vegetarian classes?

I Love Hansik, byFood Some partner classes offer vegetarian options upon prior request. If you explicitly request "vegan" or "no meat, no fish sauce" when making a reservation, you will be informed of availability. Not all classes support, so check is required.

Q4. Can I take a cooking class wearing a hanbok?

I Love Hansik offers packages in conjunction with nearby hanbok rentals. Yanghyangja Food & Coordination also has a hanbok option. If you search for “hanbok cooking class” on Klook, additional options will appear.

Q5. Is it okay to participate alone?

Most classes accept solo participation. It's a small group (4-12 people), so you won't feel awkward even if you're alone. Airbnb Experiences has many classes that allow single-person reservations.

Practical tips for foreigners 5

1. Request a recipe card: Most classes provide English recipe cards. If you don't have it, request a photo of the chalkboard recipe with your smartphone.

2. Note the names of ingredients in Korean: Korean notation is useful when reproducing at a Korean supermarket after returning to Korea.

3. Wear an apron/hair tie: Many places provide these, but if you have long hair, bring your own hair tie.

4. Food allergies must be specified at time of reservation: Salted shrimp, salted anchovy fish, sesame oil, and red pepper are key ingredients in Korean food. If you speak on the day of class, it is difficult to respond.

5. Reservation 1-2 weeks in advance: Earlier during peak season. Same-day reservations are usually not possible.
