Korean culture religion and spirituality explainer

Korea is a religiously free society — 5 types of Buddhism, Catholicism, Protestantism, Shamanism, and Confucianism + temples, sacred sites, and experiences suitable for foreigners

Korea is a pluralistic society without a single official religion. According to Statistics Korea (2024), non-religionists 56%, Protestants 19%, Buddhists 15%, and Catholics 8%. However, the influence of Confucianism and Shamanism remains throughout everyday culture, and temples, cathedrals, churches, and Seonangdang coexist. Meta organizes the religious spaces and experiences that foreign personas encounter in Korea into clusters.

1. Korean religion 5 cluster (foreigner fit)

Buddhism (1,700 years, tradition)

  • Inflow: 4th century Three Kingdoms period (via China)
  • Foreigner fit: ★★★ (temple experience, temple stay, temple food)
  • Representative temples: Bulguksa Temple (Gyeongju, UNESCO, #1115), Haeinsa Temple, Beomeosa Temple, Jogyesa Temple (Seoul)
  • Experience: Temple stay (2 days and 1 night, ₩50,000~120,000, many temples available in English)

Catholic Church (240 years of persecution history)

  • Influx: 1784, late Joseon Dynasty
  • Foreigner fit: ★★ (Pilgrimage to Holy Land, European Catholic Persona)
  • Representative sacred sites: Myeongdong Cathedral (Seoul), Jeondong Cathedral (Jeonju, #1107), Jeoldusan Holy Site, Haemieupseong Fortress
  • Special Point: Korean Catholic Church started on its own without foreign missionaries → evaluated as a unique case in Europe

Protestantism (140 years, modern times)

  • Influx: 1885 (American and British missionaries)
  • Foreigner fit: ★★ (Korean church, American persona)

  • Experience: Visits by general foreigners are limited (tour destination X)

3. Frequently asked questions for foreigners

Q. What is the dress code when visiting temples and churches?

  • Temple: Clothing that is less revealing (shorts and sleeveless are allowed, but refrain from wearing them). Many places to take off your shoes.
  • Catholic Cathedral: Dress appropriately during mass. Weekday visits are free.
  • Palace: Entrance fee waived if you wear Hanbok (Hanbok experience is popular with foreigners)

Q. Can I experience divination and fortune telling in Korea?

Many Saju cafes in Insa-dong, Hongdae, and Gangnam. Some English versions available (check in advance). Price ₩20,000~50,000. The result is light entertainment level.

Q. Can foreigners experience religion?

Mostly OK. Templestay reservations for foreigners-only programs can be made on the temple’s English website (Korea Templestay official).
