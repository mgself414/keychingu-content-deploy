Hongdae Food & Drink Guide — 60 Spots Curated for International Visitors

> Subtitle: Hongdae (홍대) · Yeonnam (연남) · Hapjeong (합정) · Mangwon (망원) Food & Bar Curation — A Deep Dive into Restaurants & Bars from the #158 Comprehensive Guide

🎯 5 Key Tips for International Visitors

1. Yeonnam (연남) · Hapjeong (합정) · Mangwon (망원) = One Hongdae Route: All 4 sub-areas are within a 10–15 min walk from Hongik University Station (홍대입구역). String them together and you can hit 5+ spots in a single outing.

2. Weekday 14:00–17:00 (2–5 PM) Golden Window: Weekends and late nights mean 1-hour+ queues. Foreign-friendly spots are quietest on weekday afternoons — and that's when English service is at its best.

3. Counter Seats & Reservations: Many compact Japanese-style spots are counter-only. Weekday around 19:00 (7 PM) is the sweet spot. English-capable venues accept reservations via Naver Booking or KakaoTalk channel.

4. Foreign Cards Widely Accepted: Hongdae is a top international-visitor corridor, so foreign cards, Samsung Pay, and KakaoPay (간편결제 — Korea's popular mobile payment apps, similar to Apple Pay or Alipay) work at most spots. For nofo (노포, 30+ year traditional restaurants) and tiny independents, carry ₩30,000 (~$22 / ~160 RMB / ~3,200 JPY) in cash just in case.

5. Insta & Photo Routing: Hanok (한옥, traditional Korean wooden house) alleyways, galleries, vintage interiors, and themed cafés are everywhere. For the best shots with fewer crowds, aim for 11:00 AM or after 19:00 (7 PM).

  • Joseon Era (조선시대) — Overpowering vintage Korean interior

> #### 🎯 Spotlight Pick — Sanullim 1992 (산울림1992)

> The essence of a Hongdae (홍대) Korean pub. True to its name, this place has held its ground since 1992 — emotionally tied to the legacy of the old Sanullim (산울림) small theater nearby. The menu skips fusion entirely and goes straight for traditional Korean (한식) foundations, backed by a solid lineup of traditional liquors and yakju (약주, refined Korean rice wine, similar to Japanese sake). Prices run slightly above the neighborhood izakayas, but the place consistently earns the praise: "The food is genuinely good — I'll be back." This is the answer for nights when you're craving real Korean food and want to drink without the noise.

>

> #### 🎯 Spotlight Pick — Jusindang (주신당)

> A cocktail bar built around the concept of Korea's 12 zodiac signs and shamanistic faith (무속신앙). Step inside and you'll wonder if you've walked into the right place — it's dim, mysterious, and immersive. The signature move: cocktails named after each of the 12 zodiac animals — Ja (쥐, Rat), Chuk (소, Ox), In (인, Tiger)… pick the one matching your birth year and drink your sign. One wall is covered in wish notes left by past visitors. Bar food is Korean-based fusion. Open 18:00–02:00 (6 PM–2 AM). Better suited as a second or third stop of the night than a first.

> #### 🎯 Spotlight Pick — Okdongshik (옥동식)

> Opened in 2017 in Seogyo-dong (서교동), this is a dedicated pork gomtang (돼지곰탕, slow-simmered pork bone broth soup — lighter and cleaner than the milky pork soup most people know) specialist. Chef Ok Dong-sik put his own name on the sign. The signature bowl is built on a clear, golden broth simmered from only the fore and hind leg meat of Jirisan Berkshire K (지리산 버크셔K) black pigs — a world away from the cloudy, milky look of regular pork gukbap (국밥, Korean rice soup). It has appeared in the Michelin Guide Bib Gourmand every year since 2018, and in 2023 was one of only a handful of Korean dishes named in the New York Times "Best Food of the Year" list. Seating is counter-only at roughly 10 seats, and the kitchen runs a 100-bowl daily limit — lunch often sells out before noon. If you're going, arrive right at opening or early lunch to be safe.

>

> #### 🎯 Spotlight Pick — Jeongdon (정돈)

> The defining tonkatsu queue in Hongdae (홍대). Rosu-katsu (pork loin cutlet) and hire-katsu (pork fillet cutlet) are the two mains, and the signature is staying moist all the way through despite the generous thickness. The space is small — expect a wait even on weekday lunches, and budget 30–60 minutes on weekends. The spot for anyone wanting to experience Japanese tonkatsu done right, in Seoul.

>

> #### 🎯 Spotlight Pick — Gyodong Jip (교동집)

> A dak-hanmari (닭한마리) and samgyetang (삼계탕, ginseng chicken soup) specialist tucked inside the alleyways of Hongdae (홍대). The course plays out in a rich chicken broth with vegetables, noodles, and dumplings — the proper way to do it. The small space means slow table turnover and longer waits, but one bowl is filling enough that a late lunch or early dinner here easily counts as the meal of the day.
