Walkable Seoul — A Walking Guide for International Tourists in Seoul

⚠ 3 Common Mistakes by International Tourists

1. "Seoul must be explored by car" — Taxi/Uber dependency pattern

This is the most common mistake for first-time international visitors. They primarily use Kakao T (카카오T) or Uber, perceiving subways and walking as secondary. In reality, subway + walking is faster and more accurate than driving for most routes within Seoul. During rush hour (07:00-09:00 AM / 18:00-20:00 PM), a 30-minute car ride on Gangnam-daero (강남대로), Jongno (종로), or Teheran-ro (테헤란로) can be a 15-minute subway ride. Furthermore, with the Climate Card, unlimited transfers for 30 days cost ₩65,000, making it overwhelmingly cost-effective. We recommend getting a Climate Card on your first day and planning your routes around subways and walking.

2. "Walking alone at night is dangerous" — Hotel confinement pattern after 22:00 (10:00 PM)

Global urban safety perceptions are often applied to Seoul. In reality, Seoul boasts one of the lowest crime rates in the OECD, ~1 million CCTVs, and abundant foot traffic at night — areas like Myeongdong (명동), Hongdae (홍대), Itaewon (이태원), Gangnam (강남), and Jongno (종로) are bustling with people and commerce 24 hours a day. International vloggers repeatedly post reviews like "Seoul at 03:00 AM safer than New York at 03:00 PM." If it's your first visit, we recommend experiencing Seoul's nighttime walkability with a stroll through the Myeongdong (명동) → Bukchang-dong (북창동) → Euljiro (을지로) course after 22:00 (10:00 PM). However, exercise caution in isolated alleyways, secluded corners, or dimly lit parking lots, as you would anywhere (common sense applies).

3. "Mountains require a trip to the city outskirts" — Hiking = day trip perception

Many international visitors are unaware that Seoul has mountains within the city. In reality, Bukhansan (북한산), Inwangsan (인왕산), Gwanaksan (관악산), Cheonggyesan (청계산), and Dobongsan (도봉산) are all directly accessible by subway — Inwangsan (인왕산) is a 10-minute walk from Dongnimmun Station (독립문역) or Muakjae Station (무악재역), and Bukhansan Dulegil (북한산 둘레길) Section 1 starts from Ui Station (우이역) (Ui-Sinseol Line (우이신설선)). There's a lack of awareness that a half-day hike + afternoon urban café can naturally fit into an international visitor's itinerary. Even with a hotel base in City Hall (시청) or Jongno (종로), a 09:00 AM hike → return to the city center by 13:00 (1:00 PM) → afternoon city course is entirely feasible.

Seoul's Walkability Landscape

Seoul, with a population of 9.4 million and an area of 605 km², has a higher population density than Tokyo or London, yet its multi-layered grid of 9 subway lines + 600+ bus routes allows access to anywhere in the city center within a short walk and typically one transfer. The 90-minute free transfer policy, 100% English signage, and the unlimited Climate Card (launched in 2024) combine to virtually eliminate travel burden for first-time international visitors.

24/7 Safety goes beyond mere crime rate statistics; it's about the 24-hour activity of people and commercial areas. Major commercial districts like Myeongdong (명동), Hongdae (홍대), Gangnam (강남), Itaewon (이태원), and Jongno (종로) maintain 24-hour convenience stores (편의점), restaurants, and cafés, supported by a network of CCTVs, women's safety escort services, and late-night buses (N-Bus (N버스)). According to the Numbeo City Safety Index, Seoul ranks within the global Top 5 as of 2024.

Urban Nature is Seoul's overwhelming strength. Five major mountains, including Bukhansan National Park (북한산국립공원) (836m), Gwanaksan (관악산) (632m), Inwangsan (인왕산) (338m), Dobongsan (도봉산) (740m), and Cheonggyesan (청계산) (618m), are located within the city limits or walking distance. Cheonggyecheon (청계천) (11km), Jungnangcheon (중랑천), and the four major tributaries of the Han River (한강) flow through the heart of the city. International vloggers repeatedly post reviews like "Seoul is the only mega-city with mountains inside it."

Yajang (야장) culture (outdoor dining/drinking culture) is a signature of Korean nightlife. It's a tradition of enjoying K-BBQ, jeon (전) (Korean savory pancakes), makgeolli (막걸리) (Korean rice wine), and draft beer at outdoor tables in places like Euljiro Nogari Alley (을지로 노가리골목), Jongno 3-ga Pojangmacha Street (종로3가 포장마차거리) (street food tent/stall), Sinchon (신촌), Hongdae (홍대), and Itaewon (이태원). From an international perspective, it's the #1 way to experience "that scene" seen in K-dramas and mukbang videos.

Seoul Dulegil (서울둘레길) (Seoul Trail) is a 156km circular trekking course along the outskirts of Seoul. It's divided into 21 sections, each taking 3-4 hours. It is foreigner-friendly with English signage, stamp tours, and well-equipped rest areas. This urban trail naturally connects five major mountains like Bukhansan (북한산), Gwanaksan (관악산), and Cheonggyesan (청계산) — high-value for trekking fans from Japan and Taiwan.

5 Reasons in Detail

① World-Class Public Transit (대중교통)

Seoul's subway system, with Lines 1-9 + Shinbundang (신분당), Ui-Sinseol (우이신설), Gimpo Gold (김포골드), GTX, and 23 other lines, totaling ~340km, is one of the world's most dense metro grids. All stations feature English, Chinese, and Japanese announcements + automatic voice guidance + English route maps. Transfer points are equipped with walking guides, elevators, and escalators.

The Climate Card (기후동행카드), launched by Seoul City in January 2024, offers unlimited subway, bus, and Ttareungyi (따릉이) (public bicycle sharing service) use for 30 days at ₩65,000. International visitors can also obtain it via mobile or physical card. It is the overwhelming #1 pass for first-time international visitors — more than pays for itself with a stay of 5 days or more.

  • Foreigner Friendliness: ★★★★★ — 100% English, 90-minute free transfer, reasonable price
  • Recommended Use: Incheon Airport (인천공항) → City Center direct (Airport Railroad (공항철도) ₩9,500 (~$7 / ~50 RMB / ~1,000 JPY)) + immediate Climate Card issuance
  • Tip: T-money Card (티머니 카드) and Climate Card are separate. The Climate Card is for Seoul city use only (Incheon Airport (인천공항) and Gyeonggi (경기) buses are separate). For short-term (5 days or less) international stays, a regular T-money Card + top-up might be more cost-effective.

② 24/7 Absolute Safety (24시간 안전)

Seoul ranks within the global Top 5 in the OECD Urban Safety Index and Numbeo Safety Index. The violent crime rate is about 1/3 of the OECD average. ~1 million CCTVs + streetlights at night + a 24-hour convenience store (편의점) grid combine to ensure visibility and foot traffic even in the early hours of the morning.

Additional safety nets include the Women's Safety Escort Service (여성안심귀가서비스) (Ansim-i app (안심이 앱)), Late-Night Bus (심야버스) (N-Bus (N버스), operating 00:00-05:00 AM), and Subway Safety Escort (지하철 안심호송) (staff accompaniment). The "nighttime street scenes" frequently seen by international visitors in K-dramas and mukbang videos are a reflection of the actual safe environment.

  • Foreigner Friendliness: ★★★★★ — 03:00 AM solo walk verified, English 911 = 112
  • Recommended Use: Experience nighttime walkability with a stroll through Myeongdong (명동) → Bukchang-dong (북창동) → Euljiro (을지로) after 22:00 (10:00 PM)
  • Tip: In emergencies, call 112 (Police) / 119 (Fire & Emergency) / 1330 (24-hour English Tourist Information). 1330 supports 11 languages including English, Chinese, Japanese, and Spanish.

③ Urban Nature Integration (도심 속 자연)

Bukhansan National Park (북한산국립공원) (836m, Dobong-gu (도봉구) / Eunpyeong-gu (은평구), etc.) — accessible by foot from Ui Station (우이역), Gupabal Station (구파발역), or Bulgwang Station (불광역). Inwangsan (인왕산) (338m, Jongno (종로)) — a 10-minute walk from Muakjae Station (무악재역) or Dongnimmun Station (독립문역), combining Gichabawi (기차바위) (Train Rock) and Seonbawi (선바위) (Zen Rock) with the Hanyangdoseong (한양도성) (Seoul City Wall) ridge. Gwanaksan (관악산) (632m, Gwanak-gu (관악구)) — accessible by foot + shuttle from Seoul National University Station (서울대입구역). Cheonggyesan (청계산) and Dobongsan (도봉산) are among the five major mountains, all within one subway transfer.

Cheonggyecheon (청계천) (11km, City Hall (시청) ~ Seongdong (성동)) — restored in 2005, a sunken stone stream promenade in the heart of the city. Hangang Park (한강공원) (11 areas, Jamsil (잠실), Banpo (반포), Yeouido (여의도), Ttukseom (뚝섬), Ichon (이촌), etc.) — cycling, yoga, picnics. Seoul Forest (서울숲) (116ha, Seongsu-dong (성수동)) — a deer park in the city center + connected to the Han River (한강).

  • Foreigner Friendliness: ★★★★☆ — 80% English hiking trail signs, no hiking shoe rental
  • Recommended Use: 09:00 AM Inwangsan (인왕산) hike → 12:00 PM Seochon (서촌) lunch → afternoon city course (half-day hike + half-day city)
  • Tip: Hiking attire and shoes are not strictly necessary (sneakers are OK for Inwangsan (인왕산), Cheonggyecheon (청계천), Seoul Forest (서울숲)). For serious Bukhansan (북한산) hikes, hiking shoes are recommended — available at outdoor gear stores in Seoul (e.g., Isu (이수), Jongno (종로)) or global brand stores.

④ Yajang Nightlife Culture (야장 문화)

Euljiro Nogari Alley (을지로 노가리골목) (Euljiro 3-ga (을지로3가)) — outdoor tables for nogari (노가리) (dried pollock snack) and draft beer, a tradition since the 1980s. Signature spots include OB Bear (OB베어) and Manseon Hof (만선호프). Jongno 3-ga Pojangmacha Street (종로3가 포장마차거리) (Nagwon-dong (낙원동) ~ Jongmyo (종묘)) — jeon (전) (Korean savory pancakes), guksu (국수) (noodles), kkochi (꼬치) (skewers) + soju (소주) (Korean distilled spirit) and makgeolli (막걸리) (Korean rice wine). Sinchon (신촌) and Hongdae (홍대) outdoor tables — K-BBQ + beer.

Yajang (야장) is that scene international visitors have seen in K-dramas and mukbang videos — outdoor plastic tables, rows of customers, string lights overhead, and hoeshik (회식) (company dinner/gathering) starting right on the street. English/Japanese/Chinese menus are gradually expanding (as of 2025, over 50% of Nogari Alley (노가리골목) establishments have English menus).

  • Foreigner Friendliness: ★★★★★ — K-drama scene experience, starts after 18:00 (6:00 PM)
  • Recommended Use: Course 3 (DDP → Nogari Alley (노가리골목) → Jongno 3-ga Pocha (종로3가 포차)) or after Course 1, Insadong (인사동) → Jongno 3-ga Pocha (종로3가 포차)
  • Tip: Cash + card combination (some small pocha (포차) only accept cash). 18:00-20:00 (6:00-8:00 PM) is peak time — recommended after 20:00 (8:00 PM) or on weekdays. It's good yajang (야장) etiquette to move to the next spot after 30 minutes to 1 hour, as table turnover is quick.

⑤ Extensive Trail Networks (둘레길)

Seoul Dulegil (서울둘레길) (Seoul Trail) — a total of 156km, 21 sections trekking course connecting the outskirts of Seoul in a loop. It links 8 mountains including Bukhansan (북한산), Dobongsan (도봉산), Suraksan (수락산), Buramsan (불암산), Achasan (아차산), and Gwanaksan (관악산). Completed in 2014, with English signage and stamp tour renewed in 2024.

Each section averages 7-8km, takes 3-4 hours, and ranges from Easy to Moderate difficulty. English signage, QR codes, well-equipped rest areas with restrooms, and water fountains are available. The stamp tour (certificate issued upon collecting 21 stamps) is high-value for international trekking enthusiasts.

  • Foreigner Friendliness: ★★★★☆ — 80% English signage, free guidebooks (Seoul Tourism Plaza (서울관광플라자))
  • Recommended Use: Section 1 (Suyu Station (수유역) → Ui Station (우이역), 8km, 3 hours) or Section 8 (Seoul Forest (서울숲) → Gwangnaru Station (광나루역), 11km, 4 hours)
  • Tip: Before starting, pick up a free English guidebook + stamp book at the Seoul Tourism Plaza (서울관광플라자) (City Hall (시청) / Myeongdong (명동)). Hiking shoes are recommended. Sections 1 and 8 are recommended for first-time international visitors.

4-Axis Mapping — 4 Recommended Walking Courses

  • ① History Course: Gyeongbokgung Palace (경복궁) → Bukchon Hanok Village (북촌한옥마을) → Insadong (인사동) → Cheonggyecheon Stream (청계천) (3-4h, Easy)
  • ② Nature Course: Muakjae Station (무악재역) → Inwangsan Gichabawi (인왕산 기차바위) → Hanyangdoseong (한양도성) → Seochon (서촌) (2.5h, Moderate)
  • ③ Nightlife Course: DDP Dongdaemun Design Plaza (동대문디자인플라자) → Euljiro Nogari Alley (을지로 노가리골목) → Jongno 3-ga Pojangmacha Street (종로3가 포장마차거리) (2-3h, Easy, after 18:00 (6:00 PM))
  • ④ Trendy Course: Seoul Forest (서울숲) → Ttukseom Hangang Park (뚝섬한강공원) → Seongsu Café Street (성수동 카페거리) (3h, Easy)

Gyeongbokgung Palace (경복궁) → Bukchon Hanok Village (북촌한옥마을) → Insadong (인사동) → Cheonggyecheon Stream (청계천)

Meta

  • Duration: 3-4 hours
  • Difficulty: ★☆☆☆☆ Easy (flat, hanbok (한복) rental available)
  • Recommended Start Time: 10:00 AM (Gyeongbokgung (경복궁) Royal Guard Changing Ceremony at 10:00 AM / 14:00 PM)
  • Foreigner Friendliness: ★★★★★

Highlights

  • Gyeongbokgung (경복궁) Royal Guard Changing Ceremony + free admission when renting a hanbok (한복) (traditional Korean attire)
  • Bukchon (북촌) Eight Scenic Views photo spots + hanok (한옥) (traditional Korean house) architectural scenery (residential area — please be mindful of noise)
  • Insadong (인사동) traditional snacks/tea, calligraphy, souvenir alleys (골목) (narrow streets/lanes)
  • Cheonggyecheon (청계천) sunken stone stream — a riverside promenade one level below the city center

Practical Tips

  • Gyeongbokgung (경복궁) Admission: Opens 09:00 AM, closed Tuesdays. Admission fee of ₩3,000 (~$2 / ~16 RMB / ~320 JPY) waived if wearing a hanbok (한복).
  • Bukchon (북촌): As it's a residential area, please be quiet outside of 11:00 AM - 17:00 PM / no loud noises / take photos only at the designated 8 scenic spots.
  • Insadong (인사동): Car-free street on Saturdays and Sundays (11:00 AM - 22:00 PM) — recommended time for walking.
  • Cheonggyecheon (청계천): Various sections for walking, including Gwanggyo (광교) ~ Dumul-dari (두물다리) ~ Muhakgyo (무학교) ~ Biudanggyo (비우당교). Cool in summer.
