⚠ 3 mistakes foreigners often make

1. "You have to travel to Seoul by car" — Taxi/Uber dependency pattern

The most common mistakes foreigners make when visiting for the first time. Kakao T and Uber are installed as main routes, and subway and walking are recognized as auxiliary routes. In reality, for most sections of downtown Seoul, subway + walking is faster and more accurate than driving. Commuting hours (7-9:00, 18-20:00) Gangnam-daero, Jongno, and Teheran-ro are 30 minutes by car and 15 minutes by subway. In addition, Unlimited transfers are available for 65,000 won for 30 days with the Climate Card, so the cost is also overwhelming. After issuing the Climate Card from the first day, it is recommended to design circulation around subway + walking.

2. "It's dangerous to walk alone at night" — Hotel stay-at-home pattern after 22:00

Global city safety awareness applies to Seoul. In reality, Seoul has the lowest crime rate in OECD + ~1 million CCTVs + abundant people at night — Myeong-dong, Hongdae, Itaewon, Gangnam, and Jongno area are crowded and commercial areas active 24 hours a day. Foreign vloggers repeatedly posted reviews saying “Seoul at 3 AM safer than New York at 3 PM”. If this is your first visit, we recommend taking the Myeong-dong → Bukchang-dong → Euljiro walking course after 22:00 to experience night walkability. However, be careful wherever you are (at the level of common sense), such as at the end of a bar alley, remote alleys, or back parking lots.

3. "You have to go to the outskirts of the city to go to the mountains" — Hiking = day trip recognition

Many foreigners do not know that there are mountains in downtown Seoul. In reality, Bukhansan Mountain, Inwangsan Mountain, Gwanaksan Mountain, Cheonggyesan Mountain, and Dobongsan Mountain are all directly accessible by subway — Inwangsan Mountain is a 10-minute walk from Dongnimmun Station and Muakjae Station, and section 1 of the Bukhansan Trail begins at Ui Station (Ui Sinseol Line). Half-day hiking + afternoon city cafe can naturally fit into a foreigner's schedule, but there is no awareness. Even from the City Hall/Jongno Hotel base, hiking at 09:00 → returning to the city center at 13:00 → afternoon downtown course is fully possible.

Seoul’s walkability landscape

Seoul has a population of 9.4 million + an area of 605㎢, making it more densely populated than Tokyo and London, but multi-level grid of subway line 9 + 600+ bus routes makes it accessible from anywhere in the city on foot and within 1 transfer. The 90-minute free transfer policy + 100% English guidance + Climate Card (launched in 2024) unlimited pass combines to reduce the travel burden for first-time foreign visitors to virtually 0**.

24-hour safety goes beyond simple crime rate figures and 24-hour activity of people and commercial areas is the key. Major commercial districts such as Myeongdong, Hongdae, Gangnam, Itaewon, and Jongno maintain 24-hour convenience stores, restaurants, and cafes, and are supported by CCTV, women's safe return service, and late-night bus (N Bus) networks. According to the Numbeo City Safety Index, Seoul is ranked in the global top 5 as of 2024.

Nature in the city is Seoul’s overwhelming strength. Five famous mountains, including Bukhansan National Park (836m), Gwanaksan Mountain (632m), Inwangsan Mountain (338m), Dobongsan Mountain (740m), and Cheonggyesan Mountain (618m), are located within the city limits or within walking distance. The four major streams of Cheonggyecheon (11km), Jungnangcheon, and Han River pass through the center of the city. Foreign vloggers repeatedly post reviews saying “Seoul is the only mega-city with mountains inside it”.

Night market culture is a signature of Korean nightlife. The tradition of enjoying K-BBQ, pancakes, makgeolli, and draft beer in an outdoor atmosphere at street tables in Euljiro Nogari Alley, Jongno 3-ga Street, Sinchon, Hongdae, and Itaewon — the #1 route where you can directly experience “the scenery” seen in K-drama and mukbang videos from a foreigner’s perspective.

Seoul Trail (Seoul Trail) is a 156km fantastic trekking course along the outskirts of Seoul. Divided into 21 sections, each section lasting 3 to 4 hours. Friendly for foreigners with English signs, stamp tours, and rest areas. An urban trail that naturally connects the five famous mountains including Bukhansan Mountain, Gwanaksan Mountain, and Cheonggyesan Mountain — high-value even for Japanese and Taiwanese trekking fans.

5 reasons details

① World-Class Public Transit

Seoul Subway has 23 lines, including Lines 1 to 9 + Shinbundang, Uisinseol, Gimpo Gold, and GTX, and has a total operating distance of ~340km, one of the densest metro grids in the world. Information in English, Chinese, and Japanese at all stations + automatic voice + English route map. Equipped with walking guide signs, elevators, and escalators when transferring.

Climate Card will be launched by Seoul City in January 2024 - Unlimited subway, bus, and shared bike rides for 65,000 won on the 30th. Foreigners can also issue mobile or physical cards. Overwhelmingly 1st priority pass for first-time foreigners — Staying for more than 5 days is enough to make up for it.

  • Foreigner Friendliness: ★★★★★ — 100% English, free transfer 90 minutes, reasonable price
  • Recommended Use: Incheon Airport → Direct connection to downtown (Airport Railroad 9,500 won) + Immediate issuance of Climate Card
  • Tip: T-money card + Climate Card are separate. Climate Card is for use within Seoul only (separately for Incheon Airport and Gyeonggi buses). For foreigners short-term stay (less than 5 days), regular T-money + recharge is more cost-effective.

② 24/7 Absolute Safety

In the OECD Urban Safety Index and Numbeo Safety Index, Seoul ranks in the global top 5. The violent crime rate is one third of the OECD average. ~1 million CCTVs + night street lights + 24-hour convenience store grid combine to secure people and visibility even during the early morning hours.

Auxiliary safety nets such as Women's safe return service (Ansimi app), Night bus (N bus, runs from midnight to 05:00), Subway safe convoy (accompanied by station staff). The “night street scenes” that foreigners often see in K-drama and mukbang videos are a reflection of actual safe scenes.

  • Foreigner friendliness: ★★★★★ — solo walk 3 AM verification, English 911 = 112
  • Recommended use: Experience night walkability with a walking course in Myeong-dong, Bukchang-dong, and Euljiro after 22:00
  • Tip: In case of emergency, call 112 (police) / 119 (fire/emergency) / 1330 (24-hour English tourist information). 1330 supports 11 languages, including English, Chinese, Japanese, and Spanish.

③ Urban Nature Integration (Nature in the city)

Bukhansan National Park (836m, Dobong-gu, Eunpyeong-gu, etc.) — Walkable from Ui Station, Gupabal Station, and Bulgwang Station. Inwangsan (338m, Jongno) — 10-minute walk from Muakjae Station and Dongnimmun Station, combined with Train Rock, Seonbawi Rock and Hanyangdoseong Ridge. Gwanaksan (632m, Gwanak-gu) — Walk + shuttle from Seoul National University Station. The five famous mountains including Cheonggyesan Mountain and Dobongsan are all within one subway transfer.

Cheonggyecheon (11km, City Hall~Seongdong) — Restored in 2005, a sunken stone stream walking trail in the middle of the city. Hangang Park (11 regions, Jamsil, Banpo, Yeouido, Ttukseom, Ichon, etc.) — Bicycle, yoga, picnic. Seoul Forest (116ha, Seongsu-dong) — Deer park in the city + connected to the Han River.

  • Foreigner Friendliness: ★★★★☆ — 80% of hiking trail signs are in English, no hiking shoes available for rental.
  • Recommended Use: Inwangsan Mountain Climbing at 09:00 → Seochon Lunch at 12:00 → Afternoon City Course (Half-day hiking + Half-day downtown)
  • Tip: Hiking clothes and hiking shoes are not required (sneakers are OK for Inwangsan Mountain, Cheonggyecheon Stream, and Seoul Forest). Hiking shoes are recommended for serious Bukhansan Mountains - Mountaineering equipment stores (Isu, Jongno) or global brand stores in Seoul.

④ Yajang Nightlife Culture

Euljiro Nogari Alley (Euljiro 3-ga) — Nogari (dried pollack) + draft beer outdoor tables that have been around since the 1980s. Signatures such as OB Bear·Manseon Hof. Jongno 3-ga food stall street (Nakwon-dong ~ Jongmyo) — Pancakes, noodles, skewers + soju, makgeolli. Sinchon·Hongdae street table — K-BBQ + beer.

The night scene is the scene foreigners see in K-drama and mukbang videos — outdoor plastic tables, customers lined up in a row, overhead light bulbs, and a company dinner starting in the middle of the street. EN/JP/CN menu gradually spread (50%+ of EN menu stores in Nogari Alley by 2025).

  • Foreigner friendliness: ★★★★★ — K-drama scenery experience, starts after 18:00
  • Recommended use: Course 3 (DDP → Nogari Alley → Jongno 3-ga Pocha) or after course 1, Insa-dong → Jongno 3-ga Pocha
  • Tip: Cash + card in combination (Small gun carriages have cash-only stores). Peak between 18:00 and 20:00 — Recommended after 20:00 or on weekdays. As the store turnover is fast, it is good manners to clean up within 30 minutes to 1 hour and then move on to the next store.

⑤ Extensive Trail Networks

Seoul Trail (Dulegil) — A trekking course that connects the outskirts of Seoul total 156km, 21 sections. It connects eight mountains including Bukhansan Mountain, Dobongsan Mountain, Suraksan Mountain, Bulamsan Mountain, Achasan Mountain, and Gwanaksan Mountain. Completed in 2014, renewed with English cover and stamp tour in 2024.

Each section averages 7~8km, takes 3~4 hours, and difficulty level is Easy~Moderate. Fully equipped with English sign, QR code, restroom, and drinking fountain. Stamp Tour (Certificate issued when collecting 21 stamps) — High-value for foreign trekking enthusiasts.

  • Foreigner Friendliness: ★★★★☆ — 80% English covers, free guidebook (Seoul Tourist Plaza)
  • Recommended use: Section 1 (Suyu Station → Ui Station, 8km, 3 hours) or Section 8 (Seoul Forest → Gwangnaru Station, 11km, 4 hours)
  • Tip: Pick up a free English guidebook + stamp book at Seoul Tourist Plaza (City Hall, Myeongdong) before the start. Hiking shoes recommended. Section 1 + Section 8 is recommended for foreigners to try for the first time.

① History Course — Time Travel & Tradition

root

Gyeongbokgung Palace → Bukchon Hanok Village → Insadong → Cheonggyecheon Stream

meta

  • Time required: 3~4 hours
  • Difficulty level: ★☆☆☆☆ Easy (flat ground, Hanbok rental available)
  • Recommended start time: 10:00 (Gyeongbokgung Palace Guard Changing Ceremony 10:00, 14:00)
  • Foreigner friendliness: ★★★★★

Highlights

  • Gyeongbokgung Palace Changing of the Guards Ceremony + Entrance fee waived for Hanbok rental
  • Bukchon 8 photo spots + Hanok architectural scenery (residential area — be careful of noise)
  • Insadong Traditional refreshments, calligraphy, and souvenirs alley
  • Cheonggyecheon sunken stone stream — Waterside promenade below level 1 in the city center

Practical tips

  • Gyeongbokgung Palace admission: Opens at 09:00, closed on Tuesdays. Admission fee of 3,000 won is waived if you wear Hanbok.
  • Bukchon: As it is a residential area, be quiet outside of 11:00 to 17:00 / No loud noises / Photos are only taken at the designated 8th view.
  • Insa-dong: Car-free street on Saturdays and Sundays (11-22 p.m.) — Recommended walking times
  • Cheonggyecheon: You can walk at each stage, including Gwanggyo ~ Dumul Bridge ~ Mukyo ~ Biudang Bridge. Cool when summer is hot
  • Budget: Free (Hanbok rental 15,000~30,000 won / Lunch Insa-dong Korean food 15,000~25,000 won)

② Nature Course — Fortress Wall & Sunset Views

root

Muakjae Stn → Inwangsan Train Rock → Hanyangdoseong / Seoul City Wall → Seochon Alleys

meta

  • Duration: 2.5 hours
  • Difficulty level: ★★★☆☆ Moderate (hiking shoes recommended, some steep sections)
  • Recommended start time: 15:00 (1.5~2h before sunset — sunset at the summit)
  • Foreigner friendliness: ★★★★☆

Highlights

  • 360-degree panorama of downtown Seoul + view of the Han River and N Seoul Tower from the summit of Inwangsan Mountain Train Rock
  • Hanyangdoseong Ridge — Trekking on the stone ridge of the 600-year-old Joseon City Wall
  • Seochon Alley — Tongin Market + Cafe·Gallery + Yun Dong-ju Literary Museum

Practical tips

  • Starting point: Muakjae Station, Line 3, Exit 1 → 10-minute walk → Inwangsan Mountain hiking trail entrance
  • Equipment: Sneakers OK (hiking shoes are safer), water and snacks required, hat and sunscreen
  • Time adjustment: Reverse sunset time — 18:00~19:00 in spring and fall, 19:00~20:00 in summer, 17:00~18:00 in winter
  • After descending the mountain: Seochon Tongin Market → Dosirak Cafe (5,000 won lunchbox) or Hyoja-dong/Changseong-dong cafe
  • Budget: Free hiking / Seochon cafe/meal 10,000~20,000 won

③ Nightlife Course — Neon Lights & Outdoor Beer

root

DDP Dongdaemun Design Plaza → Euljiro Nogari Alley → Jongno 3-ga Pojangmacha Street

meta

  • Time required: 2~3 hours (3~5 hours including eating and drinking)
  • Difficulty: ★☆☆☆☆ Easy (flat ground, all within walking distance of subway)
  • Recommended start time: After 18:00 (DDP night lighting/Nogari Alley peak 19:00~21:00)
  • Foreigner friendliness: ★★★★★

Highlights

  • DDP: Zaha Hadid design, futuristic architecture + LED rose garden night lighting
  • Nogari Alley: Night market since the 1980s — OB Bear, Manseon Hop, Nogari + Draft Beer Outdoor Table
  • Jongno 3-ga Pocha: Jeon, noodles, skewers + soju, makgeolli, just like the K-drama scene

Practical tips

  • DDP night lighting: Turns on 30 minutes after sunset, best view until 22:00
  • Nogari Alley Entry: After 18:00, Turns on quickly after 20:00 — Turns around 30 minutes to 1 hour per store
  • Menu recommendation: Draft beer + Nogari (4,000~7,000 won) + Hwayangjeok, Jeon, Skewers
  • Payment: Many stores accept credit cards at Nogari Alley, Cash recommended at Jongno 3-ga Pocha
  • Language: Nogari Alley EN menu 50%+, Jongno 3-ga Pocha points at photo + hand gesture OK
  • Budget: KRW 25,000~40,000 per person (when hopping to 3 stores)

④ Trendy Course — Hip Seoul & Forest Healing

root

Seoul Forest → Ttukseom Hangang Park → Seongsu Café Street

meta

  • Duration: 3 hours
  • Difficulty level: ★☆☆☆☆ Easy (flat, bicycle rental available)
  • Recommended start time: 14:00 (walk after lunch + cafe closes at 21:00)
  • Foreigner friendliness: ★★★★★

Highlights

  • Seoul Forest Deer Park + 116ha of green space in the city center
  • Ttukseom Hangang Park Walk along the Hangang River + bike (Ttareungi 1,000 won for 1 hour)
  • Seongsu-dong Cafe Street — Korea’s trendiest cafes, select shops, and galleries are concentrated

Practical tips

  • Entering Seoul Forest: 5-minute walk from Exit 3 of Seoul Forest Station on the Bundang Line (or Exit 8 of Ttukseom Station)
  • Deer Park: Deer feeding is limited to set times only - 11:00, 14:00, 16:00
  • Ttukseom Bicycle: Ttareungi (shared bike) Climate Card Unlimited — Hangang Bicycle Path 30-minute course
  • Seongsu-dong Cafe: Daelim Warehouse, Mesh Coffee, Blue Bottle Seongsu, London Bagel Seongsu, etc. — Quiet on weekdays from 13:00 to 18:00
  • Connecting course: Connects all 3 spots in a 30-minute walk or 10-minute walk to Ttareungi
  • Budget: Seoul Forest free / bicycle 1,000 won / cafe 7,000~12,000 won

Essential Tourist Tips — Practical Information

Transportation/Payment

  • Climate Card — 65,000 won for 30 days, unlimited subway, bus, and Ttareungi (only within Seoul)
  • T-money — For short-term stays, issued/recharged at convenience stores and stations
  • Airport → Downtown: Airport Railroad (Seoul Station 9,500 won, 43 minutes) / Limousine No. 6001 (15,000 won, 1h)
  • Taxi app: Kakao T (English available), Uber

Emergency/Information

  • 112 — Police
  • 119 — Fire/Emergency
  • 1330 — 24-hour tourist information in 11 languages, including English, Chinese, Japanese, and Spanish (linked to BBB call center)
  • 120 — Seoul Dasan Call Center (living complaints)

Weather/Season

  • Spring March~May: 10~22 degrees, cherry blossoms (early/mid April) — Best walking season
  • Summer from June to August: 22 to 33 degrees, rainy season from late June to July. Yajang/Hangang Peak
  • September to November Fall: 10 to 22 degrees Celsius, fall foliage (late October to early November) — Best walking season
  • Winter from December to February: -10 to 5 degrees, snow and strong winds. Hiking not recommended, focused on urban areas and indoor areas

Toilets, water, and amenities

  • Public restrooms: Free at all subway stations + major parks + tourist attractions + English signs
  • Convenience stores open 24 hours: GU, CU, 7-Eleven, E-Mart 24 — 1 to 3 at each subway station. Water, snacks, umbrella, hot pack
  • Free wifi: Seoul public wifi (Seoul WiFi) in all major locations
  • Pharmacy: 09:00~22:00, closed on Sundays - Jongno Pharmacy is open 24 hours a day

Hanbok rental (optional)

  • Gyeongbokgung Palace/Bukchon: KRW 15,000~30,000 / 1~4 hours — Entrance fee waived
  • Hanboknam, Oya Hanbok, etc. — English response, sizes compatible with foreigners
