Nowon Gyeongchun Line Forest Trail, in the Evening — From the Old Hwarangdae Station to the Gyeongchun Bridge Fountain, 6.8 km of Retired Railway Walked West

Subtitle: Seoul has two retired-railway parks: Gyeongui Line Forest Trail (#491), finished 2016, and Gyeongchun Line Forest Trail, fully connected only in June 2026. This piece walks the eastern trail at dusk — 1939 station lights, an evening market, an 8:30 pm bridge fountain. About 3 hours, Tue–Sun only.

ISSUE · #1363 · 2026-09-07

🎯 The Frame — The Eastern Retired Line, Best After Dark

Foreign visitors already know the Gyeongui Line Forest Trail (경의선숲길) in Hongdae (홍대) and Yeonnam (연남). Seoul has a second one like it. Crossing Nowon-gu (노원구), at the city's northeastern edge, runs the Gyeongchun Line Forest Trail (경춘선숲길) — a 6.8 km linear park built on the trackbed of the Gyeongchun Line (경춘선), which stopped carrying trains in 2010. It's a little longer than the Gyeongui Line trail, and it finished ten years later. Only on June 29, 2026 did the last 870 m open, joining Gwangun University Station (광운대역) to Hwarangdae into one continuous line.

This trail is better in the evening than by day. At the eastern end, the old station house at Hwarangdae Railroad Park (화랑대 철도공원) lights up 30 minutes before sunset; at the western end, the fountain at Gyeongchun Railway Bridge (경춘철교) runs at 7:30 pm and 8:30 pm. Between the two sits the evening market of Gongneung-dong (공릉동). So this piece walks east to west — starting at the lighting, ending at the fountain.

  • Total time: about 3 hours (roughly 6 km of walking, about 1.5 hours, plus time spent stopping — segment minutes are estimates)
  • Best time: Tuesday–Sunday, departing 30 minutes before sunset. In early September, Seoul's sunset is around 6:50 pm, so leave Hwarangdae Station (화랑대역) around 5:45 pm; by late October, sunset shifts to around 5:30 pm, so move an hour earlier. On Monday, both the light garden and the music fountain are closed.
  • Incline: none — flat, since it follows an old rail line, with separate bike and pedestrian paths

Why the Gyeongchun Line Forest Trail — The Newly Completed Eastern Version

The Gyeongchun Line is the Seoul–Chuncheon (춘천) railway, laid in 1939. When the route was relocated in 2010, the Nowon-gu section was retired; park construction began in 2013, and about 6 km opened first in 2017. What remained was an 870 m stretch on the Wolgye-dong (월계동) side — when it opened on June 29, 2026, the 6.8 km from the Gwangun University Station pedestrian overpass, past Gyeongchun Railway Bridge and Gongneung-dong, to Hwarangdae connected without a break for the first time. The music fountain on Gyeongchun Railway Bridge opened the same day, and a café on the bridge opened two weeks later. In short, this is a trail completed in 2026.

If the Gyeongui Line Forest Trail (6.3 km, completed 2016) grew up alongside the café streets of Hongdae and Yeonnam, the Gyeongchun Line trail is a residential trail running between apartment blocks, schools, and markets. It's not a tourist attraction — it's a park the neighborhood actually uses, and for foreign visitors, that's exactly the draw.

📖 Story 1 — Walking the Line Where Trains Once Ran: the old Hwarangdae Station (옛 화랑대역) house opened as "Taereung Station" (태릉역) in July 1939, was renamed Hwarangdae Station in 1958, and kept receiving trains until 2010. It's the last small station building left standing in Seoul — a Registered National Cultural Heritage (designated 2006). Trains still stand on the tracks in front of the station today, though they no longer move; they're for display. Tonight, you'll walk the ground where those tracks once carried them.

🚶 The Course — From the Eastern End to the Western End

The starting point is Exit 4 of Hwarangdae Station on Line 6. It's easy to confuse with the old station house, since both share the name "Hwarangdae" — the subway station and the old station building are 900 m apart, about a 15-minute walk.

① Hwarangdae Station Exit 4 → Hwarangdae Railroad Park (🚶 about 15 min)

  • Head east along Hwarang-ro (화랑로). At the park entrance stands the old Hwarangdae Station house (single-story wood, 1939), with display trains on the tracks in front — a narrow-gauge steam locomotive and carriages, a streetcar from overseas, and more (exact lineup per on-site signage). Inside, the station house is used as an exhibition space.
  • Arrive before sunset to see the trains and the station house once in daylight. Nearby are a gallery converted from shipping containers, a clock museum housed in a retired railcar, and a train-shaped café — hours vary by facility, so check on site.

② Nowon Light Garden Lighting (staying about 30 min)

  • The Nowon Light Garden (노원불빛정원) is a lighting display across the railroad park grounds. Tuesday–Sunday, it lights up 30 minutes before sunset and switches off at 10 pm. Closed Monday, free, and closed in bad weather. The lighting time shifts with the seasons, so remember it as "30 minutes before sunset," not a fixed clock time.
  • The moment the lights come on is this course's first climax. Take your photos here, once the lights rise over the trains and the station house, then leave within 30 minutes — the fountain at the western end starts at 8:30 pm.

③ Railroad Park → Gyeongchun Line Forest Trail, Westbound → Gongneung-dong (🚶 about 40 min · estimated)

  • Get on the forest trail heading in the old track's direction — west. Pedestrian and bike paths are separated, with apartments, schools, and houses on both sides. Walk at the same pace as the neighborhood residents out for a stroll.
  • 📖 Story 2 — A Pair of Retired Lines: Seoul turned two railway lines that once ran through the city into parks, ten years apart. The western Gyeongui Line trail in 2016, the eastern Gyeongchun Line trail in 2026. Both kept the exact width the trains once ran through, so they're narrow and long. Where the Hongdae side became a trail for cafés and travelers, this one stayed a trail for strollers and bicycles — the same design, a different neighborhood.

④ Gongneung-dong Dokkaebi Market in the Evening (🚶 about 10 min + staying about 20 min)

  • This permanent traditional market — the Gongneung-dong Dokkaebi Market (공릉동도깨비시장) — is 7 minutes from Exit 2 of Gongneung Station (공릉역) on Line 7. It's open daily, 8 am to 10 pm, so it's still going in the evening (no confirmed full closing day — many stalls close on the day of major holidays). Do a loop through the snack, side-dish, and fruit alleys for a light dinner. No store names are listed here — the market itself is the destination.
  • If you're short on time, end here: Gongneung Station is right next to it (Course A closes here). You can see the fountain another day as a stand-alone 30-minute visit from Wolgye Station (월계역).

⑤ Gongneung → Gyeongchun Railway Bridge in Wolgye-dong (🚶 about 45–50 min · estimated)

  • Get back on the forest trail heading west. This stretch is the longest and the darkest — nighttime lighting for the whole trail hasn't been confirmed (only the railroad park section is confirmed), so where there are no streetlights, walk the side that runs alongside the main road. The goal is to reach the bridge by 8:15 pm.

⑥ Gyeongchun Railway Bridge Music Fountain, 8:30 pm — Finish (about 20 min + 🚶 about 10 min)

  • On the old Gyeongchun railway bridge crossing Jungnangcheon Stream (중랑천). Opened June 30, 2026, running through October 31: Tuesday–Sunday, two shows a day at 7:30 and 8:30 pm, about 20 minutes each, closed Monday and in rain. A fountain show using lasers and mirrors unfolds over the water beneath the bridge. On the bridge is a train-shaped café (open March–November, 9 am–9 pm, closed Tuesday — a different day off than the fountain), a good spot to wait for the show.
  • Finish: about a 10-minute walk (about 800 m) to Exit 4 of Wolgye Station on Line 1. There's also an approach from the Gwangun University Station side (a pedestrian overpass), though the distance hasn't been confirmed.

🙏 Etiquette for This Course — A Park in a Residential Area

  • Houses line both sides of the trail the whole way. Don't shout at night — use earphones for music.
  • Pedestrian and bike paths are separated. Don't stand in the bike lane to take photos.
  • At the market, don't photograph products or people head-on. Don't touch items before buying.
  • Don't climb onto the light garden's or the bridge's railings. Where the fountain spray reaches, stand in the back row, not the front.
  • Carry your trash out. Trash cans are scarce in the park.

💡 Common Points of Confusion

  • The course doesn't work on Mondays: both the light garden and the music fountain are closed Monday. Taereung and Gangneung Royal Tombs (태릉·강릉) are also closed Monday.
  • Hwarangdae Station ≠ the old Hwarangdae Station: from subway Line 6 Hwarangdae Station (Seoul Women's University entrance) to the old station house is 900 m, 15 minutes. Take Exit 2 to head straight to the trail (8–10 min), or Exit 4 for the railroad park.
  • The lighting time changes daily: since it's "30 minutes before sunset," that's around 6:20 pm in early September and around 5 pm in late October. Check that day's sunset time before you set out.
  • The fountain runs only through October 31: from November on, ⑥ drops out of the course — at that point, cut the course in half and do just the Hwarangdae light garden (open to 10 pm).
  • Both close in the rain: the light garden closes in bad weather, the fountain closes when it rains. An indoor alternative is #280.
  • Cash is easier at the market: many stalls take cards, but cash is better for small amounts (#1301). Transit cards are covered at #1298.

FAQ 6

Q1. Do I have to walk the full 6 km?

  • No. There's a shorter version that ends at ④ Gongneung-dong (about 1.5 hours). You can see the fountain separately, in 30 minutes, from Wolgye Station.

Q2. How is it different from the Gyeongui Line Forest Trail?

  • The lengths are similar (6.3 km vs. 6.8 km), but the character differs. The Gyeongui Line trail is a café street; the Gyeongchun Line trail is a residential park. And only this one has the old station house, display trains, and a fountain on a bridge.

Q3. What if I go during the day?

  • During the day, you could add Taereung and Gangneung Royal Tombs beforehand — Joseon-dynasty royal tombs, 9 am–6 pm (based on Sep–Oct hours), closed Monday, 1,000 won, near Taereung Station (태릉입구역) on Line 6. But this piece is an evening piece, so it isn't included in the course.

Q4. With kids?

  • Flat, free, and restrooms are available at the park and the market. Kids tend to like the display trains and the fountain even more than adults do. But section ⑤ is long, so with a stroller, finish at ④.

Q5. How much does it cost?

  • Admission: 0 won. Dinner at the market runs around 10,000 won, plus one-way transit fare. This is one of the cheapest evening courses in Seoul.

Q6. Will it feel awkward as a foreign visitor?

  • It's a neighborhood, not a tourist site. English signage is sparse. But nobody here looks at you as a tourist — that's the appeal of this trail.
