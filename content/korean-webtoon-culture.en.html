Korean Webtoon Culture — How Vertical-Scroll Comics from Korea Took Over the World

> Webtoon (웹툰), Vertical Scroll, Naver Webtoon (네이버웹툰), Kakao (카카오), Wait-to-Free (기다리면 무료), Webtoon-Based Dramas — A Korean webtoon culture explainer for foreigners. The 'new comic format Korea created,' broken down by 'why · what · how,' all the way to ★its light and shadow.

If you've watched 〈Hellbound〉, 〈Itaewon Class〉, or 〈Moving〉 on Netflix — you've actually already stepped into the world of Korean webtoons (웹툰). All of these dramas are based on webtoons. When 〈Solo Leveling〉 blew up worldwide as an anime, its starting point was also a Korean webtoon.

A webtoon (웹툰 = web + cartoon) is a vertical-scroll digital comic that Korea created. It's neither Japanese manga (만화, 漫画) read in paper books, nor American comics (코믹스) flipped page by page. Fitted to the smartphone screen, you read it by swiping your finger from top to bottom — a full-color, entirely new format. A comic born for the screen rather than for paper from the very start — which is why it's called 'digital-native (디지털 네이티브).' This article isn't "which titles are fun" (a recommendation list), but explains why the webtoon format is special, how it captured the world, and what shadow lies behind the glamour at a foreigner's eye level. This is installment #43 of the Korean Culture explainer series.

> Key point: 'Webtoon' refers to a format, not a nationality. What states a comic's 'origin' is manhwa (만화, Korea) · manga (Japan) · manhua (만화, China), while webtoon refers to 'how it's shown to you (vertical scroll · digital).' Most popular webtoons are Korean manhwa (만화), but the format itself was invented by Korea and spread to the world.

⚠️ 3 Things Foreigners Often Get Wrong

1. "Isn't a webtoon just manga read digitally?" ❌ — The two are different in format itself. Manga is paper books moved onto a screen (flipping pages · black-and-white · left-right), whereas a webtoon is designed from the start for vertical scroll · full color · smartphones. Instead of cutting and flipping panels, it uses an infinite canvas flowing up and down, treating white space, pacing, and color as directing tools. It's not "digital manga" but a new medium.

2. "Aren't webtoons all free anyway?" ❌ — They start free, but the revenue model is sophisticated. The flagship is 'wait-to-free' (기다리면 무료, "wait and it's free") — wait a set time and the next episode unlocks for free; can't wait, and you micro-pay to read ahead. This model grew the webtoon industry (see ③ below).

3. "It's a comic, so it's for kids, right?" ❌ — Webtoons span all ages and all genres — romance, thriller, fantasy, workplace drama, even horror. 〈Misaeng (미생, "Incomplete Life")〉 depicted the reality of Korean office workers, and 〈Hellbound〉 drew a weighty social parable. Adult readers are the core audience, which is why Netflix and Disney+ scramble to turn them into dramas (see ④ below).

① What Is a Webtoon — 'Web + Cartoon'

Webtoon (웹툰) is a Korean-coined word combining web (웹) and cartoon (카툰). It was born in the early 2000s when Korean portals (Daum (다음) · Naver (네이버)) began serializing comics on the internet, and was completed in its current form when it met the smartphone era.

  • Digital by birth: Rather than scanning paper comics, they are drawn to be read on a screen from the start. That's why it's called a 'digital-native' format.
  • Korean exported to the world: 'webtoon' is now a proper noun used as-is in English. Japan's 'manga,' Korea's 'webtoon' — the format itself became a country's cultural brand.

In short, reading a webtoon isn't simply viewing a comic digitally — it's experiencing a comic grammar newly designed by Korea.

② Vertical Scroll — The Innovation the Smartphone Birthed

A webtoon's identity lies in its vertical scroll (세로 스크롤). This is the decisive difference that separates it from manga and comics.

  • Infinite vertical canvas: A single episode stretches thousands to over 10,000 pixels long vertically. With no fixed frame called a 'page,' creators stretch the white space between panels to build tension, and scroll the screen long to stage falls, twists, and jump scares. It's a 'directing of time' impossible in page comics.
  • One-thumb reading: On the subway during the commute, in bed — you swipe down with one thumb. It's 100% optimized for the vertical smartphone screen.
  • Full color · enhanced direction: Most are full color, and some titles even add background music (BGM) · sound effects · simple animation effects. It's moving beyond a 'comic you read' toward a comic you experience.

> One-line summary: If manga is the art of 'the rhythm of flipping paper,' the webtoon is the art of 'the flow of scrolling a screen.'

③ Platforms and Revenue Model — The Naver·Kakao Duopoly, 'Wait-to-Free'

The webtoon industry is driven by two giants.

Platform Features Global
Naver Webtoon (네이버웹툰) (WEBTOON) Free serialization + ads · paid previews. Discovering local creators · IP ('localization') Global WEBTOON app. 170 million+ monthly users worldwide (varies by reporting period, checking recommended)
Kakao (카카오) (KakaoPage (카카오페이지) · Kakao Webtoon (카카오웹툰)) Monetization grown via 'wait-to-free.' K-content-centered supply Japan's Piccoma (픽코마) once ranked among the world's top non-game apps by revenue

The ★ 'wait-to-free (기다리면 무료, "wait and it's free")' model is the core of the webtoon business.

  • First introduced by KakaoPage (카카오페이지) in 2014. Wait a set time (e.g., 24 hours) and the next episode opens for free, while readers who can't wait pay a small fee (cash · coins) to read ahead.
  • A delicate balance that "makes you spend on impatience, yet lets anyone finish for free if they wait" — this formula became the basic grammar of webtoon · web-novel monetization, and was exported abroad as-is under names like 'Wait Free' · 'Daily Pass.'
  • In flux: Around 2025, the model is evolving again, with monthly subscriptions (e.g., WEBTOON Premium) · expanded free access. Specific policies and prices change fast, so checking directly in the app is recommended.

④ IP Power — Why Webtoons Become Dramas, Films, and Anime

A webtoon's real strength comes from its source IP (intellectual property). A webtoon with a proven story, characters, and fandom is treated as a 'box-office guarantee' for dramas, films, and animation.

Representative webtoon-based screen adaptations (★ titles · production companies are added and change over time — checking recommended):

  • 〈Itaewon Class (이태원 클라쓰)〉 — A drama based on a Kakao (카카오) webtoon. A hit work where the original creator even participated in writing the drama script.
  • 〈Hellbound (지옥)〉 — Based on the webtoon by Yeon Sang-ho (연상호) and Choi Gyu-seok (최규석), a globally successful Netflix series.
  • 〈Moving (무빙)〉 — Based on the webtoon by Kang Full (강풀), a Disney+ drama. A mega-IP with a huge budget that ranked #1 in multiple countries.
  • 〈Misaeng (미생, "Incomplete Life")〉 — A workplace-reality webtoon by Yoon Tae-ho (윤태호), called 'the office worker's textbook' as a drama.
  • 〈Solo Leveling (나 혼자만 레벨업)〉 — A global mega-hit webtoon, internationally popular as an animation.
  • Beyond these, many romance webtoons such as 〈What's Wrong with Secretary Kim (김비서가 왜 그럴까)〉 have also been adapted into dramas.

> Why are webtoons a treasure trove of IP? ① The fandom is already proven, lowering box-office risk; ② panels, framing, and dialogue are practically at storyboard (콘티) level, making adaptation easy; and ③ the full color and flashy direction stir visual imagination. The source lode of the K-drama · K-content boom is exactly the webtoon.

⑤ Global Spread — A Korean Format Becomes the World Standard

The webtoon has established itself as another axis of the Korean Wave (한류), following K-pop and K-dramas.

  • Global WEBTOON app: Naver Webtoon (네이버웹툰) went abroad in 2014 as 'LINE Webtoon' (now WEBTOON), gathering users in the hundreds of millions worldwide (figures vary by reporting period — checking recommended).
  • Sweeping North America · Japan · Southeast Asia: In the North American webtoon market, the Naver (네이버) group holds a large share, while Kakao's (카카오) Piccoma (픽코마) dominated the Japanese market. It's also spreading fast into Indonesia, Thailand, India, and beyond.
  • Exporting the format: Even more striking, the vertical-scroll format itself that Korea created has become the world standard. Now US and Japanese creators draw in the webtoon format too, and a genre called 'vertical-scroll comic' has emerged. As the format's inventor, Korea's influence is overwhelming.

⑥ ★ Light and Shadow — The Creators' Labor Behind the Global Success

Here we must keep a balance. The webtoon is Korea's pride, but there's also a dark side: that glamorous success stands on the harsh labor of its creators.

  • Murderous deadlines: A weekly serialization structure that demands pouring out a set volume (dozens of panels) every week. Many creators work long hours, cutting into their sleep. Some point out that the labor intensity is even higher than in page comics, given the greater panel-count and coloring burden.
  • Health crisis: One field survey found the depression prevalence among webtoon creators to be markedly higher than the general population (about 28.7% vs. the 7.7% range), and mental-health indicators such as the suicide-attempt rate were also reported as serious. Physical harm is common too — musculoskeletal disorders from long hours sitting at work, and gastric · bladder ailments from being chased by deadlines.
  • A real tragedy: The passing of the creator (studio head) who drew 〈Solo Leveling (나 혼자만 레벨업)〉 from a cerebral hemorrhage linked to illness, among other sorrowful cases tied to overwork, raised alarm across the industry.
  • Concerns over unfair contracts: Because non-disclosure clauses make it hard to share contract terms, the problem that rookie creators are easily exposed to disadvantageous contracts has also been raised.

> The key message we want to convey to foreign readers: the one webtoon you enjoy may be the result of a creator cutting into their sleep. The industry recognizes this issue and is discussing improvements to labor and contracts. If you love webtoons, consuming them fairly on official platforms is the easiest way to support the creators.

⑦ For Foreigners Who Want to Read Webtoons — Where and How

> The range of apps · services · translation offerings changes fast — before you go, checking the app store and official channels is recommended.

  • Global WEBTOON app (Naver (네이버)): Offers official translations in English and several other languages. The most painless entry point for foreigners. Search 'WEBTOON' in the app store.
  • Other English-language platforms: Services like Tapas · Tappytoon · Manta · Lezhin Comics offer officially English-translated Korean webtoons in variety (title lineups · free/paid policies differ by service).
  • The original Korean Naver Webtoon (네이버웹툰): The original on the Korean Naver Webtoon (네이버웹툰) site · app is mostly Korean-only, so reading it directly requires Korean. There's a way to view it via a browser translation extension, but the official global WEBTOON app is more convenient.
  • How to read: Since it's vertical scroll, you read by swiping your finger from top to bottom. There's no page-flipping action — it feels odd at first but you adapt quickly.
  • Recommended entry flow: If you have a favorite K-drama, watching its source webtoon first has the lowest barrier to entry (since the plot and characters are already familiar).

❓ Frequently Asked Questions from Foreigners (FAQ)

  • Aren't webtoons and manga the same thing in the end? — No. Manga is a paper book (black-and-white · left-right pages), while a webtoon is vertical-scroll digital (full color · mobile). They're separate media that differ from origin point to format.
  • What's the difference between manhwa (만화) and webtoon? — manhwa (만화) = Korean comics (origin), webtoon = vertical-scroll digital format (delivery method). Most popular webtoons are Korean manhwa (만화), but webtoon is the broader term referring to a format.
  • Can I read them for free in English? — On the global WEBTOON app, you can read a good number for free on an ad-supported basis. Like 'wait-to-free,' the next episode often opens after a set time, while previews are frequently micro-paid. Check the policy in the app.
  • Is the K-drama I watched also based on a webtoon? — A good number of popular dramas like 〈Itaewon Class (이태원 클라쓰)〉 · 〈Moving (무빙)〉 · 〈Hellbound (지옥)〉 · 〈Misaeng (미생, "Incomplete Life")〉 are webtoon-based. Check the 'based on webtoon' note on a title's page and you'll find there are surprisingly many.
  • If I go to Korea, is there a webtoon-related 'place to visit'? — Webtoons are essentially content inside an app, so they aren't a 'place experience.' That said, Korea sometimes holds comic · webtoon exhibitions and festivals, so if you're interested, it's nice to look one up around your visit (this article centers on 'cultural understanding').
