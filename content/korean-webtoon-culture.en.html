Korean webtoon culture — Korean-style comics that captivate the world with vertical scrolling

Webtoon·Vertical scrolling·Naver webtoon·Kakao·Free if you wait·Drama based on webtoon — Korean webtoon culture explainer for foreigners. 'Why, what, how' and ★light and shadow of 'a new comic format created by Korea'.

If you’ve seen “Hell,” “Itaewon Class,” and “Moving” on Netflix — in fact, you’ve already entered the world of Korean webtoons. These dramas are all original from webtoons. When “Only I Level Up” exploded around the world as an animation, the starting point was a Korean webtoon.

Webtoon (webtoon = web + cartoon) is a vertically scrolling digital cartoon created in Korea. It's not Japanese manga that you read in a paper book, or American comics that you turn the pages of. It is a completely new format in full color that can be viewed from top to bottom by sliding your finger down on the smartphone screen. Comics were born for the screen, not paper from the beginning — that's why they're called 'digital-native'. This article is not about “which works are fun” (recommended list), but explains why the webtoon format is special, how it captivated the world, and what shadows lie behind its splendor from a foreigner’s perspective. ⚠️ 3 things foreigners often misunderstand

1. "Webtoon = isn't it a manga viewed digitally?" ❌ — The two have different formats. A manga is a paper book translated onto a screen (page turning, black and white, left and right), and a webtoon is designed from the beginning for vertical scrolling, full color, and smartphones. Instead of cutting through cells, blank space, speed, and color are used as production tools on an infinite canvas that flows up and down. It is not a “digital manga” but a new medium.

2. "All webtoons are free, right?" ❌ — It starts for free, but the profit model is sophisticated. A representative example is 'Free if you wait' — If you wait a certain amount of time, the next episode will be released for free, and if you can't wait, you can preview it with a small payment. This model grew the webtoon industry (③ below).

3. "Because it's a cartoon, isn't it for kids?" ❌ — Webtoons are all ages and all genres ranging from romance, thriller, fantasy, workplace drama, and horror. 〈Misaeng〉 depicts the reality of Korean office workers, and 〈Hell〉 depicts a heavy social fable. Adult readers are the main focus, so Netflix and Disney+ are racing to turn them into dramas (④ below).

① What is a webtoon — ‘Web + Cartoon’

Webtoon is a Korean coinage that combines web and cartoon. It was born in the early 2000s when Korean portals (Daum and Naver) began serializing comics on the Internet, and was completed in its current form with the advent of the smartphone era.

  • Born digital: Rather than scanning paper comics, they are drawn to be read on the screen from the beginning. That's why it's called the 'digital-native' format.
  • Korean exported to the world: 'webtoon' has now become a proper noun that is used as is in English-speaking countries. ‘manga’ in Japan, ‘webtoon’ in Korea — the format itself has become a country’s cultural brand.

In other words, watching webtoons is not simply about watching comics digitally, but experiencing Korea's newly designed cartoon grammar.

② Vertical Scroll — An Innovation Born From the Smartphone

The identity of the webtoon lies in its vertical scroll. This is the decisive difference that sets it apart from manga and comics.

  • An infinite vertical canvas: a single episode runs vertically for anywhere from a few thousand to over ten thousand pixels. With no fixed page frame, artists stretch the blank space between panels to build tension, or scroll the screen a long way down to stage a fall, a twist, or a jump scare. It's a kind of 'directing time' that a page-based comic simply can't do.
  • Reading with one thumb: on the subway commute, in bed — readers swipe down with one thumb. It's 100% optimized for a smartphone's vertical screen.
  • Full color, heightened production: most webtoons are full color, and some even add background music (BGM), sound effects, and simple animated effects. They're moving past 'comics you read' toward something closer to comics you experience.

One-line summary: if manga is the art of 'the rhythm of turning a page,' webtoon is the art of 'the flow of scrolling down a screen.'

★ The 'wait-to-free' model (기다리면 무료) is at the core of the webtoon business.

  • First introduced by Kakao Page in 2014. Wait a set amount of time (say, 24 hours) and the next episode unlocks for free; readers who don't want to wait can pay a small fee (cash/coins) to read ahead.
  • It's a delicate balance — 'let impatience open people's wallets, but let anyone read the whole thing for free if they wait.' This formula became the basic grammar for monetizing webtoons and web novels, and it was exported abroad more or less as-is, under names like 'Wait Free' and 'Daily Pass.'
  • Still evolving: starting around 2025, the model has kept evolving again, with monthly subscriptions (like WEBTOON Premium) and expanded free content. Specific policies and prices change quickly, so check directly in the app.

④ IP Power — Why Webtoons Keep Becoming Dramas, Films, and Anime

The real strength of webtoons comes from their source IP (intellectual property). A webtoon with a proven story, characters, and fandom is treated as a near-guaranteed hit when adapted into a drama, film, or anime.

Notable screen adaptations of webtoons (★ titles and production companies get added or change over time — check for the latest):

  • Itaewon Class (이태원 클라쓰) — a drama based on a Kakao webtoon; a buzzworthy adaptation where the original author even helped write the drama's screenplay.
  • Hellbound (지옥) — based on a webtoon by Yeon Sang-ho and Choi Gyu-seok, it became a global hit as a Netflix series.
  • Moving (무빙) — based on a webtoon by artist Kangfull, adapted as a Disney+ drama. A massive-budget production that topped the charts in multiple countries — a truly giant piece of IP.
  • Misaeng (미생) — artist Yoon Tae-ho's webtoon about the realities of office life, whose drama adaptation became known as 'the office worker's textbook.'
  • Solo Leveling (나 혼자만 레벨업) — a global mega-hit webtoon whose anime adaptation became a worldwide sensation.
  • Beyond these, romance webtoons like What's Wrong with Secretary Kim (김비서가 왜 그럴까) have also frequently been adapted into dramas.

Why is webtoon such a goldmine of IP? ① its fandom is already proven, which lowers the box-office risk; ② the panels, framing, and dialogue are already close to storyboard level, making adaptation easy; and ③ the full-color, dramatic staging fires up visual imagination. Webtoons are the true source vein behind the K-drama and K-content boom.

⑤ Going Global — A Korean Format Becomes the World Standard

Webtoons have established themselves as another pillar of Hallyu, alongside K-pop and K-dramas.

  • The global WEBTOON app: Naver Webtoon expanded overseas in 2014 as 'LINE Webtoon' (now WEBTOON), building an audience of several hundred million users worldwide (figures vary by reporting date — check for current numbers).
  • Dominating North America, Japan, and Southeast Asia: Naver's platforms hold a major share of the North American webtoon market, while Kakao's Piccoma has come to dominate the Japanese market. It's also spreading fast into Indonesia, Thailand, India, and beyond.
  • Exporting the format itself: even more striking is that the vertical-scroll format Korea invented has become the global standard. Creators in the US and Japan now draw in the webtoon format too, and a genre called 'vertical-scroll comic' has emerged. As the country that invented the format, Korea's influence is overwhelming.

⑥ ★ Light and Shadow — The Labor Behind Global Success

This is where balance matters. Webtoons are a source of Korean pride, but there's also a dark side — that dazzling success stands on the backs of artists' grueling labor.

  • Brutal deadlines: a weekly serialization structure forces artists to churn out a fixed volume (dozens of panels) every single week. Many artists work long hours at the expense of sleep. Some point out that the workload is even heavier than page comics, given the sheer number of panels and the coloring burden.
  • A health crisis: one survey found that webtoon artists' rate of depression was markedly higher than the general population (roughly 28.7% vs. about 7.7%), and indicators like attempted-suicide rates were also reported at alarming levels. Physical harm is common too — musculoskeletal disorders from long hours sitting at work, and digestive or bladder problems from deadline stress.
  • Real tragedies: cases like the death of the artist (a studio head) behind Solo Leveling, from a brain hemorrhage linked to an underlying condition, have raised alarm across the industry about the toll of overwork.
  • Concerns over unfair contracts: non-disclosure clauses make it hard for artists to compare contract terms with each other, and there have long been concerns that rookie artists are especially exposed to unfavorable contracts.

The key point I want to convey to foreign readers: A webtoon you enjoy may be the result of an author who lost sleep. The industry is aware of this problem and is discussing labor and contract improvements. If you love webtoons, the easiest way to support the author is to consume them legitimately on the official platform.

❓ Foreigners Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

  • Aren’t webtoons and manga the same thing in the end? — No. Manga is paper book (black and white, left and right pages), webtoon is vertical scrolling digital (full color, mobile). It is a separate medium, different from its starting point to its format.
  • What is the difference between manhwa (comics) and webtoon? — manhwa = Korean comics (origin), webtoon = vertically scrolling digital format (delivery method). Although most popular webtoons are Korean comics (manhwa), webtoon is a broader term that refers to the format.
  • Can I watch it for free in English? — You can watch a lot of it for free or on an ad-based basis on the Global WEBTOON app. In many cases, the next episode opens after a certain period of time in a 'wait and it's free' style, or the preview is often a small payment. Check policies in app.
  • Are the K-dramas I watched also based on webtoons? — Many popular dramas are based on webtoons, including <Itaewon Class>, <Moving>, <Hell>, and <Incomplete Life>. If you check the 'based on webtoon' notation on the work page, there are surprisingly many.
  • Are there any ‘places to go’ related to webtoons when you go to Korea? — Webtoons are essentially content within an app, so they are not ‘place experiences’. However, there are exhibitions and festivals related to Korean comics and webtoons, so if you are interested, it would be a good idea to look for them according to the timing of your visit (this article focuses on 'understanding culture').
