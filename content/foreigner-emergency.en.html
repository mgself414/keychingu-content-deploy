Korean Hospital Bills & Travel Insurance Claim Guide — Ask for the Certificate at the Visit, the Receipts at Payment: 5 Documents, Fee Caps, Direct Billing

Subtitle: Travelers without Korean national health insurance pay medical bills out of pocket · 5 documents you need for an insurance claim back home, and when to get each · Fee cap for a Medical Certificate (진단서) and English Medical Certificate: ₩20,000 (examination and test fees are separate; Ministry of Health and Welfare notice) · Direct billing to insurers at Asan Medical Center (서울아산병원) and Severance Hospital · 1345 and 1339 are not emergency numbers · Checked 2026-11-03

ISSUE 03 · #251 · 2026-05-19

🎯 How to Use This Guide

The treatment is over and you are standing at the payment counter. You plan to claim it back from your travel insurance once you get home, but if you don't collect the paperwork your insurer requires right now, it is hard to come back for it later. This guide answers only that moment — what to ask for, when, from whom, and how much the paperwork costs.

For which hospital to go to, see #405 Seoul Hospital Guide for Foreigners. For the step-by-step from clinic check-in to the pharmacy, see #1307 Korean Hospital Visit Procedure. For the numbers to call in a hurry, see #433 Korea Emergency & Safety Guide. If you can't breathe or are badly hurt, close this page and call 119.

🧭 At a Glance — 5 Insurance Claim Documents

Document What it is When and who to ask Issuance fee
Receipt (진료비 영수증) Proof of the amount you paid Ask for it in English at payment Not in the notified fee table — check with the hospital
Itemized Receipt (진료비 세부내역서) Amount per item: examination, tests, medication Ask for it together at payment Not in the notified fee table — check with the hospital
Medical Certificate (진단서) Diagnosis written by the doctor Ask the doctor during the visit → collect after payment Cap ₩20,000 (the English general Medical Certificate is also ₩20,000)
Outpatient Visit Confirmation / Treatment Confirmation (통원확인서·진료확인서) Outpatient Visit Confirmation = date of outpatient visit / Treatment Confirmation = details of specific treatment (tests, medication, etc.) Check the hospital's information desk for where to get it Cap ₩3,000
Prescription (처방전) Drug names and doses Receive after the visit and hand in at the pharmacy —

The fee caps are the figures in the "Standards for Items and Amounts of Medical Certificate Fees at Medical Institutions" (Ministry of Health and Welfare Notice No. 2021-34). They cover only the fee for issuing the document, not examination or test fees. Hospitals set their price anywhere from ₩0 up to the cap, and must post the amount on their website if they have one.

🧾 Before Treatment — Ask Your Insurer First

  • Get the list of documents your insurer requires before the visit. Seoul National University Hospital's International Healthcare Center also advises confirming the required documents before treatment.
  • Guarantee of Payment (지급보증서, GOP): Seoul National University Hospital advises requesting a Guarantee of Payment from your insurer before your appointment date. Asan Medical Center says to obtain it in advance if your insurer requires it, and to check the coverage limit and excluded items.
  • Direct Billing: At Asan Medical Center and Severance Hospital, the hospital bills the insurer directly only for contracted insurers. Seoul National University Hospital advises that if your insurer has no contract with it, you pay first and claim from the insurer afterward. For Asan direct billing and insurance inquiries: billing@amc.seoul.kr.

🩺 During Treatment — This Is When You Ask for the Certificate

  • A Medical Certificate is written by the doctor. Seoul National University Hospital says to ask the doctor directly "during the visit"; Asan Medical Center says that if you ask before the visit, the doctor will write it in English.
  • One line is enough: 「보험 청구용 영문 진단서 부탁드립니다」 (Medical certificate in English for my insurance, please).
  • If you were injured and your insurer's list includes an Injury Medical Certificate (상해진단서): the notified cap is ₩100,000 for under 3 weeks and ₩150,000 for 3 weeks or more (Korean-language form). The fee for an English Injury Medical Certificate is not in the notified table, so check with the hospital.
  • A Doctor's Opinion Letter (소견서) has no entry in the notified table, so the hospital sets the price. If your insurer says a Medical Certificate is enough, get only the Medical Certificate.

💳 At Payment — Get Both Receipts Together

  • Seoul National University Hospital's International Healthcare Center issues an English Receipt and an Itemized (Detailed) Receipt. These names follow Seoul National University Hospital's guidance. At other hospitals, ask at the payment counter whether English documents are issued and in what format.
  • Seoul National University Hospital's International Healthcare Center accepts cash or credit card, and says that when paying by card you can choose between Korean won and US dollars.
  • You collect the Medical Certificate after payment is complete — at Window 1 at Seoul National University Hospital, at the Administration Office (원무과) at Asan Medical Center.
  • If you need a copy of your medical records, the cap is ₩1,000 per page for pages 1–5 and ₩100 per page from page 6. A medical imaging CD is capped at ₩10,000.
  • An extra copy of a certificate such as a Medical Certificate is capped at ₩1,000.

📸 Photograph your documents with your phone before you leave the payment counter. Getting them again after you are back home may mean another visit or extra cost.

🚑 ER Costs — The Total Varies by Hospital

  • The total ER bill varies by hospital and by tests, so it can't be known in advance. Ask for an estimated cost when you register at the ER. Travelers without Korean national health insurance pay the full amount billed.
  • Korean national health insurance uses out-of-pocket rates to discourage using large ERs for minor symptoms. For care covered by national health insurance, a mild-emergency patient who uses a regional emergency medical center or similar, or a non-emergency patient who uses the ER of a regional or local emergency medical center, pays 90% of the ER treatment cost (Enforcement Rule of the National Health Insurance Act, Annex 6). How this rate applies to travelers without national health insurance is not stated in the source.
  • If it isn't urgent, go during the day to a neighborhood clinic (#1307) or a hospital with an international healthcare center (#405). How to find places open at night is also in #1307.
  • The ER may recommend that you continue with outpatient care or go to another hospital (Seoul National University Hospital guidance). Ask at registration how document issuance works in the ER, and keep every document you are given.

☎ 1345 and 1339 — Not Emergency Numbers

  • 1345 = Ministry of Justice Immigration Contact Center. A help line for everyday matters such as residence and immigration. 20 languages, weekdays 09:00–22:00, and after 18:00 only Korean, English, and Chinese. From overseas: +82-2-1345.
  • 1339 = Korea Disease Control and Prevention Agency call center. Illness consultation and infectious disease reporting; consultations are available 24 hours a day, 365 days a year. Foreigners staying in Korea get interpretation through a three-way call with 1330 or 1345, but interpretation works only within those numbers' operating hours (Korea Disease Control and Prevention Agency guidance).
  • For an emergency that needs a dispatch, call 119; for crime, 112. How to use the 1330 tourist interpretation line: #1299.

⚠ 4 Common Mistakes

  1. "I can just get the Medical Certificate by email later" — The doctor writes the Medical Certificate. Seoul National University Hospital advises requesting it during the visit, and Asan Medical Center before the visit.
  2. "One receipt is enough" — If your insurer wants amounts per item, you need the Itemized Receipt. Get both at payment.
  3. "If the hospital is contracted with my insurer, it's all handled automatically" — Direct billing applies only to contracted insurers, and a Guarantee of Payment may be needed first. Check with your insurer before treatment.
  4. "1345 and 1339 are emergency numbers" — Both are consultation and information lines. For an emergency, call 119.

⚙ FAQ 4

Q1. How much is an English Medical Certificate?

The Ministry of Health and Welfare cap is ₩20,000 for both the general Medical Certificate and the English general Medical Certificate. Examination and test fees are separate, and each hospital sets its price within the cap and posts it on its website.

Q2. Which documents should I get?

Your insurer's list comes first. If there is no list, get the English receipt, Itemized Receipt, and Medical Certificate, and if you bought medication, keep the prescription and the pharmacy receipt too.

Q3. Can I pay by card?

Seoul National University Hospital's International Healthcare Center accepts both cash and credit card, and says that for cards you can choose between Korean won and US dollars. At other hospitals, check at the payment counter.

Q4. What if we can't understand each other?

Hospitals that provide interpretation are in #405; how to use the 1330 tourist interpretation line is in #1299.
