Withdrawing Cash from a Korean ATM, the Zero-Panic Manual — Follow the Screen Exactly

🎯 How to Read This Manual — Screen Notation Rules

  • Buttons on a screen appear in brackets: [ KRW ]
  • Screen titles and prompts appear in corner brackets: 「Select Amount」
  • Actions you do by hand are bold: "insert your card IC-chip-first, all the way in"

① Spotting a Global ATM — Not Every ATM Works

Most Korean ATMs are domestic-card-only. Machines that accept foreign-issued cards are marked separately.

  • First check whether the machine's screen or side panel has Visa, Mastercard, UnionPay, or JCB logo stickers.
  • If the machine has a "Global ATM" or "Foreign Card" label on it, that's a sure sign.
  • A machine with no logo stickers at all is likely to reject your card even if you try — it's faster to find a different machine before attempting.

② Where to Find One — Convenience Store vs. Bank

Location Brands Accessibility Fee (approx.)
Convenience store CU, GS25, 7-Eleven Open 24 hours, the most common by far About ₩3,000–3,600
Bank ATM Woori Bank, KB Kookmin, Hana Bank, Shinhan Bank Mostly business hours, broad foreign-card coverage Woori Bank about ₩2,000–3,000
  • In a hurry, go convenience store; to save on fees, go bank ATM — though some bank ATM booths are locked outside business hours and inaccessible.
  • ATMs inside airports and major stations have an especially high rate of being Global ATMs.

③ The Withdrawal Master Flow — 7 Steps, Screen by Screen

Step What You See on Screen What to Do Trap
1 Initial screen Touch [ Foreign Card ] / [ Overseas Card ] / [ Global Service ] Skip this button and go into the regular menu, and the machine won't read your card at all
2 Language selection [ English ] A Global ATM supports English from the language-selection screen onward
3 Card-insertion prompt Insert your card IC-chip-first, all the way in Contactless-only (tap) cards may not be recognized on some machines
4 「Enter PIN」 Enter your 4-digit PIN → [ Enter ] Cover the keypad with your hand while entering it
5 「Select Amount」 Touch an amount, or enter a custom KRW amount via [ Other Amount ] The maximum per-withdrawal limit varies by card issuer and machine
6 「Confirm Fee / Currency」 Check the fee and currency, then [ Confirm ] This screen is the core of this manual — read ④ below first
7 「Dispensing...」 Cash, then card, then receipt come out in that order The single most common mistake is taking the cash and walking off without the card

④ The DCC Trap — the "Charge in Your Home Currency?" Screen, Always Pick KRW

Right after step 6, or on the amount-confirmation screen, many machines show a popup like this. This is the single most important screen in this manual.

```

「Currency Selection」

Charge in:

[ USD $50.00 ] ← never press this

[ KRW ₩68,000 ] ← always press this

```

  • This popup is DCC (Dynamic Currency Conversion) — converting to your home currency at a rate the ATM operator sets on its own. It's designed so the charge shows up in USD, EUR, or whatever your home currency is.
  • If you pick your home currency (USD, etc.), a hidden exchange-rate markup gets tacked on right there, worse than the standard rate your card issuer would apply afterward. The screen "helpfully" shows the amount in your own currency, but it's actually a trap that ends up costing more.
  • The takeaway is one line: no matter what the screen says, choose KRW (the local currency). Letting your own card issuer handle the conversion is always the better deal.

⑤ The Double Fee Structure — What Shows on Screen, and What Doesn't

Type Shown on screen? Details
ATM withdrawal fee Yes — displayed on the step-6 confirmation screen Convenience store about ₩3,000–3,600 / bank (Woori) about ₩2,000–3,000
Your home bank's overseas withdrawal fee No — never appears anywhere on the ATM screen Charged separately by your card-issuing bank later, on your statement. Checking your home bank's "overseas ATM withdrawal fee" terms via its app or customer service before you leave saves you from a surprise on the statement after you get home

In other words, the amount shown on the ATM screen isn't the whole story — just remember it's two layers: the visible fee plus the invisible fee from your home bank.

⑥ 10 Survival Words — When You See This Text on Screen

Romanization (Korean) Meaning
chulgeum (출금) Withdrawal — press this button to start
jan-aek johoe (잔액조회) Balance Inquiry — check your balance (don't confuse with withdrawal)
susu-ryo (수수료) Fee — the key word on the step-6 confirmation screen
hwagin (확인) Confirm — proceed to the next step
chwiso (취소) Cancel — cancels the entire transaction if you pressed something wrong
bimil beonho (비밀번호) PIN — your card's 4-digit password
geumaek ipryeok (금액입력) Enter Amount — type in your withdrawal amount directly
myeongsepyo (명세표) Receipt — records the fee and exchange rate
kadeu batgi (카드받기) Take Card — retrieve your card, never miss this
georae jungdan (거래중단) Transaction Cancelled — means the transaction was interrupted; try again

Tip: you don't need to memorize all 10. Just three get you through in practice: hwagin/Confirm (proceed) · chwiso/Cancel (escape) · kadeu batgi/Take Card (retrieve your card).

⑦ 3 Panic Scenarios

1. Your card got rejected — if it happened on a machine that did have the logo stickers, the problem is more likely a mismatch between that specific machine's bank network than your card itself. Move to a different bank's ATM (ideally a different brand) and try again.

2. The machine swallowed your card — don't try to force it out on the spot. At a bank ATM, call the customer service number posted on the booth during the bank's business hours to check the retrieval process. At a convenience-store ATM, follow the customer service info on screen (usually the ATM operator's call center).

3. It won't dispense due to a limit — you've hit either the machine's own per-withdrawal limit or your card issuer's overseas withdrawal limit. Either lower the amount and try again, or wait a day and try a different machine.

⑧ Plan B — If ATMs Just Won't Work

  • WOWPASS: a prepaid card for foreigners. Issued instantly with your passport at machines in the airport and major stations — load it with cash and use it like a domestic card. If withdrawing from an ATM feels like too much of a hassle, this is the alternative.
  • Currency exchange counters: private exchange counters in places like Myeongdong sometimes offer better rates than an ATM. The comparison between the two and how to choose is covered in #435 Currency Exchange, Cards & ATMs Overview.
