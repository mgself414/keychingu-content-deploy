Korea Sup-meong, Mul-meong and Barefoot Walking Guide — the Sup-meong (Forest Zoning Out) of Sallyeoni Forest Path, Bijarim and Seogwipo Healing Forest, the Mul-meong (Water Zoning Out) of Soesokkak, Suncheonman Wetland and Dumulmeori, and Barefoot Walking at Gyejoksan Hwangtogil and Mulmusan Haengbok Forest: 20 Places

Subtitle: 20 places with a registered Korea Tourism Organization operating listing among the forest, bamboo grove, waterside, open-sky and barefoot-path places chosen in the "Good for Zoning Out" and "Soles of the Feet (Barefoot)" themes of the Ministry of Culture, Sports and Tourism and Korea Tourism Organization "Attraction Discovery 100×100" public vote · The places with a registered admission fee are Bijarim, Jungnogwon (General KRW 3,000), Suncheonman Wetland (Adults KRW 10,000), Geoje Maengjongjuk Theme Park (Adults KRW 4,000) and others, 7 places in all · Seogwipo Healing Forest requires a reservation in advance and Suncheonman Wetland is closed on the last Monday of every month — confirmed via Korea Tourism Organization tourism data (TourAPI)

🎯 How to Use This Guide

This guide covers 20 of the places chosen in the "Good for Zoning Out" (멍 때리기 좋은) theme and the "Soles of the Feet (Barefoot)" ("발바닥 맨발") theme of the Ministry of Culture, Sports and Tourism and Korea Tourism Organization "Attraction Discovery 100×100" (명소발굴 100×100) (candidates recommended by travel experts, voted on by the public), whose operating details are registered in the Korea Tourism Organization tourism data. The ranks and vote counts in the table are the results of that vote. Where a place made both themes, both ranks are given. The places were picked to suit five kinds of setting: forests, bamboo groves, waterside, open sky and barefoot paths. So the tree-lined road (Damyang Metasequoia Road, 3rd in the barefoot vote), the old road (Mungyeong Saejae) and the city parks (Jinju Namgaram Park and Gimhae Yeonji Park) that also made the barefoot vote are not included. Beaches, historic sites and capes from the zoning-out vote, such as Hyeopjae Beach (협재해수욕장), Woljeonggyo Bridge (월정교), Daereungwon (대릉원), Windy Hill (바람의 언덕) and Seopjikoji (섭지코지), were left out for the same reason.

"Sup-meong" (숲멍) and "mul-meong" (물멍) are Korean expressions often used in the vote's recommendation reasons, meaning to rest while gazing blankly at a forest or at water. The places are grouped into five kinds: forests, bamboo groves, waterside, open sky and barefoot paths.

The operating details are copied from values registered in the Korea Tourism Organization tourism data (TourAPI). Fields with no registered value are marked "No registered value". Where a name or address on the vote list differs from the Korea Tourism Organization registration, the main text says so separately.

📍 Sup-meong (Forest Zoning Out) — Sallyeoni Forest Path, Bijarim, Seogwipo Healing Forest, Jeolmul Natural Recreation Forest and Jeju Gotjawal Provincial Park

Sallyeoni Forest Path (address registered with the Korea Tourism Organization: Bulgeun Oreum entrance, Gasi-ri, Pyoseon-myeon, Seogwipo / 서귀포시 표선면 가시리 붉은오름 입구) is a cedar forest path that starts from Bijarim-ro (비자림로) and runs by way of Mulchat Oreum (물찻오름) and Sallyeoni Oreum (사려니오름). It runs on to Sallyeoni Oreum, which is how it got its name. According to the Korea Tourism Organization description, "sallyeoni" means a sacred forest, or "to wind something such as thread into a round coil so that it does not scatter". Besides cedar, Korean oak, hornbeam, snowbell and hinoki cypress grow here, and it lies within the Jeju Biosphere Reserve designated by UNESCO in 2002. The vote list gives the address as San 64-5, Bonggae-dong, Jeju City (제주시 봉개동 산64-5).

  • Opening hours, closed days, fee: No registered value.

Bijarim (55 Bijasup-gil, Gujwa-eup, Jeju City / 제주시 구좌읍 비자숲길 55, ☎ 064-710-7911~2) is a forest designated a Natural Monument. On an area of 448,165㎡ grow more than 2,800 torreya trees that are 500~800 years old. The trees are 7~14m tall and 50~110㎝ in diameter. The Korea Tourism Organization description says it is a torreya forest rare even by world standards.

  • Opening hours: 09:00~18:00 (last entry 17:00). Closed days: Open year-round.
  • Fee (individual): General KRW 3,000, youths and children KRW 1,500. Free for preschool children.

Seogwipo Healing Forest (2271 Sanrongnam-ro, Seogwipo / 서귀포시 산록남로 2271, ☎ 064-760-3067) is a forest at 320~760m above sea level. Warm-temperate, temperate and cold-temperate forests grow evenly, and according to the Korea Tourism Organization description there are several hinoki cypress forests with an average tree age of more than 60 years. A forest healing program run by forest healing instructors is offered.

  • Opening hours: Months 4~10 08:00~16:00, months 11~3 09:00~16:00. It is used by reservation in advance, and if places remain, same-day online reservations and on-site ticketing are also available. The registered capacity is 300 people on weekdays and 600 on weekends.
  • Closed days: Open year-round.
  • Fee (individual): Adults KRW 1,000, youths KRW 600. The forest healing program costs adults KRW 20,000 for about 3 hours and adults KRW 12,000 for about 2 hours.

Jeolmul Natural Recreation Forest (584 Myeongnim-ro, Jeju City / 제주시 명림로 584, ☎ 064-728-1510) is a cedar forest recreation area. Mainly cedars planted from the mid-1960s. The walking paths are gentle with no stairs and are described as easy to walk even for the elderly and children. From the summit viewpoint of nearby Jeolmul Oreum (절물오름) you can see a horseshoe-shaped crater.

  • Opening hours: Months 3~10 07:00~18:00, months 11~2 07:00~17:00. Closed days: Open year-round.
  • Fee (individual): Adults KRW 1,000, youths KRW 600, children KRW 300.

Jeju Gotjawal Provincial Park (178 Edusiti-ro, Daejeong-eup, Seogwipo / 서귀포시 대정읍 에듀시티로 178, ☎ 064-792-6047): gotjawal (곶자왈) means a forest that grew on ground strewn with irregular lumps of rock. It has five courses, Teuri-gil (테우리길), Hansugi-gil (한수기길), Billae-gil (빌레길), Ochani-gil (오찬이길) and Gasinang-gil (가시낭길), and forest interpretation is given twice a day. You must wear hiking boots or sneakers, and you cannot enter in dress shoes, sandals or platform sneakers.

  • Opening hours: Months 3~10 entry 09:00~16:30 (visits until 18:00), months 11~2 entry 09:00~15:00 (visits until 17:30). Closed days: Open year-round.
  • Fee: General KRW 1,000, youths and soldiers KRW 800, children KRW 500.

📍 Bamboo Groves — Jungnogwon, Taehwagang National Garden, Geoje Maengjongjuk Theme Park and Seomjingang Bamboo Forest Path

Jungnogwon (119 Jungnogwon-ro, Damyang-eup, Damyang-gun / 담양군 담양읍 죽녹원로 119, ☎ 061-380-2680) is a bamboo grove that opened in May 2003. A 2.2㎞ walking path is divided into eight themed paths, including Unsudaetong-gil (운수대통길), Jukmagou-gil (죽마고우길) and the Philosopher's Path (철학자의 길). From the viewpoint you can look down on Damyang Stream (담양천), Gwanbangjerim (관방제림) and the Metasequoia Road. Lighting has been installed in the bamboo grove so that you can walk at night too.

  • Opening hours: Months 3~10 09:00~19:00 (last entry 18:00), months 11~2 09:00~18:00 (last entry 17:30). Closed days: Open year-round.
  • Fee (individual): General KRW 3,000, youths and soldiers KRW 1,500, elementary school students KRW 1,000. Free for ages 65 and over and under 6.

Taehwagang National Garden (5 Saetgangnam-gil, Jung-gu, Ulsan / 울산 중구 샛강남길 5, ☎ 052-229-3147~8) is an ecological garden along the Taehwagang River. On a total area of 835,452㎡ there are six spaces: ecology, bamboo, seasons, water plants, participation and rose of Sharon. According to the Korea Tourism Organization description, Sipri Bamboo Forest (십리대숲) is famous for the sound of bamboo knocking in the wind and its quiet path. It appears on the vote list under the name "Taehwagang National Garden Hwangto Barefoot Path" (태화강 국가정원 황토맨발길), but the Korea Tourism Organization description has no explanation of a hwangto (red clay) barefoot path, and the vote list address (154 Taehwagang Gukgajeongwon-gil / 태화강국가정원길 154) also differs from the address registered with the Korea Tourism Organization.

  • Opening hours: Always open. Closed days: Open year-round. Fee: Free.

Geoje Maengjongjuk Theme Park (700 Geojebuk-ro, Hacheong-myeon, Geoje / 거제시 하청면 거제북로 700, ☎ 055-637-0067) is a park for resting and experiences in a maengjongjuk (맹종죽) bamboo forest. According to the Korea Tourism Organization description, maengjongjuk is 10~20m tall and about 20㎝ in diameter, the thickest of the bamboos. There are maengjongjuk forest experiences, craft experiences and an adventure forest experience. It appears on the vote list under the name "Geoje Maengjongjuk Theme Park" (거제 맹종죽테마파크).

  • Opening hours: Months 3~10 09:00~18:00, months 11~2 09:00~17:30. Closed days: Open year-round.
  • Fee (individual): Adults KRW 4,000, youths KRW 3,000, children KRW 2,000. Parking is free.

Seomjingang Bamboo Forest Path (1 Wonbang-ri, Gurye-eup, Gurye-gun / 구례군 구례읍 원방리 1) is a walking path that runs about 2.5km along the Seomjingang River. It is described as letting you see the Jirisan ridgeline as you walk alongside the river. There is a crescent-moon-shaped photo zone at the entrance and a full-moon-shaped one further in, and lights come on after dark.

  • Opening hours, closed days, fee: No registered value.

📍 Mul-meong (Water Zoning Out) — Soesokkak, Suncheonman Wetland, Wiyangji, Dumulmeori, Uirimji and Banpo Hangang Park

Soesokkak (104 Soesokkak-ro, Seogwipo / 서귀포시 쇠소깍로 104, ☎ tourist information center 064-732-1562) is a place where the fresh water of the Hyodoncheon (효돈천) stream meets seawater to form a deep pool. According to the Korea Tourism Organization description, "soe" means ox, "so" means pool and "kkak" means end. Strange-shaped rocks and a pine forest blend into the valley formed by hardened lava. Small boat and teu (테우) raft experiences are offered.

  • Opening hours: Soesokkak is always open. The experiences run 09:00~18:00 in the warm season and 09:00~17:00 in the cold season, and do not operate in bad weather.
  • Closed days: Open year-round.
  • Fee: Soesokkak is free. Small boat KRW 20,000 for 2 adults, teu raft KRW 10,000 for 1 adult.

Suncheonman Wetland (513-25 Suncheonman-gil, Suncheon / 순천시 순천만길 513-25, ☎ 061-749-3114) is a place where a reed colony with a total area of 5.4㎢ spreads from where the Suncheon Dongcheon (동천) and Isacheon (이사천) streams meet to the front of the tidal flat. There are experiences such as stargazing at the observatory and exploring the reed bed tidal flat. The vote list separately includes "Suncheonman Wetland Suncheonman Reed Colony" (순천만습지 순천만갈대군락지) at Zoning Out 12th (192 votes).

  • Opening hours: Months 3~4 and 9~10 08:00~18:00, months 5~8 08:00~19:00, months 11~2 08:00~17:00. The registered value also says "visits are possible until 1 hour after the last entry".
  • Closed days: Last Monday of every month.
  • Fee (individual): Adults KRW 10,000, youths and soldiers KRW 7,000, children KRW 5,000.

Miryang Wiyangji (273-36 Wiyang-ro, Bubuk-myeon, Miryang / 밀양시 부북면 위양로 273-36) is a reservoir built in the Silla period to supply water for farming. There are five small islands in the middle of the reservoir and a pavilion, Wanjaejeong (완재정). It is described as most scenic when the fringe tree flowers bloom.

  • Opening hours: Always open. Closed days: Open year-round. Fee: Free. Parking: Free.

Yangpyeong Dumulmeori (145 Dumulmeori-gil, Yangseo-myeon, Yangpyeong-gun / 양평군 양서면 두물머리길 145, ☎ tourist information center 031-775-8700) is where the Bukhangang and Namhangang rivers merge. It is known for the early morning mist and a zelkova tree more than 400 years old, and according to the Korea Tourism Organization description it was selected as one of the Korea Tourism 100 Selections. The vote list gives the address as Yangsu-ri, Yangseo-myeon (양서면 양수리).

  • Opening hours: Always open. Closed days: Open year-round. Fee: Free.

Jecheon Uirimji (33 Uirimji-ro, Jecheon / 제천시 의림지로 33, ☎ 043-651-7101) is a reservoir built in the Three Han period, with a circumference of 1.8㎞. It is described as the lake meant when Chungcheong-do is called "Hoseo-jibang" (호서지방). Around the lake are Yeongho-jeong (영호정) pavilion built in 1807, Gyeongho-ru (경호루) built in 1948, pine trees and weeping willows several hundred years old, and a 30m natural waterfall.

  • Opening hours: Always open. Closed days: Open year-round. Fee: Free.

Banpo Hangang Park (40 Sinbanpo-ro 11-gil, Seocho-gu, Seoul / 서울 서초구 신반포로11길 40, ☎ 02-3780-0541~4) lies on the south side of the Hangang River between Hannam Bridge and Dongjak Bridge, centered on Banpo Bridge. According to the Korea Tourism Organization description, the Moonlight Rainbow Fountain on Banpo Bridge is 1,140m long and was listed by Guinness in 2008 as the world's longest bridge fountain. There is a walking course that circles Seoraeseom (서래섬), the artificial island between Banpo Bridge and Dongjak Bridge.

  • Opening hours: Always open (see the website for fountain operating hours). Closed days: Open year-round. Fee: Free.

📍 Haneul-meong (Sky Zoning Out) — Haneul Park and Anbandeok

Haneul Park (95 Haneulgongwon-ro, Mapo-gu, Seoul / 서울 마포구 하늘공원로 95, ☎ World Cup Park 02-300-5500) is inside World Cup Park. It is land that was a landfill, revived as an ecological park. In autumn the silver grass blooms, and you can take in Seoul's scenery, including Bukhansan Mountain and the Hangang River, at a glance. It is also known for its sunsets and night views. It appears in the "Soles of the Feet (Barefoot)" vote under the name "Haneul Park Sogonsogon Path" (하늘공원 소곤소곤길).

  • Opening hours: 05:00~20:30. Closed days: Open year-round. Fee: Free.

Gangneung Anbandeok (428 Anbandeok-gil, Wangsan-myeon, Gangneung / 강릉시 왕산면 안반데기길 428, ☎ Gangneung City Tourism Policy Division 033-640-5125) is a cabbage field in a highland at 1,100m above sea level. From 1965 fire-field farmers cut into the mountain to make it, and because the slope is steep the fields were plowed by human hands without machines. About 2,000,000㎡ (200만㎡) of land became highland cabbage fields. It appears on the vote list under the name "Wow Anbandeok" (와우안반데기) with the address 440-1 Anbandeok-gil (안반데기길 440-1), and the Milky Way in the night sky was cited as the reason for the recommendation.

  • Opening hours: Always open. Closed days: Open year-round. Fee: Free.

📍 Barefoot Walking — Gyejoksan Hwangtogil, Mulmusan Haengbok Forest and Daewangam Park

Gyejoksan Hwangtogil (Jang-dong, Daedeok-gu, Daejeon / 대전 대덕구 장동) is a barefoot trekking path made in 2006 by laying more than 20,000 tons of hwangto (황토, red clay). The slope is gentle, and it is described as easy to walk barefoot. There is also a separate trail for walking in shoes. At the starting point there is a forest plaza, along with facilities such as a playground and a pavilion, and a foot-washing area.

  • Opening hours, closed days, fee: No registered value.

Mulmusan Haengbok Forest (219 Mulmu-ro, Yeonggwang-eup, Yeonggwang-gun / 영광군 영광읍 물무로 219, ☎ Yeonggwang County Forest Park Division 061-350-5779) is a forest with a 10㎞ loop trail, a 2㎞ barefoot hwangto path, 2 hinoki cypress video gardens, a pine forest, hiking trails and more. The hwangto path is on the back side of Mulmusan Mountain and is divided into 0.6㎞ of muddy hwangto path and 1.4㎞ of dry hwangto path, so you can choose which to walk. It appears on the vote list under the name "Mulmusan Haengbok Forest Barefoot Hwangto Path" (물무산행복숲 맨발황톳길) with an address in Deokheung-ri, Myoryang-myeon (묘량면 덕흥리).

  • Opening hours: Always open. Closed days: Open year-round. Fee: Free.

Daewangam Park (95 Deungdae-ro, Dong-gu, Ulsan / 울산 동구 등대로 95, ☎ 052-209-3738) is a park where a 600m pine-forest path runs from the entrance to the Ulgi Lighthouse. According to the Korea Tourism Organization description, shade from pine trees grown for more than 100 years continues, and past the pine forest come coastal cliffs and large rocks. It appears on the vote list under the name "Daewangam Park Pine Forest Path" (대왕암공원 송림길). There are a suspension bridge and the Ulgi Lighthouse.

  • Opening hours: Always open. Closed days: Open year-round. Fee: No registered value.

⚠ 3 Things to Know in Advance

  1. Seogwipo Healing Forest takes reservations — It is used by reservation in advance, and the registered capacity is 300 people on weekdays and 600 on weekends.
  2. Jeju Gotjawal Provincial Park is not a place to walk barefoot — You must wear hiking boots or sneakers, and you cannot enter in dress shoes, sandals or platform sneakers.
  3. Check winter hours and closed days — In months 11~2 the closing time moves earlier at several places, including Jungnogwon, Jeju Gotjawal Provincial Park, Jeolmul Natural Recreation Forest and Suncheonman Wetland, and Suncheonman Wetland is closed on the last Monday of every month.

⚙ FAQ 3

Q. Which places are close to Seoul?

A. In Seoul there are Banpo Hangang Park and Haneul Park, and there is also Dumulmeori in Yangpyeong, Gyeonggi. All three are registered as free.

Q. Which places are introduced as good for walking barefoot?

A. Gyejoksan Hwangtogil (a barefoot trekking path) and Mulmusan Haengbok Forest (a 2㎞ barefoot hwangto path). Both have barefoot paths written in the Korea Tourism Organization description. Taehwagang National Garden and Daewangam Park were 1st and 2nd in the "Soles of the Feet (Barefoot)" vote, but the Korea Tourism Organization description has no explanation of a barefoot path.

Q. Which places cost money?

A. Bijarim (General KRW 3,000), Seogwipo Healing Forest (Adults KRW 1,000), Jeolmul Natural Recreation Forest (Adults KRW 1,000), Jeju Gotjawal Provincial Park (General KRW 1,000), Jungnogwon (General KRW 3,000), Geoje Maengjongjuk Theme Park (Adults KRW 4,000) and Suncheonman Wetland (Adults KRW 10,000). Soesokkak is free, but the boat experiences are paid separately.
