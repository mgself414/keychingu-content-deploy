① Self-Service Overview — Next to the Counters on Departure Hall Level 3, the Key Is Shorter Lines

On level 3 of Incheon Airport's departure hall, self check-in kiosks and self bag-drop (checked baggage) machines sit right next to the airline counters. Even when the counter line is long, the self-service line is often empty — that's the most practical reason to use it. With just your passport and flight info, you can go from check-in to baggage drop entirely on your own, no staff needed.

That said, self-service is an option to use when it's available to you, not a requirement. If you fall into one of the cases in the ⑤ decision table below, heading straight for the counter is the right call.

② 2-Stage Master Flow — Kiosk Check-In → Self Bag Drop

The two machines sit side by side, but they're separate procedures. Just follow them in order.

STEP A. Kiosk Check-In — Getting Your Boarding Pass

Step What's on Screen What to Do Trap
1 Opening screen Start by scanning your passport Lay your passport open to the photo page on the scanner
2 「Flight Confirmation (항공편 확인)」 Confirm this is your booked flight Double-check the date and destination for typos
3 「Seat Confirmation (좌석 확인)」 Keep your existing seat, or change it If not changing, just tap [ Next (다음) ]
4 「Print Boarding Pass (보딩패스 출력)」 [ Print (출력) ] Wait until the paper comes out of the printer

STEP B. Self Bag Drop — Checking Your Baggage

Step What's on Screen What to Do Trap
1 Opening screen Scan your boarding pass and passport You need to scan both before it moves to the next step
2 「Place Your Baggage (수하물을 올려주세요)」 Load bags onto the belt one at a time, flat and lengthwise Bags over 90cm or with long straps go through the dedicated side chute instead
3 Weight display screen Just check the number If it's over limit, the screen warns you automatically — see the ⑤ decision table
4 「Prohibited Items Check (금지품 확인)」 popup Confirm no batteries or lighters, then tap [ Confirm (확인) ] The real answer is to never pack a power bank in checked baggage in the first place (see ④)
5 Tag printout Attach it following ③ below The belt won't move until the tag is attached
6 Your bag disappears behind the belt + receipt prints Keep the receipt If your baggage is lost, this receipt is your only proof

③ Printing and Attaching the Tag — Loop-Style, Where People Slip Up

The baggage tag the machine prints out is loop-style — a self-adhesive paper band you wrap around your bag's handle. This is where the most people get stuck.

  • Wrap the tag around a sturdy, fixed handle on your bag — attaching it to something that can slip off easily, like a zipper pull or an accessory loop, risks it falling off in transit.
  • Wrap it so the barcode side faces outward — if it faces inward, machines can't scan it and you'll have to print a fresh one from scratch.
  • Be sure to tear off the detachable stub at the end of the tag (your passenger copy) and keep it — you'll need it along with the receipt when matching your bag at baggage claim.
  • If you attach one wrong, don't just stick a second one over it — follow the screen's reprint instructions, or call staff over for a new tag.

The belt only starts moving once the tag is properly attached, so if the screen won't advance, nine times out of ten it's the tag attachment you need to check again.

④ Batteries Go in Your Carry-On — Never in Checked Baggage

Power banks and spare lithium batteries can't go in checked baggage. They must go in your carry-on and stay with you on the plane. A common mistake is packing one deep inside a suitcase, putting it on the belt, then getting flagged at the ②STEP B ④ 「Prohibited Items Check (금지품 확인)」 step and having to reopen the suitcase mid-line to dig it out. Sorting power banks and lighters into your carry-on bag while you're still packing at home saves you this time at the machine.

⑤ When Self-Service Isn't an Option — Decision Table for Going Straight to the Counter

Situation Self-Service OK? Why
Routes requiring visa verification ✗ Not possible Staff need to check your documents in person
Baggage over the weight or piece limit ✗ Not possible (in most cases) Paying overweight/excess fees is normally handled at the counter
Traveling with an infant (stroller, car seat, etc.) ✗ Not possible / limited Special baggage isn't something the machine can process
Traveling with a pet ✗ Not possible Requires separate paperwork and procedures
Group/party ticketing △ Not recommended Processing each person individually gets cumbersome
Simple solo travel, 1–2 bags ✓ Fine Follow the ② flow as-is

If you're not sure which category you fall into, just standing in front of the self-service machine is also a valid approach — if you don't qualify, the screen will tell you itself to go to the counter.

⑥ Getting Ready for Security — Have Liquids Under 100ml and Electronics Out in Advance

Once check-in is done, you head straight into the security line. Pack carry-on liquids into containers of 100ml or less and gather them into a clear zip bag ahead of time, and have large electronics like your laptop ready to pull out of your bag and place in a tray separately — doing this in advance saves time in line.

⑧ 3 Panic Scenarios

1. You attached the tag wrong — don't push forward as-is; follow the screen's reprint instructions or call over a nearby staff member. Forcing your way through with a bad tag only ends up slower.

2. An overweight warning popped up — this is a moment that calls for a judgment call: repack, or pay the excess fee — either way, self-service can't handle it. Head straight to the counter.

3. The machine keeps rejecting you (unclear why) — if it won't move past two or three tries, don't hesitate — go to the counter. Self-service is only an option for saving time; when it stalls, the counter is always open.

⑨ Plan B — The Airline Counter: Self-Service Is a Choice, Not a Requirement

Self check-in and self bag drop are options for saving time in line, not a procedure you're required to use. If anything feels even slightly confusing, or you fall into one of the ⑤ decision-table cases, just go straight to the counter and hand your passport and ticket to staff. Counter staff will handle the entire process for you, so there's zero problem in approaching it with "if it doesn't work, go to the counter" as your default, no pressure attached.
