Buying Electronics in Korea — Galaxy, Dyson, Laptops: How to Get the Tax Refund, Plus 5 Voltage, Warranty and Network Traps

Subtitle: Immediate refund = a single purchase of ₩15,000 (~$11) or more and under ₩1,000,000 (~$750), with a stay total of ₩5,000,000 (~$3,750) or less · Post-purchase refund = unopened, with the customs check first on departure (checking it in as luggage voids the refund) · Korean-market Galaxy phones support 5G and eSIM, but the warranty may be limited to the country of sale · Dyson is built for 220V — as of 2026-10-12

🎯 The Question This Guide Answers

"People say it's cheaper in Korea, but is it okay to buy and take home?" — in September 2026, on Reddit alone, 4 different travelers asked about buying a Galaxy, a Dyson or a Xiaomi. The price gap may be real. But if you claim the tax refund wrongly, the voltage doesn't match at home, or the warranty doesn't cover a breakdown, that gap disappears fast. This guide lists only what to check before you buy, in order.

① Tax Refund — Immediate and Post-Purchase

Korea's VAT refund for foreigners comes in two kinds.

  • Immediate Tax Refund: The tax is deducted on the spot when you pay at a participating store. The condition is a single payment of ₩15,000 (~$11) or more and under ₩1,000,000 (~$750), with a total of ₩5,000,000 (~$3,750) or less over your stay (VisitKorea, updated 2025-10-21). The "₩30,000 (~$22) or more" on some blogs is the old rule.
  • Post-purchase refund: You get a sales confirmation slip (receipt) at the store and claim the refund at the airport when you leave. Items over ₩1,000,000 (~$750), such as laptops and high-end phones, mostly fall here. Downtown refund counters handle up to ₩6,000,000 (~$4,500) per receipt.

Post-purchase refund steps (Incheon Airport)

1. Show your passport at the store → get the sales confirmation slip (refund slip) — check that it shows your passport number, name in English, nationality and refund method (if any are missing, export confirmation is not possible, Incheon Regional Customs Service)

2. On the day you leave, tell the airline at check-in that you have items for a tax refund

3. Scan your passport and refund slip at the refund kiosk; if it flags a customs check, show the actual item at the customs desk

4. After departure immigration, go to the refund counter

⚠ If you check the item in as luggage before the customs check, you cannot get the post-purchase refund (Incheon Regional Customs Service). It is safer to carry expensive electronics in your cabin bag rather than check them (Incheon Airport guidance). The item must leave the country within 3 months of purchase (VisitKorea).

Refund rates differ by card company and refund operator, and there is no official uniform figure.

② Voltage — If You're From a Non-220V Country, Rethink Appliances

Household power in Korea is 220V. Appliances like Dyson are designed for the voltage of the country where they are bought; using them in a country with a different voltage can cause permanent damage, and using a converter is not recommended (Dyson community reply [Secondary] — we could not open the manufacturer's official original this time).

  • Europe, Australia and most of Asia (220–240V): the voltage matches. Just check the plug shape
  • United States, Canada, Japan, Taiwan (100–120V): it is safer not to buy high-power appliances such as hair dryers and vacuums (it varies by product, so check the INPUT voltage on the unit's rating label)
  • Phone and laptop chargers are mostly dual-voltage, 100–240V. Check for 'INPUT 100-240V' on the back of the charger

In short, if you buy a Galaxy in Korea, repairs after you return home may be paid. If the price gap isn't large, an officially sold unit in your home country is better.

④ Galaxy — Check Network Bands and eSIM

Specs for the Korean-market Galaxy S26 on Samsung.com (checked 2026-10-12):

  • 5G: N1/2/3/5/7/8/12/20/25/26/28/66, N38/40/41/77/78
  • LTE: B3/7/8/20 and others
  • eSIM supported (SIM 1 + eSIM / Dual eSIM)

⚠ N71 is not on the list. It is a band used by some U.S. carriers, so if you plan to use it in the U.S., compare it with your carrier's bands first. The band list above is for the base S26. Check the band combination for other models such as the Ultra against your own carrier separately.

Galaxy phones sold in Korea come in two kinds: carrier-contract phones and unlocked (jagupje, 자급제) phones. Travelers buy only the unlocked kind. Example price: S26 256GB unlocked at about ₩1,140,000 (~$855) (2026-10-12, one open-market seller [Secondary], subject to change). It is over ₩1,000,000 (~$750), so it qualifies for a post-purchase refund.

⚠ 5 Traps That Actually Cost You Money

1. Checking the item in as luggage before the customs check — the post-purchase refund disappears.

2. Opening the packaging at the hotel — if it is not unopened and unused, it is not eligible for a refund.

3. Taking a Dyson to a 110V country — risk of damage, and overseas service isn't guaranteed.

4. Believing your Galaxy warranty works at home — a country-of-sale limit may apply.

5. Expecting an immediate refund on a receipt over ₩1,000,000 (~$750) — immediate refunds are only for under ₩1,000,000. Set aside separate time for the airport refund.

⚙ FAQ 3

1. Is Korea really cheaper? — It depends on the product, exchange rate and your home taxes. The Reddit case (Dyson ₩791,000 (~$593) vs €649 in Germany) is one person's comparison example. Before you buy, calculate your home price and the post-refund price together.

2. Will a copy of my passport work? — Showing the original passport is the basic requirement for a tax refund.

3. What about tax when I enter my home country? — Duty-free allowances differ by country. Expensive electronics may need to be declared to your home customs, so check before departure.
