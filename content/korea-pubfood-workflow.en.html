Korean Pub-Food Ordering & Etiquette Workflow — From Jangsu-dong (장수동) Jokbal to Gwangjang Market (광장시장) Bossam

Subtitle: A 5-step workflow for international visitors, plus drink pairing, saeujeot (새우젓, salted fermented shrimp) etiquette, and useful English phrases

ISSUE 03 · #862 · 2026-06-09

🎯 Why You Need a Korean Pub-Food Workflow Guide

Even knowing the Korean Pub-Food 6 Types overview, ordering, pairing, and etiquette are a separate matter. Korean pub food shows up in four very different settings: (a) Jangsu-dong Jokbal Alley (a late-night favorite open 17:00~03:00), (b) Gwangjang Market Bossam (a daytime spot and the #1 vlog destination), (c) Jongno (종로) pojangmacha (포장마차, street food tents) (soju (소주, Korea's classic distilled spirit) paired with golbaengi-muchim (골뱅이무침, spicy marinated whelk salad)), and (d) hanjeongsik (한정식, Korean full-course set meal) courses (suyuk (수육, boiled pork slices) and hongeo-samhap (홍어삼합, fermented skate combo)). International visitors typically (1) don't know saeujeot etiquette, (2) don't know jokbal and bossam are meant to be wrapped and eaten as ssam (쌈), (3) don't know soju drinking etiquette, and (4) don't know hongeo is an acquired-taste specialty, not an everyday dish. This guide covers the 5-step workflow, etiquette, and useful English phrases.

★ The 5-Step Workflow at a Glance

Step Action Time Key Point for Visitors
1️⃣ Pick a channel Jangsu-dong jokbal / Gwangjang bossam / pojangmacha / hanjeongsik Instant Match to late-night, daytime, or soju-focused goals
2️⃣ Order 6 types + 1 gwan (관, a full set for 2~3 people) / individual portion 1~5 min 1 gwan serves 2~3 people
3️⃣ Receive & set up Ssam greens, garlic, saeujeot, kimchi 5~15 min Wrap it as ssam
4️⃣ Eat & pair Pair with soju, makgeolli, or beer 30~90 min Drinking etiquette (elders first)
5️⃣ Pay Card OK / markets and nopo (노포, 30+ year traditional restaurants) = some cash / no tipping 1~3 min Splitting the bill (1/N) is fine

Total time: Hanjeongsik = 1.5~2.5h / Jangsu-dong jokbal = 1~2h / Gwangjang bossam = 30 min~1h / Pojangmacha = 1~3h

🍖 Workflow by Channel

Type A — Jangsu-dong (장수동) Jokbal Alley (Seongbuk (성북), Korea's #1)

  • Signature: A late-night favorite open 17:00~03:00, ranked #1 nationwide
  • Hours: 17:00~03:00 (5 PM~3 AM)
  • Price: ₩25,000~50,000 (~$18~37) per gwan (2~3 people) + about ₩12,000~25,000 per person for a single portion
  • Menu: Jokbal (족발, braised pig's trotters) — original or spicy —, mini jokbal, half-and-half
  • Visitor-friendliness: ★★★★★ (English menus, chain reliability)
  • Late-night favorite: The #1 pairing for drinks and a midnight bite

Type B — Gwangjang Market (광장시장) Bossam Alley

  • Signature: The #1 vlog spot, popular with MZ (MZ, Korea's term for its Millennial + Gen Z cohort) visitors on Instagram, open day and evening
  • Hours: 09:00~22:00
  • Price: ₩20,000~40,000 per gwan (2~3 people)
  • Menu: Bossam (보쌈, boiled pork wraps), bossam kimchi, ssam greens
  • Visitor-friendliness: ★★★★★ (top vlog spot, MZ favorite)

Type C — Jongno (종로) & Gangnam (강남) Pojangmacha (soju anju)

  • Signature: Golbaengi-muchim, a favorite among soju drinkers, ranked #1
  • Hours: 18:00~02:00
  • Price: ₩12,000~25,000 (varies by dish)
  • Menu: Golbaengi-muchim, rolled egg, udon, ramyeon
  • Visitor-friendliness: ★★★ (mostly Korean only)
  • Soju favorite: The #1 anju (안주, food that pairs with alcohol) and soju combo

Type D — Hanjeongsik Course (Suyuk & Hongeo-samhap)

  • Signature: A traditional 6-course hanjeongsik meal (see the Hanjeongsik series)
  • Hours: 11:30~22:00 (lunch and dinner)
  • Price: ₩40,000~150,000 for the hanjeongsik course
  • Menu: Suyuk, hongeo-samhap (part of the course)
  • Visitor-friendliness: ★★★★ (some English menus)

🍯 Ssam, Saeujeot & Soju Etiquette Overview

Wrapping It as Ssam (Jokbal, Bossam, Suyuk)

1. Place ssam greens (lettuce, perilla leaves, napa cabbage) on your palm

2. Add meat + garlic + a little saeujeot + kimchi

3. Add ssamjang (쌈장, thick dipping sauce) if you like — a spicier option

4. Wrap it with your hands and eat it in one bite (so it all fits in your mouth)

5. Chew with your mouth closed (Korean etiquette)

Saeujeot Etiquette

  • Amount: Just a little (it's very salty — about 1mm's worth)
  • Purpose: Served with jokbal, bossam, and suyuk to cut the pork's gamy taste
  • For visitors: Think "salty shrimp paste" — "just a tiny bit" is the rule

Soju Etiquette (Korean-Style)

1. Elders are poured for first — the youngest person pours for the oldest

2. Pour with two hands — one hand on the bottle, the other supporting your forearm

3. Receive with two hands — when an elder pours for you

4. Turn your head away to drink — when drinking in front of an elder

5. Geonbae (건배, "cheers") — clink glasses and say "Cheers (치얼스)!"

🌶 English Phrases for Visitors

  • "One jokbal set" = "One Jokbal set (for 2~3 people)"
  • "Bossam + soju" = "Bossam with soju, please"
  • "Not spicy" = "Less spicy, please"
  • "Hongeo = fermented" = "Hongeo = fermented skate (strong smell)"
  • "1/N" = "Split the bill, please"
  • "Cheers" = "Cheers! Geonbae!"

⚠ 7 Common Mistakes International Visitors Make

1. "Jokbal and bossam are the same thing": Jokbal is the trotter itself (a Jangsu-dong specialty), while bossam is boiled pork wrapped with kimchi (a Gwangjang specialty).

2. "1 gwan is a single portion": 1 gwan is meant to be shared by 2~3 people (order a single portion separately if you're eating alone).

3. "More saeujeot is better": Saeujeot should be used sparingly — it's very salty.

4. "Ssam is just eaten plain": Ssam means wrapping it up for one bite (so it all fits in your mouth).

5. "Pouring drinks with one hand is fine": Drinks should be poured with two hands, and elders are served first.

6. "Hongeo is a regular anju": Hongeo is an acquired taste with a strong ammonia smell.

7. "Jeyuk is only an anju": Jeyuk works both as a rice side dish and as an anju (great value either way).

⚙ 8 FAQs

1. Best channel for your first pub-food meal: Gwangjang bossam (daytime, vlog favorite) plus Jangsu-dong jokbal (nighttime).

2. For Instagram/vlogs: Gwangjang Bossam Alley, Jangsu-dong Jokbal Alley, Jongno pojangmacha.

3. MZ trend: Gwangjang bossam, MZ-favorite jokbal chains, jeyuk lunchboxes.

4. For soju lovers: Golbaengi-muchim, jokbal, and jeyuk.

5. Jangsu-dong hours: 17:00~03:00 (late-night favorite).

6. Allergies and pregnancy: See the disclaimer in the Korean Pub-Food 6 Types overview.

7. Payment and tipping: Card OK / markets and old-school spots = some cash / no tipping.

8. VAT refund: Food and drink generally aren't eligible for VAT refunds.

⚠ Medical, Safety & Dietary Disclaimer

Pub food here is ordinary food. Pregnant women can eat most items in moderation (avoid hongeo, which is fermented). Children are fine (adjust the spice level). Disclose allergies (pork, seafood, chili) in advance. Alcohol is not for pregnant women or minors. If you suspect food poisoning, call ☎ 1339. This guide provides general information only and is not medical advice.

Dietary restriction fallback:

  • Halal/Kosher: All 6 types contain pork or seafood / for halal-friendly options, try bibimbap or japchae instead.
  • Vegetarian/Vegan: None of the 6 types are vegetarian / try temple food or bibimbap instead.
  • Gluten-free: Suyuk, bossam, and jokbal are OK / golbaengi-muchim's somyeon (소면, thin wheat noodles) is not / some jeyuk seasonings are not.
