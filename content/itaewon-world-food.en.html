Itaewon World Food Tour Guide — 12 Global Dining Picks for Foreign Tourists

🎯 Why a Standalone Itaewon World Food Guide

Itaewon (이태원)·Usadan (우사단)·Haebangchon (해방촌)·Hannam (한남) form the area with the highest share of foreign residents in Korea — a legacy of the U.S. military base in Yongsan, plus a dense cluster of foreign embassies and global company HQs. Since the late 2010s this has become Korea's #1 world food zone. Restaurants from the Middle East, India, Mexico, American BBQ, Japan, Thailand, Africa, and 30+ other countries sit within a 15-minute walk of each other.

For foreign tourists on a 4–5 day trip to Korea, this is the go-to course on the day the craving hits: "anything but Korean food today." It's especially the most familiar route for visitors from Muslim countries, India, Latin America, and Europe. Pairs with #144 (Itaewon/Hannam area overview) and #155 (Itaewon restaurant guide) to complete a 3-part Itaewon dining series.

⚠ 3 Common Mistakes Foreign Visitors Make

1. Seeing "Itaewon" as only a club district — and missing the dining side

The most common mistake in the area. Visitors think of Itaewon only as a club and bar zone. In fact, Itaewon is Korea's #1 global dining area. The club strip (behind Hamilton Hotel) and the dining streets (Itaewon-ro, Usadan-ro) are separate. Day and evening are for dining; night is for clubs — split by time of day.

2. Not checking halal certification

Most restaurants on Halal Street are halal, but a few are not. Ask directly in English: "Is this halal-certified?" Halal-certified venues display Korean/Arabic certification marks. Marrakesh Night and Istanbul Kebab, among other authentic spots, are recommended.

3. Underestimating the uphill walk to Usadan-ro

The Usadan-ro Indian restaurant street is a 10–15 minute walk from Itaewon Station, uphill. That's a real load in midsummer or midwinter. A Kakao taxi (₩4,000~6,000) or the village bus (Seodaemun 03/05) is recommended. Weekday afternoons, 14:00–17:00, are quietest.

The Itaewon, Usadan, Haebangchon, Hannam Food Scene

Itaewon, Usadan, Haebangchon, and Hannam are Korea's home base for global dining — a distinct flavor of global diversity, set apart from Gangnam (luxury/Korean cuisine) and Hongdae (indie/casual).

Central Itaewon is halal and American territory — within a 5-minute walk of Itaewon Station Exit 3, Halal Street, Mama's, Blue Star Burger, and other global venues cluster together.

Usadan-ro is home turf for Indian and South Asian food — a 10–15 minute uphill walk from Itaewon Station leads to 5+ Indian, Nepali, and Filipino restaurants.

Hannam is global luxury plus American BBQ — Linus' BBQ, Everest Kitchen, and others line the Hannam-daero area.

Haebangchon is global fusion and trendy — Anderson Kitchen and other hilltop venues define the neighborhood.

① Halal & Middle Eastern (3 venues)

1. Halal Street

  • Address: Near Itaewon Station Exit 3, Yongsan-gu, Seoul
  • Signature: Turkish, Pakistani, and Middle Eastern restaurants clustered together. A halal-certified street — the #1 stop for Muslim visitors.
  • Foreigner-friendliness: ★★★★★ — English/Arabic menus, halal certification, top pick for Muslim-friendly dining
  • Recommended dishes: Lamb kebab, hummus, falafel, Turkish chai
  • Price range: ₩10,000~18,000 per person (~$7~13 / ~55~100 RMB / ~970~1,740 JPY)
  • Tip: One minute's walk from Itaewon Station Exit 3. The top pick for Muslim visitors and anyone who enjoys Middle Eastern/Turkish food.

2. Marrakesh Night

  • Address: Itaewon area, Yongsan-gu, Seoul
  • Signature: Authentic Moroccan/North African restaurant. Tagine, couscous, mint tea — the top pick for Moroccan food among foreign visitors.
  • Foreigner-friendliness: ★★★★★ — English menu, Moroccan atmosphere, halal
  • Recommended dishes: Tagine (lamb, chicken, or vegetable), couscous, Moroccan mint tea
  • Price range: ₩15,000~25,000 per person
  • Tip: Weekday dinner recommended. Moroccan ambiance plus a great photo spot.

3. Istanbul Kebab

  • Address: Itaewon area, Yongsan-gu, Seoul
  • Signature: Authentic Turkish kebab and baklava restaurant. Döner kebab, İskender, baklava.
  • Foreigner-friendliness: ★★★★☆ — English/Turkish menu, halal
  • Recommended dishes: Döner kebab, İskender, baklava, Turkish chai
  • Price range: ₩12,000~20,000 per person
  • Tip: Good for lunch or dinner. Groups welcome.

② Indian & South Asian (3 venues)

4. Usadan-ro Indian Restaurant Street

  • Address: Usadan-ro area, Yongsan-gu, Seoul
  • Signature: A cluster of tandoori, curry, and naan restaurants. Rich vegetarian options — the top pick for Indian food and vegetarian travelers.
  • Foreigner-friendliness: ★★★★★ — English menus, strong vegetarian/halal options
  • Recommended dishes: Tandoori chicken, butter chicken curry, saag paneer, garlic naan
  • Hours: 12:00–22:00
  • Tip: An uphill street — a 10–15 minute walk from Itaewon Station. The top pick for vegetarian travelers. Quietest on weekday afternoons.

5. Everest Kitchen

  • Address: Hannam area, Yongsan-gu, Seoul
  • Signature: Nepali-Indian fusion. Momo (dumplings), dal bhat, Indian curry — the definitive Nepali food experience.
  • Foreigner-friendliness: ★★★★☆ — English menu, Nepali/Indian atmosphere, vegetarian options
  • Recommended dishes: Momo (Nepali dumplings), dal bhat (Nepali set meal), chicken chow mein
  • Tip: Part of the Hannam dining route. For visitors who enjoy Nepali or Indian food.

6. Anthill

  • Address: Usadan-ro 10-gil 51, Yongsan-gu, Seoul
  • Signature: Filipino fusion cuisine. Adobo, sinigang, Filipino desserts — familiar territory for Southeast Asian visitors.
  • Foreigner-friendliness: ★★★★☆ — English/Tagalog menu, vegetarian options
  • Recommended dishes: Chicken adobo, sinigang, lumpia, Filipino desserts
  • Tip: Part of the Usadan route. Familiar to Filipino and Southeast Asian visitors.

③ Mexican & American (3 venues)

7. Mama's

  • Address: Itaewon-ro area, Yongsan-gu, Seoul
  • Signature: Authentic Mexican tacos and burritos. Full English menu — familiar to American and Latin American visitors.
  • Foreigner-friendliness: ★★★★★ — English menu, Mexican atmosphere
  • Recommended dishes: Tacos, burritos, quesadillas, margaritas
  • Hours: 11:30–22:00
  • Tip: Good on weekdays and weekends. Groups welcome. An extra margarita runs ₩8,000.

8. Blue Star Burger

  • Address: Itaewon-ro, Yongsan-gu, Seoul
  • Signature: American craft burgers. A venue built for American and European visitors — English-speaking staff on hand.
  • Foreigner-friendliness: ★★★★★ — English service, American atmosphere
  • Recommended dishes: Signature burger, truffle fries, seasonal specials
  • Hours: 11:00–23:00
  • Tip: Good for lunch, dinner, or a late-night bite. Casual vibe.

9. Linus' BBQ

  • Address: Hannam area, Yongsan-gu, Seoul
  • Signature: Authentic Texas BBQ. Brisket, pulled pork, sides — the real deal for Southern American BBQ.
  • Foreigner-friendliness: ★★★★★ — English service, authentic American BBQ
  • Recommended dishes: Brisket, pulled pork, cornbread, coleslaw
  • Price range: ₩25,000~40,000 per person (~$18~29 / ~135~215 RMB / ~2,420~3,870 JPY)
  • Tip: 5-minute walk from Hangangjin Station. Best for groups of 4+. Weekday dinner recommended.

④ Global Fusion (3 venues)

10. Anderson Kitchen

  • Address: Haebangchon area, Yongsan-gu, Seoul
  • Signature: Haebangchon global fusion. A signature global menu plus a hilltop view — the #1 pick in Haebangchon.
  • Foreigner-friendliness: ★★★★☆ — English menu, Haebangchon view
  • Recommended dishes: Signature fusion course, seasonal menu, wine
  • Tip: On the Haebangchon hilltop. Best at sunset or for the night view. A great Instagram spot.

11. Gloria Jean's

  • Address: Itaewon area, Yongsan-gu, Seoul
  • Signature: Australian café fusion. Australian-style coffee and brunch — familiar to visitors from Australia and New Zealand.
  • Foreigner-friendliness: ★★★★☆ — English menu, Australian style
  • Recommended dishes: Australian flat white, brunch plate, signature dessert
  • Tip: Recommended for weekday brunch. Familiar to Australian and Commonwealth visitors.

12. Antique Street Cafes

  • Address: South of Itaewon Station Exit 3, Yongsan-gu, Seoul
  • Signature: Antique plus brunch cafés. Vintage furniture and antique interiors paired with brunch venues — an Instagram hotspot.
  • Foreigner-friendliness: ★★★★☆ — English menu, antique atmosphere
  • Recommended dishes: Brunch plate, signature coffee, seasonal dessert
  • Tip: Quietest on weekday afternoons. Combine with antique shopping and café time.

⚙ Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

Q1. Which venues have English menus?

A. All 12 venues have English menus and English-speaking service. Itaewon has Korea's highest share of foreign residents, so English works 100% of the time.

Q2. Which venues are halal-certified?

A. Top halal picks: Halal Street, Marrakesh Night, Istanbul Kebab, and the Usadan-ro Indian Restaurant Street. Look for Korean/Arabic certification marks at the entrance, and confirm in English: "Is this halal?"

Q3. Which venues have vegetarian/vegan options?

A. Indian restaurants: The richest vegetarian options (Usadan-ro Indian Restaurant Street). Vegan: Anthill, Gloria Jean's. Partial vegetarian: Marrakesh Night, Mama's.

Q4. Recommendations by price range?

A. ₩8K~18K: Halal Street, Istanbul Kebab, Blue Star Burger, Gloria Jean's, Antique Street Cafes.

₩15K~25K: Marrakesh Night, Usadan-ro Indian Restaurant Street, Everest Kitchen, Anthill, Mama's.

₩25K~40K: Linus' BBQ, Anderson Kitchen.

Q5. A one-day course for Muslim visitors?

A. 12:00 Halal Street (döner kebab) → 14:00 Usadan-ro Indian (halal) → 17:00 Marrakesh Night (Moroccan) → 21:00 back to an Itaewon hotel.

Q6. A one-day course for American and European visitors?

A. 11:00 Blue Star Burger → 13:00 Gloria Jean's → 17:00 Linus' BBQ → 20:00 Anderson Kitchen.

Q7. Hours and days off?

A. Standard hours are weekdays 11:00–22:00, weekends 11:00–24:00. During Ramadan, halal venues may shift their hours — check each venue's Instagram.

Q8. Combining Itaewon with other areas?

A. Itaewon → Hannam: 10-minute walk — global plus luxury. Itaewon → Haebangchon: 15-minute walk — add a view.

🌸 5 Seasonal & Time-of-Day Hooks

Spring (Mar–May): Haebangchon + Anderson Kitchen — a spring-view dinner. Best on weekdays in April.

Summer (Jun–Aug): Mama's, Gloria Jean's — Mexican and Australian café fare.

Fall (Sep–Nov): Linus' BBQ + Everest Kitchen — a fall BBQ pairing.

Winter (Dec–Feb): Halal Street, Marrakesh Night — warm Middle Eastern food.

Ramadan: Halal Street venues open after sunset — the top window for Muslim dining.
