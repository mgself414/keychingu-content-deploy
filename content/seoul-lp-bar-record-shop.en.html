Seoul LP Bar & Record Shop Tour — A Night Completed by One Song Request

Subtitle: From two-story LP bars tucked into Euljiro's back alleys to record shops in Hongdae and Yeonnam — a foreigner's introduction to Seoul's vinyl scene: song-request culture, etiquette, and buying tips

🎯 Why LP Bars in Seoul

Seoul nightlife has a third axis alongside clubs and pojangmacha tent bars (포차): LP bars. Narrow second-floor rooms stacked with turntables and thousands of vinyl records, where the owner spins requests written on paper slips one by one — Reddit keeps turning up foreign reviews like "found this looking for an unusual bar, best find of the trip" and "drinking while listening to the song I picked myself." Euljiro's (을지로) retro alley and the Hongdae music scene are the two anchor zones for this culture, and by day you can stop by a record shop to pick up K-pop or city-pop vinyl as a souvenir. This guide is the primer.

★ At a Glance — 3 Space Types

📍 Euljiro Axis — Retro Alley's Upper Floors (Naver-verified anchors)

LP bars cluster on the second floors of old buildings along Euljiro's printing-district alleys. Small signs and hidden entrances are the norm — a foreign review sums up the neighborhood's character: "hard to find, but that's what made it better."

  • Sunset Records (선셋레코드) (Jung-gu, Euljiro 12-gil 11, 2F) — a foreign-review-verified spot: "I got to hear the song I requested," "perfect for a pregame before dinner." The neighborhood's quirky flagship.
  • Koya Records (코야레코드) (Jung-gu, Euljiro 12-gil 28, 1F) — LP + wine + ice cream, all in one. Reviews like "the owner played every song I asked for" show the request culture is alive and well here.
  • Pyeonggyunyul (평균율) (Jung-gu, Chungmuro 4-gil 3, 2F) · Euljiro FM (을지FM) (Jung-gu, Chungmuro 5-gil 26, 2F) — alternatives on the same alley axis. If one's full, the next is a few minutes' walk away.

📍 Hongdae & Yeonnam Axis — Home Turf of the Music Scene

  • Sound Cafe Sori (사운드카페 소리) (Mapo-gu, Wausan-ro 159, 2F) · LP Bar Nangman Seyoung (낭만세영) (Mapo-gu, Yanghwa-ro 6-gil 87, 2F) — the classic Hongdae LP-bar axis.
  • Radio City Yeonnam (라디오시티 연남) (Mapo-gu, Donggyo-ro 32-gil 5) — a Yeonnam-alley bar-restaurant hybrid. Pairs well with dinner.
  • Record shops: Mieum Record (미음레코드) (Mapo-gu, Donggyo-ro 27-gil 25) · Mosaic West (모자이크웨스트) (Mapo-gu, Tojeong-ro 113) — the fun of digging through used vinyl. Over in Seongsu, there's also a themed vinyl cafe: Vinyl Seongsu (바이닐 성수) (Seongdong-gu, Achasan-ro 15-8, 2F).

🎶 Song-Request Culture — How It Works

1. Write it on paper: Most places hand you a request slip (or a napkin) to write down the artist and song title, then pass it to the counter. English is fine — pop, city-pop, and jazz all travel.

2. Wait your turn: Requests play in the order they came in. Expect a drink or two to pass before yours comes up — that's normal.

3. Don't talk over the volume: An unwritten LP-bar rule — keep your voice down when the music, not conversation, is the main event (especially late at night). Want a loud night instead? The pojangmacha scene (#1071) is the better fit.

4. Stay for your song: Leaving the room while your requested track is playing is considered rude to the owner.

🛍 Record Shop Buying Tips

  • K-pop vinyl is a top-tier souvenir: New idol LPs often come in limited editions, worth buying while you're here. City-pop and Korean indie reissues are another category foreign collectors seek out.
  • Check condition when digging used: It's standard practice to slide the record out of its sleeve and check for scratches before buying — "Can I check the vinyl?" is all you need to say.
  • Carrying it home: Carry-on, not checked luggage — records can warp or crack in cargo. For a 12-inch record, check it fits diagonally in your carry-on first.
  • Prices run roughly ₩30,000–60,000 for new; used varies widely — go by the shop's price tag.

⚠ 3 Common Mistakes

1. "I can show up late, like at a club" — Many LP bars peak before midnight and have limited seating. 8–10 p.m. is the safe window.

2. "Submit a whole list of requests at once" — One or two songs at a time is the etiquette. Handing over a whole list pushes other guests' turns back.

3. "Visit a record shop at night" — Many buying-focused shops close early in the evening. Shop by day, drink by night.

⚙ 3 FAQs

1. Can I go alone? — LP bars actually see plenty of solo guests. Sitting at the bar just listening to music isn't awkward at all.

2. Does English work? — The request slip itself is a written exchange, so the language barrier is low. A translation-app screen covers ordering just fine (#1339).

3. Is there a cover charge? — Most places have none — you just pay for drinks. That said, one drink per person is standard etiquette.
