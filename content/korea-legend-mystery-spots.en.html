Korea Folklore and Mystery Places Guide — The Dragon Stories of Munmudaewangneung, Gameunsaji and Daewangam Park, the Mysteries of Eoreumgol, Pyochungbi, Maneosa and the Jindo Sea Parting, and Gyerim and Yeonorang and Seonyeo: 20 Places

Subtitle: 20 places chosen from the "K-Mystery Destinations" theme of the Ministry of Culture, Sports and Tourism and Korea Tourism Organization "Attraction Discovery 100×100" public vote, whose Korea Tourism Organization listing describes a legend or a mysterious phenomenon · The places with a registered admission fee are Maisan Tapsa (adults 3,000 won), Nakhwaam (adults 2,000 won), Jeongbang Waterfall (adults 2,000 won) and Seongryugul (adults 5,000 won) · The places closed on Mondays are the Yeonorang and Seonyeo Theme Park Gwibigo and Seongryugul — confirmed via Korea Tourism Organization tourism data (TourAPI)

🎯 How to Use This Guide

This guide covers 20 of the 100 places chosen in the "K-Mystery Destinations" theme of the Ministry of Culture, Sports and Tourism and Korea Tourism Organization "Attraction Discovery 100×100" (명소발굴 100×100) (candidates recommended by travel experts, voted on by the public), whose Korea Tourism Organization tourism data listing describes a legend or a mysterious phenomenon. The ranks and vote counts in the table are the results of that vote.

The legends are only the stories written in the Korea Tourism Organization listings. Historical records and handed-down stories are mixed together, so where the main text says "legend has it", "is said to" or "there is a story that", read it as legend, not as fact.

Four places have a registered admission fee, and the rest are registered as free or have no registered fee value. The operating details are copied from values registered in the Korea Tourism Organization tourism data (TourAPI).

📍 Kings Who Became Dragons and Dragon Legends — Munmudaewangneung, Gameunsaji, Daewangam Park, Yongduam and Buseoksa

Gyeongju Munmudaewangneung (30-1 Bonggil-ri, Munmudaewang-myeon, Gyeongju, ☎ 054-779-8166) is the underwater tomb of King Munmu (문무왕) (reigned 661~681), the 30th king of Silla (신라). It lies 200m offshore from Bonggil-ri. According to the Korea Tourism Organization listing, King Munmu, who unified the Three Kingdoms, left a will asking that he be cremated and his ashes buried in the East Sea, saying he would become a dragon and guard against Japanese raiders, and he was buried as he wished. This rock is called Daewangam.

  • Opening hours: Always open. Closed days: Open year-round. Fee: Free.

Gyeongju Gameunsaji (17 Yongdang-ri, Munmudaewang-myeon, Gyeongju, ☎ 054-772-9289) is the site of a temple that King Munmu began building to ward off Japanese raiders and that his son King Sinmun (신문왕) completed in 682. Today only two three-story stone pagodas and the building foundations remain. According to legend, King Munmu, who had become a sea dragon, came and went through a drainage channel under the Geumdang (main hall). The Korea Tourism Organization tourism data carries an introduction from the Korea Heritage Service saying that the two pagodas in front of the Geumdang are the largest stone pagodas in Korea.

  • Opening hours: Always open. Closed days: Open year-round. Fee: Free. Parking: Free.

Daewangam Park (95 Deungdae-ro, Dong-gu, Ulsan, ☎ 052-209-3738) is introduced as the place where, according to legend, the queen of King Munmu became a guardian dragon after her death and sank beneath a rock in the East Sea off Ulsan. A 600m pine-forest path runs from the entrance to the Ulgi Lighthouse, and the park has a suspension bridge and the Ulgi Lighthouse.

  • Opening hours: Always open. Closed days: Open year-round.

Yongduam (15 Yongduam-gil, Jeju City, ☎ 064-740-6000) is a rock carved by waves and wind into the shape of a dragon's head. According to legend, a dragon that stole the jade ball of the Hallasan (한라산) spirit was shot with an arrow by the spirit and dropped onto the shore, where it hardened into stone. There is also a legend that a white horse that tried to become a dragon and rise to heaven was caught by a warrior and turned into the rock. The listing says English, Chinese and Japanese guide services are registered.

  • Opening hours: Always open. Closed days: Open year-round. Fee: Free.

Buseoksa (345 Buseoksa-ro, Buseok-myeon, Yeongju, ☎ 054-633-3464) is a temple founded by the Great Master Uisang (의상대사) in 676 and is a UNESCO World Heritage Site. It is said to have been named "floating stone" (Buseok) because the large rock west of the Buddha hall floats without touching the rock beneath it. A story is told that the guardian dragon of Master Uisang lived in the well west of Muryangsujeon Hall.

  • Opening hours: Always open. Closed days: Open year-round. Fee: Free.

📍 Phenomena Hard to Explain — Miryang Eoreumgol, Pyochungbi, Maneosa, the Shaking Rock and the Jindo Sea Parting

According to the Korea Tourism Organization listing, Miryang Eoreumgol, Pyochungbi and the gyeongseok (resonating stones) of Mount Maneosan (original wording: Eosan Buryeong gyeongseok (어산불영경석)) are called the "Three Mysteries of Miryang".

Miryang Eoreumgol (1647 Sannae-ro, Sannae-myeon, Miryang, ☎ 055-356-5640) is a valley on the northern mid-slope of Mount Jaeyaksan. Ice starts to form in the rock crevices around early March and generally lasts until mid-July, but the listing says that in recent years there have been many rainy days, so the ice does not stay as long as it used to. In winter, by contrast, warm steam rises from the crevices. The average summer temperature in the crevices where the ice forms is about 0.2℃.

  • Opening hours: Call to ask. Closed days: Open year-round. Fee: Free.

Pyochungbi (4 Dongbudong-an-gil, Muan-myeon, Miryang, ☎ 055-359-5640) is a monument erected in 1742 to honor the loyalty of the Great Master Samyeongdaesa (사명대사). It is introduced as the "Sweating Pyochungbi" because of the story that the stone sweat whenever something momentous was about to happen to the country.

  • Opening hours: Always open. Closed days: Open year-round. Fee: Free.

Maneosa (776 Mano-ro, Samnangjin-eup, Miryang, ☎ 055-356-2010) is a temple on Mount Maneosan, 674m above sea level. According to a story recorded in the Samguk Yusa (삼국유사), a great many fish and dragons of the East Sea, moved by the Buddha's sermon, gathered on the mountain and turned into stones, and those stones made the sound of a chime stone. Even today, striking the stones in front of the temple gives a clear ringing sound, so they are also called bell stones, and they are designated a Gyeongsangnam-do Monument.

  • Opening hours: 08:00~18:00. Closed days: Open year-round.

Seorak Heundeulbawi (1140-200 Seoraksan-ro, Sokcho, ☎ Seoraksan National Park Office 033-801-0900) is a round boulder that rests on a wide rock at the entrance to Gyejoam. It looks as though the slightest touch would topple it, but the listing says it shakes about the same whether one person pushes or many people push. It takes about 1 hour from Seoraksan Sojiwon.

  • Opening hours: Always open. Closed days: Open year-round. Fee: Free.

Jindo Sinbiui Badatgil (148 Sinbiuibadatgil, Gogun-myeon, Jindo-gun, ☎ Jindo County Office Tourism Division 061-540-3407) is a place where a sea path 30~40m wide and 2.8km long opens between Hoedong Village in Gogun-myeon and Modo in Uisin-myeon. The path is exposed for about 1 hour and then closes. According to legend, the villagers who fled tigers to Modo left behind Grandmother Ppong; she prayed to the Dragon King, the sea path opened and she met her family, but she then breathed her last, and from then on the villagers held a memorial rite every year. The listing says it became widely known when the French ambassador to Korea introduced the phenomenon as the "Miracle of Moses" in 1975. There is an experience hall, so you can experience it regardless of when the sea path opens.

  • Opening hours, closed days, fee: No registered value. The dates when the sea path actually opens are not in the Korea Tourism Organization information (the "last day of the second lunar month" in the legend is not an opening schedule).

📍 Founding Myths and Old Stories — Gyeongju Gyerim, Yeonorang and Seonyeo, Ondalsanseong and Nakhwaam

Gyeongju Gyerim (Gyo-dong, Gyeongju, ☎ 054-779-8585) is a forest between Cheomseongdae and Wolseong. Legend has it that Alji (알지), the founding ancestor of the Gyeongju Kim clan, was born here. It is said that in the reign of King Talhae of Silla, people who went to see after hearing a rooster crow in the forest found a shining golden box hanging from a branch, and a baby boy came out of it. The monument on the grounds was erected in 1803 and carries an inscription recording the birth of Kim Alji.

  • Opening hours: Always open. Closed days: Open year-round. Fee: Free.

Yeonorang and Seonyeo Theme Park Gwibigo (3012 Homi-ro, Donghae-myeon, Nam-gu, Pohang, ☎ 054-289-7955) deals with the story of Yeonorang and Seonyeo recorded in the Samguk Yusa. The story goes that when the couple Yeono and Seo-nyeo, who lived on the East Sea coast, crossed over to Japan, the sun and moon of Silla lost their light, and the light returned when a rite was held with the silk that Seo-nyeo had woven. The Korea Tourism Organization listing describes it as Korea's only sun-and-moon myth. The exhibition hall Gwibigo presents the tale through animation, VR and media experiences.

  • Opening hours: Park always open, Gwibigo exhibition hall and Silla Village 10:00~18:00 (last entry 17:30).
  • Closed days: Every Monday (the next first weekday if it is a public holiday), January 1, the days of Seollal and Chuseok. Fee: Free.

Danyang Ondalsanseong (Ha-ri, Yeongchun-myeon, Danyang-gun, ☎ Ondal Tourist Site 043-423-8820) is a half-moon-shaped stone fortress built on Mount Seongsan, 427m above sea level. The listing says the Samguk Sagi (삼국사기) records that Ondal (온달), son-in-law of King Pyeongwon of Goguryeo, built this fortress and died in battle fighting when Silla troops invaded. It is a place where the story of Fool Ondal and Princess Pyeonggang (평강공주) has been handed down.

  • Opening hours: Always open. Hiking is banned after 16:00 for the sake of safety and to prevent people being stranded. Closed days: Open year-round.

Nakhwaam (Ssangbuk-ri, Buyeo-eup, Buyeo-gun, ☎ 041-830-2884) is a cliff on the north side of Mount Busosan facing the Baengmagang River (백마강). The Samguk Yusa tells a story that when Baekje fell, court ladies threw themselves from this spot. It is said that the court ladies were likened to falling flowers, which is how it came to be called Nakhwaam. The characters "Nakhwaam" written by Song Si-yeol (송시열) are carved into the rock face.

  • Opening hours: Mar~Oct 09:00~18:00, Nov~Feb 09:00~17:00. Closed days: January 1, the days of Seollal and Chuseok.
  • Fee (individual): Adults 2,000 won, youths and soldiers 1,100 won, children 1,000 won. Includes Busosanseong Fortress and Goransa Temple.

📍 Stories of People and Nature — Maisan Tapsa, Yongmunsa, Hwangji Pond, Kkotji Beach, Jeongbang Waterfall and Seongryugul

Maisan Tapsa (367 Maisannam-ro, Maryeong-myeon, Jinan-gun, ☎ 063-433-0012) is known for the 80-odd stone pagodas built by the hermit Lee Gapryong (이갑룡). Legend has it that Lee Gapryong came to Maisan at the age of 25 and kept building pagodas until he died at 98. The Korea Tourism Organization listing says the pair of Cheonji pagodas behind Daeungjeon Hall are the largest.

  • Opening hours: Mar~Nov 08:00~18:30, Dec~Feb 08:00~18:00. Closed days: Open year-round.
  • Fee: Adults 3,000 won, middle and high school students 2,000 won, elementary school students 1,000 won.

Yongmunsa (782 Yongmunsan-ro, Yongmun-myeon, Yangpyeong-gun, ☎ 031-773-3797) has a ginkgo tree estimated to be about 1,100 years old and 42m tall, designated a Natural Monument. Two legends are told about this tree: that Prince Mauitaeja (마의태자), son of King Gyeongsun of Silla, planted it on his way to Mount Geumgangsan, and that it grew from the walking staff that Great Master Uisang stuck into the ground. It runs a Templestay program.

  • Opening hours: Always open. Closed days: Open year-round.

Hwangji Pond (12 Hwangjiyeonmot-gil, Taebaek, ☎ Taebaek City Tourist Information Center 033-550-2828) is the source of the Nakdonggang River. The listing says there is a legend that when Rich Man Hwang, who lived here, shoveled manure onto an old monk who asked for alms, heaven and earth shook and the site of his house became a pond. About 5,000 tons of water gush up per day.

  • Opening hours: Always open. Closed days: Open year-round. Fee: Free.

Kkotji Beach (Seungeon-ri, Anmyeon-eup, Taean-gun, ☎ Dangam-ri Tourist Information Center 041-670-2543) has Grandmother Rock and Grandfather Rock. According to legend, when Seungeon, the base commander of Silla's Jang Bogo (장보고), did not return from a campaign, his wife Mido, who waited gazing only at the sea, died and became Grandmother Rock. At low tide the two rocks are connected by a sandbar.

  • Opening hours: Mar~Oct 10:00~17:00, Nov~Feb 10:00~16:00. Closed days: Open year-round.

Jeongbang Waterfall (37 Chilsimni-ro 214beon-gil, Seogwipo, ☎ 064-733-1530) is described in the Korea Tourism Organization listing as the only waterfall in Korea that falls from land directly into the sea. There is a story that Seobul (서불), who came on the order of Qin Shi Huang to find the herb of immortality, did not find it, carved the characters "Seobulgwaji (서불과지)" into the cliff and went back.

  • Opening hours: 09:00~17:30. Closed days: Open year-round.
  • Fee: Adults 2,000 won, youths (ages 13~24), children (ages 7~12) and soldiers (corporal and below) 1,000 won.

Seongryugul (221 Seongryugul-ro, Uljin-gun, ☎ 054-789-5404) is a limestone cave 915m long (including the underwater section). The listing says it was originally called Seonyugul, meaning a place where immortals played; when Prince Bocheon (보천태자), son of King Sinmun of Silla, built a temple inside the cave it was called Seongryusa; and because of the small holes in the rock wall it became Seongryugul. A story is also told that during the Imjin War (임진왜란) some 500 residents took refuge in the cave, and Japanese soldiers blocked the entrance so that all of them starved to death.

  • Opening hours: Mar~Oct 09:00~18:00, Nov~Feb 09:00~17:00 (entry until 30 minutes before closing).
  • Closed days: Every Monday (the next day if it is a public holiday).
  • Fee (individual): Adults 5,000 won, youths and soldiers 3,000 won, children 2,500 won, seniors 1,000 won. The registered values also list free admission for those aged 65 and over, national merit recipients, people with disabilities and preschool children (with proof), which does not match the senior fee, so check on site.

⚠ 3 Things to Know in Advance

  1. A legend is a legend — What the main text writes as "legend has it" or "there is a story that" is the handed-down story carried in the Korea Tourism Organization listings.
  2. Check the Jindo Sea Parting dates separately — The dates when the sea path opens and the operating hours are not registered.
  3. Check hours and closed days — Hiking at Ondalsanseong is banned after 16:00, and Seongryugul and the Yeonorang and Seonyeo Theme Park Gwibigo are closed on Mondays.

⚙ FAQ 3

Q. Which places have dragon stories?

A. Munmudaewangneung, Gameunsaji, Daewangam Park (guardian dragon), Yongduam, Buseoksa (the well where a guardian dragon is said to have lived) and Maneosa (the story of fish and dragons turning into stones). Munmudaewangneung and Gameunsaji in Munmudaewang-myeon, Gyeongju, are linked by the same story.

Q. Which places cost money?

A. Maisan Tapsa (adults 3,000 won), Nakhwaam (adults 2,000 won, includes Busosanseong Fortress and Goransa Temple), Jeongbang Waterfall (adults 2,000 won) and Seongryugul (adults 5,000 won).

Q. What are the "Three Mysteries of Miryang"?

A. Eoreumgol, Pyochungbi and the gyeongseok of Mount Maneosan. According to the Korea Tourism Organization listing, these three are called the "Three Mysteries of Miryang". The three are in Sannae-myeon, Muan-myeon and Samnangjin-eup respectively.
