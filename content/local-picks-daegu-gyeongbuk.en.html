Locals' Picks ② Daegu & Gyeongbuk — 22 Eateries Korean Viewers Named in the Comments, Naver-Verified

Subtitle: An axis the Daegu one-day meta (#1001), Gyeongju KTX (#1115), and Andong Hahoe (#1053) never covered — pulled 22 Daegu and Gyeongbuk spots out of a nationwide roundup of local eateries Korean viewers recommended in YouTube comments, verified existence, address, and business type on Naver local search, and skipped ratings entirely. Daegu 9 → Daegyeong Line axis Waegwan and Gumi 5 → Gyeongju, Pohang, Andong, Mungyeong, Cheongdo 8. Gumi, Waegwan, Mungyeong, and Cheongdo are all first appearances on KeyChinGu.

ISSUE 06 · #1383 · 2026-09-18 · Locals' Picks ②

🎯 Why "Comment Recommendations" Is the Bar

This corner has one source. Viewers left comments naming their neighborhood eateries under a Korean YouTube channel's "nationwide matjip" video, and the most systematically organized comments by region got turned into a table of 217 spots. Since it's a list Koreans recommended to Koreans, foreign reviews are nearly nonexistent, and many are small-town restaurants that never make tourist lists. Part ① (#1382) covers this corner's criteria and limits.

KeyChinGu checked only whether the name, address, and business type actually exist on Naver local search (2026-09-17). Of 24 Daegu (대구) and Gyeongbuk (경북) spots, 22 were confirmed; the 2 that couldn't be confirmed (one each in Gumi and Gyeongju) were dropped. There are no visit reviews, rankings, or star ratings, and no hours or prices — check Naver Place the same day you go.

What sets this part apart from Part ① is rail. The Daegyeong Line (대경선), a commuter railway that opened December 2024, links Gumi (구미)–Waegwan (왜관)–Daegu–Gyeongsan (경산) by train, and the Jungbu Naeryuk Line KTX-Eum (중부내륙선 KTX-이음) that opened the same November put Mungyeong about 90 minutes from Pangyo. Half of the 22 spots sit on those two lines.

① Daegu — 9 Spots, Subway Lines 1·2·3

If the Daegu one-day course (#1001) and Palgongsan (#1005) are the tourist circuit, these nine are the neighborhood eateries viewers wrote in. Gomtang, gukbap, bindaetteok, saenggogi, Chinese food, bunsik — what Daegu locals eat on a weekday.

  • Oneuldo Bindaetteok (오늘도빈대떡) · 7-16 Dalgubeol-daero 446-gil, Jung-gu, Daegu (대구 중구 달구벌대로446길 7-16) · Korean · jeon/bindaetteok · Bangcheon Market flagship — Gyeongdae Hospital Station (Line 2), Exit 3, about 500m on foot

Bangcheon Market sits next to Kim Kwang-seok Street. Bindaetteok (also called nokdujeon, 녹두전, a mung bean pancake) is the market's signature food, usually ordered with makgeolli.

  • Pungjin Gomtang (풍진곰탕) · 11 Bongdeok-ro 1-gil, Nam-gu (남구 봉덕로1길 11) · Korean · gomtang/seolleongtang · Near Geondeulbawi Station (Line 3), bus 730 to Bongdeok-dong

Gomtang is a clear soup made by simmering ox bones and meat for a long time. Season it yourself with salt and scallion (same tip as the gomtang in #1191).

  • Namsane (남산에) · 20 Jungang-daero 66-gil, Jung-gu (중구 중앙대로66길 20) · Japanese restaurant · Downtown Jungang-daero — nearest station: check Naver Map's walking directions

A Japanese restaurant downtown between Jungang-ro and Banwoldang. For downtown Daegu, orient yourself around Banwoldang Station (반월당역), where Lines 1 and 2 cross.

  • Hoengseong Ttukbaegi (횡성뚝배기) · 624 Ansim-ro, Dong-gu (동구 안심로 624) · Korean · gukbap · Ansim Station (Line 1)

A gukbap spot in the Ansim area at the eastern end of Line 1. Ttukbaegi is the name of the thick earthenware bowl gukbap comes in.

  • Uga Saenggogi (우가생고기) · 17-4 Dalgubeol-daero 641-gil, Suseong-gu (수성구 달구벌대로641길 17-4) · Korean · raw beef/meat · Eastern Suseong-gu — check Naver Map for station/bus

Saenggogi, or mungtigi (뭉티기) as it's called in Daegu, is the city's well-known raw beef dish. It's uncooked beef, so it helps to know that going in if you're not used to it.

  • Buchu Japchae (부추잡채) · 290 Jibeom-ro, Suseong-gu (수성구 지범로 290) · Korean · Southeastern Suseong-gu residential area — bus/taxi from the station (check Naver Map)

The name is the dish. Buchu japchae is a Chinese-style stir-fry of chives and meat, eaten as a side with rice.

  • Dalgo Tteokbokki (달고떡볶이) · 54 Yaoeeumakdang-ro 39-gil, Dalseo-gu (달서구 야외음악당로39길 54) · Bunsik · general bunsik · Duryu Park — Duryu Station (Line 2), Exit 14

A bunsik spot next to Duryu Park and E-World. Tteokbokki, twigim (튀김, fried snacks), sundae (순대, blood sausage), and gimbap (김밥, rice rolls) are the basic bunsik set.

  • Obok Banjeom (오복반점) · 146 Myeongcheon-ro, Hwawon-eup, Dalseong-gun (달성군 화원읍 명천로 146) · Chinese · Chinese restaurant · Hwawon Station (Line 1), Exit 2, walking distance

A Chinese restaurant in Hwawon at the southern end of Line 1. Banjeom (반점) is an old-fashioned term for a Chinese restaurant.

  • Wonjo Ibang Ajimae (원조이방아지매) · 5 Hyeonpung-ro 6-gil, Hyeonpung-eup, Dalseong-gun, Sangseol Market area (달성군 현풍읍 현풍로6길 5 상설시장동) · Korean · Transfer to a bus from Seolhwamyeonggok Station (Line 1 terminus) (route/time: check Naver Map)

Hyeonpung is Daegu's far southwest corner, a restaurant inside Hyeonpung Market. Ajimae (아지매) is the Gyeongsang-do dialect word for ajumma.

For foreign visitors — Daegu's subway has three lines, 1, 2, and 3, and downtown transfers happen at Banwoldang Station (Lines 1 and 2). Gomtang and gukbap can be ordered solo; saenggogi usually comes in single-serving portions too, just confirm it's raw beef before you sit down.

② Daegyeong Line Axis — Waegwan 2 · Gumi 3

The Daegyeong Line, a commuter railway that opened 2024-12-14, connects Gumi–Sagok–Buksam (added 2026-02-28)–Waegwan–Seodaegu–Daegu–Dongdaegu–Gyeongsan. Trains run about every 19 minutes at rush hour and about every 30 minutes off-peak. Gumi to Daegu is about 30 minutes, so you can base in Daegu and cover Waegwan and Gumi in a half-day.

  • Malligung (만리궁) · 7 Seokjeon-ro 12-gil, Waegwan-eup, Chilgok-gun (칠곡군 왜관읍 석전로12길 7) · Chinese · Chinese restaurant · Daegyeong Line Waegwan Station
  • Kkakkuri Jjamppong (까꾸리짬뽕) · 21-10 Pyeongjang-gil, Waegwan-eup, Chilgok-gun (칠곡군 왜관읍 평장길 21-10) · Chinese · Chinese restaurant · Daegyeong Line Waegwan Station

Two Chinese restaurants in Waegwan-eup. Jjamppong (짬뽕) is spicy seafood noodle soup, and together with jjajangmyeon it's one of the two pillars of Korean-Chinese food. Both are inside the town from Waegwan Station — check Naver Map for walking minutes.

  • Kim Tae-ju Seonsan Gopchang (김태주선산곱창) · 77 Songwonseo-ro, Gumi-si (구미시 송원서로 77) · Korean · gopchang/makchang · Around Gumi Station (Daegyeong Line/KTX) — walking/bus: check Naver Map
  • Daehan Sikdang (대한식당), Daehan Gopchang · 44 Nammun-ro, Seonsan-eup, Gumi-si (구미시 선산읍 남문로 44) · Korean · gopchang/makchang · Bus 170 between Gumi Station and Seonsan Terminal (171-1, 171-4 as backup)

Two gopchang (곱창) spots in Gumi. Seonsan gopchang is the name for a stir-fried beef small-intestine dish that started in Gumi's Seonsan-eup, and the list has both the downtown branch and the original Seonsan shop.

  • Jeokdong Guksu (적동국수) · 1039 Gangdong-ro, Sandong-eup, Gumi-si (구미시 산동읍 강동로 1039) · Korean · noodles · Sandong-eup — bus route from Gumi Station/terminal: check, taxi is more realistic

A noodle shop in Sandong-eup on Gumi's east side. Guksu here mostly means janchi guksu (잔치국수), noodles in anchovy broth, as the default.

For foreign visitors — Gopchang is stir-fried beef intestine, so it splits opinion. Many places require a minimum order of two servings, and finishing with fried rice is the local way. Noodles and Chinese food can be ordered solo.

③ KTX Axis — Gyeongju (경주) 3 · Pohang (포항) 1 · Andong (안동) 1 · Mungyeong (문경) 2 · Cheongdo (청도) 1

  • Chunghyo Sikdang (충효식당) · 476 Taejong-ro, Gyeongju-si (경주시 태종로 476) · Korean · Bus toward downtown from Gyeongju Station (경주역) (formerly Singyeongju Station, renamed 2023-12-28) — stop/time: check Naver Map
  • Nai Pad Thai (나이팟타이) · 734-1 Taejong-ro, Gyeongju-si (경주시 태종로 734-1) · Thai food · Taejong-ro — bus from Gyeongju Station, check

A Korean restaurant and a Thai restaurant on Taejong-ro, the street that cuts east-west through downtown Gyeongju. Gyeongju's tourist circuit (#1115, #1366) runs through Hwangnidangil and Daereungwon, and Taejong-ro is the residential area to its west.

  • Almond Chicken (아몬드치킨) · 5-1 Naa 2-gil, Yangnam-myeon, Gyeongju-si (경주시 양남면 나아2길 5-1) · Chicken · Bus 150 from Gyeongju Express Bus Terminal (toward Yangnam), about 1 hr 20 min, roughly hourly

A chicken restaurant in Yangnam-myeon, Gyeongju's southeastern coast. It's on the same road as the Yangnam columnar joints (jusangjeolli, 주상절리), so it makes sense to pair them on the same day.

  • Ikkadaeto (이까대토) · 148 Guryongpo-gil, Guryongpo-eup, Nam-gu, Pohang-si (포항시 남구 구룡포읍 구룡포길 148) · Korean · Guryongpo 1 bus from Pohang Station (first bus 07:00, last bus 18:30, about every 40 minutes)

Guryongpo is a port town known for gwamegi (과메기, wind-dried saury) and its street of Japanese-era houses. With the last bus at 18:30, dinner here is a stretch.

  • Dongakgol Geumjae Garden (동악골금재가든) · 166 Dongakgol-gil, Waryong-myeon, Andong-si (안동시 와룡면 동악골길 166) · Korean · dakbokkeumtang · Andong Station (KTX-Eum) → bus 510, 511–514 toward Waryong-myeon

A dakbokkeumtang (spicy braised chicken) spot in Waryong-myeon, on the opposite side from Hahoe Village (#1053). Garden (가든) is Korea's naming convention for a large suburban restaurant.

  • Yeongsun Sikdang (영순식당) · 269 Yeongsun-ro, Yeongsun-myeon, Mungyeong-si (문경시 영순면 영순로 269) · Korean · baekban/home-style · Mungyeong Station (Jungbu Naeryuk Line KTX-Eum, about 90 minutes from Pangyo, 8 round trips a day) → taxi to Yeongsun-myeon, check
  • Solbat Sikdang (솔밭식당) · 6 Jungang 6-gil, Mungyeong-si (문경시 중앙6길 6) · Korean · Downtown Mungyeong — access from the station/terminal: check

Mungyeong was only reachable by bus until KTX-Eum arrived on 2024-11-30. Baekban (백반) means a full table of rice, soup, and side dishes, and Yeongsun Sikdang is that kind of baekban place.

  • Tambok Minari Garden (탐복미나리가든) · 382 Hanjae-ro, Cheongdo-eup, Cheongdo-gun (청도군 청도읍 한재로 382) · Korean · Across from Cheongdo Station (Mugunghwa) terminal → bus/taxi toward Hanjae (route: check)

Hanjae is Cheongdo's minari cultivation area, and eating minari with samgyeopsal (삼겹살, pork belly) in spring (Feb–Apr) is the local custom. The season is short, so check timing.

For foreign visitors — Gyeongju, Andong, Mungyeong, and Pohang stations are all outside downtown or away from the old town center. Budget time assuming you'll need one more bus or taxi ride, not a walk straight from the station. For taxis, just show the Korean-script address.

🚄 Fitting This Into a Regional Trip

  • Daegu base + Daegyeong Line — the 9 Daegu spots by subway, the 5 Waegwan/Gumi spots by Daegyeong Line. Gumi Station is about 30 minutes out, so lunch in Waegwan and dinner in Gumi work in one day.
  • Fold into a Gyeongju (#1115) overnight — the two Taejong-ro spots are near downtown, and Yangnam's Almond Chicken fits a half-day toward the jusangjeolli columnar joints. #1372–1374 cover in detail how Gyeongju Station sits outside downtown (the old Singyeongju Station).
  • Seoul to Mungyeong in a day — about 90 minutes by KTX-Eum from Pangyo. Only 8 round trips a day, so check the timetable first.
  • Andong (#1053) — from Andong Station (KTX-Eum), Hahoe is to the west and Waryong-myeon is to the east. Doing both the same day is tight.
  • Pohang Guryongpo — bus from Pohang Station every 40 minutes, last bus 18:30. Plan on a lunch visit.
  • Changnyeong and Geochang (#1382) — those routes run mainly out of Daegu's West Bus Terminal, so it's better to tack them onto this piece's Daegu itinerary.

⚠ 3 Common Mistakes

1. Walking from Gyeongju Station into downtown — Gyeongju Station is the former Singyeongju Station, outside downtown. Plan on one bus or taxi ride.

2. Visiting Guryongpo in the evening — the last Guryongpo 1 bus from Pohang Station is at 18:30. Plan it for lunch.

3. Treating the Daegyeong Line like a KTX timetable — it's a commuter train running every 30 minutes off-peak. Just tap your transit card for Waegwan and Gumi.

⚙ 3 FAQ

1. Who picked this list? — It's a table of local eateries Korean YouTube viewers wrote in the comments. KeyChinGu only confirmed existence, address, and business type, and didn't rank anything.

2. Do eup and myeon restaurants take cards? — More do now, but cash and bank transfer are still the smoother culture. See #1301 for cash prep.

3. Why does Daegu get nine spots? — In the roundup, Daegu and Gyeongbuk ranked second only to Seoul, and 9 of them are inside Daegu proper. See #1001–1005 for Daegu's tourist circuit.
