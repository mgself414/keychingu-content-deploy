Korea's Cash-Only Zones, No-Panic Manual — Exactly Where and How Much

> Subtitle: Complete guide to the cash-only exception zones in Korea, one of the world's most card-driven societies — cash-needed/not-needed decision table · scenario-based sense for "how much cash to carry today" · the real moments you actually use cash · how to deal with leftover change · a one-paragraph ATM summary for when you run out of cash · 8 survival words · pojangmacha & traditional market panic scenarios

① Korea Is a Card Society — So Why Worry About Cash at All

Korea runs on card payments at a level few countries match. Convenience stores, cafes, restaurants, taxis, marts — anywhere you go, one card ends the transaction. The problem sits right outside that "anywhere." Pojangmacha (포장마차) street food tents, old stalls in traditional markets, some street vendors, and rural areas either have no card infrastructure at all, or the owner simply only takes cash. If you trust the "it's a card society" assumption and carry nothing but cards, you can end up stuck at exactly the most "authentically Korean" spots.

② Cash-Needed / Not-Needed Decision Table — Which Category Is Where You're Headed

Category Place Note
Cash needed Most pojangmacha (포장마차) tents Many have no card terminal at all
Cash needed Old traditional-market stalls (e.g. bindae-tteok/빈대떡 pancake stalls at Gwangjang Market) The older and more open-air the stall, the more likely it's cash-only
Cash needed Some street vendors (street snacks, etc.) Small cash-only transaction culture
Cash needed Rural areas / small provincial towns Card terminal coverage is lower than Seoul or Busan
Mixed (prepare for both) Tourist-area street stalls (Myeongdong, etc.) Some stalls take cards, varies stall by stall
Cash not needed Convenience stores, cafes, most restaurants, taxis, marts Card / mobile payment is the default

> Rule of thumb: the older the stall or cart looks, and the older the owner appears, the higher the odds you'll need cash. If you're not sure, just ask "Kadeu dwaeyo? (카드 돼요?)" before you order (see ④ survival words).

③ "How Much Cash Should I Carry Today" — Scenario-Based Cash Sense

This is the heart of this piece. You don't need to carry the same amount every day — just adjust it to that day's plan.

Today's plan Recommended cash Why
Market / pojangmacha touring day About ₩50,000 This is the day cash-only payments pile up. Without cash, you can barely eat your way through it
General sightseeing day ₩20,000–30,000 as a buffer Cards are the default, but you want a buffer for mixed zones (Myeongdong street stalls, etc.)
Card-heavy day ₩10,000 as a buffer A route centered on cafes, restaurants, and shopping. This is your just-in-case minimum
  • Think of your buffer cash as "insurance for when the card doesn't work," not "money to spend" — that framing makes the right amount easier to judge.
  • The safest approach for your first few days is to start generous, at the market/pojangmacha-day benchmark (about ₩50,000), see how much you actually use, and scale down from the next day on.

④ The Moments You Actually Use Cash

  • Paying at pojangmacha tents and old traditional-market stalls: the same cash-needed zones from ②.
  • Charging a transit card with cash: at subway station chargers or convenience-store counters, putting cash directly into a T-money or similar transit card. This is one of the few everyday moments where you put in cash instead of tapping a card.
  • Temple donation boxes: donation boxes at tourist-visited temples run on cash by default.
  • Not for tipping: Korea has no tipping culture (billing procedure covered in #1275). The cash discussed here isn't for tips — it's a payment method for zones where cards simply don't work. Don't mix the two up.

⑤ 8 Survival Words — All You Need to Get Through a Cash Situation

Romanization (한글) Meaning
Hyeongeum (현금) Cash
Kadeu dwaeyo? (카드 돼요?) Card OK? — the essential question before you pay
Kadeu an dwaeyo (카드 안 돼요) No card (when the owner says it first)
Hyeongeum-man dwaeyo (현금만 돼요) Cash only
Gyejwa-iche (계좌이체) Bank transfer
Jandon (잔돈) Change
Eolma-yeyo? (얼마예요?) How much?
Chungjeon (충전) Top-up / recharge (for transit card charging)

> The key is one habit: ask Kadeu dwaeyo? (카드 돼요?) before you pay. That one line wipes out the entire awkward-rejection moment.

⑥ Dealing With Leftover Change — Clearing Out Coins and Bills at the End of Your Trip

Korean cash comes in small coin denominations, and if you're still carrying them by the end of your trip, they become dead weight.

  • Clear it out via a pre-departure transit card top-up: charge your remaining cash onto a T-money or similar transit card and use it for your last rides, including getting to the airport. A small leftover balance afterward isn't a big deal.
  • Clear it out via small convenience-store purchases: on your last day, buy water or snacks at a convenience store with cash to work through your coins first.
  • Airport re-exchange is the last resort: coins often can't be re-exchanged, or the fee is steep. Clearing them out with the two methods above first is the better deal.

⑦ When You Run Out of Cash — ATM in One Paragraph

If you run out of cash, don't just grab any ATM — look for one with a Global ATM logo (common at convenience stores and major bank branches). The full guide to the foreign-card withdrawal procedure, fee structure, and the DCC ("charge you in your home currency?") trap, walked through screen by screen, is in #1286 ATM cash withdrawal guide.

⑧ 2 Panic Scenarios — What Actually Happens

1. You tried to pay with a card at a pojangmacha and got turned down — no reason to panic. Most street stalls simply have no card terminal to begin with. Smile and ask back, "Hyeongeum-man dwaeyo? (현금만 돼요?)" — "Cash only?", and if you have none, head to a nearby convenience-store ATM or a different stall that takes cards.

2. A market vendor suggests a bank transfer — locals are comfortable with bank transfers, but it's realistically impossible for a traveler without a Korean bank account or app. In that case, politely say, "Hyeongeum-euro naelgeyo (현금으로 낼게요)" — "I'll pay in cash", and most vendors will accept that. Just note this keeps adding coins to your pile, so keep ⑥'s clear-out method in mind.

⑨ Plan B — If You Want to Solve Cash and Transit Card in One Move

If calculating a cash amount every day feels like a hassle, WOWPASS (a foreigner currency-exchange + transit card combo) is an alternative. Airport and major-station issuing machines let you exchange currency and get transit card functionality at the same time — with that card, you can also cover some of the ②-listed cash-needed zones. Issuing procedure and how to use it are covered in #1298 Transit card guide.
