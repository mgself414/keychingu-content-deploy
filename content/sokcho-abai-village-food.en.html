Sokcho's Food Came Down from the North — Abai Village (아바이마을) and 18 Places Serving the Flavors of Displaced Families

Subtitle: Sokcho is usually introduced as the base camp for Seoraksan, and its food story often ends with one line: dakgangjeong (닭강정, sweet-crunchy fried chicken). But the lineage of Sokcho's food points north, not south. Abai Village (아바이마을) was built on the Cheongho-dong (청호동) shoreline during the Korean War by refugees from Hamgyeong Province (함경도); here you find Abai Sundae (아바이순대) carried on into its third generation, and a Hamhung naengmyeon (함흥냉면) house that opened in 1951. From the Gaetbae (갯배), a boat pulled across the water by hand on an iron cable, to the Haksapyeong (학사평) tofu village — how to read Sokcho through its meals

🎯 Why "Came Down from the North"

Articles about Sokcho food mostly start with dakgangjeong and end with dakgangjeong. It is tasty and it really is a Sokcho specialty. But walk around downtown Sokcho and the menus carry unfamiliar names: Abai Sundae (아바이순대), Ojingeo Sundae (오징어순대, squid sundae), Myeongtae-hoe Naengmyeon (명태회냉면) — names you don't often see in Seoul. Put Sokcho's Hamhung naengmyeon (함흥냉면) alongside them,

These names all point in one direction. Hamgyeong Province (함경도).

During the Korean War, around the January 4th Retreat (1·4후퇴, January 1951), people from Hamgyeong Province (함경도) followed the South Korean army south. Even after the fighting ended, the road back to their hometowns stayed blocked, and they built homes on a shore not far from the armistice line. That place is Cheongho-dong (청호동), Sokcho — today's Abai Village (아바이마을). The village name comes from Hamgyeong dialect: "abai" (아바이) means an older man, and since many of the refugees were elderly men, the word is said to have become the name of the village.

That is why Sokcho food is the food of displaced families. Abai Sundae is the sundae (Korean blood sausage) those people brought with them; Hamhung naengmyeon is the cold noodle dish of Hamhung (함흥) in South Hamgyeong Province (함경남도); and Myeongtae-hoe Naengmyeon puts pollock caught in the East Sea on top of that method. This is not a meal to grab before climbing Seoraksan — it is food that is a whole story in itself.

On top of this comes food shaped by Sokcho's geography: grilled fish and mulhoe (물회, chilled raw fish soup) from the harbors, sundubu (순두부, soft tofu) of Haksapyeong (학사평) at the gateway to Seoraksan, and dakgangjeong you eat by hand.

🛶 Abai Village (아바이마을) — A 50 m Crossing by Gaetbae (갯배)

Abai Village sits across the channel where Cheongcho Lake (청초호) meets the sea. The waterway is just over 50 m wide, but going around by car takes a long time. So the villagers made the Gaetbae (갯배).

The Gaetbae has no engine. An iron cable is stretched across the water, and a person on board pulls the cable along with a hook to move the boat. Visitors are often let try pulling it themselves. A device displaced families built for their own way across became a Sokcho landmark.

Once across, the restaurants line up along a single street, Abai Maeul-gil (아바이마을길).

  • Dancheon Sikdang (단천식당) (Abai Maeul-gil 17) — known for Abai Sundae. A family that settled in Abai Village after the war has run the shop for three generations, from mother to son to grandson, over more than 40 years. The name Dancheon (단천) is also a place name in South Hamgyeong Province (함경남도). Its menu holds the whole point of this piece — Abai Sundae, Ojingeo Sundae (오징어순대, squid sundae), Abai Sundae Gukbap (아바이순대국밥), Myeongtae-hoe Naengmyeon (명태회냉면). 08:30–19:00 (last order 18:30), open year-round — the fewest time limits of any place here.
  • Sindasin (신다신) (Abai Maeul-gil 22) — one minute on foot from Dancheon Sikdang, on the same street.
  • Abai Hoe Guksu (아바이회국수) (Cheongho-ro 115-12) — hoe guksu (회국수) is noodles topped with raw fish tossed in a tangy sauce.

Ojingeo Sundae (오징어순대) needs explaining. Instead of pig intestine, the filling goes inside a squid body, which is steamed and sliced. In a region where squid was abundant on the East Sea, the casing of the sundae changed.

🍜 The Noodles the Displaced Families Brought

Hamhung Naengmyeon-ok (함흥냉면옥) (Cheongchohoban-ro 299) opened in 1951. It started in the middle of the war and has lasted more than 70 years, and on that record it was designated a "Baekyeon Gage" (백년가게, Century Shop) by the Ministry of SMEs and Startups in 2018. 10:30–20:30, closed Wednesdays.

Hamhung naengmyeon differs from Pyongyang naengmyeon. Where Pyongyang naengmyeon means buckwheat noodles in a clear broth, Hamhung naengmyeon has thin, chewy noodles mixed with seasoning, and you drink the broth separately. Add myeongtae-hoe (명태회, seasoned raw pollock) on top and it becomes Sokcho-style Myeongtae-hoe Naengmyeon (명태회냉면) — the result of putting an East Sea fish on the Hamgyeong (함경도) method.

Makguksu (막국수) belongs to a different family. It is a Gangwon buckwheat noodle dish, and this piece includes two makguksu shops plus one naengmyeon shop in the same county, Goseong.

  • Baekchon Makguksu (백촌막국수) (Baekchon 1-gil 10, Toseong-myeon, Goseong-gun) — known for a single thing, buckwheat noodles. 10:00–17:00 (break 13:55–14:10), closed Wednesdays (some sources say it is also closed Tuesdays) — check the weekday before you go.
  • Namgyeong Makguksu main branch (남경막국수 본점) (Daepohang Huimang-gil 47, Sokcho) — it's near Daepo Port, so it pairs with a harbor itinerary.
  • 3-Dae-ga Hamkke-hanun Jeontong Omi Naengmyeon (3대가 함께하는 전통 오미냉면) (Ayajin Haebyeon-gil 73, Toseong-myeon, Goseong-gun) — toward Ayajin Beach in Goseong.

🐟 Harbor Meals

Sokcho has several harbors. Dongmyeong, Daepo, Bongpo and Jangsa Ports lie in different directions, so the trick is to pick the harbor closest to where you're staying.

  • Dongmyeong Port Ojingeo Nanjeon — nanjeon means a street stall. You buy straight at the harbor.
  • 88 Saengseon Gui — saengseon gui comes as several kinds of fish on one table.
  • Cheongchosu Mulhoe Sokcho main branch — Mulhoe is raw fish with a cold, seasoned broth poured over it. It is known as a summer dish, but Sokcho serves it year-round.
  • Bongpo Meoguri Jip — meoguri is a word for a diver.

🥘 Haksapyeong Sundubu Village — Since 1965, Seawater Tofu

At Haksapyeong, at the gateway to Seoraksan, sundubu restaurants cluster. Among them, Kim Yeongae Halmeoni Sundubu sets its tofu with seawater instead of the usual coagulant.

  • Kim Yeongae Halmeoni Sundubu main branch — opened in 1965. The tofu is made with domestic soybeans and seawater. 07:00–14:00, closed Tuesdays — it closes at 2 p.m. The schedule fits a breakfast before you climb Seoraksan.
  • Changbawi Sikdang — on the same Wonam Haksapyeong-gil (원암학사평길). It's in the baeksuk and samgyetang family.
  • Gamnamujip Gamja Ongsimi — it's on the Central Market side, not Haksapyeong, but it is the same family of Gangwon food. Gamja Ongsimi is a soup with dumplings made from grated potato dough dropped in.

🍗 Sokcho You Eat by Hand

  • Mangseok Dakgangjeong main branch — a shop that has made only dakgangjeong for 30 years in Sokcho. 10:00–20:00, open year-round. Dakgangjeong is fried chicken coated in sauce, made to hold its taste even after it cools, so many people take it to go and eat it on the train.
  • Sokcho Oksusu Sogeumppang — salt bread with corn.
  • Gangwon-do Makgeolli Sulppang — stall no. 54 inside Central Market. Sulppang is a steamed bun fermented with makgeolli.

☕ A Shipyard Turned Cafe

Chilseong Joseonso is a cafe that kept the site of a former shipyard as it was, where boats used to be built. You can see Cheongcho Lake, and it operates as a cafe that doubles as a small museum and history-and-culture space. Opens at 11:00. Closing time (19:00 or 20:00) and closing days (closed Wednesdays or open year-round) differ by source, so check before visiting.

Since it is a place where the fact that Sokcho was a fishing town remains in the building, reading it on the same axis as Abai Village makes the day hang together.

💰 Budget and Diet — Can You Avoid Meat?

Prices fall into three tiers. The figures below are not menu prices but the average spend per person recorded by Revit Guide (레빗가이드) users — which is why a bakery can pass ₩10,000: people buy several items, not one. Use them only to tell the tiers apart.

  • Up to the ₩10,000s (₩5,000–13,600) — Gangwondo Makgeolli Sulppang (강원도막걸리술빵) about ₩5,000, Chilsung Boatyard (칠성조선소) about ₩10,700, Omi Naengmyeon (오미냉면) about ₩11,300, Hamhung Naengmyeon-ok (함흥냉면옥) and Abai Hoe Guksu (아바이회국수) about ₩12,000, Kim Youngae Halmeoni Sundubu (김영애할머니순두부) about ₩13,000, Sokcho Oksusu Sogeumppang (속초옥수수소금빵) about ₩13,600 (buying several).
  • Around ₩20,000 (₩19,900–22,000) — Dancheon Sikdang (단천식당) about ₩19,900, 88 Saengseon Gui (88생선구이) and Bongpo Moguri-jip (봉포머구리집) about ₩20,000, Namgyeong Makguksu (남경막국수) about ₩20,300, Mansuk Dakgangjeong (만석닭강정) about ₩20,000–22,000 (one takeaway tub), Sindasin (신다신) about ₩13,000–20,000.
  • ₩25,000 and up — Cheongchosu Mulhoe (청초수물회) about ₩25,500, Baekchon Makguksu (백촌막국수) about ₩26,100 (with side dishes such as pressed pork), Dongmyeong Port Squid Nanjeon (동명항 오징어난전) about ₩29,000 (what you pay to buy and carry out), Changbawi Sikdang (창바위식당) about ₩67,500 (a whole chicken shared between people).

Can you avoid meat? The list splits three ways.

  • Pork — Abai Sundae and Abai Sundae Gukbap (아바이순대국밥) at Dancheon Sikdang (단천식당). The signature dish of this piece happens to be pork.
  • Seafood — mulhoe (물회), grilled fish, squid sundae, the diver's house. If you are avoiding pork, this is the widest lane.
  • Soybean, buckwheat, potato — sundubu (순두부) at Kim Youngae Halmeoni Sundubu (김영애할머니순두부), made with domestic soybeans and seawater; makguksu (막국수) at Baekchon and Namgyeong; gamja ongsimi (감자옹심이) at Gamnamujip (감나무집). This is the closest thing to vegetarian here.

That said, sundubu and makguksu can still carry meat or seafood in the broth or seasoning. None of the shops in the table is vegetarian-only or halal-certified — if you keep to it strictly, treat asking about the broth before ordering as a given.

🚗 Route — Some Stretches on Foot, Some by Car

In Sokcho, you move on foot around Abai Village and Central Market, and by car to Haksapyeong, the harbors, and Goseong. Without a car, see the bus format in #1212, but buses to Haksapyeong and Goseong are infrequent.

Day 1 — the walking stretch

1. Morning, 08:30 — cross to Abai Village by Gaetbae → Abai Sundae at Dancheon Sikdang. Sindasin is one minute on foot.

2. Lunch — back downtown, Hamhung naengmyeon or Myeongtae-hoe Naengmyeon at Hamhung Naengmyeon-ok. Closed Wednesdays.

3. Afternoon — walk a loop of Central Market (gamja ongsimi, makgeolli sulppang, corn salt bread). Mangseok Dakgangjeong main branch (Cheongchohoban-ro 72) is about 1.7 km south of the market, so it needs a separate stop on a walking route.

4. Before dinner — Cheongcho Lake from Chilseong Joseonso. Closing days differ by source, so check before visiting.

Day 2 — alongside Seoraksan

1. Morning, 07:00–09:00 — Kim Yeongae Halmeoni Sundubu. Closes at 14:00, closed Tuesdays. At the gateway to Seoraksan, it fits as the meal before a hike.

2. After your Seoraksan plans, head to the harbor — Dongmyeong Port, or Daepo Port (Namgyeong Makguksu).

A variation that adds Goseong: go north from Sokcho to Baekchon Makguksu → Ayajin (Omi Naengmyeon) → toward Geojin. Because Baekchon Makguksu is closed Wednesdays (Tuesdays too, per some sources) and closes at 17:00, that day and time set the schedule.

⚠ 3 Common Mistakes

1. Scheduling downtown on a Wednesday — Hamhung Naengmyeon-ok closes on Wednesdays, and Baekchon Makguksu in Goseong is also closed Wednesdays (Chilseong Joseonso is also said to close Wednesdays, but sources differ). On Wednesday, steer toward Abai Village (Dancheon Sikdang is open year-round) and the harbors.

2. Trying to eat sundubu for lunch — Kim Yeongae Halmeoni Sundubu closes at 14:00. It opens at 07:00, so it works if you pair it with a Seoraksan plan, but if you head out slowly for lunch, the door will be shut. Its Tuesday closing overlaps too.

3. Seeing Abai Village only as a "Gaetbae experience" — the Gaetbae crosses in five minutes. What this village holds comes after you cross — the restaurants on Abai Maeul-gil and the names on their menus. If you ride the Gaetbae and turn back, you have missed half of it.

⚙ 3 FAQs

Q. How is Abai Sundae different from Ojingeo Sundae?

Abai Sundae is Hamgyeong-style sundae, made thick by stuffing pig large intestine. Ojingeo Sundae differs in the casing — instead of pig intestine, the filling goes into a squid body, which is steamed and sliced. In a region where squid was abundant on the East Sea, the ingredient changed. Dancheon Sikdang serves both.

Q. Is Hamhung naengmyeon spicy?

It is mixed with seasoning, so it has a sauce — if you expect the clear broth of Pyongyang naengmyeon, it's different. By Korean standards, though, it is not extremely spicy, and since the broth comes separately, you can mix to adjust. Myeongtae-hoe Naengmyeon adds fermented pollock hoe on top for a tangy note.

Q. How many days does Sokcho need?

For food alone, two days and one night covers about half this list. Day 1 is the walking stretch (Abai Village, downtown, Central Market), Day 2 is Haksapyeong and the harbors. To see Seoraksan properly you need one more day, and going up to Goseong makes three days and two nights comfortable.
