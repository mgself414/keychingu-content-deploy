Hiking & Trekking in Korea — 12 Picks: Bukhansan, Inwangsan, Gwanaksan, and the 156km Seoul Dullegil

Series cross-ref: #148 Seoul Hiking Cluster · #149 Seoul Nature · #363 Seoul Trekking · #511 Seoul Hiking Matrix · #176 Walkable Seoul · #586 24h Hangang · #205 Seoul Parks Full Guide

Why Hiking in Seoul

Seoul is one of the few big cities with mountains right in its center. You can reach the summit of Bukhansan (북한산) (836m) by subway, and Inwangsan (인왕산) (338m) can be climbed within 30 minutes of Gyeongbokgung Station (경복궁역). Two things stand out for foreign visitors: no admission fee (except some cultural heritage zones inside national parks), and gear rental at the Hiking Tourist Centers. You can hike the basics without bringing any gear.

Seoul Hiking Tourist Centers (서울 등산관광센터) are located at 3 sites: Bukhansan (북한산), Gwanaksan (관악산), and Ansan (안산). They offer Korean, English, Chinese, and Japanese guidance, and low-cost rentals of hiking boots, poles, and crampons (about 5,000~15,000 won per day). Numerous media reports say the number of foreign users has risen noticeably as of 2024.

3 Common Mistakes Foreigners Make

1. Attempting the summit without hiking boots

Bukhansan (북한산) and Gwanaksan (관악산) have many rock sections. Every year there are accidents of people slipping in sneakers. Rent hiking boots at the Seoul Hiking Tourist Center (서울 등산관광센터) (confirmation recommended). If you can't, at least wear sneakers with good sole grip.

2. Under-preparing water and snacks

Convenience stores around Bukhansan (북한산) exist only at the foot of the mountain. For the Baegundae (백운대) course, carry at least 1L of water plus snacks. Even with a summer dawn start, watch for dehydration.

3. Misjudging descent time

"It'll take an hour to get down from the summit, right?" — on rocky courses the descent can actually take longer. For Bukhansan Baegundae (북한산 백운대), plan 2~2.5h down to be safe. After sunset, trail signs are hard to read.

In-Depth Info on Each Mountain

1. Bukhansan (북한산) (Baegundae 836m)

Location: Straddles Gangbuk and Eunpyeong-gu (은평구) in Seoul and Goyang-si (고양시) in Gyeonggi. Inside Bukhansan National Park.

Course: Gugi Visitor Center (구기탐방지원센터) → Daenammun (대남문) → Baegundae (백운대) (about 10km round trip, 4~5h). Beginners can stop at Daenammun (대남문) and still have plenty.

Foreigner fit: Official English guidance and maps from the national park. A Hiking Tourist Center (등산관광센터) at the Gugi-dong (구기동) entrance (gear rental, multilingual).

Admission: Free (for trails). Some temples inside the national park may charge a separate cultural heritage fee — on-site confirmation recommended.

Season: Azaleas in spring (Apr–May), autumn foliage (Oct–Nov). Crampons are essential when icy in winter.

Safety: Watch the rock ridge near the summit. Trails are closed in bad weather. Check the status on the entrance notice board.

Access: Line 3 Gupabal Station (구파발역) → Bus 704 → Gugi-dong (구기동) entrance (or Bukhansanseong (북한산성) entrance).

2. Inwangsan (인왕산) (338m)

Location: On the border of Jongno-gu (종로구) and Seodaemun-gu (서대문구), Seoul. Right west of Gyeongbokgung (경복궁).

Course: Inwangsan Dullegil (인왕산 둘레길) entrance (near Jahamun Tunnel (자하문터널)) → summit (about 4km round trip, 1~1.5h). You can descend to Seochon (서촌) or Hongje-dong (홍제동).

Foreigner fit: No. 1 view of downtown Seoul. Gwanghwamun (광화문), Namsan (남산), and Bukhansan (북한산) in one frame. However, English on guide signs is only partial.

Admission: Free.

Season: Accessible all four seasons. A cluster of spring azaleas. Watch for slipperiness when snow accumulates in winter.

Safety: Some rocky ridge sections. Overall difficulty is low.

Access: Line 3 Gyeongbokgung Station (경복궁역) → 25 min on foot or Bus 7022 → Inwangsan entrance.

Linked: After descending, Seochon (서촌) cafes and Tongin Market (통인시장) (#589 cross).

3. Gwanaksan (관악산) (629m)

Location: Gwanak-gu (관악구) and Geumcheon-gu (금천구), Seoul, bordering Gwacheon (과천) and Anyang (안양) in Gyeonggi.

Course: Seoul Nat'l Univ. entrance course (서울대입구 코스) (Nakseongdae Park (낙성대공원) → summit, 6~8km round trip, 3~4h). A Gwacheon (과천) side course also exists.

Foreigner fit: Views of southern Seoul. Rock scale similar to Bukhansan (북한산) but less known, so it's not crowded. A Gwanaksan Hiking Tourist Center (관악산 등산관광센터) exists (gear rental, multilingual guidance).

Admission: Free.

Season: Spring and autumn are best. Summer brings spectacular greenery on the rocky cliffs. Crampons are essential in winter.

Safety: Rocky ridge sections are of high difficulty. Some sections have steeper rock slopes than Bukhansan (북한산).

Access: Line 2 Seoul Nat'l Univ. Station (서울대입구역) → 20 min on foot or by bus.

4. Seoul Dullegil (서울둘레길) (156km, 8 Sections)

Location: A long-distance trekking route that links the mountains and outskirts of Seoul in one loop.

Section layout: Section 1 Surak·Bulamsan (수락·불암산) / Section 2 Yongma·Achasan (용마·아차산) / Section 3 Godeok·Iljasan (고덕·일자산) / Section 4 Daemo·Umyeonsan (대모·우면산) / Section 5 Gwanaksan (관악산) / Section 6 Anyangcheon (안양천) / Section 7 Bongsan·Aengbongsan (봉산·앵봉산) / Section 8 Bukhansan (북한산). (12~20km per section)

Foreigner fit: Official English app and maps (Seoul City Dullegil app (서울시 둘레길 앱)). All sections accessible by subway. Each section can be completed independently.

Admission: Free.

Season: Strongly recommended in spring and autumn. In summer, choose forest sections.

Access: Each section start connects to a subway station on foot. The app lets you enter a section based on your current location.

Tip: A full-route completion stamp book (operated by the City of Seoul, handed out on site) — there is a completion-certified souvenir (confirmation recommended).

5. Cheonggyesan (청계산) (Okneyeobong 618m)

Location: On the border of Seocho-gu (서초구) in Seoul and Seongnam (성남), Gwacheon (과천), and Uiwang (의왕) in Gyeonggi.

Course: Cheonggyesan-ipgu Station (청계산입구역) → Okneyeobong (옥녀봉) (about 6km round trip, 2~3h).

Foreigner fit: Close to Gangnam (강남). Many families on weekends. Feels safe given the crowds.

Admission: Free.

Access: Shinbundang Line Cheonggyesan-ipgu Station (청계산입구역) → 15 min on foot.

6. Namsan (남산) (262m)

Location: Central Seoul. Between Myeongdong (명동) and Itaewon (이태원).

Course: Myeongdong (명동) → Namsan cable car, or Chungmuro Station (충무로역) → Namsan Park → N Seoul Tower (1~1.5h on foot).

Foreigner fit: A must-do on foreign tourist routes. Complete English, Chinese, and Japanese guidance. N Seoul Tower observatory (paid). Cable car in operation (paid, about 12,000 won one way — on-site price confirmation recommended).

Admission: Park is free. Tower observatory is separate.

Access: Line 4 Myeongdong Station (명동역) Exit 3 → 15 min on foot or to the cable car boarding point.

Season: Year-round. The night view is especially good after evening.

7. Dobongsan (도봉산) (Jaunbong 739m)

Location: Dobong-gu (도봉구), Seoul, and Uijeongbu (의정부), Gyeonggi. The Dobong district of Bukhansan National Park.

Course: Dobongsan Station (도봉산역) → Cheonchuksa (천축사) → Dobong main ridge (도봉주능선) → Jaunbong (자운봉) (about 8km round trip, 3~4h). Access to the summit rock-climbing section is restricted for unlicensed climbers — use the bypass.

Foreigner fit: The same national park as Bukhansan (북한산). Spectacular rock scale. English guidance available.

Admission: Free.

Access: Line 1·7 Dobongsan Station (도봉산역) → 15 min on foot.

8. Suraksan (수락산) (638m)

Location: Nowon-gu (노원구), Seoul, and Uijeongbu (의정부) and Namyangju (남양주), Gyeonggi.

Course: Suraksan Station (수락산역) → Gichabawi ridge (기차바위 능선) → summit (about 6km round trip, 2.5~3.5h).

Foreigner fit: Views of strange-rock ridges. Quieter than Bukhansan (북한산). Convenient access.

Admission: Free.

Access: Line 7 Suraksan Station (수락산역) → 10 min on foot.

9~12. Other Nearby Mountains Around the City Center

  • Bulamsan (불암산) (508m): Line 7 Nowon Station (노원역) → on foot + bus. Can be linked to the Suraksan (수락산) ridge.
  • Ansan (안산) (296m): Seoul Hiking Tourist Center (서울 등산관광센터) Ansan branch. Near Line 5 Seodaemun Station (서대문역). Dullegil linked to Hongjecheon (홍제천).
  • Achasan (아차산) (295m): Line 5 Achasan Station (아차산역) → 10 min on foot. Views east of the Hangang (한강). Includes Goguryeo fortress ruins (보루).
  • Daemosan (대모산)·Guryongsan (구룡산) (293m): Near Line 3 Irwon Station (일원역). A short trek close to Gangnam (강남).

Practical Info: Transport, Gear, Language, Safety

Transport

Most major Seoul mountains are reached from a subway station on foot or with one bus ride. One T-money card (티머니) covers subway + bus + transfer discounts. T-money at the airport: can be bought immediately at convenience stores in Incheon Airport Terminals 1 and 2.

Gear

Items available to rent at the Seoul Hiking Tourist Centers (서울 등산관광센터) (Bukhansan Gugi-dong (북한산 구기동) · Gwanaksan (관악산) · Ansan (안산) branches):

  • Hiking boots, hiking poles, crampons, raincoats (some branches)
  • Rental fee: about 5,000~15,000 won per day (phone confirmation of price and hours before visiting recommended)

If buying your own, you can pick up inexpensive hiking gear at Namdaemun Market (남대문시장) and the Dongdaemun (동대문) sports street.

Language

  • Bukhansan (북한산) and Gwanaksan (관악산) national parks → English, Chinese, and Japanese guide signs and maps are relatively complete
  • Namsan (남산) → guidance in 4 languages
  • Cheonggyesan (청계산), Suraksan (수락산), and Bulamsan (불암산) → English guidance is partial (carrying a Korean map alongside is recommended)
  • Seoul Dullegil (서울둘레길) → the official app supports English
  • Naver Map (네이버 지도) English mode → trailhead entrances are searchable

Safety

  • Emergency numbers: 119 (fire and ambulance), 112 (police), 1330 (24h tourist interpretation), 1339 (infectious disease consultation)
  • National park trail closures: some sections are closed depending on weather and season. Check the entrance notice board or the Korea National Park Service (국립공원공단) app.
  • Night hiking: after sunset, trail signs are hard to read and the accident rate is high. Not recommended without a headlamp.
  • In lightning or heavy rain: descend from the ridge immediately. The Bukhansan (북한산) and Gwanaksan (관악산) summit areas have long exposed sections with a lightning risk.
  • Insurance: check before departure whether your overseas travel insurance covers injuries while hiking.

3 Full-Day Courses

Course 1. Beginner Full Set (Inwangsan + Seochon)

  • 10 AM — Depart from Line 3 Gyeongbokgung Station (경복궁역), Inwangsan (인왕산) summit (1~1.5h)
  • Noon — Take in the summit view, then descend toward Seochon (서촌)
  • 1 PM — Tongin Market (통인시장) dosirak (도시락) lunch box (#589 cross)
  • 2~3 PM — Seochon (서촌) cafes and alleys (#580 cross)
  • Total budget: 30,000~60,000 won (transport + meals)

Course 2. Intermediate Bukhansan (Day Trip)

  • 7~8 AM — Depart from Line 3 Gupabal Station (구파발역), ride Bus 704
  • 8:30 AM — Gugi-dong (구기동) entrance, rent gear at the Hiking Tourist Center (등산관광센터) (if needed)
  • 1 PM — Baegundae (백운대) summit (about 4h)
  • 3~4 PM — Descent complete
  • 5 PM — Meal in Eunpyeong-gu (은평구) or Hongje-dong (홍제동)
  • Total budget: 20,000~50,000 won (including gear rental)

Course 3. Seoul Dullegil Half-Day Experience (Section 2 Achasan)

  • 9 AM — Depart from Line 5 Achasan Station (아차산역)
  • 9:30 AM — Achasan (아차산) entrance, loop around the Goguryeo ruins
  • 12 PM — Toward the Guri (구리) Hangang (한강) riverside or return to the start
  • Total budget: 10,000~30,000 won (transport + snacks)

FAQ

Q1. For a foreigner's first hike, where should I start?

Inwangsan (인왕산) (338m) or Namsan (남산) (262m). 1~1.5h round trip, no special gear needed, views of downtown Seoul. Both mountains are easy to reach by subway. If you're intermediate or above, go straight to Bukhansan Baegundae (북한산 백운대).

Q2. Can I hike without gear?

Yes. Bukhansan (북한산), Gwanaksan (관악산), and Ansan (안산) have Hiking Tourist Centers (등산관광센터) where you can rent hiking boots, poles, and crampons cheaply. Approaching the summit without crampons when icy in winter is not recommended.

Q3. How much does it cost?

  • Mountain admission: Free
  • Gear rental: 5,000~15,000 won per day (on-site confirmation)
  • Cable car (Namsan (남산)): about 12,000 won one way (on-site confirmation recommended)
  • Guide (optional): around 50,000~100,000 won (searching guide companies separately is recommended)
  • Transport: use T-money, about 1,550~2,000 won one way

Q4. What is the best season?

Spring (Apr–May) for azalea and cherry blossom season, autumn (Oct–Nov) for foliage. These two seasons are the peak for foreign visitors. Summer is doable with a dawn start. Winter snowscapes are beautiful, but crampons are essential and body temperature management matters.

Q5. Any recommended maps and apps?

  • The official Seoul Dullegil app (서울둘레길 공식 앱) (English supported) — section guidance and stamps
  • The Korea National Park Service app (국립공원공단 앱) — trail closure status for Bukhansan (북한산) and Dobongsan (도봉산)
  • Naver Map (네이버 지도) English mode — practical for searching trailhead entrances
