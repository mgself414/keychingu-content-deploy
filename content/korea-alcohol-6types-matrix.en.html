Korea's 6 Types of Alcohol Matrix — From Soju to Traditional Liquor, a Complete Guide to Korean Drinking Culture

Subtitle: A visitor's guide to Korea's 6 alcohol types — soju, beer, makgeolli, cheongju, traditional liquor, and highball — plus enthusiast picks and food-pairing signatures

ISSUE 03 · #828 · 2026-06-08

🎯 Why a Korean alcohol matrix

The biggest pain point for visitors with Korean alcohol is "I only know soju." Korean alcohol ranks right behind K-drama and K-pop as a global entry point into Korean culture, and it's central to work dinners, chimaek, and hanjeongsik pairings. Under the umbrella of "Korean alcohol" sit very different drinks: soju (소주 — globally known as Jinro/Chamisul and Chum Churum), beer (Cass, Terra, Hite), makgeolli (traditional fermented rice wine), cheongju (authentic refined rice wine), traditional liquor (Andong Soju, Hansan Sogokju, Jeonju Moju), and highball (whisky + soda, an MZ trend) — each with its own strength, pairing, and culture. Visitors commonly (a) don't know soju is about 16–17% ABV, (b) don't know the makgeolli-shaking and pouring etiquette, (c) don't know the soju-to-beer ratio for somaek, and (d) don't know how to politely decline a forced bomb-shot round. This guide covers the 6-type matrix, pairings, and a recommended order for beginners.

🥃 Deep dive into Korea's 6 alcohol types

🍶 Soju — the global #1

  • Signature: Korea's #1 globally recognized liquor and the top pairing for work dinners, chimaek, and barbecue
  • Varieties:
  • Chamisul (참이슬, by Jinro) — Korea's #1 market share (Jinro founded in 1924)
  • Chum Churum — marketed for smoothness
  • Saero — low-ABV (16.5% down to 12%)
  • Fruit soju: grapefruit, peach, green grape, strawberry (the #1 starter pick for visitors)
  • ABV: 16–17% (low-ABV versions 12–14%)
  • Visitor-friendliness: ★★★★★ (globally known)
  • Price: KRW 4,000–6,000/bottle (restaurant) / KRW 1,500–2,500 (convenience store)
  • Recommended pairing: barbecue (grilled pork belly), fried chicken, sashimi, gukbap

🍺 Beer (Maekju)

  • Signature: Korea's #1 starter drink and the base of the globally trending "chimaek" (chicken + beer)
  • Varieties:
  • Cass — Korea's #1 market share (best value)
  • Terra — marketed as crisp and light
  • Hite — a classic
  • Imports: Tsingtao, Asahi, Heineken, Guinness (popular at Itaewon pubs)
  • ABV: 4–5%
  • Visitor-friendliness: ★★★★★ (globally known)
  • Price: KRW 4,000–8,000/bottle, KRW 5,000–8,000/glass (draft)
  • Recommended pairing: fried chicken, barbecue, raw fish restaurants, street snacks

🍯 Makgeolli — the traditional #1

  • Signature: Korea's traditional fermented rice-based liquor, sweet and smooth
  • Varieties:
  • Jangsu Makgeolli — Korea's #1 brand
  • Kooksoondang — authentic, also trending with MZ drinkers
  • Neurin Maeul — a premium line
  • Craft makgeolli breweries — Andong and Jinju favorites among enthusiasts
  • MZ trend flavors: milk makgeolli, peach makgeolli, strawberry makgeolli
  • ABV: 5–8%
  • Visitor-friendliness: ★★★★★ (smooth taste plus a following among tradition enthusiasts)
  • Price: KRW 5,000–10,000/bottle (750ml)
  • Recommended pairing: kimchi pancake, seafood pancake, bossam, mung bean pancake (the #1 choice on a rainy day)

🍵 Cheongju — the authentic choice

  • Signature: Korean refined rice wine (similar to Japanese sake), a classic pairing for authentic Korean cuisine
  • Varieties:
  • Kooksoondang Cheongju
  • Baekhwasoobok — a traditional, well-known brand
  • Non-alcoholic cheongju — used to pair with Korean course meals
  • ABV: 13–17%
  • Visitor-friendliness: ★★★★ (authentic, pairs with Korean course dining)
  • Price: KRW 8,000–25,000/bottle
  • Recommended pairing: hanjeongsik (Korean table d'hote), royal court cuisine, sashimi, Japanese cuisine

🏛 Traditional liquor (Jeontongju) — for enthusiasts

  • Signature: Regional traditional liquors made through authentic brewing methods, popular with connoisseurs
  • Varieties:
  • Andong Soju (45% authentic distilled spirit, diluted 20–22% versions also sold) — Andong's signature liquor
  • Hansan Sogokju (16%, from Hansan, South Chungcheong) — a 1,500-year history
  • Jeonju Moju (10–15%) — Jeonju's signature (made with medicinal herbs)
  • Jindo Hongju (40%) — Jindo's authentic liquor
  • Ikangju (25%) — from Jeonju
  • ABV: 15–45%
  • Visitor-friendliness: ★★★ (for enthusiasts, found at specialty shops)
  • Price: KRW 15,000–80,000/bottle
  • Recommended pairing: hanjeongsik, royal court cuisine, formal entertaining

🥃 Highball — the MZ trend

  • Signature: Whisky + soda water + lemon — smooth, and the top MZ generation trend
  • Varieties:
  • Whisky highballs: Suntory, Jack Daniel's, Chivas
  • Gimlet and gin-tonic (MZ variations)
  • Soju highball (an MZ variation)
  • ABV: 5–8% (diluted)
  • Visitor-friendliness: ★★★★ (MZ trend)
  • Price: KRW 8,000–15,000/glass
  • Recommended pairing: Japanese cuisine, fried chicken, MZ-style street food

Somaek (소맥, soju + beer) — Korea's #1 work-dinner drink

  • Ratio: 1:3 (1 part soju to 3 parts beer) or 1:2.5
  • How to make it: beer glass → pour beer → drop in a shot glass of soju → shake to mix
  • Popular with visitors: the classic drink of Korean work dinners

🍶 How to drink makgeolli

Makgeolli etiquette

1. Shake it: sediment settles at the bottom and clear liquid rises to the top — shake to mix before pouring

2. Pouring for others: pour with two hands for elders

3. Receiving a pour: hold your glass with two hands

4. Toasting: say "geonbae!" or "wihayeo!"

5. First glass: a sip or the whole glass — personal preference

English phrases for visitors

  • "Makgeolli han byeong" = "One makgeolli, please"
  • "Soju du byeong" = "Two bottles of soju"
  • "Somaek" = "Somaek (soju + beer)"
  • "Geonbae!" = "Cheers!"
  • "An masilraeyo" = "I don't drink, thanks"
  • "Han jan-man" = "Just one shot, please"

⚠ 7 common mistakes visitors make

1. "Soju is as strong as vodka or gin": Soju is 16–17% — less than half the ABV of 40% vodka. It's smooth, but Korean work-dinner culture involves many rounds, so total alcohol intake can add up.

2. "Makgeolli is weak": Makgeolli is 5–8%, relatively mild, but its fast fermentation can mean a stronger hangover the next day.

3. "You can't refuse a forced somaek round": Modern Korean drinking culture allows politely declining. Say "I don't drink" or hold your glass palm-up over the table.

4. "Highball is weak": The whisky base is 40%+. It's diluted down to 5–8%, but each glass still delivers about 1.5–2 standard drinks worth of alcohol.

5. "You don't need to shake makgeolli": Makgeolli must be shaken to mix the sediment (yeast) back in with the clear liquid on top.

6. "You can drink and drive in Korea": Korea's legal limit is 0.03% BAC = license suspension. Drinking and driving is strictly prohibited (fines plus license suspension).

7. "Traditional liquor is commonly consumed": Traditional liquor is mostly for enthusiasts, found at specialty shops. At ordinary bars, soju, beer, and makgeolli dominate.

⚙ 8 FAQ

1. Recommended order for beginners: beer (starter) → makgeolli (traditional) → soju (global) → somaek (work dinners) — in that order.

2. Instagram/vlog spots: makgeolli breweries, traditional liquor specialty shops, MZ highball bars.

3. MZ trends: fruit soju, low-ABV soju, highball bars, milk makgeolli.

4. Work dinners and entertaining: soju and beer (casual), cheongju and traditional liquor (formal entertaining).

5. Drinking etiquette: pour and receive with two hands for elders, turn your head away when drinking in front of an elder.

6. Drinking and driving: Korea's limit is 0.03% BAC = license suspension. Absolutely prohibited.

7. Allergies: disclose in advance — gluten (beer), soy (makgeolli, cheongju), fruit (fruit soju).

8. VAT refund: food and drink purchases generally aren't eligible.

⚠ Medical, safety, and legal disclaimer

Korean alcohol ranges from 4–45% ABV. Drinking and driving carries a 0.03% BAC license suspension, plus fines and criminal penalties (strictly enforced). Pregnant travelers and those on medication should not drink. Legal drinking age is 19 — minors may not drink under any circumstances. Disclose allergies in advance (gluten, soy, fruit, nuts). For suspected food poisoning or overdrinking, call 1339 (medical hotline, English available). This guide is general information, not medical advice.

Drinking etiquette:

  • It's fine to decline a round (accepted in modern Korean work-dinner culture)
  • Say "I don't drink" or hold your glass palm-up
  • Turn your head away when drinking in front of an elder (traditional custom)

Dietary-restriction fallback:

  • Halal/kosher: alcohol is not halal or kosher by default. Non-alcoholic options include sikhye and sujeonggwa (traditional sweet drinks)
  • Non-alcoholic: sikhye, sujeonggwa, and chrysanthemum tea (traditional beverages), or non-alcoholic beer and non-alcoholic cheongju
