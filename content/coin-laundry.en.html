One-line Summary

Seoul coin laundromats are best summed up by three phrases: 24 hours, unstaffed, 30-minute turnaround. They cost roughly one-tenth of hotel laundry, and any traveler staying four nights or more will run into one. This guide answers the three questions every foreigner has — where, how much, how — in a single pass.

Who This Is For

  • Self-guided travelers staying 4+ nights — anyone who does not want one-third of a suitcase taken up by dirty laundry
  • Pairs or groups of 2+ — one cycle clears a week's worth of clothes
  • Hotel-laundry sticker-shock victims — a single dress shirt at a hotel runs ₩6,000+, the same price as one full coin-laundry load
  • Long-stay Airbnb guests — the closest fallback when the unit has no washer
  • Active travelers — runners, hikers, or anyone needing yesterday's sweat-soaked clothes back tonight

4. Foreigner-Friendly Walkthrough — Six Steps to Finish in 30 Minutes

4-1. Arrival → Machine Selection (5 min)

1. At the entrance, distinguish the washer line from the dryer line — typically washers on the left, dryers on the right

2. Check the LED color next to each open machine: green = available, red = in use, blinking = finishing

3. First-timers should pick a Small or Medium unit. Even a full carrier fits in one 14kg medium

4-2. Loading + Detergent (5 min)

  • Roughly 90% of stores use automatic detergent dispensers — no need to bring your own
  • Sorting is optional — Korea's standard cycle (40°C standard) is designed to handle mixed colors safely
  • Exception: white dress shirt + brand-new jeans in the same drum risks dye transfer → wash whites separately

4-3. Payment (3 min)

Payment method Coverage Notes
Credit card (VISA / Mastercard / JCB / UnionPay) 95% Side of machine or central kiosk
Samsung Pay · Apple Pay 60% Newer machines
Transit card (티머니, T-money) 70% Most stable cashless option in Korea
App (런드리24 · 워시엔조이) 30% May require Korean phone number
Coins (₩100 · ₩500) 50% Newer stores increasingly do not accept coins

→ Best foreigner combo: T-money primary + credit card backup.

4-4. Wait Out the Wash Cycle (30–40 min)

  • Standard cycle: 30 minutes
  • Heavy / bedding cycle: 45–50 minutes
  • Most stores have chairs and USB charging outlets — a good slot for clearing email or Instagram
  • Do not leave the store unattended — Korea is generally safe, but unattended new unstaffed shops do see occasional petty theft

4-5. Drying (20–30 min)

  • When the wash ends, the LED blinks → move clothes to a dryer
  • Standard dry cycle: 30 min for ₩2,000–3,000
  • Tip: cotton pants and T-shirts dry in 30 min; denim needs 40 min

4-6. Pack Up and Leave (5 min)

  • If you leave clothes in the dryer for over 5 minutes after the cycle ends, the next customer may pull them out and stack them on the counter (local etiquette)
  • Use the in-store folding table to fold lightly and load back into your carrier
  • Take detergent containers and scent samples with you — leave the store clean

6. Frequently Asked Questions

Q1. Do I need to bring my own detergent or fabric softener?

A. About 90% of stores auto-dispense detergent — bringing your own is unnecessary. If you have allergies, ask the store whether they offer an "additive-free" cycle.

Q2. Will Korean detergent residue stay on my clothes?

A. The standard cycle includes two rinses by default → near-zero chemical residue. Safe even for sensitive-skin travelers.

Q3. They're called 24-hour, but is it really safe to go in the middle of the night?

A. Chain stores in Gangnam, Hongdae, and Itaewon see 3–5 a.m. customers regularly. That said, isolated stand-alone stores in quieter alleys are not recommended for a solo late-night visit.

Q4. The Korean name is "coin laundromat" — do I really not need coins?

A. The name is legacy. Card / app payment now covers 90% of transactions. No need to set aside coins.

Q5. Can I wash an entire suitcase in one go?

A. One 22kg large unit ≈ one 7-night traveler's full suitcase. The exception is suit fabrics and dry-clean-only items, which should be separated.

Q6. Can I wash shoes and sneakers too?

A. Many chains (Cleantopia, Laundry24) provide dedicated shoe washers at ₩4,000–5,000.

Q7. What if my clothes are lost or damaged?

A. Every store has entrance CCTV and a 24-hour chain hotline (Laundry24 1670-3737, WashNJoy 1577-1330). For losses, retain your receipt and call immediately.

Q8. Do they accept currencies other than Korean won?

A. No. Card · transit card · app payment only — which is precisely why no won coins are needed.
