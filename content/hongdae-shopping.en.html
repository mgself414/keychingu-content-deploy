Hongdae Shopping & Photo Guide: 60 Spots — Curated for International Tourists

Subtitle: Hongdae (홍대) · Yeonnam (연남) · Hapjeong (합정) · Mangwon (망원) Shopping & Photo Curation — A Deep Dive into the Shopping & Photo Category from #158 Comprehensive Guide

🎯 5 Key Tips for International Tourists

1. Yeonnam (연남) · Hapjeong (합정) · Mangwon (망원) = One Hongdae Route: All 4 sub-areas are within a 10–15 minute walk from Hongik University Station (홍익대학교역). String them together and you can hit 5+ spots in one go.

2. Weekday 14:00–17:00 Golden Hours: Weekends and late nights mean 1-hour+ waits. English-friendly spots are quietest — and most responsive in English — on weekday afternoons between 14:00 (2 PM) and 17:00 (5 PM).

3. Counter Seats & Reservations: Many smaller Japanese-style shops are counter-only. The golden window is around 19:00 (7 PM) on weekdays. English-capable venues accept reservations via Naver or KakaoTalk channel.

4. Foreign Cards Welcome at Many Spots: Hongdae is a top international tourist corridor, so foreign cards, Samsung Pay, and Kakao Pay (간편결제 — Korea's widely-used mobile payment apps, similar to Apple Pay or Alipay) are accepted at most spots. For traditional old shops (노포, nofo — 30+ year legacy restaurants) or tiny independents, carrying ₩30,000 (~$22 / ~160 RMB / ~3,200 JPY) in cash is recommended.

5. Insta & Photo Routes: Hanok alley (한옥 골목 — traditional Korean wooden house alley), galleries, vintage interiors, and themed cafés are everywhere. For the best shots, aim for 11:00 (11 AM) or after 19:00 (7 PM) when crowds thin out.

🎯 Spotlight Pick — Sihyeonhada (시현하다)

Founded in 2016 by photographer Kim Si-hyeon (김시현), this is the studio credited with redefining the ID photo. Instead of the standard white-background, formal-attire format, Sihyeonhada (시현하다) introduced colorful backdrops and retouching that captures each person's individual vibe — turning what used to be a bureaucratic formality into what SNS-generation Koreans now call an "identity photo." Celebrities including comedian Park Na-rae (박나래) have been photographed here, boosting its fame further. Today it operates multiple branches beyond the original Hongdae (홍대) space. Every set of photos is packaged in a cassette-tape-shaped case, designed to be collected one slot at a time over the years. Bookings run in 30-minute slots, with the full shoot, consultation, and retouching typically taking 60–90 minutes. Competition for reservations is high — book 1–2 weeks ahead as a baseline.

🎯 Spotlight Pick — Gentle Monster Hongdae (젠틀몬스터 홍대)

The most ambitious flagship a Korean eyewear brand has ever dared to build. Step inside and massive installation art and kinetic sculptures grab your attention before a single pair of glasses does. The signature move: every season, the entire interior concept changes and the space reopens like a gallery relaunch. Coming here to see the exhibition — not to shop — is a perfectly valid reason to visit. A standard stop on international tourist itineraries and essential viewing for anyone curious about what Korean contemporary design looks like right now.

🎯 Spotlight Pick — Thanks Books (땡스북스)

"Thanks Books (땡스북스)" is as close to the original model of an independent bookstore as Hongdae (홍대) has to offer. The curation is remarkably deliberate — bestsellers take a back seat to editor-recommended titles, self-published works, and art and design books. You'll discover books here that simply don't exist on the shelves of large chains like Youngpoong (영풍문고). A natural pairing: pick up a book and finish the afternoon at a nearby café.

🎯 Spotlight Pick — Small Pencil Shop Heuksim (작은연필가게 흑심)

The name says it all: this shop sells pencils, and only pencils. Pencils imported from Japan, Germany, the Czech Republic, the United States, and beyond are arranged in rows by type and lead hardness. Choosing one pencil at Heuksim (흑심) is something close to a ritual — you test it on paper, roll it between your fingers, and finally commit to the one that feels right. It has become a pilgrimage site for stationery lovers, calligraphers, and designers. The shop sits one alley off Hongdae's (홍대) main street — first-timers often miss it, so check the map before you go.
