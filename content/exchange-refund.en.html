Returns and Exchanges in Korea, the Zero-Panic Manual — Online and In-Store Run on Different Rules

Subtitle: Online purchases get 7 days by law, in-store does not — that one line is the whole story · chain-set windows like Daiso's 14 days and Olive Young's 15 days · what to do with no receipt · why opened items, sale items, and underwear get refused · foreign-only consultation lines exist too (20 languages). Includes Korean phrases to show staff directly

ISSUE 06 · #1393 · 2026-09-18 · korea-first-timer series

🎯 How to Read This Manual — Notation Rules

The part that trips people up most when they try to return something bought in Korea is "I thought the law guaranteed it, but it didn't." Online and in-store sit on completely different rules. This guide draws that line first, then lists what to bring to each window.

  • Signage and notice text goes in corner quotes: 「No exchange or refund for simple change of mind」
  • A hands-on action is bold: bring your receipt along with the card you paid with
  • A phrase to show staff directly is code: 환불하고 싶어요 (hwanbul-hago sipeoyo)

⚠ Notation principle: The windows below reflect the law and each chain's official guidance as of September 2026. For places like department stores and standalone roadside shops where it varies store to store, this guide does not give a day count. How a store handles the return of an instant tax-refund item internally could not be confirmed, so it isn't stated here — asking the store directly is the accurate move.

① Know This First — Online and In-Store Follow Different Rules

Category Legal Basis Can You Get It Reversed
Online purchase Article 17 of the Act on Consumer Protection in Electronic Commerce (전자상거래법 제17조) The rule is a right of withdrawal (청약철회) within 7 days of receiving it. The law guarantees it
In-store purchase Fair Trade Commission (공정거래위원회) Consumer Dispute Resolution Standards (소비자분쟁해결기준) Not a legal obligation. It's only a recommended standard — if the store posted notice in advance, refusing isn't illegal

This difference is the whole point. An in-store exchange or refund is customer service, not a right. That's why each chain sets its own window, and following it is enough.

Online is the opposite. Even if a site posts 「No refund for simple change of mind」, that notice alone doesn't erase the right online. It can only be limited if one of the statutory exceptions in ③ below applies.

② Returning In-Store — Windows Each Chain Sets

Here are the official standards for the two stores travelers actually visit most.

Store Window Conditions
Daiso Within 14 days of purchase Bring your receipt. Refunds only at the store you bought it from; exchanges work at any directly run store nationwide
Olive Young Within 15 days of purchase Unopened, undamaged item + receipt (app receipt counts) + the payment method you used. Refunds only at the store you bought it from

For department stores, standalone roadside shops, and traditional markets, this guide does not give a day count. It varies by store and brand. What you need to bring is the same everywhere, though — the receipt, the physical card you paid with, and the packaging with accessories.

Traveler tip — "refunds only at the store you bought it from" is the rule that trips people up most. If you're traveling and getting back to that specific location is hard, deciding right away, immediately after buying, is the safer move.

③ When You Can't Reverse It — The Five the Law Recognizes

The reasons a right of withdrawal (청약철회) can be limited for an online purchase are set out in the law.

1. The item was lost or damaged through your own fault — opening the packaging just to check the contents does not count here

2. Its value dropped significantly because it was used or partly consumed

3. Enough time passed that its value has dropped to where it's hard to resell

4. The packaging was opened on a product that can be copied or reproduced

5. Provision of a service or digital content has already begun

A made-to-order item can only be restricted if the seller gave notice in advance and got your written consent. Without that advance notice, you can still reverse it.

What actually trips people up: light-colored clothing, knitwear, underwear, leggings (they show marks the moment you wear them), opened cosmetics (falls under #2 if there's a sign of use), fresh food. That said, simply opening it to look does not by itself count as a restriction reason — it has to be damaged to the point it can't be resold.

「No exchange or refund on sale items」 is weak grounds on its own. Online, it can be a problem; in-store, it's up to the store's discretion if notice was given in advance.

④ When You Don't Have a Receipt

There's no rule set by law, so it's the store's discretion. That said, there are methods that work in practice.

  • Showing your card payment history often lets the store cross-check it against the slip they keep on file and process it. Bring the physical card you paid with, too.
  • Stores that use e-receipts let you pull the receipt up in the app. Daiso has cases of processing a return using card or membership payment history even without a paper receipt.
  • Paying cash with no receipt at all is the hardest case. Hold onto the receipt whenever you pay cash.

⑤ Reversing an Item You Got a Tax Refund On

At an instant tax-refund (즉시환급) store, you already pay the price minus the refund amount at the time of purchase. So the reversal process isn't the same as for a regular item.

This guide does not state how the store handles it internally — it could not be confirmed. But this much is clear.

  • Tell the store you already received the refund, first. Bring the sales confirmation slip or your passport along.
  • If your departure is close, reversing it at all is a time risk. It affects the airport procedure (#1304).
  • An item bought at an airport departure-area duty-free shop follows yet another procedure (#1332).

⑥ When Language Is a Barrier — There's a Foreigner-Only Line

Line Number Language & Hours
1330 Tourist Hotline 1330 24 hours, multilingual. The first line for reporting travel hassles like overcharging or rudeness
Korea Consumer Agency (한국소비자원) Foreigner Consultation 043-880-5400 English, Chinese, Vietnamese, Thai, Japanese, and more — 20 languages, weekdays 09:00–18:00 (excluding lunch 12:00–13:00)

The general consumer-consultation number (1372) is mostly Korean-language. For foreigners, the dedicated numbers above are faster. For help on the spot, call 1330; for formal dispute mediation, go through the Korea Consumer Agency.

⚠ 3 Common Mistakes

1. Believing an in-store refund is legally guaranteed — only online gets the 7-day legal guarantee. In-store is a service, and refusing is legal if notice was given in advance.

2. Demanding a refund at a different branch — for both Daiso and Olive Young, refunds only happen at the store you bought it from. Exchanges sometimes work at a different branch.

3. Leaving the card you paid with at home — canceling a card payment often has to go back onto that same card. Bring only the receipt and you'll be sent back for it.

⚙ 3 FAQ

1. Can I reverse an online purchase while I'm still in Korea? — the rule is 7 days from the day you received it. But the return address is inside Korea, so check first whether the process finishes before you fly out.

2. Is it automatically off the table once I open it? — No. Opening it just to check the contents isn't a valid reason to refuse. Only when it's damaged enough that it can't be resold.

3. Where do I go if I get refused? — 1330 on the spot, or the Korea Consumer Agency's foreigner consultation (043-880-5400) for formal mediation.
