Korean Hanbok Culture — Why Wear Hanbok to a Palace? Meaning, Structure & Revival of Korea's Traditional Dress

> Hanbok (한복) · Jeogori (저고리, upper jacket) · Chima (치마, skirt) · Obangsaek (오방색, five cardinal colors) · Free Palace Admission — an explainer on Korea's traditional dress for foreigners. Not a "pretty costume," but the "why, what, and how."

If you've ever walked in front of Gyeongbokgung (경복궁, Gyeongbok Palace), you've probably seen the scene: people in vividly colored hanbok (한복) taking photos against the palace's stone walls, and about half of them are foreigners. In fact, over the course of 2025, more than 2.07 million visitors entered Gyeongbokgung (경복궁), Changdeokgung (창덕궁), Deoksugung (덕수궁), Changgyeonggung (창경궁), and Jongmyo (종묘) dressed in hanbok (한복) — a jump of more than 260% in three years from roughly 540,000 in 2022. Royal-court costumes in K-dramas, the modern hanbok (한복) that BLACKPINK and BTS wear on stage, and the Bukchon Hanok Village (북촌 한옥마을) that has become a top photo spot — hanbok (한복) is now the hottest "wearable experience" in Korean travel. Yet most foreigners see hanbok (한복) as just a "pretty traditional dress." In truth, every curve, color, and occasion of hanbok (한복) carries meaning that is centuries old. This article isn't about "where to rent it" (→ place guide); instead it explains the structure, aesthetics, why people wear it, and how it was revived at a foreigner's eye level. This is installment #57 in the Korean culture explainer series.

★ At a Glance — The Composition of Hanbok

> Key Distinction: The jeogori (저고리) is shared by men and women; only the bottom differs — women wear the chima (치마, skirt), men the baji (바지, trousers). Rather than fastening with buttons like Western clothing, hanbok (한복) is closed with otgoreum (고름, ribbon ties) — its single most defining structural feature.

⚠️ Three Things Foreigners Often Misunderstand

1. "Hanbok (한복) = costume/cosplay" ❌ — Hanbok (한복) is not event makeup but Korea's official national dress. It is a living garment, still worn at life's important moments such as holidays, weddings, and a baby's first birthday (돌잔치, doljanchi). Rental outfits for photos are simply an easy entry point into that culture.

2. "Hanbok (한복) is just a flashy dress" ❌ — The colors and forms of hanbok (한복) carry meaning. The five colors (obangsaek (오방색, five cardinal colors)) come from the yin-yang and five-elements philosophy, and the curved beauty created when straight cloth meets the body is the core of hanbok (한복) aesthetics. It is not gratuitously flashy but a garment of restraint and harmony.

3. "Hanbok (한복) is old clothing in a museum" ❌ — Hanbok (한복) is not a fossilized past. From saenghwal hanbok (생활한복, everyday hanbok) worn as casually as jeans, to the fusion hanbok (퓨전 한복) BTS and BLACKPINK put on stage, all the way to the Milan runway — hanbok (한복) is fashion in present progress, still evolving today.

① What Is Hanbok — The Clothing of Koreans

Hanbok (韓服 / 한복) literally means "Korean clothing." It is Korea's own traditional dress, handed down since the Three Kingdoms period, and the form we picture today (jeogori (저고리) + chima (치마), jeogori (저고리) + baji (바지)) was largely established during the Joseon (조선) Dynasty (1392–1910).

The most fundamental feature of hanbok (한복) is that the top and bottom are separate. On top, both men and women wear the jeogori (저고리); on the bottom, women wear the chima (치마, skirt) and men the baji (바지, trousers). Unlike a Western suit cut to follow the body's lines, hanbok (한복) drapes loosely around the body, creating a natural line that moves with the wearer. That is why people say hanbok (한복) is not "clothing fitted to the body" but "clothing that holds the body."

② The Structure of Hanbok — Jeogori, Chima, Baji, Otgoreum

The first thing foreigners find strange when they put on hanbok (한복) is that there are no buttons. Hanbok (한복) is closed with ties. Let's go through the key parts.

  • Jeogori (저고리, upper jacket): The short upper garment worn on top — the basic item shared by men and women. It is made up of several parts.
  • Git (깃, collar): The collar that wraps around the neckline.
  • Dongjeong (동정, white collar trim): A white strip of cloth laid over the collar. A symbol of tidiness and cleanliness, it is always kept pure white.
  • Seop (섶, front overlap panels): The left and right panels that overlap when closing the front.
  • Somae · Baerae (소매·배래, sleeve · curved sleeve underline): The curve beneath the sleeve (baerae (배래)) is one axis of hanbok (한복) curved beauty.
  • Otgoreum (고름, ribbon tie): The long tie that closes the front of the jeogori (저고리). A long goreum (고름) and a short goreum (고름) are tied to one side into a single-loop bow (oegoreum (외고름)). Instead of buttons, this goreum (고름) fastens the hanbok (한복), and the knot's shape and the trailing line themselves become decoration — the most iconic detail of hanbok (한복).
  • Chima (치마, skirt): The women's bottom. Several widths of cloth are joined, pleated at the top, and tied above the chest (below the armpits) so it falls in fullness. Its broad width creates a rippling silhouette when sitting or walking.
  • Baji (바지, trousers): The men's bottom. Very roomy in the leg, making it comfortable to sit cross-legged. The ankles are gathered and tied with daenim (대님, ankle ties).
  • Durumagi (두루마기, overcoat): A coat-like outer garment worn over the jeogori (저고리) when going out. Its name means "to wrap all around," and in men's hanbok (한복) it is regarded as a mark of propriety when dressing formally.

Add the vest-style baeja (배자, vest) and the decorative knotted ornament norigae (노리개, pendant ornament) hung from the waist or goreum (고름), and a full ensemble is complete.

③ Color and Curve — The Two Axes of Hanbok Aesthetics

The reason hanbok (한복) is not merely a "flashy garment" lies in the way of thinking embedded in its color and line.

(1) Obangsaek — Wearing the Order of the Universe

The traditional color scheme of hanbok (한복) is rooted in obangsaek (五方色 / 오방색, the five cardinal colors), the five base colors derived from the yin-yang and five-elements philosophy.

  • Cheong (청, blue) — east, spring, life / Jeok (적, red) — south, summer, passion / Hwang (황, yellow) — center, earth, authority / Baek (백, white) — west, autumn, purity / Heuk (흑, black) — north, winter, wisdom.

These five colors were not simple hues but symbols of the order and harmony connecting nature, humanity, and the universe. For example, a new bride's yellow jeogori (저고리) with a crimson chima (치마), or the saekdong (색동, multicolored striped) sleeves of a child's first-birthday outfit, all carried wishes for good fortune and health. (Obangsaek (오방색) runs through not only hanbok (한복) but also dancheong (단청, traditional eave painting), food, and tradition everywhere → #1147)

(2) Curved Beauty — The Line Made by Straight Cloth

Spread flat, hanbok (한복) cloth is simply a straight line (a rectangle). Yet wrapped around the body, the baerae (배래) of the jeogori (저고리), the pleats of the chima (치마), and the flow of the goreum (고름) meet to bring a supple curve to life. This "flow of line," where straight and curved invert and intertwine, is the essence of hanbok (한복) aesthetics. Traditional hanbok (한복) also used natural materials like ramie, hemp, and silk, with natural dyes such as indigo and safflower red, producing soft, deep tones.

④ When Is It Worn — At Every Knot of Life

Hanbok (한복) is less an everyday garment than clothing that marks the important moments of life. Following a Korean's lifetime shows where hanbok (한복) appears.

  • Holidays — On Seollal (설날, Lunar New Year) and Chuseok (추석, autumn harvest holiday), people wear hanbok (한복) to perform sebae (세배, New Year's bows) to elders or to hold ancestral rites. The image of a small child in saekdong (색동) hanbok (한복) is a quintessential holiday scene. (→ #1136)
  • Doljanchi (돌잔치, first-birthday party) — At a baby's first birthday, the child is dressed in saekdong (색동) hanbok (한복) and a small hat (boys = bokgeon (복건), girls = jobawi (조바위)) to wish for health and fortune.
  • Weddings · Pyebaek — In a traditional wedding, the bride wears the hwarot (활옷) or wonsam (원삼) with a jokduri (족두리, coronet), and the groom dons the samo-gwandae (사모관대, official's robe and hat). Today, after a Western-style wedding, the bride and groom change into hanbok (한복) for the pyebaek (폐백) ceremony, bowing to the groom's elders.
  • Coming-of-age, hwangap (환갑, 60th birthday), and more — Hanbok (한복) is also worn as formal dress for coming-of-age rites and an elder's milestone birthdays.

In this way, hanbok (한복) has served to visually express the meaning of each moment at every rite of passage (통과의례, tonggwauirye) such as birth, coming of age, and marriage. (On rites of passage overall → #1144)

⑤ ★Modern Revival — Free Palace Admission, the Rental Boom, and K-Pop Hanbok

Hanbok (한복), once nearly confined to a museum, has returned to the very center of foreign travel. Several clear forces drove that revival.

(1) Free Admission to the Five Grand Palaces in Hanbok — the Decisive Move

Under a policy in place since 2013, wearing hanbok (한복) grants free entry to Joseon (조선)'s five grand palaces (Gyeongbokgung (경복궁), Changdeokgung (창덕궁), Changgyeonggung (창경궁), Deoksugung (덕수궁), and Gyeonghuigung (경희궁)). It is an official guideline of the Korea Heritage Service (국가유산청, formerly the Cultural Heritage Administration), and because its aim is "popularizing and globalizing hanbok (한복)," it applies to Koreans and foreigners alike. This simple perk — "wear hanbok (한복) and enter the palace for free" — sparked a boom in hanbok (한복) rental shops in front of the palaces.

(2) The Hanbok Rental Boom

Hanbok (한복) rental shops are packed throughout Gyeongbokgung (경복궁), Bukchon (북촌), Insa-dong (인사동), and the Jeonju Hanok Village (전주 한옥마을). Stocking traditional hanbok (한복) as well as king-and-queen outfits, fusion hanbok (퓨전 한복), and hair ornaments and norigae (노리개), they have made it standard to dress up and head out to snap "life shots" in the palaces and hanok alleys. It started out aimed at foreigners, but now Korean teens and twentysomethings have joined in, keeping it booming. The result is the figure noted earlier — 2.07 million palace visitors in hanbok (한복) in 2025 — a symbol of K-tourism shifting from "travel you watch" to "travel you wear."

(3) Everyday Hanbok and Fusion Hanbok

Easing the discomfort of traditional hanbok (한복), saenghwal hanbok (생활한복, everyday hanbok / modern hanbok) — reinterpreted as washable daily wear in denim and cotton — has taken hold. It keeps the lines of the jeogori (저고리) and chima (치마) but swaps silk and starch for denim, lace, and pastels. More and more people wear it like casual clothing on university campuses and in Seochon (서촌) cafes.

(4) K-Pop Hallyu Hanbok

The global engine of the hanbok (한복) revival is K-pop. BTS reinterpreted the dopo (도포, scholar's robe) and gat (갓, traditional hat) in a modern way in music videos and performances, and right after BLACKPINK's stage for "How You Like That" (2020), reports said sales at one modern hanbok (한복) brand surged. Overseas fans began buying modern hanbok (한복) in the U.S., Europe, and Southeast Asia, and the awareness that "that outfit is hanbok (한복)" quickly took root. Just as efforts to promote hanbok (한복), like the Hanbok Day (한복의 날, October 21) established in 1996, once sought to raise its profile, that profile is now spreading worldwide on the back of K-pop.

⑥ A Foreigner's Hanbok Experience in Practice — Where, How Much, How

> Prices and operations are estimates as of 2026 and vary by shop — checking before your visit is recommended.

  • Where to wear it: The Gyeongbokgung (경복궁), Bukchon Hanok Village (북촌 한옥마을), and Insa-dong (인사동) area is the densest cluster of rental shops. In the provinces, the Jeonju Hanok Village (전주 한옥마을) is the standout. You change at the rental shop and head straight out into the palaces and hanok alleys.
  • Cost · time: A basic 1–2 hour rental runs about ₩15,000–25,000 (about $11–18) per person. King-and-queen outfits or full hair-and-accessory packages cost more. Most open around 9 a.m. and take returns by 6–7 p.m.
  • Why free admission matters: Wearing hanbok (한복) makes admission to the five grand palaces free (general admission to Gyeongbokgung (경복궁) is about ₩3,000). Because part of the rental fee is offset by the admission perk, it's good value.
  • Language: Most rental shops in tourist areas can serve in English, Chinese, and Japanese, and staff help with hair ornaments and dressing.
  • Tips: ① The knots of the goreum (고름) and chima (치마) ties come loose easily, so learn how to put it on once. ② For photos, the early morning (fewer crowds) or palace stone walls and hanok alleys make the best spots. ③ In cold seasons, layer a durumagi (두루마기) or baeja (배자) for both warmth and style. ④ Since you can usually wear your own shoes, bring comfortable footwear.

❓ Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ) for Foreigners

  • Can foreigners get free palace admission in hanbok (한복) too? — Yes. Because the policy's aim is "globalizing hanbok (한복)," it applies to Koreans and foreigners alike for anyone wearing hanbok (한복). Not only traditional hanbok (한복) but rental-shop hanbok (한복) is generally accepted (checking each palace's detailed guideline is recommended).
  • Can men rent hanbok (한복) too? — Of course. Men wear a jeogori (저고리) + baji (바지) + durumagi (두루마기) (or king / young-nobleman outfits), and couple and family sets are popular too.
  • Won't it look like cosplay? — Not at all. Wearing hanbok (한복) in front of a palace is a very natural and welcomed cultural experience in Korea. Koreans may even ask to take a photo with you.
  • Does fusion hanbok (퓨전 한복) qualify for free admission too? — Hanbok (한복) that keeps a traditional form is generally accepted, but heavily altered outfits may vary by each palace's guideline. Asking the rental shop for "free-palace-admission" hanbok (한복) is the safe move.
  • Which season is best for hanbok (한복)? — All four seasons work. Palace backdrops are especially lovely during spring blossoms and autumn foliage, and in winter, layering a durumagi (두루마기) keeps you warm while adding atmosphere.
