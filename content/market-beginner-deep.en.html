⚠️ 3 Common Mistakes Foreigners Make

Mistake 1: Showing Up Without Cash

Street stalls and small shops at markets like Gwangjang Market and Mangwon Market often don't have card terminals. Bring ₩30,000-50,000 in cash as a baseline. Nearby bank ATMs — Citibank Korea (한국씨티은행)·KEB Hana (KEB하나)·Shinhan (신한) — accept foreign cards for withdrawals. Fees run roughly ₩2,000-5,000 per withdrawal.

Mistake 2: Visiting on a Sunday

Namdaemun Market (closed most Sundays) and Tongin Market (closed every Sunday) shut down on Sundays. At Gwangjang Market, some shops close on the 1st and 3rd Sunday of each month. Always confirm before a Sunday or holiday visit, and consider Noryangjin Fisheries Market or Garak Mall Dawn Market as year-round alternatives.

Mistake 3: Trying to Haggle Right Away

Bargaining at Korean traditional markets is optional. Food stalls at Gwangjang Market generally have fixed prices, though at clothing and general-goods shops, asking jogeumman kkakka-juseyo (조금만 깎아주세요, "can you give a small discount?") sometimes works. But first-time visitors who push too hard for a discount can create an awkward atmosphere. Tasting a little first, then buying naturally, is the best approach.

Deep-Dive Guide by Market

Gwangjang Market (광장시장) (#582) — The Textbook of Seoul Market Culture

Hours: Daily, roughly 09:00-23:00 (the street-stall section scales down after 22:00). Some shops close on the 1st and 3rd Sunday of the month.

Foreigner fit: ★★★★★. A market that has appeared repeatedly in YouTube and Netflix documentaries. Bindaetteok (빈대떡, mung bean pancake)·mayak gimbap·yukhoe (육회, Korean beef tartare) are the three signature dishes.

Price range (as of 2026, confirm on-site):

  • Bindaetteok, 1 piece: ₩3,000-4,000
  • Mayak gimbap, 1 roll: ₩2,000-3,000
  • Yukhoe, small plate: ₩15,000-25,000

Access: Subway Line 1 or Line 5, Jongno 5-ga Station (종로5가역), Exit 8, 2-minute walk.

English availability: Some major stalls display partial English menus. Pointing at the photo menu solves most situations.

Bargaining/tasting etiquette: Food stalls don't have a tasting culture. You order, then eat. Fixed quantities are standard rather than price haggling.

Namdaemun Market (남대문시장) (#581) — Seoul's Largest Wholesale-Retail Complex Market

Hours: Mon-Sat, roughly 07:00-18:00 (varies by item). Closed most Sundays. The pre-dawn wholesale section operates separately from 02:00-06:00.

Foreigner fit: ★★★★★. The largest market where clothing·accessories·imported food·souvenirs are all in one place. The galchi-jorim (갈치조림, braised cutlassfish)·kalguksu (칼국수, knife-cut noodles) restaurant row also draws many foreign visitors.

Price range (as of 2026, confirm on-site):

  • Clothing: ₩10,000-50,000 (varies widely by item/brand)
  • Kalguksu·sundae (순대, Korean blood sausage) restaurants: ₩7,000-12,000
  • Ginseng·health-food gift sets: ₩20,000-100,000+

Access: Subway Line 4, Hoehyeon Station (회현역), Exit 5, 2-minute walk.

English availability: With heavy foreign traffic, a good number of shops can handle basic English. Some Japanese and Chinese too.

Bargaining/tasting etiquette: Clothing and general-goods shops sometimes give a small discount for bulk purchases. Restaurants are fixed-price.

Tongin Market (통인시장) (#589) — The Yeopjeon Lunch-Box Experience

Hours: Tue-Sun, roughly 09:00-18:00. Closed every Sunday. Yeopjeon lunch-box program: 10:00-17:00 (per the token-exchange booth).

Foreigner fit: ★★★★★. An experiential system where you exchange cash for ₩500-denomination yeopjeon (엽전, coin-shaped tokens) and freely buy food at participating stalls around the market. Especially good for solo or small-group visits.

How the yeopjeon lunch box works:

1. Exchange cash for yeopjeon at the booth by the market entrance (₩5,000 = 10 yeopjeon)

2. Rent a lunch box (no deposit reported, confirm on-site)

3. Walk the market and buy food from participating stalls with your yeopjeon

4. Return the box; any leftover yeopjeon can reportedly be refunded in cash (confirm on-site)

Access: Subway Line 3, Gyeongbokgung Station (경복궁역), Exit 3, about a 15-minute walk. Or take a bus toward Gwanghwamun (광화문) and walk from there.

Cash required: Yeopjeon exchange is cash-only. ₩10,000-15,000 is enough for a full meal.

Mangwon Market (망원시장) (#592) — Local Vibes, MZ-Generation Visitors

Hours: Tue-Sun, roughly 08:00-21:00. Closed every Monday.

Foreigner fit: ★★★★. Compared to the famous tourist markets, this one feels more local. Fruit·banchan (반찬, side dishes)·street snacks — hotteok (호떡, sweet pancake)·eomuk (어묵, fish cake)·kkultarae (꿀타래, honey-thread candy) — are cheap and good quality. Connects well with the Mangwon-dong cafe street.

Price range (as of 2026, confirm on-site):

  • Hotteok: ₩1,000-2,000
  • Fruit, 1 bag: ₩3,000-8,000
  • Ready-made banchan: ₩2,000-5,000

Access: Subway Line 6, Mangwon Station (망원역), Exit 1, 5-minute walk.

English availability: Low. A translation app is essential.

Noryangjin Fisheries Market (노량진수산시장) (#596) — Dawn Auctions and Buying Live Fish Direct

Hours: Open year-round. Wholesale auctions from 5am. The retail sashimi section runs roughly 07:00-22:00.

Foreigner fit: ★★★★. A system where you pick your own live fish, crab, or lobster and have it prepared to eat at a second-floor restaurant. Widely regarded as excellent freshness for the price.

How to use it:

1. Pick your fish/seafood and specify the weight at the first-floor retail seafood section

2. Have it prepared on the spot to take away, or use a second-floor restaurant

3. Second-floor restaurants: table/setup fee of ₩5,000-10,000 per person, plus drinks and rice purchased separately

Price range (as of 2026, confirm on-site):

  • Flounder sashimi (small): ₩30,000-50,000
  • Crab (per kilogram): ₩20,000-40,000 (prices fluctuate significantly)

Access: Directly connected to Subway Line 1, Noryangjin Station (노량진역).

Bargaining etiquette: Haggling over seafood weight isn't common. Prices follow that day's market rate. Confirm the price clearly before buying.

Garak Mall Dawn Market (가락몰 새벽시장) (#587) — The Produce Auction Experience

Hours: Open year-round from 2am. General visitors can reportedly tour the auction floor (confirm on-site). The retail section runs from the morning.

Foreigner fit: ★★★. The auction itself is a uniquely cultural experience. That said, it runs 2am-4am, so plan your schedule around it. A nearby hotel or taxi is recommended.

Access: Subway Line 8, Jangji Station (장지역), Exit 5, 10-minute walk.

Note: Only registered wholesale brokers may bid at the auction. Foreign visitors are there to watch and experience.

✅ Practical Info Full Set for Foreigners

Transportation

Subway: Seoul's subway has 100% English signage. Ride with a T-money transit card (buy at any convenience store, ₩3,000) or a single-use ticket. Transfer discounts apply.

Taxi: The KakaoT (카카오T) app is easiest — English interface plus destination entry. Base fare (as of 2026): about ₩4,800 (standard Seoul meter).

Walking route: About a 20-minute walk from Gwangjang Market to Namdaemun Market, heading from Jongno 5-ga Station toward Hoehyeon Station.

Payment

VAT refund: At Global Blue and Tax Refund member stores, purchases over ₩30,000-50,000 qualify for an airport refund. Look for the in-store Tax Free sign.

Language

5 basic Korean phrases:

  • ee-geo ha-na ju-se-yo (이거 하나 주세요) — "One of this, please"
  • eol-ma-ye-yo (얼마예요?) — "How much?"
  • gam-sa-ham-ni-da (감사합니다) — "Thank you"
  • maeb-ji an-ke (맵지 않게 해주세요) — "Not spicy, please"
  • sa-jin jji-geo-do dwae-yo (사진 찍어도 돼요?) — "Can I take a photo?"

Translation apps: Papago's (네이버's) camera translation has strong Korean recognition. Point your camera at a menu or price tag for real-time translation.

FAQ

Q1. Where Should a First-Time Foreign Visitor Start?

We recommend the order Gwangjang Market → Tongin Market → Namdaemun Market. Gwangjang Market is great for food and getting a feel for the atmosphere, Tongin Market is experiential so you're actively participating, and Namdaemun Market lets you handle souvenirs and shopping all at once.

Q2. How Should I Prepare for Payment?

As a baseline, bring ₩30,000-50,000 in cash per person plus a card as backup. ATMs at subway stations and convenience stores mostly accept foreign cards (Visa·Mastercard·UnionPay). Fees run about ₩2,000-5,000 per transaction.

Q3. What If English Doesn't Work At All?

Papago's camera translation, pointing at the menu, and showing numbers on a calculator solve most situations. Gwangjang Market and Namdaemun Market see heavy foreign traffic, so many shops handle basic English.

Q4. Which Days and Times Are Best?

Tuesday through Saturday, 11am-2pm, is when goods are freshest and crowds are manageable. Sunday is best avoided since Namdaemun, Tongin, and others are often closed. After 2pm things quiet down, so you can browse at ease.

Q5. Can I Sample the Food?

Food stalls generally don't offer free tasting — you eat what you buy. Some dried-goods and nut shops allow a taste, but this varies by shop, so it's best to ask first. Just say mat-bwa-do dwae-yo? (맛봐도 돼요?, "Can I try it?")

5 Tips for Foreign Enthusiasts

1. Bring ₩30-50K in cash — use ATMs at convenience stores or subway stations

2. Point at the photo menu — the surest way to order

3. Shared seating is fine — sitting with strangers is part of Korean market culture

4. Gwangjang Market + Tongin Market + Namdaemun Market = the first-visit full set

5. Skip Sundays, visit Tuesday-Saturday mornings — solves both closures and freshness
