📍 Seoul Forest — Seoul Forest and Seoul Forest Insect Botanical Garden

Seoul Forest (서울숲) (273 Ttukseom-ro (뚝섬로), Seongdong-gu, ☎ 02-460-2905~6) is an urban forest that opened around Ttukseom Sports Park (뚝섬체육공원) on June 18, 2005. The site was Seoul's first waterworks source reservoir, built in 1908. It is made up of four spaces, the Culture and Arts Park (문화예술공원), Experiential Learning Center (체험학습원), Ecological Forest (생태숲) and Wetland Ecological Park (습지생태원), and it borders the Han River (한강). According to the Korea Tourism Organization introduction, it is the first park built through citizen participation from creation to program operation.

  • Opening hours: Always open. Usage hours differ by facility. Closed days: Open year-round. Closed days differ by facility.
  • Parking: Available (211 spaces).

Seoul Forest Insect Botanical Garden (서울숲 곤충식물원) (same address, ☎ 02-460-2905) is a nature learning space inside Seoul Forest. You can see tropical plants and a variety of insects, and it has a themed botanical garden (테마식물원), a specimen exhibition room (표본전시실) and a butterfly ecology hall (나비생태관).

  • Opening hours: Summer season Tuesday~Sunday 10:00~17:00, winter season Tuesday~Sunday 11:00~16:00. Closed days: Every Monday.
  • Fee: Free. Parking: Parking lot available.

📍 Seongsu-dong — Handmade Shoe Street, Understand Avenue and Ttukseom Art Museum

Seongsu-dong Handmade Shoe Street (성수동 수제화거리) (Seongsu-dong 2-ga (성수동2가), Seongdong-gu, ☎ Seongdong-gu Local Economy Division (지역경제과) 02-2286-7783) is a street where handmade shoe businesses are gathered. From the 1970s on, handmade shoe businesses moved into Seongsu-dong, where land was relatively cheap. According to the Korea Tourism Organization introduction, it is the largest handmade shoe industry area in Korea, with more than 350 finished handmade shoe makers and more than 100 intermediate processing and raw and subsidiary material distributors, and more than 70% of Korea's handmade shoe manufacturers are gathered there. Everything from design and development to shipping and sales takes place in one neighborhood.

  • Opening hours and closed days: Vary by shop. Parking: Not available.

Understand Avenue (언더스탠드에비뉴) (63 Wangsimni-ro (왕십리로), Seongdong-gu, ☎ 02-725-5526) is a public cultural space that opened on April 18, 2016. It is a social contribution project built together by the public sector, the private sector and companies, with shops of young entrepreneurs set up in 116 container boxes, and it also links to jobs for seniors and employment for vulnerable groups. According to the Korea Tourism Organization introduction, about 8 million people have visited since opening, and more than 1.3 million visit every year. It has select shops (편집숍), small online shops (소호몰), restaurants, cafes, an exhibition and performance hall, a flea market, and festivals.

  • Opening hours: 10:00~22:00. Varies by shop. Closed days: The day before and the day of Seollal (설) and Chuseok (추석). Varies by shop.
  • Parking: Not available. Credit cards: Accepted. Restrooms: Available.

Ttukseom Art Museum (뚝섬미술관) (33 Achasan-ro (아차산로), Seongdong-gu, ☎ 0507-1420-5035) is an art museum in the basement of the building you see immediately on your right after taking the escalator down from Exit 3 of Ttukseom Station (뚝섬역) on Subway Line 2. It mainly shows contemporary art, and many hands-on exhibitions are held. If you enter your contact number in the waiting registration machine to the right of the entrance, a KakaoTalk (카카오톡) notification arrives when it is your turn to enter. The registration is cancelled automatically 30 minutes after the call.

  • Opening hours: Weekdays 13:00~19:00, weekends 11:00~19:00 (last entry 18:20). Closed days: Open year-round.
  • Fee: Adults 12,000 won, children 10,000 won, special ticket 8,000 won.

📍 Ttukseom Hangang Park — Seoul Saenggak Maru (Ttukseom Jabeolle)

Seoul Saenggak Maru (Ttukseom Jabeolle) (서울생각마루 · 뚝섬 자벌레) (2202 Gangbyeonbuk-ro (강변북로), Gwangjin-gu (광진구), ☎ 02-3780-0517) is a citizen cultural space in Ttukseom Hangang Park (뚝섬한강공원). The building stretches long in the shape of an inchworm (jabeolle), and you can see the Han River through its windows. The 1st floor has the Ten Million Citizens' Bookshelf (천만시민의 책장), whose book list was put together with citizen participation, and the Maru Playground (마루 놀이터), where 4 floor games such as land-grabbing and rock-paper-scissors are played. The 2nd floor has the Saenggak Maru Bookshelf (생각마루책장), where you read books while looking at the Han River, and a meeting space, and the 3rd floor is a paid rental space.

  • Opening hours: Weekdays 10:00~19:00, weekends 10:00~20:00. Closed days: Every Monday and legal public holidays.
  • Fee: Free. Parking: 1,000 won for the first 30 minutes, 200 won more for every 10 minutes, maximum 10,000 won.

⚠ 3 Things to Know Before You Go

  1. Some places are closed on Monday — Seoul Forest Insect Botanical Garden and Seoul Saenggak Maru are closed every Monday. Seoul Saenggak Maru is also closed on legal public holidays.
  2. Ttukseom Art Museum opens in the afternoon on weekdays — It opens at 13:00 on weekdays and 11:00 on weekends, and the last entry is 18:20.
  3. No parking at Seongsu-dong Handmade Shoe Street and Understand Avenue — Both are registered as having no parking.

⚙ FAQ 2

Q. Which places can I enter for free?

A. According to the Korea Tourism Organization registered information, Seoul Forest Insect Botanical Garden (closed Mondays) and Seoul Saenggak Maru (closed Mondays and legal public holidays) are free. Seoul Forest is always open.

Q. Which places are good to visit with children?

A. Seoul Forest Insect Botanical Garden (themed botanical garden, specimen exhibition room, butterfly ecology hall) and the Maru Playground on the 1st floor of Seoul Saenggak Maru (4 floor games).
