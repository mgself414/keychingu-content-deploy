🎯 How to Use This Guide

The 2024 Nobel Prize in Literature went to the Korean writer Han Kang (한강). This guide gathers literature museums and writers' houses where you can meet Korean literature outside the books. In Jongno-gu, Seoul, there are the Yun Dong-ju Literature Museum, the Poet's Hill, Yi Sang's House and the Cheongun Literature Library, and in Dobong-gu there is the Kim Su-young Literature Museum. Beyond that, there are writer memorial spaces in Chuncheon, Wonju, Tongyeong, Namwon and Beolgyo.

The operating details are copied from values registered in the Korea Tourism Organization tourism data (TourAPI), and the information on Han Kang's prize follows official Nobel Foundation data. All 9 places except the Poet's Hill are registered as closed every Monday.

🏅 Han Kang — 2024 Nobel Prize in Literature

According to official Nobel Foundation data, Han Kang was born in Gwangju in 1970 and received the 2024 Nobel Prize in Literature. The reason for the selection is "for her intense poetic prose that confronts historical traumas and exposes the fragility of human life." Searching by birth country in the Nobel Foundation laureate data (using current country names), Han Kang is the only literature laureate born in Korea.

📍 Seoul Jongno — Yun Dong-ju Literature Museum, Poet's Hill, Yi Sang's House, Cheongun Literature Library

Yun Dong-ju Literature Museum

The Yun Dong-ju Literature Museum (119 Changuimun-ro, Jongno-gu (종로구 창의문로 119), ☎ 070-4531-4176) was founded in 2012 to remember the poet Yun Dong-ju. According to the Korea Tourism Organization, it was made by renovating the Cheongun Water Pressurizing Station (청운수도가압장) and a water tank that had been left abandoned at the foot of Inwangsan (인왕산). It received the Korea Public Architecture Award in 2012 and the Seoul Architecture Award in 2014, and it was designated a memorial site (현충시설) in 2015. It is said that while Yun Dong-ju was a student in the literature department of Yonhi College (연희전문학교), he lodged at the house of the novelist Kim Song (김송) in Nusang-dong, Jongno-gu, and climbed Inwangsan to polish his poems.

  • Opening hours: 10:00~18:00 (last entry 17:30). Every day 13:30~14:00 is cleaning and disinfection time.
  • Closed days: Every Monday, January 1, Seollal and Chuseok Day.
  • Admission: Free. Cards: Accepted.

Yun Dong-ju Poet's Hill

The Yun Dong-ju Poet's Hill (Cheongun-dong, Jongno-gu, at the foot of Inwangsan (종로구 청운동, 인왕산 자락)) is connected to the Yun Dong-ju Literature Museum, and Changuimun Gate (창의문) is across from it. According to the Korea Tourism Organization, there is a stone monument for the poem "Prologue" (서시) and a stone staircase carved with poems, and a sign reading "Seoul Night View" (서울 밤 경) stands in front of the "Prologue" monument, so it is presented as a good spot for night views.

  • Opening hours: Open at all times, year-round. Parking: Not available.

Yi Sang's House

Yi Sang's House (18 Jahamun-ro 7-gil, Tongin-dong (자하문로7길 18, 통인동), ☎ 070-8837-8374) is, according to the Korea Tourism Organization, the site of the house where the modernist writer Yi Sang (이상) lived for more than 20 years from the age of three. It was at risk of demolition, and in 2009 the Cultural Heritage Trust (문화유산국민신탁) bought it with citizen donations and corporate sponsorship and now preserves it. Inside there are a bust and portrait of Yi Sang and an archive of his works collected by year, and if you climb the dark stairs behind the black iron gate there is a door that leads out to the rooftop.

  • Opening hours: 10:00~17:00 (break 12:00~13:00). Closed days: Every Monday.
  • Parking: Not available.

Cheongun Literature Library

The Cheongun Literature Library (40 Jahamun-ro 36-gil, Cheongun-dong (자하문로36길 40, 청운동), ☎ 070-4680-4032) is, according to the Korea Tourism Organization, a culture-focused library with the quiet charm of a hanok. It holds literary books centered on poetry, novels and essays, and it reused more than 3,000 hanok roof tiles torn down in the Donuimun New Town (돈의문 뉴타운) area.

  • Opening hours: Weekdays 09:00~21:00, weekends 09:00~19:00.
  • Closed days: Every Monday, January 1, Seollal and Chuseok holidays.
  • Admission: Free. Parking: Available.

📍 Seoul Dobong-gu — Kim Su-young Literature Museum

The Kim Su-young Literature Museum (80 Haedeung-ro 32-gil, Banghak-dong, Dobong-gu (도봉구 해등로32길 80, 방학동), ☎ 02-3494-1127) is, according to the Korea Tourism Organization, a memorial hall built by Dobong-gu in honor of the poet Kim Su-young, and it opened on November 27, 2013. Dobong-gu is where Kim Su-young wrote poems during his lifetime, and it has his family home, his grave and a poem monument. The museum displays his works and everyday belongings and holds lectures and poetry reading events.

  • Opening hours: 09:00~18:00.
  • Closed days: Every Monday (the next day if Monday is a public holiday), January 1, Seollal and Chuseok holidays.
  • Admission: Free.

📍 Chuncheon — Kim Yu-jeong Literary Village

The Kim Yu-jeong Literary Village (1430-14 Kim Yu-jeong-ro, Sindong-myeon, Chuncheon-si (춘천시 신동면 김유정로 1430-14), ☎ 033-261-4650) opened in 2002, according to the Korea Tourism Organization, centered on the birthplace site of Kim Yu-jeong (김유정), who wrote Bom Bom ("Spring, Spring", 봄·봄) and Dongbaek-kkot ("Camellia Flower", 동백꽃). It has a restored ㅁ-shaped birthplace house, a memorial exhibition hall and the Kim Yu-jeong Story House (김유정이야기집) added in 2016, and after your visit you can walk the "Sille Story Road" (실레이야기길), a walking course linking places from his works.

  • Opening hours: Mar–Oct 09:30~17:30, Nov–Feb 09:30~17:00.
  • Closed days: Every Monday, January 1, Seollal and Chuseok Day.
  • Parking: Available.

📍 Wonju and Tongyeong — Park Kyong-ni

Park Kyong-ni Literary Park (Wonju)

The Park Kyong-ni Literary Park (1 Toji-gil, Dangu-dong, Wonju-si (원주시 토지길 1, 단구동), ☎ 033-762-6843) is, according to the Korea Tourism Organization, a park that preserves the old house where Park Kyong-ni (박경리) completed Parts 4 and 5 of her epic novel Toji ("The Land", 토지). It was completed in May 1999, and it has the vegetable garden the author tended, the old house, and the five-story "Park Kyong-ni House of Literature" (박경리 문학의 집) with exhibition rooms.

  • Opening hours: 10:00~17:00 (break 12:00~13:00).
  • Closed days: Every Monday, January 1, Seollal and Chuseok holidays.
  • Parking: Available (free).

Park Kyong-ni Memorial Hall (Tongyeong)

The Park Kyong-ni Memorial Hall (173 Sanyang Jungang-ro, Sanyang-eup, Tongyeong-si (통영시 산양읍 산양중앙로 173), ☎ 055-650-2541) was built in 2010 in honor of Park Kyong-ni, a native of Tongyeong, according to the Korea Tourism Organization. It has her handwritten manuscript of Toji, belongings such as her passport and letters, a video room and a reference room.

  • Opening hours: 09:00~18:00 (last entry 17:00).
  • Closed days: Every Monday, the day after a statutory public holiday.
  • Admission: Free.

📍 Namwon — Honbul Literature Museum

The Honbul Literature Museum (52 Nobongan-gil, Samae-myeon, Namwon-si (남원시 사매면 노봉안길 52), ☎ 063-620-5744) opened on October 20, 2004. According to the Korea Tourism Organization, Nobong Village (노봉마을), where the museum stands, is the main setting of Choi Myeong-hui's (최명희) epic novel Honbul (혼불), and inside the hanok building there are the author's belongings, a recreated writing room and dioramas depicting scenes from the novel. Honbul is described as a work the author wrote over 17 years.

  • Opening hours: Jul–Aug 09:00~18:00, Nov–Feb 09:00~17:00.
  • Closed days: Every Monday, January 1.
  • Activities: Woodcraft, natural dyeing, pottery. Parking: Available (free).

📍 Beolgyo — Taebaek Mountains Literature Museum

The Taebaek Mountains Literature Museum (89-19 Hongam-ro, Beolgyo-eup, Boseong-gun (보성군 벌교읍 홍암로 89-19), ☎ 061-850-8653) displays materials on Jo Jeong-rae's (조정래) novel The Taebaek Mountains ("Taebaek Sanmaek", 태백산맥), according to the Korea Tourism Organization. It stands at the foot of Jeseoksan (제석산), where the Hyeon Bujat-jip (현부잣집) and Sohwa's House (소화의 집), the setting of the novel's first scene, are located, and it was built based on a design by the architect Kim Won (김원). The handwritten manuscript of The Taebaek Mountains is on display.

  • Opening hours: Mar–Oct 09:00~18:00, Nov–Feb 09:00~17:00.
  • Closed days: Every Monday, January 1, Seollal and Chuseok Day.
  • Admission: Individual adults 2,000 won, teenagers and soldiers 1,500 won, children 1,000 won.

⚠ 3 Things to Know Before You Go

  1. Most places are closed on Mondays — All 9 places except the Poet's Hill are registered as closed every Monday (the Kim Su-young Literature Museum closes the next day if Monday is a public holiday). Only the Yun Dong-ju Poet's Hill is open at all times.
  2. Some places close at lunchtime — Yi Sang's House and the Park Kyong-ni Literary Park are both registered with a break from 12:00 to 13:00.
  3. The Yun Dong-ju Literature Museum closes for 30 minutes in the afternoon — Every day 13:30~14:00 is cleaning and disinfection time.

⚙ FAQ 2

Q. When are the 4 places in Seoul Jongno closed?

A. The Yun Dong-ju Literature Museum, Yi Sang's House and the Cheongun Literature Library are closed every Monday, and the Poet's Hill is open at all times (year-round). The Yun Dong-ju Literature Museum is registered with cleaning and disinfection every day 13:30~14:00, and Yi Sang's House with a break from 12:00 to 13:00.

Q. Is there an admission fee?

A. The Yun Dong-ju Literature Museum, Cheongun Literature Library, Kim Su-young Literature Museum and Park Kyong-ni Memorial Hall are registered as free. The Taebaek Mountains Literature Museum is 2,000 won for adults. For the Poet's Hill, Yi Sang's House, Kim Yu-jeong Literary Village, Park Kyong-ni Literary Park and Honbul Literature Museum, the Korea Tourism Organization data has no admission entry.
