Korean Hair Salon Culture — Why a Korean Salon Visit Feels Different

> Cut · Perm · Color · Head Spa · Designer-by-Name — a Korean hair salon culture explainer for international visitors. Not "a place to get your hair cut," but "why it's this detailed, and what, and how."

There's something that surprises almost every foreigner on their first visit to a Korean salon. You booked just a single cut, yet a whole set follows — shampoo, scalp massage, blow-dry, and styling — drinks and snacks come out, and when it's over, you don't tip. Look at the perm menu and there isn't just one or two options: it branches ten ways into setting-perm (셋팅펌), magic straightening (매직스트레이트, magic), down-perm (다운펌), volume-magic (볼륨매직), and more. When you book, you don't just take "anyone" — you request a specific dijaineo (디자이너, stylist). In Korea, a salon is less a barbershop and more a detail-driven industry that 'designs your style and manages your hair.' That's why a new kind of beauty tourism has emerged, with foreigners coming to Korea on purpose for a perm or a head spa (헤드스파) to copy K-drama and idol hair (so much so that National Geographic has called Korea "the new face of beauty tourism"). This article isn't "go to which salon" (→ cross-link); it explains, at a foreigner's eye level, why a Korean salon is so different — the details of its procedures, booking, and service. > Key: A Korean salon's "perm" is not one single thing. Perms that add curl (setting-perm (셋팅펌)) and perms that straighten or flatten (magic (매직) · down-perm (다운펌)) are all grouped together as "perm" — the point foreigners find most confusing (see ② below).

⚠️ 3 Things Foreigners Often Get Wrong

1. "Perm = making hair curly" ❌ — In Korea, a peom (펌, perm) is not only about adding curls. Magic straightening (매직스트레이트) (frizzy → straight) and down-perm (다운펌) (flattening sticking-out hair) are all "perms" too. In other words, a perm is the umbrella term for "any procedure that reshapes hair with chemicals + heat." If you just say "perm," you might get the exact opposite of what you wanted, so you should name the specific type.

2. "Tipping is polite" ❌ — Korean salons have no tipping culture. The menu price is the final price, and the stylist doesn't expect a tip. There's no need to add a U.S./European-style 15–20% tip — not tipping is in no way rude.

3. "They'll handle my hair (curly / non-Asian) just fine too" ⚠️ — Not all "foreigner-friendly" salons are alike. Speaking English and a stylist having experience with curly or non-Asian hair are two different things. If you go with curly hair to a place used mostly to straight Korean hair, the result can be disappointing. Confirming their experience with your hair type in advance is key.

① What a Korean Salon Is — Not "a Cut" but "a Full Service Set"

The first trait of a Korean salon is that even a single cut comes with full service.

  • A typical cut runs as one flow: shampoo → scalp & neck massage → cut → blow-dry & styling. So it's not "just a snip" — it includes the finishing styling.
  • Many stores offer free extras like drinks & snacks, luggage lockers, and phone charging.
  • So even for the same "cut," in Korea you sit for a long time (a cut is usually 1–1.5 hours), and the experience itself feels closer to "being cared for."

This "detail + service" orientation is the backbone of Korean salon culture. The mindset isn't to cut fast and finish, but to look at your hair, style, and scalp together.

② Types of Procedures — Properly Telling Perm, Color, and Clinic Apart

A Korean salon menu is hard for foreigners because the single word 'perm' contains too much. It's easy if you understand it in three groups.

(1) Perms that add curl

  • Setting-perm (셋팅펌): Thick S-curls and springy curls fixed with a high-heat rod at about 120–180°C. The flagship Korean perm. Natural-looking and long-lasting.
  • Cold perm (일반펌, ilbanpeom): The traditional method of forming curls with chemicals only, without heat.

(2) Perms that straighten or flatten

  • Magic straightening (매직스트레이트, magic): Straightening curly/wavy hair into straight hair with a flat iron. Beyond making hair straight, it dramatically reduces volume.
  • Down-perm (다운펌): A procedure that calmly presses the sides and back of the hair down toward the scalp. Especially popular with men.

(3) Color & care

  • Color (염색): Ordinary color without bleach takes about 3 hours; with bleach it can take 3–5 hours.
  • Hair clinic / treatment (헤어 클리닉 / 트리트먼트): Intensive care that restores damaged hair. Sometimes done alongside a perm or color to protect the scalp.
  • Head spa (헤드스파, scalp spa): Intensive scalp care like deep cleansing, exfoliation, massage, and steam. A core menu of foreign beauty tourism (see ⑥ below).

> Practical tip for foreigners: Showing the result you want as a photo is the surest way. Pin down the type: "magic (매직)" to straighten curls, "setting-perm (셋팅펌)" to add curls, "down-perm (다운펌)" to just tame sticking-out hair. Even without a shared language, one photo usually gets the point across.

③ Requesting a Designer — Who Does Your Hair Sets the Price

The second big trait of a Korean salon is the 'designer-by-name' system. It's the part most unfamiliar to foreigners.

  • Korean salons have a rank system — roughly intern → designer → siljang (실장, head stylist) → jeomjang (점장, store manager) → wonjang (원장, salon director). (Depending on store size, some ranks may not exist.)
  • An intern usually handles shampoo and assisting, while the person actually doing the cut or perm is a dijaineo (디자이너, designer) or above. The internship alone usually takes 2–3 years — that's how skilled a designer is.
  • When booking, you can request a specific dijaineo (디자이너, designer), and the higher the experience and rank, the higher the price. That's why even the same "cut" costs more from a wonjang (원장, director) than from a regular designer.
  • The store's homepage and Naver list each designer's profile and specialty (cut specialist / perm specialist / color specialist), so you can see and choose.

> Tip for foreigners: For a first visit, it's good to request someone strong in the procedure you want — like "a designer good at cuts" or "a perm-specialist designer." It doesn't have to be the most expensive wonjang (원장, director) — a procedure-specialist designer often offers better value.

④ Booking Culture — 'Naver Booking' Over Walk-ins

Korean salons now run on reservations by default. Some take walk-in customers, but popular stores and popular designers fill up almost entirely by booking.

  • Naver Booking (네이버 예약): The most widely used platform. Integrated into Naver Map, it lets you find a store → pick a designer → book a time all at once. Reviews, first-visit discounts, and Naver Pay points are attached too.
  • Kakao Hairshop (카카오헤어샵): Another major booking app.
  • For popular stores and English consultations, booking ahead is strongly recommended. Many places also take reservations via the store's Instagram DM.

> Foreigner reality: Naver and Kakao bookings are mostly in Korean, which is a barrier. So foreigners often book via Instagram DM, through foreigner-facing booking platforms (Klook, KKday, Trazy, etc.), or by securing an English-capable store in advance. When booking, it's good to also confirm "English OK?" and your desired procedure / designer.

⑤ Service Details — Shampoo, Massage, Blow-dry, No Tip

What ultimately makes a Korean salon feel "different" is the detail and service.

  • Shampoo + scalp & neck massage: A shampoo goes in before and after a cut, perm, or color, and many places give a scalp and neck massage then. That's where the "being cared for" feeling starts.
  • Blow-dry & styling included: When the procedure ends, you get a pretty blow-dry and set that very day. So the reflection in the mirror at the end is a finished look.
  • Extra services: Drinks & snacks, luggage storage, magazines, tablets, etc.
  • No tip: To stress it again — there's no tipping. The menu price is the end of it. That said, for a perm or color the final amount can rise depending on hair length, condition, and extra procedures, so it's safest to lock in "the total" during the consultation (this is an item that occasionally sparks extra-charge disputes).

> Price caution: Some stores have been reported to charge a consultation price different from their online price list. Before the procedure, be sure to confirm the final price including extra charges.

⑥ Men's Hair — Salon, Barbershop, Men-Only

Korean men's hair options split roughly into three.

  • Ordinary salon (일반 미용실): A salon serving both men and women. Men also get cuts, perms (especially down-perm and men's setting-perm), and color.
  • Men-only chains (e.g., Blue Club (블루클럽)): Launched in the late 1990s as Korea's first men-only salon concept. It drew popularity with a bright, clean environment and cheap cuts (early cuts were very cheap; prices have since risen to around the ₩10,000 range, per reports — confirmation by store recommended). It claims to offer not just a basic trim but style coordination.
  • Barbershop (바버샵): A trend on the rise again lately. Pomade and classic styles, with some places also including shaving. Popular with younger men interested in fashion and grooming. Seoul has everything from value barbershops under ₩30,000 to premium ones.

> Tip for foreign men: If you just want a clean cut, a men-only chain or barbershop is fast and good value. If you want a K-style like a perm or down-perm, you're better off requesting a designer good at men's hair at an ordinary salon.

⑦ K-Hair Hallyu — The Rise of Foreign Beauty Tourism

As the popularity of K-drama and K-pop spread even into hair and scalp care, a kind of beauty tourism — coming to Korea on purpose to do your hair — has taken root.

  • K-perm · K-style: Foreigners visit Korean salons to copy idols' and actors' natural setting-perms and trendy curls and colors.
  • Head spa (헤드스파, scalp spa): The hottest foreign menu lately. Salons in areas like Myeongdong and Gangnam feature 15–18-step scalp spas (deep cleansing, exfoliation, massage, steam, therapy) as a signature. For foreigners it's a relatively new experience even within K-beauty, so it's a talking point.
  • Multilingual support: Stores with high foreign demand offer English, Chinese, and Japanese consultations, and some run packages spanning precise cut, perm, color, and even makeup.
  • Overseas media like National Geographic have covered Korea as "the new face of beauty tourism," making hair and scalp care one pillar of the K-beauty industry.

⑧ Practical Salon Guide for Foreigners — Booking, Terms, Etiquette

> Prices & operations vary by store — confirmation before visiting recommended.

  • Where: Foreigner-friendly salons cluster in Hongdae, Myeongdong, Gangnam, and Itaewon. Myeongdong and Gangnam are strong on multilingual support and head spa; Hongdae tends to be trendy with more English sessions.
  • Booking: Naver Booking / Kakao Hairshop have a Korean-language barrier, so use Instagram DM, foreigner booking platforms, or English-capable stores. When booking, confirm procedure · designer · "English OK?"
  • Stating the procedure: Showing a photo of the result you want is the strongest move. For perms, pin down the type (setting-perm (셋팅펌) / magic (매직) / down-perm (다운펌)). If you have curly or non-Asian hair, first confirm whether they have experience with that hair.
  • Clearing time: Cut 1–1.5 hours, perm about 3 hours, color (with bleach) 3–5 hours. For a perm or color, clear half a day.
  • Price & tip: Lock in the total including extra charges at the consultation. No tip — the menu price is the end.
  • Etiquette: It's natural to receive the finishing styling before you leave. If you don't like it, it's OK to politely ask for an adjustment on the spot.

❓ Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

  • Can I go to a salon even if I don't speak Korean? — Totally possible. Showing a photo of the hair you want gets the point across most of the time. It's even easier with an English-capable store or an Instagram DM booking.
  • Can I just say "perm"? — ❌ In Korea, perms come in many types. To add curls say setting-perm (셋팅펌), to straighten curls say magic straightening (매직스트레이트), to just tame sticking-out hair say down-perm (다운펌) — name the type.
  • Should I tip? — No. Korean salons have no tipping culture. The menu price is the final price.
  • Will they handle my curly hair well? — "Foreigner-friendly" ≠ "lots of experience with curls." Confirm before booking whether the designer has experience with non-Asian / curly hair.
  • How long does it take? What about price? — Cut 1–1.5 hours, perm about 3 hours, color 3–5 hours (with bleach). Price depends on the procedure, length, and designer's rank, so lock in the total at the consultation (the figures here are estimates — confirmation recommended).
  • Do I have to request a designer? — Not required, but recommended. Picking a designer strong in your desired procedure raises satisfaction. It doesn't have to be the most expensive wonjang (원장, director).
