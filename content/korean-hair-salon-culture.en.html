Korean hair salon and hair culture — Why Korean hair salons are different

Cut·Perm·Dye·Head Spa·Designer Nomination — Explainer of Korean beauty salon culture for foreigners. Not ‘a place to get a haircut’, but ‘why so much detail, what and how’.

There is something that foreigners who visit a Korean beauty salon for the first time are usually surprised by. I only reserved one cut, but it comes with a set of shampoo, scalp massage, blow dry, and styling, drinks and snacks are provided, and when you're done, you don't have to tip. If you look at the perm menu, there are not just one or two types, but ten different types, including setting perm, magic straight, down perm, and volume magic. When you make a reservation, you nominate a specific designer, not just “anyone.” In Korea, hair salons are not just about haircuts, but are closer to a detail industry that designs styles and manages hair. So these days, there is even a beauty tourism where foreigners who want to imitate K-drama/idol hair styles deliberately get perms and head spas in Korea (so much so that National Geographic is treating Korea as the 'new face of beauty tourism'). This article is not about “which hair salon should you go to” (→ cross-link), but explains why Korean beauty salons are so different and the details of procedures, reservations, and services from a foreigner’s perspective. Key point: There is more than one type of ‘perm’ at Korean beauty salons. Curling perm (setting perm) and flattening or pressing perm (magic/down perm) are all grouped together as ‘perm’ — the most confusing point for foreigners (② below).

⚠️ 3 things foreigners often misunderstand

1. "Perm = making curls" ❌ — In Korea, perm is not just about adding curls. Magic straight (curly → straight hair) and Down perm (pressing straight hair) are all ‘perms’. In other words, perm is a general term for “all procedures that change the shape of hair using drugs and heat.” If you just say “perm,” you might get the exact opposite of what you want, so you have to specify the type.

2. "It's polite to tip" ❌ — Korean hair salons have no tipping culture. The price on the menu is the final price, and the designer doesn't expect a tip either. There is no need to tip 15-20% the American/European style - it is not rude at all not to tip.

3. "They will treat my hair (curly/non-Asian hair) well" ⚠️ — Not all 'foreigner-friendly' hair salons are the same. It is one thing to speak English, and it is another thing for the designer to have experience working with curly and non-Asian hair. If you go for curly hair in a place where you are accustomed to straight Korean hair, the results may be disappointing. The key is to check your hair type experience in advance.

① What is a Korean hair salon — not ‘cuts’ but ‘one set of services’

The first characteristic of Korean hair salons is that even a single cut is accompanied by a full service.

  • Usually, when you get a cut, the following steps are done in one flow: Shampoo → Scalp/Neck Massage → Cut → Drying/Styling. In other words, it is not just a “cut off the hair” but includes finish styling**.
  • Many stores provide free additional services such as drinks and snacks, luggage storage lockers, and mobile phone charging.
  • So, even with the same 'cut', in Korea sitting time is long (cuts are usually 1-1.5 hours), and the experience itself is close to the feeling of being 'managed'.

This “detail + service” orientation is the backbone of Korean beauty salon culture. Rather than cutting it quickly and finishing it, it is a way of thinking that looks at hair texture, style, and scalp together.

② Type of treatment — Properly distinguish between perm, dye, and clinic

What makes a Korean salon's menu hard for foreigners is that the single word "perm" packs in far too much. It's easier to understand as three broad groups.

(1) Perms that add curl

  • Setting perm: Uses hot rollers at roughly 120-180°C to set thick S-curls or bouncy curls. The flagship Korean perm — natural-looking and long-lasting.
  • Regular perm (cold perm): The traditional method — curls made with chemical solution, no heat.

(2) Perms that straighten or press down

  • Magic straight ("magic"): Uses a flat iron to straighten curly or wavy hair into fully straight hair. Beyond just straightening, it also dramatically reduces volume.
  • Down perm: Gently presses hair that sticks out at the sides and back down toward the scalp. Especially popular with men.

(3) Color and care

  • Color: A regular dye job without bleaching takes about 3 hours; with bleaching, it can take 3-5 hours.
  • Hair clinic / treatment: Intensive care to repair damaged hair. Sometimes added alongside a perm or color job to protect the scalp.
  • Head spa (scalp spa): Intensive scalp care — deep cleansing, exfoliation, massage, steam. A core item on the foreign beauty-tourism menu (⑥ below).

Practical tip for foreigners: showing a photo of the result you want is the surest way to communicate. Name the type specifically — "magic" to straighten curls, "setting perm" to add curl, "down perm" to just tame flyaways. Even without shared language, a single photo usually gets the point across.

③ Designer Designation — Who Does Your Hair Sets the Price

The second big feature of Korean salons is the "designer designation" system — the part foreigners find most unfamiliar.

  • Korean salons run on a rank system — roughly intern → designer → team leader (siljang) → store manager → master (wonjang), in that order (some ranks may be absent depending on the salon's size).
  • Interns usually handle shampooing and assisting; the people actually doing cuts and perms are designer rank or above. The internship alone typically takes 2-3 years — which shows how much skill "designer" represents.
  • When booking, you can name a specific designer, and the higher their experience and rank, the higher the price. That's why the same "cut" costs more with a wonjang (master) than with a regular designer.
  • Each designer's profile and specialty (cut specialist / perm specialist / color specialist) is usually posted on the salon's website or Naver page, so you can browse and choose.

Tip for foreigners: if it's your first visit, it's smart to designate someone strong in the specific treatment you want — a "cut specialist" or a "perm specialist," for example. It doesn't have to be the priciest wonjang — a specialist designer is often the better value.

④ Reservation Culture — "Naver Booking" Over Walk-Ins

Korean salons now run primarily on reservations. Walk-ins are still accepted at some places, but popular salons and popular designers are almost always booked solid.

  • Naver Booking: the most widely used platform. Built into Naver Maps, so you find a salon, pick a designer, and book a time slot all in one place — complete with reviews, first-visit discounts, and Naver Pay points.
  • Kakao Hairshop: another major booking app.
  • For popular salons and English-speaking consultations, advance booking is strongly recommended. Many salons also take bookings through Instagram DM.

The reality for foreigners: Naver and Kakao booking are mostly in Korean, which is a real barrier. So foreigners often book through Instagram DM, through foreigner-oriented platforms (Klook, KKday, Trazy, etc.), or by lining up an English-speaking salon in advance. When booking, it helps to confirm "English OK?" along with the treatment and designer you want.

⑤ Service Details — Shampoo, Massage, Blow-Dry, No Tipping

What ultimately makes a Korean salon feel "different" comes down to detail and service.

  • Shampoo + scalp/neck massage: shampooing is worked in before and after the cut, perm, or color, and many salons throw in a scalp and neck massage at that point — the starting point of that "being cared for" feeling.
  • Blow-dry and styling included: once the treatment is done, you leave with your hair already blow-dried and styled for the day — so the face in the mirror when you're done is the finished look.
  • Extra amenities: drinks and snacks, luggage storage, magazines or a tablet, and more.
  • No tip: worth repeating — there is no tipping. The menu price is the final price. That said, for perms and color, the final total can rise depending on hair length, condition, and extra steps, so it's safer to pin down "how much in total" during the consultation — this is a common point of disputed add-on charges.

A pricing caution: some salons have been reported to quote a different price in person than what's listed online. Always confirm the final price including any add-ons before the treatment starts.

⑥ Men's Grooming — Salons, Barbershops, and Men-Only Chains

Korean men's hair options break down into three broad categories.

  • Regular salons: unisex salons. Men get cuts, perms (especially down perms and men's setting perms), and color here too.
  • Men-only chains (e.g., Blue Club): emerged in the late 1990s as Korea's first men-only salon concept. It built its popularity on a bright, clean setting plus cheap cuts (prices were extremely low in the early days; they're now known to run in the low tens-of-thousands-won range — confirm per store). It bills itself as offering style coordination, not just a basic haircut.
  • Barbershops: a trend making a comeback recently. Pomade, classic styles, and some places even include a shave. Popular with young men interested in fashion and grooming. Seoul has everything from budget barbershops under 30,000 won to premium ones.

Tip for foreign men: if you just want a clean cut, a men-only chain or barbershop is fast and good value. If you want a K-style look like a perm or down perm, it's better to designate a designer at a regular salon who's strong with men's hair.

⑦ The K-Hair Wave — The Rise of Foreign Beauty Tourism

As the popularity of K-dramas and K-pop spreads into hair and scalp care, beauty tourism — foreigners deliberately getting their hair done in Korea — has taken hold.

  • K-Perm·K-Style: Foreigners who want to imitate the natural setting perm and trendy curls and colors of idols and actors visit Korean beauty salons.
  • Head Spa (Scalp Spa): The hottest foreign menu recently. Salons in Myeongdong and Gangnam offer 15~18 step scalp spa (deep cleansing, exfoliation, massage, steam, therapy) as their signature. It is a hot topic among foreigners as it is a relatively new experience among K-beauty.
  • Multilingual support: Stores with a high demand from foreigners provide consultation in English, Chinese, and Japanese, and also offer packages that include precision cut, perm, color, and makeup.
  • Hair and scalp care has become one of the pillars of the K-beauty industry, with overseas media such as National Geographic treating Korea as 'the new face of beauty tourism'.

❓ Foreigners Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

  • Can I go to the hair salon even if I don’t speak Korean? — It’s completely possible. If you show a photo of the hair you want, it will mostly work. It is more convenient to make reservations at English-speaking stores or through Instagram DM.
  • Can I just say ‘perm’? — ❌ There are many types of perms in Korea. If you want to add curls, you should call it setting perm, if you want to straighten out the curls, you must call it magic straight, and if you want to straighten out the curls, you should call it down perm.
  • Do I have to tip? — No. Korean hair salons have no tipping culture. The price on the menu is the final price.
  • Can you handle my curly hair too? — ‘Foreigner-friendly’ does not mean ‘experience with curly hair’. Check to see if the designer has experience with non-Asian/curly hair before making a reservation.
  • How ​​long does it take? What is the price? — Cut 1~1.5 hours, perm approximately 3 hours, dyeing 3~5 hours (including bleaching). The price varies depending on the procedure, length, and designer’s position, so the total amount is confirmed during consultation (it is recommended to estimate and confirm the figures here).
  • Is it necessary to nominate a designer? — Not required, but recommended. Satisfaction is high if you choose a designer who is strong in the treatment you want to perform. It doesn’t necessarily have to be the most expensive ledger.
