Nowon Guide — Gongneung-dong Dokkaebi Market, Sanggye Jungang Market, Gyeongchunseon Forest Path, Hwarangdae Railway Park and Nowon Train Village, Bulamsan and Suraksan

Subtitle: Gongneung-dong Dokkaebi Market (공릉동도깨비시장), cards accepted · Sanggye Jungang Market (상계중앙시장), 08:00–20:00, varies by shop · Gyeongchunseon Forest Path (경춘선숲길), always open · Hwarangdae Railway Park (화랑대 철도공원), 24 hours · Nowon Train Village (노원기차마을), adults 4,000 won, closed Mondays · Bulamsan and Suraksan (불암산·수락산) — confirmed via Korea Tourism Organization tourism data (TourAPI)

🎯 How to Use This Guide

Nowon-gu has markets and parks linked to the old Gyeongchun Line. There is Gongneung-dong Dokkaebi Market, which began with the opening of the Gyeongchun Line in 1939, Gyeongchunseon Forest Path, where the abandoned railway was turned into a park, Hwarangdae Railway Park at the old Hwarangdae Station, and Nowon Train Village, a model train exhibition hall. In Sanggye-dong (상계동) there is Sanggye Jungang Market, and on the border between Nowon-gu and Namyangju there are Bulamsan and Suraksan.

This guide covers only the places above, and the operating details are copied from values registered in the Korea Tourism Organization tourism data (TourAPI).

👹 Gongneung-dong Dokkaebi Market — A Market That Began with Stalls on the Railway

Gongneung-dong Dokkaebi Market (53 Dongil-ro 180-gil, Nowon-gu, Seoul (서울 노원구 동일로180길 53), Gongneung-dong, ☎ 02-976-4143) began with street stalls that gathered near Hwarangdae Station when the Gyeongchun Railway opened on July 25, 1939. It started as a traditional dokkaebi market, where, when a crackdown came, the stalls vanished as if a dokkaebi (goblin) had passed through, then gathered on the tracks again once it was over. Together with the creation of Gyeongchunseon Forest Park in 2019, it is growing into a cultural tourism market.

  • What the market looks like: There is a dokkaebi mural at the entrance, and an arcade roof lets you shop even in rain or snow. Shops line both sides of an uphill and downhill road.
  • What is sold: Farm products, livestock products, seafood, fruit and vegetables, and sundry goods.
  • Opening hours and closed days: They differ by shop.
  • Cards: Accepted. Parking is available, and there are restrooms.

🛒 Sanggye Jungang Market — Sanggye-dong Neighborhood Market

Sanggye Jungang Market (33 Sanggye-ro 23na-gil, Nowon-gu (노원구 상계로23나길 33), Sanggye-dong, ☎ 070-4141-1116) is a permanent traditional market opened in 2010. Opinion was divided over whether the market should survive amid the redevelopment boom, but through a modernization project it gained underground power lines, an arcade, and a parking lot.

  • Opening hours: 08:00–20:00, varying by shop.
  • What is sold: Food, clothing, and household sundries.
  • Getting there: 5–7 minutes on foot from Exit 2 of Sanggye Station on Subway Line 4.
  • Facilities: Restrooms and parking are available.

🚂 Gyeongchunseon Forest Path and Hwarangdae Railway Park

Gyeongchunseon Forest Path (107-2 Hagye-dong, Nowon-gu (노원구 하계동 107-2), ☎ Visitor Center 02-3783-5977) is a path made by turning the old Gyeongchun Line tracks into a park. The Gyeongchun Line, which opened as a private railway on July 25, 1939, was closed after its last train on December 20, 2010 (2010-12-20), and became a park in stages from 2013. Always open, open year-round.

Hwarangdae Railway Park (608 Hwarang-ro, Nowon-gu (노원구 화랑로 608), Gongneung-dong, ☎ Nowon-gu Office Leisure City Division 02-2116-0668) is a place where the old Hwarangdae Station, which was Seoul's last small station, was turned into a park.

  • Exhibits: On the abandoned line sit a 1950s Mikado locomotive and a narrow-gauge train, and streetcars from the Czech Republic and Hiroshima, Japan. The old Hwarangdae Station is an exhibition hall.
  • Opening hours: Park 24 hours. Tram Library 10:00–19:00 (closed every Monday), cafe 11:00–21:00.
  • Parking: Free for 2 hours, then 150 won every 5 minutes.

🚞 Nowon Train Village — Model Train Exhibition Hall

Nowon Train Village (622 Hwarang-ro, Nowon-gu (노원구 화랑로 622), Gongneung-dong, ☎ 02-723-7793) is an exhibition hall that shows cities of the world with model trains.

  • Exhibits: The Swiss Hall (Bern, the Matterhorn, and the Jungfrau recreated at a scale of 1/87), the Italian Hall (miniatures of 41 landmarks), the N-gauge experience zone (adjust the train speed with a lever), and the 2nd-floor unusual trains exhibition hall.
  • Opening hours: 10:00–19:00, last entry 18:00.
  • Closed days: Every Monday, the days of Seollal and Chuseok.
  • Admission: Adults 4,000 won, teens and children 2,000 won.

⛰ Bulamsan and Suraksan

  • Bulamsan (Sanggye-dong, Nowon-gu (노원구 상계동), ☎ Nowon-gu Office Green City Division 02-2116-4939): At 508m, it is the lowest of the five mountains on Seoul's border, but its summit area is rocky. Royal azaleas bloom in spring. Always open.
  • Suraksan (Sanggye-dong, Nowon-gu (노원구 상계동), ☎ Nowon-gu Office Green City Division 02-2116-3950): It neighbors Bulamsan across Deongneung Pass. The Seoul citizens' favorite routes introduced by the tourism organization are three. One goes from in front of Bulamsan Station on Line 4 past Hangnimsa Temple and Yonggulam Hermitage, one goes from Suraksan Station Exit 1 on Line 7 at Surakgol Valley past Yeombulsa Temple, Sinseongyo Bridge, and Kkaldakgogae Pass, and one goes from Suraksan Station Exit 3 at Nowongol Valley to Dosolbong Peak. Always open.

⚠ 3 Common Mistakes

1. Going to see trains on a Monday — Nowon Train Village and the Tram Library at Hwarangdae Railway Park are closed every Monday. The park and the forest path are open.

2. Seeing Dokkaebi Market only as a neighborhood market — It is a market that began with stalls on the old Gyeongchun Line tracks, and together with the creation of Gyeongchunseon Forest Park in 2019, it is growing into a cultural tourism market.

3. Not checking the route before climbing the mountains — The Suraksan routes start from Bulamsan Station on Line 4 and Suraksan Station on Line 7, respectively.

⚙ FAQ 2

Q. Where can I see trains?

A. The real trains at Hwarangdae Railway Park and the model trains at Nowon Train Village. Train Village is closed on Mondays.

Q. Can I use a card?

A. According to the tourism organization, cards are accepted at Gongneung-dong Dokkaebi Market. Card information is not registered for Sanggye Jungang Market.
