Why Cheongnyangni General Market Sub Now — Standalone General & Traditional Focus Among 9 Markets

#598 Herb Medicine Market (Cheongnyangni·Gyeongdong) is the full 9-market cluster set. This #609 sub-deep is a standalone sub for Cheongnyangni General Market + Traditional Market + Night Market Old-School Eateries = JP·EN segment + Korean-food purist enthusiasts.

Cheongnyangni (청량리) is Seoul's largest transit hub in the northeast. Line 1, the Gyeongui-Jungang Line, the Gyeongchun Line, the Suin-Bundang Line, and KTX/ITX-Cheongchun services to Gangneung all converge here. The markets cluster within a 5-minute walk of the station, making same-day access easy.

This matrix covers 3 General Market picks + 3 Traditional Old-School picks + 3 Night Market picks + 3 Nearby Cross picks = 12 picks across 4 axes. Of all 12 picks, only verified real shop names and districts are listed; items marked as general clusters are written as district guides without fabricating specific shop names.

3 Mistakes Foreigners Make

Mistake 1. Visiting on Sunday

Many permanent shops inside Cheongnyangni General Market are closed on Sundays. In particular, many vendors in the produce and seafood wholesale districts pack up starting Sunday morning. Even the old-school eatery street has fewer than half its shops open compared to weekdays. Visiting Tuesday through Saturday is recommended; Monday also offers narrower choices since some districts use it for restocking.

Mistake 2. Bringing only a card

Old-school eateries and street stalls are often cash-only. Some shops in the General Market main building have card terminals but may refuse small payments (under ₩10,000) or add an extra fee. Withdrawing ₩30,000–50,000 in cash before heading out avoids any trouble. ATMs are available inside Cheongnyangni Station and at convenience stores near the station exit.

Mistake 3. Going to old-school eateries during peak hours (11:00–13:00)

Cheongnyangni's seolleongtang and gukbap old-school shops see office-worker lunch demand surge between 11:30 and 13:00, producing 30–60 minute waits. For foreign visitors who want to sit down without a language barrier, arriving between 10:00–11:00 or after 13:30 is more practical. Some shops that run out of ingredients close as early as before 14:00.

12 Picks, 4 Axes — District Deep Dive

Axis 1: Cheongnyangni General Market (3 Picks)

#1 Cheongnyangni General Market Main Building

Cheongnyangni General Market has been Seoul's largest wholesale-retail complex market in the northeast since the 1960s. Dried goods, grains, nuts, and seasonings are concentrated in one building.

  • Hours: 06:00–20:00 (varies by shop; many closed Sundays)
  • Prices: Dried goods ₩2,000–8,000 per 100g (varies widely by item, 2026)
  • Foreigner Fit: ★★★★ — Visually enjoyable to browse, some shops offer small-portion purchases
  • Access: Line 1 Cheongnyangni Station, Exit 1, 5-minute walk
  • English: Some shops handle basic English (numbers, item names); a translation app helps
  • Timing: 9:00–11:00 has the widest selection. Shops begin closing up after 18:00

Popular purchases among foreign visitors include small packs of dried seaweed, kelp, sesame seeds, japchae glass noodles, and red pepper powder. Choosing shops that offer vacuum packing makes it easier to meet airline carry-on rules.

#2 Cheongnyangni Produce Wholesale District

The produce wholesale district continues from the outdoor stalls and connected buildings on the market's outskirts. Box-unit wholesale deals are standard, but some vendors also sell retail, so single-item purchases are possible.

  • Hours: 04:00–16:00 (wholesale peak 05:00–09:00, retail 10:00–15:00)
  • Prices: Strawberries, 1 pack (500g) ₩5,000–8,000 / whole watermelon ₩15,000–25,000 (varies by season and variety, 2026)
  • Foreigner Fit: ★★★ — Bulk purchase units may be inconvenient for small-quantity buyers
  • English: None; communication by pointing and calculator
  • Timing: 10:00–14:00 is realistic for retail purchases

#3 Cheongnyangni Seafood Alley

A seafood-specialty alley connects to the back of the General Market. Fresh fish, dried goods (dried croaker, corvina, squid, pollock), and salted seafood are concentrated here.

  • Hours: 06:00–19:00 (for dried-goods shops; fresh fish may close as early as 14:00–15:00)
  • Prices: Dried croaker, 10 pieces ₩15,000–40,000 (by size and variety), squid, 1 piece ₩2,000–4,000 (2026)
  • Foreigner Fit: ★★★★ — An intense market experience with strong smells and visuals; vacuum packing available on request
  • English: None
  • Timing: Mornings offer the freshest selection. Dried goods can be bought in the afternoon too

Axis 2: Cheongnyangni Traditional Old-School Eateries (3 Picks)

#4 Cheongnyangni Seolleongtang·Gukbap Street

Old-school shops specializing in seolleongtang (설렁탕, ox-bone soup), seonji-gukbap (ox blood soup with rice), and sundae-gukbap cluster the back streets around Cheongnyangni Station. Several have been operating for 30–50+ years. Many keep their original signage and interior from decades ago, which resonates especially well with the JP segment drawn to retro atmosphere.

  • Hours: 06:00–15:00 (may close early once ingredients run out); some open 24 hours
  • Prices: Seolleongtang, 1 bowl ₩9,000–12,000 / gukbap (국밥, rice-and-soup) ₩8,000–11,000 (2026)
  • Foreigner Fit: ★★★★★ — Simple ordering (1–3 menu items), self-serve side dishes, sign translation apps work well
  • Access: Line 1 Cheongnyangni Station, Exits 2–3, 3–7 minute walk into the back streets
  • English: None. Point at the menu board or use Google Lens
  • Timing: 10:00–11:00 or after 13:30 (avoid peak hours 11:30–13:00)
  • Note: The bone-broth base has a strong aroma — if this is a first Korean gukbap experience, seolleongtang has a lower entry barrier than sundae-gukbap

#5 Cheongnyangni Gomtang Alley

A cluster of old-school shops specializing in gomtang (곰탕), made by simmering ox bones and oxtail for hours. The broth is clearer and milder than seolleongtang, making it an easier entry point for foreigners. Mixing rice into the broth is standard, and kkakdugi (깍두기, radish kimchi) and its brine pair well with gomtang.

  • Hours: Roughly 07:00–15:00 (varies by shop; many closed Sundays)
  • Prices: Gomtang, 1 bowl ₩9,000–13,000 (2026)
  • Foreigner Fit: ★★★★ — The clear broth means less unfamiliarity
  • English: None
  • Timing: Morning 7:00–10:00 for breakfast, or before the 11:00 break

#6 Cheongnyangni Street-Food & Stall Cluster

Stalls and small street-food shops selling tteokbokki (떡볶이, spicy rice cakes), sundae (순대, Korean blood sausage), fish cake, and fried snacks gather at the market's alley entrances and back streets. A full food experience is possible for ₩3,000–8,000 per person — one of Seoul's cheapest.

  • Hours: 10:00–22:00 (varies by shop; night stalls open from evening)
  • Prices: Tteokbokki, 1 serving ₩3,000–4,000 / sundae ₩3,000 / fried snack, 1 piece ₩500–1,000 (2026)
  • Foreigner Fit: ★★★★★ — Point-and-order style, low cost, photogenic
  • English: None; pointing and calculator
  • Timing: Lively right before lunch (10:00–11:00) or in the evening (17:00–20:00)

Axis 3: Cheongnyangni Night Market (3 Picks)

#7 Cheongnyangni Night Stalls (20:00–02:00)

Once the sun sets, pojangmacha (포장마차, street food tents) and night stalls fill the back streets around Cheongnyangni Station. Grilled squid, chicken skewers, hotteok, and ramyeon are the core late-night menu. Solo foreign visitors can access these easily, but a companion is recommended late at night since intoxicated patrons may be present.

  • Hours: 20:00–02:00 (extended during peak season and weekends; many pack up around midnight on weekdays)
  • Prices: Grilled squid ₩5,000–8,000 / chicken skewers, 2 pieces ₩3,000 (2026)
  • Foreigner Fit: ★★★ — Good if the goal is experiencing the night atmosphere
  • English: None
  • Timing: 21:00–23:00 has the most stalls and best energy

#8 Cheongnyangni Night Market Enthusiast Cluster

Restaurants that stay open into the night are scattered through the dining alley around the General Market. Haejangguk (해장국, hangover soup), grilled pork belly, and chicken-to-go are the main late-night draws, and some restaurants have single-seat layouts where solo foreign diners can sit comfortably.

  • Hours: 18:00–01:00 (varies by shop)
  • Prices: Grilled pork belly, 200g ₩15,000–18,000 / haejangguk ₩9,000–12,000 (2026)
  • Foreigner Fit: ★★★ — Wide menu choice, aimed at a nighttime Korean-food experience
  • English: None; menu photos and translation apps needed

#9 Cheongnyangni Dawn Auction District (#587 cross)

The dawn seafood and produce wholesale auctions concentrate between 04:00 and 06:00. Sightseeing access is limited, and entering the auction floor is difficult unless you're there for wholesale trade. If dawn markets interest you broadly, the #587 (Noryangjin·Garak·Majang) cross-reference is more practical.

  • Hours: Dawn 04:00–07:00 (auction peak)
  • Foreigner Fit: ★★ — Difficult for sightseeing purposes
  • Access: For general tourists, it's more realistic to experience the dawn seafood stalls around Cheongnyangni Station itself

Axis 4: Nearby Cross (3 Picks)

#10 Gyeongdong Market (경동시장) Herbal Medicine District (#598 cross)

Gyeongdong Market is a 10-minute walk (about 700m) from Cheongnyangni General Market. It's a district concentrated with herbal medicine, ginseng, red ginseng, and health-food wholesale, and many foreign visitors come specifically to shop here. Pairing it with the #598 Yangnyeongsi deep dive completes a half-day route.

  • Hours: 08:00–18:00 (varies by shop; some closed Sundays)
  • Prices: Red ginseng extract, 30 packets ₩30,000–80,000 / ginseng root ₩15,000–50,000 (by quality grade, 2026)
  • Foreigner Fit: ★★★★★ — Some shops speak English, Chinese, or Japanese. Some shops display Tax Refund signs
  • Access: Line 1 Cheongnyangni Station → 10-minute walk, or Line 1 Jegi-dong (제기동) Station, Exit 1, 3-minute walk
  • English: Some shops (many have experience serving foreign customers due to the herbal wholesale trade)
  • Timing: Morning through afternoon offers the widest choice. Shops start closing after 18:00

#11 Yangnyeongsi (약령시) Herb Medicine Market (#598·#365 cross)

Gyeongdong Market's herbal medicine street, Yangnyeongsi, has been a hub for herbal medicine since the Joseon dynasty. Shops handling traditional herbs like dried ginseng, deer antler, angelica root, and astragalus root cluster here. Most foreign visitors come not for herbal prescriptions but to buy health supplements or gift-worthy herbs. See #598 and #365 for details.

  • Hours: 08:00–18:00
  • Foreigner Fit: ★★★★ — Visually interesting, with K-Medical experience value
  • English: Some shops have basic ability

#12 Noryangjin (노량진)·Garak (가락)·Majang (마장) (#587 Full-Set Cross)

For a deeper dawn-market experience beyond Cheongnyangni, pairing with the #587 dawn market deep dive (Noryangjin Fish Market, Garak Market, Majang-dong Livestock Market) is practical. These are reachable by subway, and a full 03:00–06:00 dawn course can be designed.

Practical Info — Transit, Payment, Language

Transit

  • Basic access: Line 1 Cheongnyangni Station — Exits 2–3 (toward the old-school eatery street), Exit 1 (toward the General Market)
  • Metro-area access: Transfer at Cheongnyangni Station to the Gyeongui-Jungang Line, Gyeongchun Line, and Suin-Bundang Line. It's also a departure point for KTX and ITX-Cheongchun to Gangwon Province (Gangneung, Chuncheon), so it can double as a stopover before regional trips
  • Bus: Numerous buses serve every direction of the northeastern metro area. Getting to Gyeongdong Market can be by bus or on foot
  • Taxi·Kakao T: Convenient for night and dawn visits via app-based taxi. A taxi stand is available in front of Cheongnyangni Station at all times

FAQ 5

Q1. Where should I start on a first visit?

From Line 1 Cheongnyangni Station Exit 1 → enter the General Market main building (#1) and loop through the dried-goods and grain district. Then move toward Exits 2–3 into the back streets for lunch at an old-school seolleongtang shop (#4) or street-food stalls (#6). A half-day route is enough.

Q2. How is this different from the #598 Yangnyeongsi deep dive?

#598 is the full 9-market cluster covering Cheongnyangni + Gyeongdong Yangnyeongsi. #609 (this piece) is a sub-deep focused on Cheongnyangni General Market + Traditional Old-School Eateries + Night Market. If you're interested in herbal medicine, read #598 first; if market food and old-school eatery experiences are the goal, #609 is the more efficient starting point.

Q3. What's the price range?

One meal per person ₩8,000–13,000 (gukbap, seolleongtang), stall snacks ₩3,000–8,000. A realistic gift budget for Gyeongdong Market herbal souvenirs is ₩30,000–100,000. A full half-day course can be completed for ₩20,000–30,000.

Q4. Is it okay to visit on Sunday?

Some General Market shops and many old-school eateries are closed on Sundays. If Sunday is unavoidable, it's more practical to build a route around the night stalls (#7·#8) and 24-hour haejangguk shops. Checking Google Maps hours before visiting is recommended.

Q5. Can I go alone without speaking Korean?

Yes. Point-and-order (pointing at what you want) and calculator conversations work in most situations. Scanning Korean menu boards with a translation app's camera mode makes ordering manageable. Some shops in the Gyeongdong Market herbal district can handle basic English.

5-Season Axis

  • Spring (March–May): Season for seasonal spring greens, strawberries, and Korean melon. Lots to see at the produce district (#2). Pleasant weather also suits night-stall experiences.
  • Summer (June–August): Peak wholesale season for watermelon, Korean melon, and peaches. Morning produce district is lively. Night stalls concentrate after 22:00 due to the heat.
  • Fall (September–November): Peak season for dried goods and grain harvest. Dried shrimp, fall gizzard shad, and prawns arrive at the Seafood Alley (#3) during this period. JP tourist visits peak in fall.
  • Winter (December–February): Peak season for gomtang and seolleongtang old-school shops (#4·#5). Ideal for visits centered on hot broth. Designing an indoor-focused route works fine even in a cold snap.
  • Monsoon (part of June–July): Simplify the route around indoor markets (General Market main building). Night stalls may pack up in rain.

5 Tips for Foreign Enthusiasts

1. Line 1 Cheongnyangni Station as base camp — Different exits lead to different districts. Exit through Exit 1 (market) versus Exits 2–3 (old-school back streets) to save time.

2. #598·#365 herbal cross half-day — The route from Cheongnyangni General Market to Gyeongdong Yangnyeongsi connects on foot. Tax Refund for herbal souvenirs is available only at shops with the sign displayed — confirm before you go.

3. #587 dawn full set — For a deeper dawn-market experience, Noryangjin·Garak·Majang is more complete. Treat Cheongnyangni's dawn hours as a warm-up.

4. Bring ₩30,000–50,000 in cash — Cash is essential across old-school eateries, stalls, and night pojangmacha. Use the ATM inside Cheongnyangni Station.

5. JP·EN purist enthusiast fit — Best for travelers who want to experience Korea's real everyday markets rather than an Instagram aesthetic. A segment that focuses on taste and atmosphere over photos.
