ISSUE 03 · #1268 · 2026-07-24

🎯 Why Read the Aisle by Brand

Korea's convenience store and supermarket snack shelves are effectively an oligopoly of 5 confectionery giants (Nongshim, Orion, Lotte Wellfood (롯데웰푸드), Haitai, Crown). Rather than memorizing snacks one by one, learning to read 5 brand logos is far faster — each brand has its own character (the potato chip house, the chocolate house, the original-story house). While the existing convenience store cluster (#26 beginner's 18 picks, #1261 4-chain comparison, #1262 category roundup) covered "where to buy what," this issue is the enthusiast's axis of "who made it and why it blew up" — reading all the way down to the manufacturer printed on the back of the bag.

⚠️ Data note: Seasonal limited editions and collab releases are short-lived — availability and prices of individual products depend on the shelf at the time of your visit. The 15 classics have been steady sellers for decades, so they are always in stock.

⚠ 3 Mistakes Foreign Visitors Often Make

1. "All Choco Pies are from the same company": In Korea, Orion Choco Pie 情 (초코파이 정) and Lotte Choco Pie (롯데 초코파이) are two separate lineages — the original-story claim belongs to Orion (1974). The one that became a national snack in Vietnam, Russia, and China is also the Orion line. If you're buying gifts, check for the '情' logo on the box.

2. "Pepero = Pocky": They look alike, but Pepero created a national holiday, 'Pepero Day' on November 11 — a Korean cultural phenomenon in its own right. On that day, half the convenience store shelves turn into Pepero gift box displays. Korea-only lineups like Almond and Crunky are also more varied.

3. "Convenience store prices are the standard": The same snack is 20-30% cheaper at hypermarkets and Daiso (다이소). For single-bag tastings, go convenience store (check the 1+1 deals); for filling a souvenir box, hypermarkets and Daiso are the answer. See #48 must-buys and #579 mart map.

① Nongshim (농심) — The Original House of Snacks

1. Saeukkang (새우깡) ★ Korea's First Snack

  • Launched in 1971 — the starting point of Korean snack history. Ground real shrimp gives it a savory, salty character
  • Why it took off: the entire nation can sing its "hands keep reaching" jingle — and feeding Saeukkang to seagulls (Hangang River (한강), Incheon (인천) ferries) grew into a tourism meme of its own
  • Variants: Maeun Saeukkang (매운새우깡, spicy) and Ssal Saeukkang (쌀새우깡, rice) — the original red bag is the default

2. Banana Kick (바나나킥)

  • A fluffy, melt-in-your-mouth banana-flavored corn snack — an icon pair of 'Korean banana flavor' together with Banana Flavored Milk (바나나맛우유) (#1262)
  • Why it took off: childhood nostalgia trigger + the milk-pairing ritual

3. Kkul Kkwabaegi (꿀꽈배기)

  • A twisted-doughnut-shaped snack glazed with acacia honey — the textbook of sweet-and-salty
  • Tip: Nongshim = the company behind Shin Ramyun (신라면). For the ramyun deep dive, head to #833 ramyun pairing

② Orion (오리온) — Choco Pie 情 and the Global Comeback

4. Choco Pie 情 (초코파이) ★ The Legend of the National Snack

  • Launched in 1974 — a marshmallow-sandwich chocolate cake. The single-character '情 (jeong)' marketing became a symbol of Korea's culture of sharing
  • Why it took off: the meme that it circulates like currency in the army + the export legend of becoming a Vietnamese ancestral-rite offering and a Russian national snack
  • Gift grammar: a snack you buy by the box and hand out — individually wrapped, so it travels safely in your suitcase

5. Kkobuk Chip (꼬북칩 / Turtle Chips) ★ The Overseas Viral Comeback

  • A corn chip with a 4-layer crunch — the Choco Churros flavor in particular went viral at US Costco and on Amazon, then boomeranged home
  • Why it took off: "layer-by-layer crunch" challenge videos + overseas celebrity mentions — currently the #1 snack in foreign visitors' shopping baskets
  • Tip: the classic move is comparing two bags — Original (corn soup flavor) vs Choco Churros

6. Poca Chip (포카칩)

  • Korea's #1 best-selling potato chip, a steady seller — thin and clean-tasting in Original and Onion flavors
  • Why it took off: not flashy, but it owns the "never-fails default" position — beer pairing (#843 late-night snack grammar)

③ Lotte Wellfood (롯데웰푸드) — The Brand That Created a Holiday

7. Pepero (빼빼로) ★ The Snack That Made a National Holiday

  • A chocolate stick launched in 1983 — the star of Pepero Day on November 11 (four 1s = four sticks)
  • Why it took off: elevated into a seasonal culture of exchanging with partners, friends, and coworkers — in early November, convenience stores and marts transform into Pepero pop-up displays
  • Lineup: Original, Almond, Crunky, Nude (chocolate-filled) — gift box sets are season-limited

8. Kkokkal Corn (꼬깔콘)

  • A cone-shaped corn snack — the national ritual of wearing them on all 10 fingertips is a beloved social media photo format
  • Why it took off: the original play-plus-snack combo — disarms kids and foreign visitors alike

9. Chic Choc (칙촉)

  • A soft, tender chocolate chip cookie — a steady coffee companion
  • Tip: Lotte has the widest portfolio of any confectioner, spanning Ghana Chocolate (가나초콜릿) to World Cone (월드콘) (#1265 frozen treats)

④ Haitai (해태) — The Syndrome Maker

10. Honey Butter Chip (허니버터칩) ★ The 2014 Sellout Syndrome

  • A potato chip with a honey + butter sweet-salty coating — right after its 2014 launch it triggered the biggest syndrome in Korean snack history: nationwide sellouts, secondhand resale markups, and one-bag-per-person limits
  • Why it took off: the textbook case of scarcity marketing — "the harder to find, the more you crave it." It's available everywhere now, so knowing the backstory makes it twice as fun to eat
  • Tip: this bag is the original behind all the honey-butter copycats that followed

11. Home Run Ball (홈런볼)

  • Bite-sized choux puffs filled with chocolate cream — the #1 pick for parties and gatherings across generations
  • Why it took off: easy sweetness + built for sharing — doubles as a café dessert substitute

12. Matdongsan (맛동산)

  • A peanut-coated fried snack launched in 1975 — its "eat Matdongsan and enjoy the party" jingle is a steady classic
  • Why it took off: sits between hangwa (한과, traditional sweets) and Western confectionery — a bridge into traditional snacks (pairs with #1218 yakgwa (약과) trend)

⑤ Crown (크라운) — The House of Retro

13. Jolly Pong (죠리퐁)

  • A puffed-wheat cereal-style snack — the original of Korean-style cereal you pour milk over
  • Why it took off: cafés brought it back as Jolly Pong lattes and bingsu toppings — a staple ingredient of the retro dessert trend

14. Corn Chip (콘칲)

  • The original Korean-style corn chip, born in the 1970s — nutty and sturdy corn character
  • Why it took off: a steady beer companion + the star of the "who's the original" debate

15. Big Pie (빅파이)

  • A strawberry jam sandwich pie — the icon of old-school corner store memories
  • Why it took off: the retro package kept as-is — a newtro-vibe photo prop

🔥 What's Rising Now — 4 Trends for 2026

1. Dubai chocolate spin-offs: The kadaif + pistachio formula is spreading beyond chocolate into cookies, pies, and snacks — all 5 major brands are testing spin-off lines. Spot the word 'pistachio' (피스타치오) on a shelf and it's part of that family.

2. Chicken and restaurant brand collab chips: A wave of collab potato chips recreating actual fried chicken menu flavors — many are convenience-store exclusives, so the convenience store shelf is your first hunting ground.

3. The export boomerang: Kkobuk Chip Choco Churros, Pepero, and Choco Pie went viral abroad first → buying "the homeland version" on a Korea trip is now an established ritual.

4. Zero and low-sugar lines: Zero candies, low-sugar chocolate, and other health-leaning extensions — the alternative axis for sugar-conscious travelers.

⚙ 6 FAQs

1. Where is it cheapest?: Hypermarkets (E-Mart (이마트), Homeplus (홈플러스)) > Daiso > convenience stores, in that order. Convenience stores can flip the ranking if you catch 1+1 or 2+1 deals (#1261).

2. Suitcase space problems: Bagged snacks are light for their bulk but crush easily — boxed types (Choco Pie, Pepero, Home Run Ball) travel safest. Put bagged types in your carry-on.

3. Halal or vegan?: Most Korean snacks have no halal certification — check ingredient labels for pork-derived gelatin and dairy. Saeukkang contains shrimp (shellfish).

4. When are the seasonal limited editions?: Pepero Day in November (the biggest season), summer ice cream pairings, and holiday gift box seasons (January-February and September).

5. Recommended gift box combo: 1 box of Choco Pie + 3 kinds of Pepero + Kkobuk Chip Choco Churros + Honey Butter Chip + Jolly Pong = a 'K-snack brand sampler' for around 20,000 KRW.

6. How do I check the manufacturer?: Look at the maker's mark at the bottom of the back of the bag — memorize just the logos and the brand landscape of any shelf snaps into focus.

🗺 3 Real Shopping Routes

A. Hypermarket Souvenir Box (1 hour)

  • Hypermarket snack aisle → scan the 15 picks, one signature per brand → load up mostly on boxed types → check the promo stickers before checkout (#579 mart map)

B. Convenience Store Brand Bingo (30 minutes)

  • In a single convenience store, pick one snack from each of the 5 brands and complete a 5-square bingo — single-bag tastings and late-night pairings (#26, #1262)

C. Daiso + Mart Value Combo (half a day)

  • Daiso snack aisle (small packs, low prices) → hypermarket bulk sizes → tasting session at your hotel — after taste-testing, do your final box run at a mart near the airport right before departure
