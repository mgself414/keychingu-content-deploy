Seoul E-W Axis ⑤ Gunja, in the Morning — from the North Gate of Seoul Children's Grand Park to the Gopchang Alley

Subtitle: A side branch two stops off Line 7 from the Line 2 eastern half-loop (#1359). Enter Seoul Children's Grand Park through the gate closest to Gunja Station, circle the free park in the morning, pass the gopchang alley on Neungdong-ro, and loop back to the station — about two hours in all. A neighborhood KeyChinGu has never covered before. No shop names. Seoul E-W Axis, Part 5.

ISSUE · #1360 · 2026-09-07

🎯 The Frame — Two Stops Off the Axis

The E-W Axis overview (#1359) stopped only once, at Konkuk University Station (건대입구역). Transfer to Line 7 there, pass Children's Grand Park Station (어린이대공원역), and one more stop brings you to Gunja Station (군자역) — a transfer point in Gwangjin-gu (광진구), where Line 5 and Line 7 meet. It's not on any tourist map. But ten minutes from the station stands the gate of one of Seoul's largest free parks, and the alley in front of the station is lined with gopchang restaurants.

This piece links the two in the morning. Circle the park when it's quietest, pass through the gopchang alley as it's starting lunch prep, and loop back to the station. A flat course that works whether you're with kids or on your own.

  • Total time: about 2 hours (about 50 minutes walking + time in the park)
  • Best time: depart 9–10 AM — the park is open from early morning, but the zoo opens for viewing at 10 AM (varies by season and circumstances). The gopchang alley is quiet before lunch
  • Incline: none — flat ground aside from gentle hills inside the park

🚶 The Route — In Walking Order

The starting point is Gunja Station. Two stops from Konkuk University Station on Line 7, or reachable from Dongdaemun History & Culture Park (동대문역사문화공원) on Line 5.

① Gunja Station Exit 6 → Neungdong-ro (능동로) → North Gate of Seoul Children's Grand Park (🚶 about 10 minutes)

  • Come out Exit 6 and head south along Neungdong-ro. The park's main gate is in front of Children's Grand Park Station on Line 7, and the back gate is near Achasan Station (아차산역) on Line 5, but from Gunja Station the Neungdong-ro-side gate is the closest — per official guidance, the main gate, back gate, Guui Gate (구의문), and Neungdong Gate (능동문) all open from 5:00 AM to 10:00 PM (confirm officially before visiting).
  • 📖 Story 1 — The Park of 1973: Seoul Children's Grand Park opened on May 5, 1973, Children's Day. At the time it was the largest comprehensive children's amusement facility in East Asia, with admission at 30 won for children and 50 won for adults. It became free starting October 2006. Today its area is about 530,000㎡ — smaller than at opening, but a free park of this size is rare in the city center.

② A Loop Through the Park — Past the Zoo and Botanical Garden (🚶 about 20 minutes + time in the park)

  • Pass through the gate and a walking path circles the outer edge of the park. The zoo (viewing 10:00–17:00, free), botanical garden, musical fountain, and outdoor stage sit inside. In the morning, the space in front of the zoo enclosures is empty, which makes it good for viewing with kids.
  • Guides to each individual facility are at #232, #284, and #340. Here, the course passes through in order: north gate → zoo → botanical garden → fountain → toward the main gate.

③ Main Gate (Children's Grand Park Station) → Back Along Neungdong-ro (🚶 about 10 minutes)

  • Exit through the main gate and you're at Children's Grand Park Station on Line 7. You could head straight back to the axis (#1359) from here, but this piece follows Neungdong-ro back to Gunja Station — the alley is still ahead.

④ The Gopchang Alley on Neungdong-ro (🚶 about 10 minutes)

  • Near Gunja Station's Exit 6, gopchang restaurants (grilled beef and pork intestines) line both sides of Neungdong-ro. The alley's real stage is evening, but pass through in the morning while lunch prep is underway and the smell of charcoal and the sound of ingredients being prepped are the alley's face. No shop names here — come back in the evening and the place with the line is the answer.
  • 📖 Story 2 — The Neighborhood Next to the Park: After the park opened in 1973 and Line 7 (1996) came through, Neungdong-ro became a road where park visitors and residents mixed. The gopchang alley is the neighborhood's evening, and the park is its morning. This piece runs the two in reverse — the park in the morning, the alley before lunch.

⑤ Wrap-Up at Gunja Station (🚶 about 5 minutes)

  • The course ends at Gunja Station. Take Line 7 to Konkuk University Station to return to the Line 2 axis (#1359), or take Line 5 toward Dongdaemun and Gwanghwamun (connects with the #1356 axis).

🙏 Manners — the Core of This Course

  • The park is a morning exercise space for residents. Don't linger for long photo sessions in the middle of the walking path.
  • Don't tap on the zoo enclosures or feed the animals. No flash photography.
  • The gopchang alley sits right against a residential area. In the morning, don't peer into shops that are still prepping to open.
  • If you're with kids, watch the traffic side of Neungdong-ro — some stretches have a narrow sidewalk.

💡 Where You Might Get Thrown Off

  • Don't go looking for the "back gate": the back gate is near Achasan Station, far from Gunja Station. From Gunja Station, use the Neungdong-ro-side gate.
  • The zoo opens at 10 AM: the park opens from early morning, but animal viewing starts at 10 AM (varies by season). If you arrive at 9, walk the path first.
  • The gopchang alley is closed in the morning: come back in the evening to eat. This piece is a course for "passing through" the alley.
  • Station exit: Exit 6 leads toward the park and Neungdong-ro. Come out the other way and you'll have to cross a main road.

6 FAQs

Q1. Is this a neighborhood worth a tourist's time?

  • It's less a "must-see" than a "breathing room." You walk a free park with a zoo in the morning, and watch the lunch prep of an alley that isn't a tourist spot. Two stops off the axis (#1359), so there's no real cost to adding it.

Q2. Is it good to visit with kids?

  • This is the most kid-friendly piece in the series. Flat ground, free, a zoo, play facilities. For the full family guide, see #232 and #340.

Q3. What if I want to eat gopchang?

  • Come in the evening. This piece only passes through the alley in the morning. No shop is specified — go with the one that has a line.

Q4. Can I start from the main gate instead?

  • You can — the main gate is right in front of Children's Grand Park Station on Line 7. But then the gopchang alley comes first in the course, which flips this piece's "park in the morning, alley before lunch" order.

Q5. How does this connect to the main-line piece (#1359)?

  • Two stops on Line 7 from Konkuk University Station. Walk this piece, then take Line 7 back to Konkuk University Station from either Children's Grand Park Station or Gunja Station, and continue on the Line 2 inner loop.

Q6. What's the difference between Children's Grand Park and Seoul Forest (서울숲) or the Hangang parks?

  • Seoul Forest is an urban forest, the Hangang parks are riverside, and Children's Grand Park is a "family park" with a zoo and play facilities. For a park comparison, see #205.
