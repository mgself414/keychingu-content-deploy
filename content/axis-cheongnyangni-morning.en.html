Seoul E-W Axis ③ Cheongnyangni, in the Morning — from Gyeongdong Market and Yangnyeongsi to the Last Stop of Line 720

Subtitle: The eastern end of Seoul's east-west axis — a morning walk through the wholesale markets just outside the old city gate. From Jegi-dong Station, the course passes the traditional stalls of Gyeongdong Market, the medicinal-herb street of Seoul Yangnyeongsi, and a café built into an old theater, ending at Cheongnyangni Station — about ninety minutes in all. It's the eastern counterpart to the N-S Axis's Yangjae morning piece (#1355). Vendor lists are left to #598 and #609; this piece covers only the walking order and the smell. Seoul E-W Axis, Part 3.

ISSUE · #1358 · 2026-09-07

🎯 The Frame — East of the Old Wall, Seoul's Warehouse for Medicinal Herbs

The E-W Axis overview (#1356) passed through Heunginjimun (흥인지문) and out beyond the old city wall, stopping at Jegi-dong for just 15 minutes. This piece stretches that 15 minutes into ninety. The Cheongnyangni area is Seoul's largest traditional wholesale district, nine markets packed side by side — among them, Gyeongdong Market opened in June 1960, and the medicinal-herb shops west of Gosanja-ro (고산자로) were designated Seoul Yangnyeongsi in June 1995. A large share of Korea's medicinal-herb trade moves through these alleys.

The time slot is morning — after wholesale trading wraps up and before retail customers arrive, the window when the market lays out its goods. If the N-S Axis's Yangjae (양재) flower-market morning (#1355) was the axis's southern end, this piece is the E-W Axis's eastern end — both axes end where a wholesale market sits.

  • Total time: about 1 hour 30 minutes (about 35 minutes walking + time in the market and museum)
  • Best time: depart Tuesday–Saturday, 9–10 AM — most Yangnyeongsi shops are closed on Sundays, and the museum is closed Mondays. Mornings are when the smell and the sound are sharpest
  • Incline: none — flat ground the whole way

🚶 The Route — In Walking Order

The starting point is Jegi-dong Station, Subway Line 1, Exit 2. Come out and you're headed straight toward Gyeongdong Market.

① Jegi-dong Station Exit 2 → the Traditional Stalls of Gyeongdong Market (🚶 about 5 minutes)

  • Follow the main road from the exit and stalls and shopfronts begin right at the roadside. This is the entrance to the traditional stalls, dried seafood, wild greens, and vegetables stacked box by box. In the morning, the sound of vendors setting out their goods reaches you before anything else.
  • 📖 Story 1 — The Market Outside the Gate: Wangsan-ro (왕산로), outside Heunginjimun, has been the road bringing goods into the walled city from the east since the Joseon dynasty. Medicinal herbs and farm produce from Gangwon-do and eastern Gyeonggi-do came in on this road and were traded just outside the gate, and in 1960 the Gyeongdong Public Market opened on that same spot. If the area inside the wall, Jongno (종로), was where goods were sold, the area outside the gate, Cheongnyangni, was where they came in.

② The Traditional Stalls → Across Gosanja-ro to Seoul Yangnyeongsi (🚶 about 10 minutes)

  • Cut west through the market's inner passages and cross Gosanja-ro, and the air changes — the smell of medicinal herbs. From here it's Seoul Yangnyeongsi, a street lined with herbal medicine shops, wholesalers, and dealers one after another. Dried roots and fruits on display shelves, scales, and cutting blades make up the scenery of this alley.
  • Passing through is the principle here. #598 covers 12 picks of medicinal herbs and a Yangnyeongsi vendor guide, and #593 and #546 cover Korean medicine clinics. Here, you just walk the street once.

③ Yangnyeongsi → the Seoul Yangnyeongsi Museum of Korean Medicine (🚶 about 10 minutes)

  • Inside the Yangnyeongsi street stands the Seoul Yangnyeongsi Museum of Korean Medicine (서울약령시한의약박물관), a small museum showing the history of Korean medicine and its ingredients. Free (hours 10:00–17:00, closed Mondays — confirm officially before visiting). Thirty minutes covers it.
  • 📖 Story 2 — The Name "Yangnyeongsi": "Yangnyeongsi" was the name for a periodic market where medicinal herbs were gathered and sold in the Joseon era (Daegu Yangnyeongsi (대구 약령시) is the oldest — #393). This street in Seoul had herbal-medicine merchants gathering here from the 1960s on, and formally received the name in 1995. The name is old; the market is still working.

④ The Museum → the Gyeongdong Market Main Building → the Old Theater Café (🚶 about 10 minutes)

  • Cross Gosanja-ro again to reach the Gyeongdong Market main building. On the third and fourth floors sits a large café built into the Gyeongdong Theater (경동극장), built in the 1960s and closed in 1994 — Starbucks Gyeongdong 1960, where the theater's sloped seating rows are still the seats. A single representative case. A cup of coffee in the middle of the market is this piece's comma.
  • Beyond this, the market grounds continue on into the Cheongnyangni Wholesale Market (청량리종합시장), a produce market, and a fish market (#609). In the morning, the sight of crates stacking up in the produce alley is worth seeing.

⑤ Gyeongdong Market → Wrap-Up at Cheongnyangni Station (🚶 about 10 minutes)

  • Walk east along Wangsan-ro and you reach Cheongnyangni Station — where Line 1 and the Gyeongui-Jungang Line meet and KTX trains stop. Line 720's last stop within the city and the eastern end of this series.
  • To ride the axis in reverse, catch Line 720 toward Jingwan-dong from the Cheongnyangni Station stop — facing Heunginjimun head-on as you enter the walled city, on to Gwanghwamun and Independence Gate (the reverse of #1356).

🙏 Manners — the Core of This Course

  • This market's real customers are wholesale buyers. Don't photograph a stall mid-negotiation, and give way to hand carts and forklifts.
  • The herbs are merchandise — don't pick them up to touch or smell them. The display is visible enough from outside.
  • If you're just looking, don't step inside the shop.
  • Keep quiet in the museum. Don't let your group block the market passageways.

💡 Where You Might Get Thrown Off

  • On Sunday, this course doesn't work — most Yangnyeongsi shops are closed. Monday, the museum is closed. Tuesday–Saturday mornings are the right answer.
  • The exit number matters: Jegi-dong Station Exit 2 is the way to Gyeongdong Market and Yangnyeongsi. Start from Cheongnyangni Station instead and it's ten more minutes of walking to reach the market.
  • Hours vary by hall: the traditional stalls open from early morning, Yangnyeongsi from around 9 AM — check official information for the exact times.
  • If you want to buy medicinal herbs: export rules vary by country, so dried teas are the safer choice. See #598 and #1352 for detail.

6 FAQs

Q1. Is this a market worth visiting as a tourist?

  • Yes — but it's a "working market," not a "sightseeing market." That's what sets it apart from Gwangjang Market (광장시장) (#1257), and why you come in the morning.

Q2. I don't know anything about Korean medicine — is it still interesting?

  • The smell and the displays are the exhibit. The museum gives you context in thirty minutes. It's worth the walk even if you don't buy anything.

Q3. What's different from Gwangjang Market and Tongin Market (통인시장)?

  • Gwangjang and Tongin are food retail; Gyeongdong and Yangnyeongsi are wholesale and medicinal herbs. The scale is much bigger too. For food-focused options, see #1257 and #380.

Q4. Where can I get a cup of coffee?

  • The old theater café in the market's main building is the single representative case. For a broader café and old-shop list, see #609.

Q5. Can I bring kids?

  • Fine — flat ground and a free museum. Just hold hands while walking, since there are a lot of hand carts.

Q6. What does it mean to be the counterpart to the N-S Axis's Yangjae (#1355)?

  • Both series end their axis at a wholesale market, and both are morning courses. Yangjae is flowers, Cheongnyangni is medicinal herbs. Seoul's southern end and eastern end close out on the same structure.
