Seoul Architecture Time Walk — Traditional, Modern & Contemporary: 15 Picks + An Architectural Appreciation Lens

Subtitle: From Gyeongbokgung's wooden post-and-beam construction (가구식) → the modern brick and stone of Myeongdong Cathedral (명동성당) and Culture Station Seoul 284 (문화역서울284) → the contemporary skyscrapers of DDP and Lotte World Tower (롯데월드타워) — a deep dive that reads 600 years of Seoul architecture in chronological order. Not another palace tour (#207), museum guide (#206), or DDP night guide (#172) — the first time lens that looks at "architecture itself."

ISSUE 03 · #1267 · 2026-07-23

🎯 Why a Seoul Architecture Time Walk

Seoul is a city where 600 years of dynastic wooden palaces → the Western-style brick and stone of the open-port and colonial era → the contemporary skyscrapers of world-star architects are compressed within a few subway stops. Unlike New York or Paris, where a single era's style dominates, Seoul's strength is that eras of style are layered on top of each other, letting you read the entire history of architecture just by walking.

KeyChinGu (키친구) has covered palaces and temples (#207), museums (#206), and DDP night shopping (#172) by area and theme, but this is the first time we've offered a lens for reading the flow of architectural style and era itself. In particular, modern architecture (1880s-1945) — Myeongdong Cathedral (명동성당), the old Seoul Station, the Bank of Korea (한국은행), and the Jeongdong (정동) area — is a hidden top pick for foreign architecture and history enthusiasts, yet it was an area KeyChinGu had never covered. This is the true highlight of this piece.

⚠ 3 Common Mistakes Foreigners Make

1. "Korean traditional architecture is similar to China and Japan": On the surface, both feature wooden structures with tiled roofs, but the underlying principles differ. Korea's essence lies in restraint that doesn't fight nature (Jongmyo Jeongjeon (종묘 정전) overwhelms with 101m of corridor alone, without ornate decoration) + borrowed scenery, chagyeong (借景) (Changdeokgung Huwon (창덕궁 후원) didn't build a garden — it "borrowed" the mountainside itself) + the structural beauty of entasis columns (배흘림기둥) and gongpo bracket sets (공포, 栱包). This is a different sensibility from China's grandeur and Japan's precision. See lens ① below for more.

2. "Modern architecture is just old buildings": Myeongdong Cathedral (명동성당), the old Seoul Station, and the Bank of Korea (한국은행) are historical records with the history of the open-port era (1876), colonial rule (1910-45), and the Korean Empire engraved into every brick. Once you know "who built it, why, and in what style," the whole of Seoul's modern history opens up to you. What's more, most of these buildings are free and within downtown walking distance (the Jeongdong (정동) course packs 8 modern buildings into 2km). A hidden top pick for foreign photo ops and history deep dives.

3. "Contemporary architecture = DDP only": DDP (Zaha Hadid) is famous, but it isn't the starting point of Seoul's contemporary architecture. Kim Swoo-geun (김수근) and Kim Chung-up (김중업) — Korea's first-generation masters of the 1960s-70s (the Space Group Building / 공간사옥, Samil Building / 삼일빌딩) — planted the roots, and there's a 60-year lineage running from them through DDP, Lotte Tower (롯데타워), Leeum (리움), and Amorepacific (아모레퍼시픽). It's also remarkable how densely the works of world-renowned architects (Botta, Nouvel, Koolhaas, Chipperfield, Herzog & de Meuron) are packed into Seoul.

① Traditional — Joseon's Wooden Architecture (1395~) · Appreciation Lens + Top 3

Individual visits to palaces, Jongmyo, and temples are covered in detail in #207. Here we focus only on 3 lenses for "how to look at the architecture."

3 Appreciation Lenses

  • Post-and-beam wooden framework (가구식 목구조): A joinery system (결구, 結構) that fits wood together without nails. The gongpo (공포, 栱包) bracket sets atop the columns disperse the roof's load and let the eaves extend far out — these deep eaves block the summer sun while letting in the winter light (science + aesthetics).
  • Hierarchy with nature and borrowed scenery: Buildings are placed in accordance with nature. Changdeokgung Huwon (창덕궁 후원) is the prime example — rather than constructing a garden, it "borrows" the surrounding mountains and valleys as they are.
  • Dancheong (단청) and empty space: Palaces and temples are ornately painted in dancheong, while Jongmyo (종묘) and seowon (서원, Confucian academies) are extremely restrained. The same wooden construction, but with color calibrated to purpose.

1. Gyeongbokgung Geunjeongjeon (경복궁 근정전) ★ Joseon's Main Royal Palace Hall

  • Location: 161 Sajik-ro, Jongno-gu (Line 3, Gyeongbokgung Station)
  • Completed: Built 1395, rebuilt 1867 (rebuilt by the Heungseon Daewongun after being destroyed in the Japanese invasions)
  • Foreign visitor rating: ◎◎ — the pinnacle of Joseon architecture · a top photo spot for the Royal Guard Changing Ceremony
  • Appreciation point: the hierarchy of the two-tiered stone platform, the double-hipped roof, the rank stones, and the paved courtyard. Admission ₩3,000 (~$2.25) (free in hanbok). See #207 for details.

2. Jongmyo Jeongjeon (종묘 정전) ★ UNESCO World Heritage · World's Longest Wooden Structure

  • Location: 157 Jongno, Jongno-gu (Lines 1/3/5, Jongno 3(sam)-ga Station)
  • Completed: Built 1395, rebuilt 1608, later extended to 101m
  • Foreign visitor rating: ◎◎ — the pinnacle of restrained beauty without ornamentation. The space architects love most in Seoul
  • Appreciation point: a single corridor stretching endlessly across 19 bays. Minimalism with all decoration stripped away. See #207 for details.

3. Changdeokgung Huwon (창덕궁 후원) ★ The Garden of Borrowed Scenery

  • Location: 99 Yulgok-ro, Jongno-gu (Line 3, Anguk Station)
  • Completed: 1405 (the rear garden developed from the 17th century onward)
  • Foreign visitor rating: ◎ — a garden that borrows nature · reservation-only viewing
  • Appreciation point: Buyongji Pond (부용지), Juhamnu Pavilion (주합루), Aeryeonjeong Pavilion (애련정). The archetype of the Korean garden, with artifice kept to a minimum. See #207 for details.

② Modern — The Brick and Stone of the Open-Port Era and Modernization (1880s-1945) · Highlight

The period when Western-style architecture was transplanted and adapted through the open-port era (1876), the Korean Empire (1897), and Japanese colonial rule (1910-45). KeyChinGu's first coverage of this area, and the central axis of this piece.

4. Myeongdong Cathedral (명동성당) ★ Korea's First Brick Gothic

  • Location: 74 Myeongdong-gil, Jung-gu (Line 4, Myeongdong Station · Line 2, Euljiro 1(il)-ga Station)
  • Completed: 1898 (designed by Father Eugène Coste) · Historic Site No. 258
  • Foreign visitor rating: ◎◎ — Korea's first pure Gothic building · a spire built from over 20 kinds of red brick
  • Signature: pointed arches, rib vaults, a rose window. A symbol of Korean Catholicism and a sacred site of modern democratization. Admission free (interior viewing available outside Mass hours)

5. Culture Station Seoul 284 (문화역서울284) ★ The Former Seoul Station Building

  • Location: 1 Tongil-ro, Jung-gu (Lines 1/4, AREX, Seoul Station)
  • Completed: 1925 (designed by Tsukamoto Yasushi) · Historic Site No. 284
  • Foreign visitor rating: ◎◎ — Renaissance style plus a Byzantine-style dome. The former Gyeongseong Station reborn as a cultural complex
  • Signature: the central domed hall, the first-class waiting room. Now an exhibition and performance space, admission free (varies by exhibition). Worth seeing right after arriving at Seoul Station

6. Bank of Korea Money Museum (한국은행 화폐박물관) ★ Designed by Tatsuno Kingo

  • Location: 39 Namdaemun-ro, Jung-gu (Line 2, Euljiro 1(il)-ga Station · Line 4, Hoehyeon Station)
  • Completed: 1912 (former head office of the Bank of Joseon, designed by Tatsuno Kingo, 辰野金吾) · Historic Site No. 280
  • Foreign visitor rating: ◎ — a Seoul work by the master who designed Tokyo Station · Renaissance stonework
  • Signature: a symmetrical granite facade, a domed roof. Open free as a money museum. Combine with a visit to Shinsegae's flagship store and Myeongdong

7. Deoksugung Seokjojeon (덕수궁 석조전) ★ The Korean Empire's Western-Style Palace

  • Location: 99 Sejong-daero, Jung-gu (Lines 1/2, City Hall Station)
  • Completed: 1910 (designed by J.R. Harding) · Neoclassical
  • Foreign visitor rating: ◎ — a European-style stone palace built within a palace's grounds. East (the traditional palace) and West (the stone hall) within a single wall
  • Signature: an Ionic colonnade, the Korean Empire History Hall (reservation required). Deoksugung admission ₩1,000 (~$0.75). See #207 for details.

8. Jeongdong Historic District (정동 근대건축군) ★ An Open-Air Museum of Modern Architecture on Foot

  • Location: The Jeongdong area, Jung-gu (Lines 1/2, City Hall Station) — densely packed within a 2km walk
  • Composition: Jeongdong First Methodist Church (정동제일교회) (1897, Korea's first Protestant church building, Historic Site No. 256) · the former Russian Legation (구 러시아공사관) (1890, the stage of the King's flight to the Russian legation, in Jeongdong Park) · Baejae Hakdang East Building (배재학당 동관) (1916) · Ewha Girls' High School Simpson Memorial Hall (이화여고 심슨기념관) (1915) · the former Shin-A Ilbo Annex (구 신아일보 별관), among others
  • Foreign visitor rating: ◎◎ — the No. 1 modern-history walk, connected to the Deoksugung Stone Wall Road (덕수궁 돌담길). Most sites are free
  • Signature: Western architecture left behind by missionaries and diplomats of the open-port era, all in one neighborhood. See the modern-era one-day course below

9. Dilkusha (딜쿠샤) ★ A Westerner's House in Seoul

  • Location: 17 Sajik-ro 2-gil, Jongno-gu (Line 3, Dongnimmun Station)
  • Completed: 1923 (the house of American Albert Taylor, the name meaning "Palace of Heart's Delight")
  • Foreign visitor rating: ○ — a red-brick mansion beside a 400-year-old gingko tree · the life of a foreigner during Japanese colonial rule
  • Signature: restored and now a house museum, admission free (reservation recommended). Combine with the site of General Gwon Yul's (권율) house and the Haengchon-dong (행촌동) alleys

③ Contemporary — Skyscrapers and Deconstructivism (1960s~) · A 60-Year Lineage

From the first-generation masters Kim Swoo-geun (김수근) and Kim Chung-up (김중업) (1960s-70s) → to the landmarks of world-star architects (2000s~). DDP, Leeum, and Amorepacific are covered in #172 and #206 from an area/exhibition perspective; here the focus is on architects and style.

10. Arario Museum in Space (아라리오뮤지엄 인 스페이스) (former Space Group Building, 구 공간사옥) ★ Ground Zero of Korean Contemporary Architecture

  • Location: 83 Yulgok-ro, Jongno-gu (Line 3, Anguk Station)
  • Completed: 1971 and 1977 (designed by Kim Swoo-geun, 김수근) · now a museum
  • Foreign visitor rating: ◎ — an ivy-covered black brick building. Kim Swoo-geun's signature work, called "the sacred ground of Korean contemporary architecture"
  • Signature: a maze-like skip-floor structure staggered by half-levels. Set beside Changdeokgung (창덕궁) in dialogue with tradition. Museum admission (paid)

11. DDP Dongdaemun Design Plaza (DDP 동대문디자인플라자) ★ Zaha Hadid

  • Location: 281 Eulji-ro, Jung-gu (Lines 2/4/5, Dongdaemun History & Culture Park Station)
  • Completed: 2014 (designed by Zaha Hadid, 2004 Pritzker Prize winner) · freeform parametric design
  • Foreign visitor rating: ◎◎ — the world's largest freeform structure · column-free streamlined curved surfaces
  • Signature: 45,000 aluminum panels, LED light-up at night. General exhibitions free. See #172 and #206 for night shopping and exhibition details

12. Lotte World Tower (롯데월드타워) ★ Korea's Tallest Building at 555m

  • Location: 300 Olympic-ro, Songpa-gu (Lines 2/8, Jamsil Station)
  • Completed: 2017 (designed by KPF) · 123 floors, 555m
  • Foreign visitor rating: ◎◎ — a silhouette drawn from the curves of a brush and porcelain · dominates the Seoul skyline
  • Signature: the Seoul Sky (서울스카이) observatory on floors 118-123 (paid; checking the price and hours before visiting is recommended) · a glass sky deck at extreme height

13. Leeum Museum of Art (리움미술관) ★ Three Masters, Three Buildings

  • Location: 60-16 Itaewon-ro 55-gil, Yongsan-gu (Line 6, Hangangjin Station)
  • Completed: 2004 · Mario Botta (Museum 1, ceramics and terracotta) + Jean Nouvel (Museum 2, corroded steel) + Rem Koolhaas (the Children's Education & Culture Center)
  • Foreign visitor rating: ◎◎ — three masters, each in their own language, on a single site. Worth visiting for the architecture alone
  • Signature: the permanent collection free, special exhibitions paid (reservation required). On a hill in Hannam-dong (한남동). See #206 for collection details

14. Amorepacific HQ (아모레퍼시픽 본사) ★ David Chipperfield

  • Location: 100 Hangang-daero, Yongsan-gu (Line 1/Line 4, Sinyongsan Station)
  • Completed: 2017 (David Chipperfield) · a cube inspired by the white porcelain moon jar (달항아리)
  • Foreign visitor rating: ◎ — a cube carved out at its center · a courtyard and roof garden. Restrained minimalism
  • Signature: the ground-floor lobby, the museum (APMA), an open courtyard. A pilgrimage site for K-beauty headquarters architecture

15. Seoul City Hall New Building (서울시청 신청사) ★ Yoo Kerl, A Glass Wave

  • Location: 110 Sejong-daero, Jung-gu (Lines 1/2, City Hall Station)
  • Completed: 2012 (designed by Yoo Kerl, 유걸) · glass curtain wall
  • Foreign visitor rating: ○ — a glass wave rising behind the old city hall (1926 Renaissance style, now the Seoul Library, 서울도서관). The curve of the traditional eave reinterpreted for today
  • Signature: a massive indoor green wall, the Citizens' Hall (시민청). Open free. Frames the old city hall (modern) and new city hall (contemporary) in a single view

+ Additional viewing points

  • Samil Building (삼일빌딩) (1970, Kim Chung-up, 김중업) — Jongno's first high-rise office building, steel-framed in the style of Mies van der Rohe
  • Sewoon Sangga (세운상가) (1968, Kim Swoo-geun, 김수근) — Korea's first mixed-use megastructure, revived through the "Sewoon Re:Newal" (다시세운) project
  • 63 Building (63빌딩) (1985) — 249m on Yeouido (여의도), golden glass, once Asia's tallest building
  • Sebitseom (세빛섬) (2014) — three artificial islands on the Han River at Banpo (반포), a night-photo spot
  • Starfield Library (별마당도서관) (2017) — a 13m grand bookshelf at COEX, free
  • SongEun Art Space (송은아트스페이스) (2021, Herzog & de Meuron) — in Cheongdam (청담), a triangular concrete mass

⚙ 6 FAQs

1. Can you see all three eras in one day? Possible. Starting from City Hall: modern (Jeongdong, Deoksugung) → walk → traditional Jongno (Jongmyo, Gyeongbokgung) → subway → contemporary (DDP, Lotte Tower). But given the density, half a day per era is recommended. See the courses below.

2. Which buildings can be seen for free? Myeongdong Cathedral, Culture Station Seoul 284, the Bank of Korea Money Museum, most of the Jeongdong modern architecture, Dilkusha, DDP's general exhibitions, Leeum's permanent collection, the Amorepacific lobby, Seoul City Hall, Starfield Library. Almost all modern architecture is free.

3. Where does wearing hanbok help? At the palaces (Gyeongbokgung, Changdeokgung, Deoksugung), hanbok gets you free admission. It doesn't matter for modern/contemporary sites, but photos against the backdrop of Jeongdong and Seokjojeon are popular.

4. What are the best photo spots for architecture? The corridor of Jongmyo Jeongjeon, the facade of Myeongdong Cathedral, the colonnade of Seokjojeon, DDP's curved surfaces at night, Lotte Tower reflected in Seokchon Lake (석촌호수), the courtyard at Amorepacific.

5. Is English signage available? Culture Station Seoul 284, the Money Museum, Leeum, DDP, and the Jeongdong signage all include English. Myeongdong Cathedral and Seokjojeon have English leaflets.

6. Alternatives on a rainy day? The Money Museum, Culture Station Seoul 284, Leeum, Starfield Library, and the Amorepacific museum are all indoors, so rain won't matter.

🌸 5 Seasons
