Why Seoul's Vegan Cafes and Restaurants Matter

Seoul consistently ranks among Asia's top 5 vegan cities on HappyCow's global rankings. The question foreign vegan and vegetarian tourists ask most often is "isn't it hard to eat vegan in Korea?" — but knowing the three axes of Itaewon (이태원), Hongdae (홍대), and Gangnam (강남) solves that for an entire month of meals. This guide focuses on those three areas plus app infrastructure, with only information you can use on the spot.

Deep Dive — The 12 Picks Across 4 Axes

Axis 1: Gangnam (3 Picks)

1. Gangnam Vegan Restaurant Cluster (#713, #771)

Area character: Within a 1km radius of Gangnam Station. Fewer dedicated vegan specialty restaurants than "restaurants with a strong vegan menu." Gangnam's general standard for interior design and plating is high, and its rate of English menus is higher than Seoul's average (65%).

Practical info

  • Access: Densely clustered within 5 minutes on foot of Gangnam Station Exit 11, Line 2
  • Hours: Lunch 11:30 AM-2:00 PM, dinner 5:30-9:00 PM. Lunch sets run 20-30% cheaper than ordering à la carte
  • Price: Lunch set ₩15,000-22,000, dinner mains ₩25,000-40,000
  • English: Use HappyCow's "has English menu" filter to prioritize restaurants with English menus
  • Vegan certification: Official domestic vegan certification (Korea Vegan Certification Center) is still rare. Rely on HappyCow's "vegan-friendly" tag, and show a card that reads "No meat, no fish, no dairy, no eggs" when ordering
  • Tip: One or two "vegetarian buffet"-style restaurants operate near Gangnam Station. Search "Gangnam" in the HappyCow app and apply the "vegan" filter to confirm what's currently open

Foreigner fit: foodie ★★★★★ / luxury ★★★★ / solo ★★★★ / couple ★★★★★

2. Cheongdam/Apgujeong Vegan Luxury (#771)

Area character: Adjacent to Cheongdam-dong's luxury shopping street. Mostly vegan fine dining, with some spots offering wine or natural-wine pairings. Suits foreign tourists in the luxury or wellness persona.

Practical info

  • Access: 5-10 minutes on foot from Cheongdam Station, Line 7, or Apgujeongrodeo Station, Bundang Line
  • Reservation: Book at least 3-7 days ahead for weekends. Use Naver Reservation or the restaurant's Instagram DM (confirm English availability)
  • Price: Course menu ₩60,000-120,000 per person. À la carte ₩20,000-50,000
  • English: Most have English menus and English-speaking staff. Ask "vegan course available?" when booking
  • Vegan certification: Request in advance from the chef when building a course. Distinguish between restaurants offering a "fully vegan tasting menu" and those that are simply "vegan adaptable"
  • Timing: Weekday lunch is 30-40% cheaper than dinner and less crowded

Foreigner fit: luxury ★★★★★ / wellness ★★★★★ / couple ★★★★★ / foodie ★★★★★

3. Gangnam Vegan Cafes (#742, #717)

Area character: A dense cafe zone near Gangnam, Seolleung, and Yeoksam stations. Non-dairy options like oat milk and almond milk are now standard. Several cafes specialize in vegan bakery items (vegan croissants, vegan cakes).

Practical info

  • Oat milk upcharge: Mostly ₩500-1,000 extra. Starbucks Korea also offers oat milk
  • Vegan bakery timing: Visit before 11:00 AM — popular items sell out early
  • Payment: Card 100%. Most run cashless
  • English: Many have English-language menus. Pointing at a drink also works for ordering
  • Using HappyCow: Adding a "café" filter to the "vegan" category in the Gangnam area turns up 10+ spots (as of 2026)

Foreigner fit: MZ ★★★★★ / solo ★★★★★ / Instagram ★★★★★

Axis 2: Hongdae/Hapjeong (3 Picks)

4. Hongdae Vegan Cluster (#711, #138)

Area character: From the Hongdae main gate to the Sangsangmadang area. The vegan zone foreign backpackers and MZ-generation foreign visitors visit most. High quality for the price, with relatively easy English communication.

Practical info

  • Access: 5 minutes on foot from Hongik Univ. Station Exit 9 (Line 2/AREX/Gyeongui-Jungang Line)
  • Price: Vegan burgers/pasta ₩12,000-18,000, ₩18,000-25,000 per person including a drink
  • English: Many spots are run by young owners and staff, with a high rate of English communication. Showing a HappyCow listing's English reviews when ordering also works
  • Confirming vegan status: "Is this fully vegan?" or "No animal products at all?" are effective questions. A dish labeled "vegetarian" sometimes still contains egg or dairy, so always confirm
  • Peak hours: 30 minutes to 1 hour of waiting on weekends between 1:00-4:00 PM. Weekday lunch is recommended
  • Street food: Vegan hot dog and vegan tteokbokki street stalls exist near Hongdae's "Walking Street." A simple "is this vegan?" confirms it

Foreigner fit: MZ ★★★★★ / budget ★★★★ / solo ★★★★★ / backpacker ★★★★★

5. Hapjeong/Mangwon Vegan Cafes (#711, #142)

Area character: Two stops from Hongdae. Hapjeong Station to Mangwon-dong is dense with Instagram-aesthetic vegan cafes. Less crowded than Hongdae, with a stronger local vibe.

Practical info

  • Access: 5 minutes on foot from Hapjeong Station Exit 1 (Lines 2/6), or 5 minutes on foot from Mangwon Station Exit 1
  • Instagram spots: Vintage interiors combined with vegan cake and vegan bingsu (shaved ice) show up repeatedly on foreign visitors' social feeds
  • Price: Drinks ₩6,500-9,000, vegan desserts ₩6,000-12,000. ₩25,000-35,000 for two people
  • English: A mix of cafes with and without English menus. Point-ordering usually works even without one
  • Mangwon Market connection: Visiting a Mangwon-dong cafe followed by browsing Mangwon Market is a popular route with foreign visitors

Foreigner fit: Instagram ★★★★★ / MZ ★★★★★ / couple ★★★★

6. Sangsu Vegan + Indie Cafes

Area character: The Sangsu Station area, between Hapjeong and Hongdae. Small independent cafes with vegan options are scattered around here. Suits visitors who want to avoid Hongdae's crowds and sit somewhere quiet.

Practical info

  • Access: 3 minutes on foot from Sangsu Station Exit 1, Line 6
  • Character: No large franchises, mostly independent cafes. Owner-operated, so requests for vegan adjustments are handled flexibly
  • Price: Drinks ₩5,500-8,500. Adding a vegan option costs ₩1,000-2,000 extra
  • English: Some owners speak English. A translation app resolves most communication
  • Using HappyCow: Searching within a 500m radius of Sangsu Station turns up 5-8 listings. Check that reviews are recent before visiting

Foreigner fit: solo ★★★★★ / indie/aesthetic ★★★★★ / budget ★★★★

Axis 3: Itaewon and Foreigners (3 Picks)

7. Itaewon Vegan Restaurants (#710)

Area character: The center of Seoul's foreign community. The densest cluster in Seoul of restaurants offering vegan, gluten-free, and halal options together. HappyCow's #1 area in Seoul.

Practical info

  • Access: 3 minutes on foot from Itaewon Station Exits 1 or 4, Line 6
  • English: English-capable across the entire district. Both menus and staff are essentially 100% English
  • Price: Vegan burgers ₩14,000-18,000, vegan tacos ₩12,000-16,000, vegan Indian food (dal, sabzi) ₩13,000-22,000
  • Vegan certification: Many Middle Eastern, Indian, and Mexican restaurants in Itaewon have naturally vegan menu items (no meat used to begin with), so vegan meals are available even without formal certification
  • Halal overlap: Many halal-certified restaurants near Itaewon's mosque (Seoul Central Masjid, run by the Korea Muslim Federation) also offer vegan-capable menu items. The best area for travelers who need both vegan and halal simultaneously
  • Timing: Friday and Saturday evenings draw the biggest crowds. Tuesday-Thursday lunch and dinner are more relaxed
  • HappyCow: Searching within a 1km radius of Itaewon Station turns up 30-50 listings. Filter: vegan / rating 4.0+ / a review within the last 6 months

Foreigner fit: foreign residents ★★★★★ / vegan ★★★★★ / halal ★★★★★ / solo ★★★★★ / family ★★★★

8. Hannam Luxury Vegan (#710, #771)

Area character: A 10-minute walk from Itaewon. Hannam-dong is dense with galleries, boutique shops, and upscale restaurants. Fine-casual to fine-dining restaurants with vegan menus are increasingly common.

Practical info

  • Access: 5 minutes on foot from Hangangjin Station Exit 1, Line 6, or 10 minutes on foot from Itaewon Station
  • Price: Fine-casual ₩20,000-40,000 / fine-dining course ₩60,000-100,000+
  • Reservation: Required for weekend dinners (1-2 weeks ahead). Use Naver Reservation or the Catchtable app
  • English: Most have English menus and English-speaking staff. Start with "Do you have a fully plant-based option?"
  • Atmosphere: A mix of design-gallery-cum-cafes and plant-filled interiors — Instagram-aesthetic meets luxury
  • Natural wine: A growing number of Hannam-dong's natural-wine bars offer solid vegan options. Search HappyCow alongside the Instagram hashtag "#HannamdongVegan" ("Hannam-dong vegan")

Foreigner fit: luxury ★★★★★ / wellness ★★★★★ / couple ★★★★★ / foodie ★★★★★

9. Gyeongnidan-gil Vegan Cafes and Young Foreign Enthusiasts

Area character: A 15-minute walk from Itaewon Station, with the distinctive atmosphere of a sloped alleyway. Rent stabilization compared to its peak has brought a wave of small, distinctive vegan cafes back into the neighborhood.

Practical info

  • Access: Itaewon Station Exit 2 → 15 minutes on foot, or bus 405/421 to Noksapyeong Station, then 5 minutes on foot
  • Price: Drinks ₩6,500-9,000, vegan brunch ₩12,000-18,000
  • English: Being adjacent to Itaewon, many shops communicate well in English
  • Atmosphere: A mix of local Koreans and foreign residents. A genuine neighborhood-cafe feel without excessive touristification
  • HappyCow: Search within a 500m radius of Noksapyeong Station, or search the text "Gyeongnidan-gil" to confirm which vegan cafes are currently operating

Foreigner fit: indie/aesthetic ★★★★ / solo ★★★★ / foreign residents ★★★★

⚠️ 3 Common Mistakes Foreigners Make

Mistake 1: Assuming "vegetarian" ("채식") means fully vegan

In Korea, a "vegetarian" label often still includes egg or dairy. Always confirm before ordering: "No eggs, no dairy, no meat, no fish, no seafood?" Filtering for the "vegan" tag on HappyCow reduces this confusion.

Mistake 2: Showing up without a reservation on a weekend evening

Well-known vegan restaurants in Itaewon and Gangnam routinely fill up on weekend evenings. Fine-dining and course-menu restaurants in particular need a reservation 1-2 weeks ahead. Secure one in advance via Naver Reservation or the Catchtable app.

Mistake 3: Assuming Itaewon alone covers all of Seoul's vegan scene

Itaewon is certainly the easiest, but Hongdae, Hapjeong, and Gangnam all have rich vegan options too. Brunch is often better in Hapjeong/Mangwon, and fine dining is often better in Cheongdam/Hannam.

Practical Info

Transport

  • Itaewon: Itaewon Station Exits 1/4, Line 6
  • Hongdae: Hongik Univ. Station Exit 9 (Line 2/AREX/Gyeongui-Jungang Line)
  • Hapjeong: Hapjeong Station Exit 1, Lines 2/6
  • Gangnam: Gangnam Station Exits 10-11, Line 2
  • Cheongdam/Hannam: Cheongdam Station, Line 7; Apgujeongrodeo Station, Bundang Line; Hangangjin Station, Line 6
  • T-money transit card (bought at convenience stores, ₩3,000-4,000 deposit) — 20% cheaper than paying cash on subway/bus

Reservation Methods

  • Naver Reservation: Korean-language, but usable via Google Translate. Some listings offer card pre-payment
  • Catchtable: Has an English UI. Focused on upscale restaurants
  • Instagram DM: Small cafes and indie restaurants often take reservations via Instagram DM. Many accept messages in English
  • Call 1330: If booking is difficult, call 1330 for interpretation support

Language Support

  • A basic order card: Search "vegan order card Korean" on Google, print the Korean-language phrase (저는 완전 채식주의자입니다. 고기·생선·계란·유제품·꿀을 먹지 않습니다 — "I am a strict vegetarian. I don't eat meat, fish, eggs, dairy, or honey"), and carry it with you
  • Google Translate: The camera-translation feature can instantly translate a Korean menu
  • Papago: Naver's translation app, specialized for Korean, with high accuracy on food vocabulary

Halal Overlap

Itaewon is the one area in Seoul where vegan and halal needs can both be met. Around the mosque (Seoul Central Masjid), halal-certified restaurants in the Hannam/Itaewon area often carry naturally vegan menu items — ideal for Muslim vegan travelers too.

FAQ

Q1. Is it hard to find vegan food in Seoul?

Not if you know the three areas of Itaewon, Hongdae, and Gangnam. Searching "Seoul" in the HappyCow app turns up 200+ listings. Itaewon alone has 30-50.

Q2. Can I order without speaking Korean?

Itaewon is 100% English. Hongdae and Gangnam can mostly be handled with English menus and point-ordering. Carrying a Google Translate camera and a "vegan order card" is your best bet.

Q3. How much does it cost?

Cafe drinks ₩6,500-9,000, a regular meal ₩12,000-22,000, fine dining ₩40,000-120,000. Hongdae and Hapjeong offer a satisfying meal for ₩20,000-30,000 per person.

Q4. Are K-pop idols vegan too?

Some idols — BTS's RM, NewJeans's Hanni among them — openly practice vegetarianism or veganism, which has raised interest. This is part of what's driving the growth of vegan travelers among K-culture fans.

Q5. Is temple food vegan too?

Yes, by default (fully vegan when the five pungent vegetables — garlic, green onion, chives, wild chives, asafoetida — are excluded). The general public can experience temple-food restaurants around Jogyesa. Free food-sharing events happen around Buddha's Birthday (May).

Foreigner Pain-Point Guide

  • Language: 100% English in Itaewon. Hongdae and Gangnam rely on Google Translate plus point-ordering. Printing a "vegan order card" is recommended
