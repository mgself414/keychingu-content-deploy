34_Jjimjilbang Etiquette (For Foreign Visitors)

One-Line Intro

Jjimjilbang (찜질방): Korea's social sauna where you eat eggs, watch TV, and sleep overnight — a one-of-a-kind Korean cultural experience.

Who This Is For

  • Travelers who want to experience Korean bath culture firsthand
  • Travelers looking for an affordable overnight stay or a way to unwind after a long day

How to Use a Jjimjilbang (Step-by-Step)

Step What to Do Tips
1. Entry Put your shoes in a locker → receive a key Remember your locker number
2. Payment Pay entry fee at the counter → receive your uniform Towels & jjimjil outfit (찜질복) usually included
3. Changing Room Store your clothes and belongings in a personal locker Men's and women's changing rooms are fully separate
4. Bathing Area (Optional) Hot bath, cold bath, shower Enter fully nude — swimwear not allowed
5. Put On Your Uniform Change into the provided jjimjil outfit (찜질복) Now you can access the co-ed common area
6. Common Lounge Area Watch TV, nap, eat eggs Lying on the heated floor is totally normal
7. Food Area (Optional) Sikhye (식혜, sweet rice drink), eggs, ramen (라면), bossam (보쌈) Paid separately
8. Check Out Return your key, clear out your locker

Tips for Foreign Visitors

  • Useful Korean Phrases
  • "Jjimjilbang isseoyo? (찜질방 있어요?)" — "Is there a jjimjilbang nearby?" (handy when asking locals directly)
  • "Suweon juseyo (수건 주세요)" — "A towel, please"
  • "Gyeran du gae juseyo (계란 두 개 주세요)" — "Two eggs, please"
  • Cultural Heads-Up
  • Bathing area: full nudity required — no swimwear or underwear allowed. It feels awkward at first, but this is standard Korean bath culture.
  • Common lounge area: everyone wears the provided jjimjil outfit (찜질복). Sleeping on the heated floor is completely normal.
  • Food and drinks are only allowed in the designated dining/snack areas — never in the bathing zone.
  • Keep your voice down and avoid phone calls in the lounge.
  • Payment: Cash and card both accepted at entry. Purchases inside (food, extras) are charged to your wristband barcode and settled upon checkout.

Simple English Summary

A jjimjilbang (찜질방) is a Korean public bathhouse and communal relaxation space — a uniquely Korean experience you won't find anywhere else. Entry costs ₩10,000–20,000 and includes a uniform (shorts and t-shirt), towels, and access to heated rooms ranging from cedar to salt to charcoal saunas. The bathing area (separate for men and women) requires full nudity — no swimwear allowed. The common lounging area is co-ed; sleeping on the heated floor is completely standard. Dragon Hill Spa (드래곤힐 스파) in Yongsan (용산) is the most foreigner-friendly option, with English signage throughout.

Estimated Total Cost

  • Budget: ₩12,000 (entry only — bathing + jjimjilbang lounge)
  • Standard: ₩20,000 (entry + eggs + sikhye (식혜) + ramen (라면))
  • Luxury: ₩40,000 (premium jjimjilbang + massage add-on + full meal)
