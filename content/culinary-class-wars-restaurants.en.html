The Chef's Restaurant You Saw on Netflix — A Seoul Pilgrimage Guide to Culinary Class Wars

Subtitle: If you watched Culinary Class Wars (흑백요리사) seasons 1 and 2, you can eat that same food in Seoul in person — from season 1 winner Napoli Matfia's Vesuvio to a walking route through the Seongsu and Dosan clusters. But first you need to understand Korea's reservation battle. Includes a breakdown of reservation apps, alcohol-order policies, and no-photography rules.

ISSUE · #1317 · 2026-08-22

🎯 Why a "Chef's Restaurant Pilgrimage"

If a K-drama filming location pilgrimage (#222) is about "the place you saw on screen," this is about eating "the dish you saw on screen, cooked by that chef, in that chef's kitchen." Culinary Class Wars (흑백요리사) is the Korean survival competition that topped Netflix's global charts — and with Season 2 following Season 1 (2024), the restaurants run by its competing chefs have become new landmarks on the Korean food-travel map. Reservation difficulty spiked at many of them after the show aired, which makes this one of the categories where knowing what to prepare for makes the biggest difference between getting in and being shut out.

① First, the Reservation Battle — 3 Rules for Korean Fine-Dining Reservations

1. The reservation app is Catch Table (캐치테이블) — most of the popular chefs' restaurants take reservations only through this app. Next month's slots open on a fixed date each month, and popular spots sell out within minutes. There's a global version of the app (Catch Table Global), so you can sign up with a foreign card.

2. Read the alcohol and deposit policy in advance — some places, like the winner's restaurant Vesuvio, require an alcohol order (a full bottle is mandatory on weekends and holidays). No-show deposits are also common.

3. Check the photography rules — more restaurants are banning commercial filming (YouTube, monetized Reels). Photos for personal memories are usually fine, but follow any posted signage if there is one.

For the full picture on Korea's reservation and waiting culture, #1274 "Korean Restaurant Waiting & Reservations: The Zero-Panic Manual" walks through it screen by screen.

② The Seoul Pilgrimage List — 8 Verified Anchor Spots

1. Vesuvio (베수비오) — Kwon Seong-jun (권성준), "Napoli Matfia" (나폴리 맛피아), Season 1 Winner

  • Season 1 winner's Neapolitan cooking. The restaurant expanded and relocated to Yaksu (약수) from its original Seongsu (성수) location, "Via Toledo," and the course lineup grew along with it.
  • The hardest reservation to land in Seoul — booked on Catch Table, and slots sell out the instant they open. Alcohol order required (a glass is fine on weekdays; a full bottle on weekends). Minors can't be seated without an adult, and monetized filming is banned.
  • If your travel dates are flexible, don't force this one — the casual options below make a good alternative.

2. Choi.Dot (쵸이닷) & Jungang Gamsokgi (중앙감속기) — Choi Hyun-seok (최현석)

  • The chef from the judges' table. Choi.Dot (Dosan-daero, 도산대로) is his signature fine-dining spot, while Jungang Gamsokgi (Seongsu, 성수) is a converted-factory casual space — you can pick either face of the same chef.

3. Tian Mi Mi (티엔미미) — Jung Ji-sun (정지선)

  • Chinese cuisine from the "Dim Sum Queen." A different, authentic-dim-sum axis from Korean-style Chinese food (#1214).

4. Hongbogak (홍보각) — Yeo Kyung-rae (여경래)

  • Premium Chinese cuisine in Sinnonhyeon (신논현). The pinnacle of Korea's Chinese-cuisine chef lineage — a good pick for a formal occasion or a meal with parents.

5. Tride (트리드) — Kang Seung-won (강승원), "Triple Star" (트리플스타)

  • One of the most talked-about nicknames from the show. Contemporary dining.

6. Negi Live (네기라이브) — Jang Ho-jun (장호준)

  • Seongsu (성수). A live-counter format — bar seating that's comfortable for solo travelers too.

7. EVETT (에빗) — Joseph Lidgerwood (조셉 리저우드)

  • Fine dining where an Australian-born chef builds dishes from Korean ingredients — jang (장, fermented sauces) and fermentation. The reverse angle of "Korean flavor as seen by a foreigner" tends to make it an easier restaurant to connect with as a foreign traveler. Sinsa (신사).

8. Seongsu Banchan-gage (성수 반찬가게) — Song Ha-seul-lam (송하슬람), the "Banchan Chef" (반찬 셰프)

  • The Seongsu (성수) shop from the banchan (side-dish) chef who became known through the show. This isn't fine dining — approach it as an everyday Korean food (banchan) experience instead. A budget-friendly entry point to start your pilgrimage.

③ Cluster Routes — How to String a Day Together

  • Seongsu (성수) axis: Jungang Gamsokgi → Song Ha-seul-lam's banchan shop → Negi Live. Fill the gaps in between with the cafes and concept stores from #263, 50 Picks in Seongsu-dong.
  • Dosan (도산) and Sinsa (신사) axis: Choi.Dot → EVETT. Overlaps with a stroll through Dosan Park and the Gangnam-area route in #1111, Date Course Guide.
  • Chinese-cuisine axis: Tian Mi Mi → Hongbogak. Compare it against the #1214 Korean-style Chinese food guide, and the "Korean-style vs. authentic" question sorts itself out in one sitting.

⑤ One-Points Outside Seoul — Chefs' Regional Bases

  • Gangneung (강릉): The city where chef Nam Jung-seok (남정석, White Spoon) left Seoul to settle down — pairs well with a Gangneung trip (#264, Gangneung & Pyeongchang).
  • Busan (부산): The city that the show's competing chefs call "the food destination right now" — connects with the Busan series (#255 onward).
  • Okcheon (옥천): Chef Song Ha-seul-lam's hometown — not on the foreign-traveler route yet, but a candidate for the next wave of local food travel.

🧳 Prep Checklist (3–4 Weeks Before Your Trip)

1. Install the Catch Table Global app and register your card

2. Check the reservation opening date for your target restaurant (the restaurant's Instagram announcement is usually the fastest source)

3. Have a backup plan for when your first choice fails — line up a casual spot in the same cluster as your second choice

4. Check the alcohol policy, dress code, and rules on minors

5. Screenshot your reservation confirmation text or app screen (to show at check-in)
