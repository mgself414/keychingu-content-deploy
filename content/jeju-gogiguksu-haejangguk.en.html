Four Jeju-Style Broths — Gogi Guksu, Bomal Kalguksu, Haejangguk, Gosari Yukgaejang

Subtitle: Ask about Jeju food and most people think of black pork and raw fish (#1060), but what Jeju locals eat every day is broth and noodles. Gogi Guksu (고기국수), medium-thick noodles in a pork-boiled broth, is a dish that took root in Jeju; bomal (보말) is the Jeju dialect word for sea snails; and Jeju's haejangguk (해장국) shop opens at 6 a.m. How to slot the four dishes into your meals — haejangguk on the morning you arrive, gogi guksu for lunch, then head to Seogwipo (서귀포) for bomal kalguksu

🎯 Why "Four Broths"?

Jeju food guides usually skim ten dishes in one line each. Black pork, hairtail, seafood stew, gogi guksu... but they rarely tell you which meal to eat what at. This piece goes the other way. It keeps only four broth-and-noodle dishes and fits them into a day's schedule.

The reason for picking these four is clear. Gogi Guksu (고기국수) is a dish that took root in Jeju. Thick medium noodles go into a milky pork-boiled broth topped with Dombe Gogi (돔베고기, sliced boiled pork) — Seoul has specialty shops serving the Jeju style too, but Jeju is where it took root deeply enough to have a whole street devoted to this noodle. Bomal (보말) is the Jeju dialect word for sea snails — a word you won't find on a standard-Korean menu. Jeju's haejangguk (해장국) isn't the hangover food you eat the morning after drinking, as it is on the mainland; it's breakfast. That's why the flagship shops open at 6 a.m. And Gosari Yukgaejang (고사리육개장) is made by hand-picking Jeju bracken (fernbrake), then simmering it for a long time with buckwheat flour until it turns thick like porridge rather than soup.

They share one more trait: all four are strongest at breakfast or early lunch, and many shops close in the afternoon. These are dishes for which "let's go eat there in the evening" doesn't work well in Jeju. That's why meal placement is the core of this piece.

🥩 Pork-Based — Read This First If You Eat Halal or Vegetarian

Two of the four are pork-based, and haejangguk varies by shop. Gogi Guksu (고기국수) is built on a broth of simmered pork bones and meat, and Gosari Yukgaejang (고사리육개장) also uses pork. Mom-guk (몸국), introduced alongside them, also puts gulfweed into a pork broth. Haejangguk (해장국) — Eunhuine Haejangguk (은희네해장국) is the beef-and-blood-sausage type, while the signature dish at Ujin Haejangguk (우진해장국) is the pork-containing Gosari Yukgaejang (고사리육개장).

If you need to avoid pork, Bomal Kalguksu (보말칼국수) or Eunhui-ne Haejangguk (은희네해장국) is the answer. Bomal Kalguksu is seafood-based, drawing its broth from top shells with seaweed and tot (톳), and Eunhui-ne Haejangguk is the beef type. Note, though, that the same shops usually serve pork dishes as well, so separate cookware is unlikely — if you keep strict halal, a certified restaurant is a safer bet than this list. Vegetarian is harder still. Broth is the heart of all four dishes, so take the broth away and the dish no longer exists.

Solo dining is not a concern. Noodles and haejangguk (해장국) are served one bowl per person, so walking in alone is normal, and the shops in the table turn tables quickly.

🍜 Gogi Guksu (고기국수) — A Noodle That Took Root in Jeju

Thick medium noodles go into a broth made by boiling pork bones and meat for a long time, topped with sliced boiled pork (Dombe Gogi, 돔베고기). The broth is milky and rich, so if you were expecting a clear noodle soup, the first spoonful is surprisingly heavy. Prices are generally in the ₩8,000–10,000 range (Gasi Abang Guksu ₩8,000, Jamae Guksu ₩10,000).

Jeju-si has a Noodle Street (국수거리) where shops selling only this dish cluster together. It's around Samseong-ro (삼성로) in Ildo 2-dong (일도2동), and the table's Samdae Guksu Hoegwan main branch (Samseong-ro 41, 삼성로 41) and Guksu Madang main branch (Samseong-ro 65, 삼성로 65) are both on that street. It matters for travelers for one reason — if one shop has a long wait or has run out of ingredients, you can walk next door. In Jeju it's common to aim for a single shop and make a wasted trip; Noodle Street lowers that risk.

By route:

  • Meal at airport arrival/departure → Sammu Guksu (삼무국수) (Sammu-ro 3-gil 46-1, 삼무로3길 46-1). It's close to the airport.
  • One meal in downtown Jeju-si → Noodle Street (국수거리) (Samdae Guksu Hoegwan, Guksu Madang). Samdae Guksu Hoegwan's last order is 1:20 a.m., so it works for late meals too.
  • If you pick just one bowl → Jamae Guksu (자매국수) (Hanggol Nam-gil 46, 항골남길 46). It has a break time (14:30–16:10), so if you arrive at the tail end of lunch it will be closed.
  • East coast itinerary (Seongsan Ilchulbong · Seopjikoji) → Gasi Abang Guksu (가시아방국수) (Seopjikoji-ro 10, 섭지코지로 10). One of the few noodle shops that stays open into the evening (20:30).

🐚 Bomal Kalguksu (보말칼국수) — Bomal Is the Jeju Word for Sea Snails

Bomal (보말) is the Jeju dialect word for sea snails. Cooked into knife-cut noodle soup it becomes Bomal Kalguksu (보말칼국수); cooked into porridge, Bomal-juk (보말죽). The broth is on the clear side, with the sweetness of the snails layered over the sea aroma of seaweed (미역) and hijiki (톳). If gogi guksu is the heavy one, bomal kalguksu is its opposite.

One thing to know: each shop has a different broth base. Some use kelp and mussels together, others add hijiki. So "I've had bomal kalguksu" doesn't end at one shop.

  • Seogwipo · Jungmun itinerary → Jungmun Suduri Bomal Kalguksu (중문수두리보말칼국수) (Cheonjeyeon-ro 192, 천제연로 192). 08:00–16:00 (last order 15:30), closed Tuesdays — it's shut if you go in the afternoon. It fits naturally as lunch on a day you're seeing Cheonjeyeon Falls (천제연폭포) and the Jungmun area.
  • Jeju-si · Dongmun Market itinerary → Jeju Bomal Kalguksu (제주보말칼국수) (Gwandeok-ro 8-gil 40, 관덕로8길 40). It's within the same radius as Dongmun Market (#634), so it pairs with a market visit.
  • Staying in Sinjeju (신제주) → Sinjeju Bomal Kalguksu main branch (신제주보말칼국수) (Seondeok-ro 5-gil 19, 선덕로5길 19).

🍲 Haejangguk (해장국) — In Jeju, It's Breakfast

On the mainland, haejangguk is food for someone who drank the night before. In Jeju it's different. You start the morning with haejangguk. That's why the flagship shops open at 6 a.m., and many, like Eunhui-ne Haejangguk, close around 2–3 p.m. (Ujin Haejangguk stays open until night) If you arrive on the first morning flight to Jeju, this schedule actually fits well.

  • Ujin Haejangguk (우진해장국) (Seosa-ro 11, 서사로 11) — 06:00–22:00, no closing day. The fewest time constraints of the four. But it's known for a standard wait of 1–2 hours, so lines form even early. An annex (08:00–16:00, closed Tuesdays) also stands next to the main building. The sign says haejangguk, but the signature dish is the Gosari Yukgaejang (고사리육개장) explained below.
  • Eunhui-ne Haejangguk main branch (은희네해장국) (Goma-ro 13-gil 8, 고마로13길 8) — weekdays 06:00–15:00 / weekends 06:00–14:00, closed Thursdays. Close to a single-menu shop in the beef and blood-curd (선지) style. Showing up on a Thursday to a closed door is a common mistake.
  • Sanji Haejangguk main branch (산지해장국) (Imhang-ro 34, 임항로 34) — near Jeju Port. It has several branches in Jeju (Seongsan (성산), Sehwa (세화), Hamdeok (함덕), Jungmun (중문), Seogwipo (서귀포), Daejeong (대정)), so pick the one that fits your route.

🌿 Gosari Yukgaejang (고사리육개장) and Mom-guk (몸국) — Two You Can't Guess From the Name

Gosari Yukgaejang (고사리육개장) is made by hand-picking bracken grown in Jeju and simmering it with pork and buckwheat flour for a long time, until the bracken has completely broken down. The result is thick, closer to porridge than soup. If you expect mainland yukgaejang (육개장) (beef, scallions, spicy broth), you'll get a completely different dish.

Why bracken became a Jeju ingredient is explained by the seasons. Jeju has a term, Gosari Jangma (고사리장마) — "bracken rainy season," in April–May, a stretch of rain that suits bracken growth. Bracken picked and dried then goes into the year's cooking.

Mom-guk (몸국) is a soup made by simmering sargassum seaweed (모자반) — called "mom" (몸) in Jeju dialect — in pork broth. It was food cooked in a big cauldron for feasts and big family events, so it's less a tourist menu than a family-ceremony dish that made its way into restaurants.

You can get both at Ujin Haejangguk (Seosa-ro 11, 서사로 11) alone — Gosari Yukgaejang, Mom-guk, and Sagol Haejangguk (사골해장국) all run in the ₩10,000 range. Ordering two Jeju broths at one shop and sharing is the most efficient way.

🗓 Meal Placement — Up to Three in One Day

Shops in Jeju are split between Jeju-si and Seogwipo, so the districts are not connected on foot (within Noodle Street you can walk). Instead, arrange by meal. What follows assumes a rental car; without one, also see #1212 (on foot/bus) and #1402 (taxi tour).

Arrival day (centered on Jeju-si)

1. Morning 06:00–08:00 — Gosari Yukgaejang + Mom-guk at Ujin Haejangguk (Seosa-ro 11, 서사로 11). Lines form even early — if the line is long, look at the annex (opens 08:00, closed Tue).

2. Lunch — Head to Noodle Street (Samseong-ro, 삼성로) for gogi guksu. Samdae Guksu Hoegwan or Guksu Madang. If one is blocked, the shop next door.

3. Afternoon — Browse Dongmun Market (#634). You can also fit Jeju Bomal Kalguksu (Gwandeok-ro 8-gil 40, 관덕로8길 40) near the market at this point.

Day you cross over to Seogwipo

1. Morning — Eunhui-ne Haejangguk main branch (Goma-ro 13-gil 8, 고마로13길 8), as long as it's not Thursday.

2. Lunch (arriving in Jungmun) — Jungmun Suduri Bomal Kalguksu (Cheonjeyeon-ro 192, 천제연로 192). Last order is 15:30 and it is closed Tuesdays — those two conditions set your schedule.

Day with an east coast (Seongsan · Seopjikoji) itinerary

  • Gasi Abang Guksu (Seopjikoji-ro 10, 섭지코지로 10) is open until 20:30, so it works as a meal even if you come down late from Seongsan Ilchulbong. Just avoid Wednesdays.

⚠ 3 Common Mistakes

1. "I'll just go in the afternoon" — The dishes in this piece are morning and lunch businesses. Eunhui-ne Haejangguk closes at 3 p.m. (2 p.m. on weekends) and Jungmun Suduri Bomal Kalguksu has last order at 3:30. Only Ujin Haejangguk (22:00), Samdae Guksu Hoegwan (last order 01:20), and Gasi Abang Guksu (20:30) stay open into the evening.

2. Going without checking the closing day — Jungmun Suduri Bomal Kalguksu is closed Tuesdays, Eunhui-ne Haejangguk main branch Thursdays, and Jamae Guksu and Gasi Abang Guksu Wednesdays. Many Jeju restaurants close on a particular weekday rather than on weekends.

3. Mistaking a branch for the main branch — Sanji Haejangguk, Eunhui-ne Haejangguk, Hallim Kalguksu, and Mipung Haejangguk each have several branches in Jeju, and each branch has different hours. When searching, check the branch name, not just the shop name.

⚙ 3 FAQs

Q. Can I eat gogi guksu outside Jeju?

A few shops in Seoul and elsewhere serve Jeju-style gogi guksu, but Jeju is the only place where it has taken root as a dish. Eating it in Jeju is the default, and Jeju-si even has a street (Noodle Street, 국수거리) devoted to it.

Q. Is it spicy?

Gogi guksu, bomal kalguksu, and mom-guk have a clear or milky broth, so they are not spicy, and gosari yukgaejang is also unlike mainland yukgaejang, which is driven by chili powder. Haejangguk seasoning differs from shop to shop, so if you can't eat spicy food, ask them to leave out the seasoning (per Eunhui-ne Haejangguk's guidance).

Q. What do I search for on Naver Map?

Searching in Korean is more accurate — Gogi Guksu (고기국수) / Bomal Kalguksu (보말칼국수) / Haejangguk (해장국) / Gosari Yukgaejang (고사리육개장). Searching in English cuts the results down sharply. You can also paste the street addresses from the table as they are.
