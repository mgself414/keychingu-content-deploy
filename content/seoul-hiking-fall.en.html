Cheonggyesan and Gwanaksan Guide: Cheonggyesan, Cheonggyesan Malgeun Forest Park, Gwanaksan, Gwanaksan Nature Park, Gwanaksan Barrier-Free Forest Trail, Seoul Trail Course 11

Subtitle: Cheonggyesan (always open) · Cheonggyesan Malgeun Forest Park, Uiwang (always open) · Gwanaksan (632.2m, open year-round) · Gwanaksan Nature Park (always open) · Gwanaksan Barrier-Free Forest Trail (slope under 8%) · Seoul Trail Course 11 Gwanaksan Course (5.7km, about 2 hours 30 minutes) — confirmed via Korea Tourism Organization tourism data (TourAPI)

🎯 How to Use This Guide

This guide gathers places to walk and climb on two mountains in southern Seoul, Cheonggyesan and Gwanaksan. They are Cheonggyesan and a forest park on the Uiwang side, Gwanaksan with the nature park and barrier-free forest trail inside it, and the Seoul Trail course named after Gwanaksan. All six places are registered as open year-round, and no fee values are registered.

The operating details are copied from values registered in the Korea Tourism Organization tourism data (TourAPI). This guide covers only places registered in the Korea Tourism Organization tourism data.

📍 Cheonggyesan — Cheonggyesan and Cheonggyesan Malgeun Forest Park

Cheonggyesan (청계산) [Wonji-dong (원지동), Seocho-gu, ☎ Green City Division (푸른도시과) 02-2155-6869] is a mountain on the border of Seoul and Seongnam (성남), Gwacheon (과천) and Uiwang (의왕) in Gyeonggi-do. It is also called Cheongnyongsan (청룡산) because a blue dragon is said to have risen to the sky from here. The mountain slopes that surround Seoul Grand Park (서울대공원), Seoul Land (서울랜드) and the National Museum of Modern and Contemporary Art (국립현대미술관) are Cheonggyesan. With its forest, valleys, Cheonggyesa Temple (청계사), a forest bathing area and many hiking trails, it is a popular family hiking destination.

  • Summit Manggyeongdae (망경대) (618.2m): Government facilities are there, so you cannot climb it.
  • Oknyeobong (옥녀봉), Hyeoleupjae (혈읍재) and Geumjeongsu (금정수): Climbing from the Juam-dong (주암동) side toward Manggyeongdae, you reach Oknyeobong, where the burial site of Kim Nogyeong (김노경), the biological father of Chusa Kim Jeong-hui (김정희), used to be. You then pass Hyeoleupjae, which the Joseon scholar Jeong Yeo-chang (정여창) is said to have crossed, and just below Manggyeongdae is the mineral spring Geumjeongsu.
  • Opening hours: Always open. Closed days: Open year-round. Parking: Available.

Cheonggyesan Malgeun Forest Park (청계산맑은숲공원) [Cheonggye-ro 475 (청계로 475), Uiwang-si, ☎ Uiwang City Hall (의왕시청) Parks and Green Space Division (공원녹지과) 031-345-3533] is a park made for phytoncide bathing and forest bathing in the woods. It has a healing space in a fir and metasequoia forest, a rest plaza, sports facilities for daily life and walking paths, and it is laid out as a barrier-free park that people with limited mobility can also use.

  • Opening hours: Always open. Closed days: Open year-round.
  • Parking: Available. Stroller rental: Not available.

📍 Gwanaksan — Gwanaksan, Gwanaksan Nature Park and Gwanaksan Barrier-Free Forest Trail

Gwanaksan (관악산) [Gwanak-ro (관악로), Gwanak-gu, ☎ Gwanak-gu Leisure City Division (여가도시과) 02-879-6552] is a 632.2m mountain rising south of the Han River, named because the rocks at the summit resemble a hat. It was designated an urban nature park in 1968. It is close to the city center, so you can climb it in a day. In spring cherry blossoms bloom near the entrance and an azalea festival is sometimes held when the azaleas bloom, and you can see autumn leaves in fall and snow scenery in winter. At the summit are Wongaksa Temple (원각사) and Yeonjuam Hermitage (연주암), and all the hiking trails converge at Yeonjudae (연주대) on the cliff.

  • Main hiking trails: the trail that starts at the entrance of Seoul National University (서울대학교) in Sillim-dong (신림동), Gwanak-gu, and the trail that climbs behind Gwacheon Middle School (과천중학교) in Jungang-dong (중앙동), Gwacheon-si.
  • Closed days: Open year-round. Parking: Available.

Gwanaksan Nature Park (관악산자연공원) [San 27-1 (산27-1) Sillim-dong, Gwanak-gu, ☎ Gwanak-gu Office (관악구청) Parks and Green Space Policy Division (공원녹지정책과) 02-879-6521] is a park built on Gwanaksan. It has a lake park with a pavilion and a fountain, a forest library with children's books and environmental books, a traditional outdoor small theater, a barrier-free forest trail and an observatory.

  • Opening hours: Always open. Closed days: Open year-round. Parking: Available.

Gwanaksan Barrier-Free Forest Trail (관악산 무장애숲길) [Gwanaksan Nadeulgil 149 (관악산나들길 149), Gwanak-gu, ☎ Gwanak-gu Office Leisure City Division 02-879-6551] is a deck forest trail inside Gwanaksan. The slope is under 8% along its entire length, so it can be climbed even with a wheelchair or stroller. There is a loop-type forest trail for strolling and a climbing-type forest trail where you follow a zigzag uphill path to see the views. There are rest areas, chairs and picnic tables along the way.

  • Opening hours: Always open. Closed days: Open year-round.

📍 Walking Trail — Seoul Trail Course 11 Gwanaksan Course

Seoul Trail Course 11 Gwanaksan Course (서울둘레길 11코스 관악산코스) [Dongjak-daero Jiha 3 (동작대로 지하3), Dongjak-gu, ☎ 02-779-7902~4] is a course themed 'a path where folk belief and Buddhism meet' on the Seoul Trail (서울둘레길), which circles Seoul in 21 courses totaling 156.5km. There are historical and cultural sites along the way. It is close to Sadang Station (사당역) and Gwanaksan Station (관악산역).

  • Length and time: 5.7km in total, about 2 hours 30 minutes.
  • Opening hours: Always open. Closed days: Open year-round.

⚠ 3 Things to Know Before You Go

  1. You cannot climb Manggyeongdae (618.2m), the summit of Cheonggyesan — Government facilities are there.
  2. The slope of the Gwanaksan Barrier-Free Forest Trail is under 8% — You can climb it with a wheelchair or stroller.
  3. Seoul Trail Course 11 takes about 2 hours 30 minutes — It is 5.7km in total and close to Sadang Station and Gwanaksan Station.

⚙ FAQ 2

Q. Where do you start climbing Gwanaksan?

A. According to the Korea Tourism Organization registered information, the main trail starts at the Seoul National University entrance in Sillim-dong, Gwanak-gu, and there is also a trail that climbs behind Gwacheon Middle School in Jungang-dong, Gwacheon-si.

Q. Is there a forest path for people who find walking hard?

A. The Gwanaksan Barrier-Free Forest Trail (slope under 8%, wheelchairs and strollers allowed) and Cheonggyesan Malgeun Forest Park in Uiwang (a barrier-free park). Both are always open.
