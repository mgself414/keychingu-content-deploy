Busan's Three Drinking-Table Dishes — Nakgopsae, Gomjangeo and Yanggopchang, 9 Places

Subtitle: After pork soup, milmyeon and raw fish, the next slot is dinner. Three things Busan locals eat with a glass of soju in front of them — nakgopsae, which began as a factory worker's side dish; gomjangeo, which is not an eel; and yanggopchang, which is not lamb. From a place that has been stir-frying small octopus since 1972, to a coal-briquette old-timer and the straw fire of Gijang — how to get the names right before you eat

🎯 Why "A Drinking Table"

When people talk about Busan food, pork soup (dwaeji-gukbap, 돼지국밥), milmyeon (밀면) and raw fish (hoe, 회) come first (#1320). All of them are daytime food. After dark, the Busan table changes. There are three dishes that go with soju.

Nakgopsae (낙곱새) is stir-fried small octopus, beef small intestine and shrimp, spicy, served over rice. The name is the first syllable of each of the three ingredients, and the Korea Tourism Organization (한국관광공사, VisitKorea) presents it as a dish that originated in Busan. Its roots are in Jobang (조방) in Beomil-dong (범일동). Jobang is short for Joseon Textile (조선방직), the first cotton-spinning mill in Joseon, built on this site in 1917. A commercial district grew up around the factory, stir-fried small octopus (nakji, 낙지) shops moved in, word of the taste spread, and the name "Jobang Nakji" (조방낙지) travelled across the country. The factory closed in 1968, but the name stayed.

Gomjangeo (곰장어) is not an eel. Its standard Korean name is meokjangeo (먹장어), in English it is hagfish, and in Busan it is often called kkomjangeo (꼼장어). When the skinned fish hits a high flame, the heat makes the body twitch. In the Jagalchi alleys it is grilled seasoned over coal briquettes (yeontan, 연탄); in Gijang (기장), on the eastern edge of Busan, it is grilled whole over a straw fire (jipbul, 짚불).

The "yang" in yanggopchang (양곱창) is not lamb. It is a cow's first stomach. English-language articles often translate it as "lamb tripe", which is wrong. In the alley beside Jagalchi Station (자갈치역) there is a house said to date back to around 1959–63 that grills tripe and small intestine over coal briquettes.

🐙 Nakgopsae — From Stir-Fried Octopus Outside the Factory

Gaemijip Main Store is in a Nampo-dong alley. It hangs a sign reading "SINCE 1972" (per a review posted on DiningCode), is described as having carried on since 1972 with stir-fried small octopus alone, and is designated a Century Store (백년가게). Nakgopsae stir-fry is 15,000 won and stir-fried small octopus is 14,000 won, and both come with a bowl of rice. It closes at 21:00, with LO at 20:30.

Gaemijip Gukje Market Main Store, despite its name, is at Gunam-ro 34 in Haeundae. Do not go looking for it near Gukje Market. It is open 24 hours, year-round, so it works for a late arrival or from a Haeundae hotel. Nakgopsae is 15,000 won, seafood hot pot (haemul jeongol, 해물전골) is 18,000 won, and extra noodles (sari, 사리) are 2,000 won.

Jobang Nakji Beomil Direct Branch is the house that uses the name "Jobang" outright. Nakgopsae stir-fry is 12,000 won and stir-fried small octopus is 11,000 won, a little cheaper than Nampo-dong. It also has big dishes such as live small octopus (sannakji, 산낙지) and yeonpotang (연포탕, small octopus soup) (40,000–70,000 won).

Wonjo Halmae Nakji is on Goldtema-gil in Beomcheon-dong. Its menu is set up per person, as in "octopus + intestine + shrimp (1 person) + rice, 11,000 won", so it is easy to order even if you are alone. It opens at 09:30, the earliest on this list.

How to eat: the stir-fried nakgopsae arrives and you mix it into the rice. VisitKorea describes the taste as "sweet and spicy". Many shops let you add extra noodles (sari: glass noodles or udon).

🐍 Gomjangeo — Not an Eel but a Hagfish

Jeil San Kkomjangeo Jangeo-gui and Juyeonne Gomjangeo sit side by side, 31 m apart, in the kkomjangeo alley on the waterfront side of Jagalchi Market. At Jeil San Kkomjangeo you choose between domestic and U.S. fish when ordering (small portions are 50,000 won domestic and 40,000 won U.S.). Juyeonne Gomjangeo's menu shows no gomjangeo price; a visitor review gives 50,000 won for a domestic small. At the end you mix fried rice (2,000 won per reviews) into the leftover sauce. Juyeonne Gomjangeo is open until 23:30.

Wonjo Jipbul Gomjangeo Gijang Oegajip is in Gongsu Village, Gijang-eup, on the eastern side of Busan. Gijang is counted as the home of gomjangeo. This house grills the hagfish whole over a straw fire. VisitKorea describes it as the original house that led to the Gongsu Village gomjangeo alley forming. Besides straw-fire gomjangeo it has seasoned gomjangeo and gomjangeo spicy stew (maeuntang, 매운탕), and it is open year-round. It is far from the city center, so it is best combined with Haedong Yonggungsa (해동용궁사) (#1025) and the Gijang side of an itinerary.

🔥 Yanggopchang — The "Yang" Is a Cow's First Stomach

Baekhwa Yanggopchang is in the alley right next to Jagalchi Station. According to a review posted on DiningCode it is said to have carried on since around 1959–1963, and the owner grills over coal briquettes by hand, serving it piece by piece. Yanggopchang is 45,000 won, the mixed plate (modeum, 모둠) is 39,000 won, and yang fried rice is 12,000 won. It is closed on the 1st, 3rd and 5th Sundays of each month.

Daejeong Yanggopchang is in the Bupyeong-dong yanggopchang alley. You choose salt-grilled, stone-plate seasoned or hot pot at the same price (small 40,000 won, medium 45,000 won, large 50,000 won). On Friday and Saturday it is open until 1 a.m., the latest-opening end of this list.

Yanggopchang is sold by the plate. It is not a one-person menu: you order a small, medium or large plate, or a mixed plate, and share it. If you are alone, nakgopsae or gomjangeo is easier.

🌙 Courses by Time of Day

Nampo and Jagalchi evening (2–3 hours)

Start with nakgopsae at Gaemijip Main Store (go first, since it closes at 21:00) → walk about 450 m to the Jagalchi kkomjangeo alley (Jeil San Kkomjangeo, Juyeonne Gomjangeo) → finish at Baekhwa Yanggopchang or Daejeong Yanggopchang. All five places are within 650 m. It fits well after a Gukje Market (국제시장) and BIFF Square (BIFF 광장) itinerary (#244, #1022).

Late night

Places open past 22:00: Juyeonne Gomjangeo (23:30), Baekhwa Yanggopchang (24:00), Daejeong Yanggopchang (24:00, Fri and Sat 01:00), Gaemijip Gukje Market Main Store (Haeundae, 24 hours).

Beomil-dong lunch

Jobang Nakji and Wonjo Halmae Nakji are about 400 m apart. Wonjo Halmae Nakji opens at 09:30, so it also works for an early lunch.

Gijang daytime

Wonjo Jipbul Gomjangeo Gijang Oegajip pairs with a Gijang and Haedong Yonggungsa itinerary.

💰 Budget and Diet — What Will You Be Eating?

Prices come in three tiers. The amounts below are menu prices (DiningCode and VisitKorea, confirmed 2026-10-02). Values marked "(review)" come from visitor reviews, not the menu.

  • About 10,000 won per person — nakgopsae: Wonjo Halmae Nakji 11,000 won (1 person, rice included), Jobang Nakji 12,000 won, Gaemijip 15,000 won (rice included). Extra noodles 2,000 won.
  • 40,000–50,000 won per plate — gomjangeo: Jeil San Kkomjangeo U.S. small 40,000 won and domestic small 50,000 won, Juyeonne Gomjangeo domestic small 50,000 won (review). Fried rice 2,000 won (review).
  • 39,000–50,000 won per plate — yanggopchang: Baekhwa Yanggopchang mixed plate 39,000 won and yanggopchang 45,000 won, Daejeong Yanggopchang small 40,000 won to large 50,000 won.

Diet — know the ingredients before you order.

  • Beef offal — the "gop" in nakgopsae is beef small intestine, and yanggopchang is beef stomach and small intestine.
  • Seafood — gomjangeo (hagfish), small octopus, shrimp. To avoid beef offal, order stir-fried small octopus or naksae (낙새, octopus and shrimp) instead of nakgopsae. Both Gaemijip branches have both on the menu.
  • Pork — not a main ingredient of these three dishes. However, some houses, like Gaemijip Main Store, have a pork belly add-on (samgyeopsal, 삼겹살) menu, so if you avoid pork, check any menu item with "sam" (삼) in the name before ordering.
  • Halal and vegetarian — none of the places in the table is halal-certified or a vegetarian restaurant. The seasoning and broth ingredients have not been checked.

⚠ 3 Common Mistakes

1. Looking for "Gaemijip Gukje Market Main Store" at Gukje Market — this branch is at Gunam-ro 34 in Haeundae. If you are on the Nampo-dong and Gukje Market side, go to Gaemijip Main Store (Jung-gu-ro 30beon-gil 21-1).

2. Going on a Sunday evening with only Baekhwa Yanggopchang in mind — it is closed on the 1st, 3rd and 5th Sundays of each month. Daejeong Yanggopchang, in the same neighborhood, has no closing day listed.

3. Looking for Nampo-dong nakgopsae after 9 p.m. — Gaemijip Main Store closes at 21:00 (LO 20:30). If you are late, go to the 24-hour branch in Haeundae or switch to gomjangeo in Jagalchi.

⚙ 3 FAQs

Q. Is gomjangeo an eel?

No. Its standard Korean name is meokjangeo, and in English it is hagfish. Korea Herald describes the texture as chewy and springy. In Busan it is commonly called kkomjangeo.

Q. Is yanggopchang lamb?

No. The "yang" is a cow's first stomach. Gopchang is also the cow's small intestine. In English it is closer to beef tripe.

Q. Can I eat alone?

Nakgopsae can be ordered per person (Wonjo Halmae Nakji marks "1 person" on the menu, and Gaemijip's price is a one-person price with rice included). Gomjangeo and yanggopchang are sold by the plate, so they are easier with two or more.
