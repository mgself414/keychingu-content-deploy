A Foreign Couple's Wedding in Korea — Traditional Ceremonies, Hotel Weddings and Palace Wedding Photos at 4 Places: Unhyeongung (운현궁), Korea House (한국의집), The Shilla Seoul Yeongbingwan (서울신라호텔 영빈관), Gyeongbokgung (경복궁) (Official Values, October 2026)

This edition is for foreign couples who want to marry in Korea or leave wedding photos here. It has three branches. Traditional ceremonies (Unhyeongung and Korea House), hotel weddings (The Shilla Seoul Yeongbingwan, 서울신라호텔 영빈관, which has an official wedding page), and hanbok and wedding photography (the shooting rules at Gyeongbokgung and other palaces, and at Unhyeongung). Prices are only the ones we opened ourselves this time on each institution's or hotel's official website. Legal procedures such as marriage registration and visas are not covered here; we only leave official agency links at the bottom.

🏮 Traditional Ceremonies — 2 Places in Seoul

  • Unhyeongung (운현궁) — 464 Samil-daero, Jongno-gu, Seoul, customer center 02-766-9090 (Unhyeongung official website). The ceremony is held at Norakdang (노락당)
  • 2026 daytime ceremony: Saturdays and Sundays, April 4 to June 14 and September 5 to November 15, at 11:00/13:00/15:00, 30~40 minutes each. ₩1,534,500 (VAT included, deposit ₩440,000). Consultations and bookings for the 2026 daytime ceremony are already closed
  • 2026 evening ceremony: Saturdays and Sundays in months 10~11, at 18:00, ₩2,167,000 (VAT included, deposit ₩444,000). Applications are taken by phone
  • Included: ceremony staff (officiant, MC, 4 bride's attendants (sumo, 수모), assistants), wedding costumes (groom: samo, dallyeong, mokhwa boots (목화) / bride: wonsam, jokduri, binyeo hairpin (비녀), daenggi braid ribbon (댕기); shoes are separate), the costume of the wild-goose bearer (girgeogabeom, 기럭아범), the ceremonial table setting (blue-and-red thread, gourd cups, chestnuts, jujubes and so on), a guest book and envelopes. The pyebaek (폐백, the bride's greeting ritual for the groom's family) at Norakdang is an extra ₩220,000
  • Consultation: you can only sign after booking a consultation and having the host (the couple's parent) or a delegate come in person. Without a booking, a consultation is difficult
  • We could not confirm the 2027 schedule — check the notices on the official website
  • Korea House (한국의집) — 10 Toegye-ro 36-gil, Jung-gu, Seoul, wedding inquiries 02-3011-9236 (Korea House official website). Built in 1957 as a guesthouse, and it has held traditional weddings since 1982
  • 2026 schedule: months 3~6 and 9~11, Saturdays at 17:00. The ceremony is held in the central courtyard (outdoors)
  • Included: venue rental (courtyard), ceremonial table setting, basic wedding costumes for the couple, a celebration performance (samhyeon yukgak, 삼현육각), ritual items, officiant, assistant, and the costume of the wild-goose bearer (기럭아범). Styling and staging (including fresh flowers) is contracted separately with a selected outside vendor
  • Reception: a hanjeongsik (한정식, Korean set-course meal) at ₩150,000 per person (tax included, drinks and alcohol not included), minimum 60 and maximum 80 guests
  • The basic ceremony price is not on the page — it says "options and extra costs by package: inquire at the wedding office." A consultation visit requires a prior booking
  • Namsangol Hanok Village (남산골한옥마을) has also run traditional weddings (Seoul City), but this time we could not reach its official website, so we could not confirm the 2026~2027 schedule or prices.

🏨 Hotel Wedding — The Shilla Seoul Yeongbingwan

  • The Shilla Seoul Yeongbingwan (서울신라호텔 영빈관) — 249 Dongho-ro, Jung-gu, Seoul (Shilla Hotel official). A standalone venue with Korean traditional beauty, described as having 2 gardens (indoor and outdoor) and 3 banquet halls, hosting everything from house weddings to outdoor ceremonies
  • Capacity (set-course dinner): Ruby 150 guests · Topaz 130 · Emerald 130 · Indoor Garden 200 · Outdoor Garden 430 (Shilla Hotel official)
  • Contact: +82-2-2230-3321, Instagram @theshillawedding
  • Price: not published on the official page — consultation required
  • We did not include other hotel wedding venues because we could not open their official wedding pages this time. To choose a hotel, see Foreigner Korea Luxury Hotel Deep Dive

📸 Hanbok and Wedding Photography — Rules at the Palaces and Unhyeongung

  • Gyeongbokgung and other palaces, Jongmyo Shrine, and the Joseon Royal Tombs (Royal Palaces and Tombs Center) — even a visitor's souvenir shoot needs prior shooting permission if any of the following applies, and shooting without permission can lead to being stopped or having your entry restricted
  • You wear formal costume (hanbok, a wedding dress, etc.) and bring in equipment other than 2 cameras (tripod, lights, reflector, etc.)
  • You wear formal costume and 2 or more staff enter per shoot — a photographer, a private snap photographer, studio staff, and an acquaintance who does the shooting also counts as staff
  • You use non-everyday clothing other than formal costume, or props
  • Application deadline: from 60 days before the shoot to 5 days before (7 days before if weekends are included)
  • On-site bans: no makeup or changing clothes inside the palace. On crowded days, the number of shoots per day and shooting on the halls' stairs and the raised terrace (woldae, 월대) may be restricted
  • Gyeongbokgung admission: ₩3,000 for foreign adults (full age 19~64). Both traditional and everyday hanbok qualify for free admission, but you must wear both the top (jeogori, 저고리) and the skirt or trousers. Wearing only a coat (durumagi, 두루마기), or only a jeogori with jeans, is not free (Royal Palaces and Tombs Center)
  • Unhyeongung (운현궁) (Unhyeongung official website) — hanbok snaps by wedding photographers, hanbok rental shops and studios qualify for free shooting
  • Shooting hours: daily 09:30~17:30, within 60 minutes per team
  • Application: submit the commemorative-photography permit form and the cultural-heritage protection pledge by email (unhyeongung_official@naver.com) by 3 days before the shoot
  • Equipment: only 1 tripod and 1 selfie stick may be brought in. Lights, audio equipment, changing clothes, makeup and soap bubbles are banned. Shooting that uses relics such as the stepping stone (nodutdol, 노둣돌), the well or the mortar is not allowed either
  • Unhyeongung is closed on Mondays (open as usual if it is a public holiday)
  • To rent a hanbok, see Renting a Hanbok: The No-Panic Manual — Deposits, Fitting, and the Free Palace Entry; for studio shoots, Korea Hanbok Studio Photoshoot

🧰 Wedding Practicalities — Booking, Payment, Legal Procedures, Getting Around

  • Booking order: at both Unhyeongung and Korea House it is book a consultation → visit for a consultation → sign the contract. At Unhyeongung the host or a delegate must visit in person (Unhyeongung). If you are abroad, first ask whether a family member or planner in Korea can act as your delegate
  • Language: we could not confirm from the official pages whether either place can run the ceremony in English — ask about an interpreter at the consultation
  • Payment: the deposit comes first (Unhyeongung daytime ₩440,000). For payment methods, see Foreigner Korea Payment System Deep Dive
  • Guest meals: the Korea House reception is a hanjeongsik course. If you have vegetarian or halal guests, tell them in advance at the consultation, and for a card that says it for you, see Less Spicy, Vegan, Halal: The No-Panic Manual — Dietary Requests You Just Show
  • Legal procedures (marriage registration, visa): not covered in this guide. Marriage registration is explained by the Supreme Court's Electronic Family Relation Register System (대법원 전자가족관계등록시스템), and residence and marriage-migrant visas by the Ministry of Justice's HiKorea (법무부 하이코리아). Requirements differ by nationality, so check directly with your own embassy and the Korean agencies
  • Getting around: Choosing Your Korea Transit Card: The No-Panic Manual — T-money, WOWPASS, or Climate Card

⚠ 4 Common Mistakes

1. "If a friend takes the photos, no permit is needed" — the Royal Palaces and Tombs Center counts an acquaintance who does the shooting as staff too. In hanbok or a wedding dress with 2 or more staff, prior permission is required (Royal Palaces and Tombs Center)

2. "We'll change into the dress in the palace restroom" — both the palaces and Unhyeongung ban changing clothes and makeup on the grounds (Royal Palaces and Tombs Center · Unhyeongung)

3. "A traditional wedding is possible in any month" — both Unhyeongung and Korea House run on a spring and autumn season. The Unhyeongung 2026 daytime ceremony is already closed (Unhyeongung · Korea House)

4. "Just draping a jeogori gets you free hanbok admission" — you must wear both top and bottom (Royal Palaces and Tombs Center)
