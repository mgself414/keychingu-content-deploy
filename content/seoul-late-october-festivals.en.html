Seoul's Mid-to-Late October Festival Calendar 2026 — Seoul Silver Grass Festival, Seoul Music Festival, Jeongdong Night Walk, Halloween: 16 Dated Events, Oct 13–Nov 2

Subtitle: What's left in Seoul after the first week of October (#1375) — Haneul Park's Seoul Silver Grass Festival (Oct 17–23), Nodeul Island's Seoul Music Festival (Oct 24, free but reservation required), Jeongdong Night Walk (Oct 29–31), the Lotte World Adventure 'Dark Fantasy Festival' parade (through Nov 15), and the Seoul Lantern Festival opening at Cheonggyecheon (Nov 1). Plus municipal museums open until 9 p.m. (Seoul Culture Night) and nearby Halloween and night arboretums — three weeks, laid out date by date

🎯 Why "Mid-to-Late October"

The first weekend of October was a special window packed with the Gangnam Festival parade and the Jamsu Bridge Gourmet Road around the National Foundation Day (개천절) holiday (#1375). The three weeks after that have a different character — instead of one big event, one mid-sized event lands every week. Week three brings the Seoul Silver Grass Festival, week four brings Nodeul Island's free music festival, the final week brings Jeongdong Night Walk and Halloween, and November 1's Cheonggyecheon Lantern Festival opening carries things into the winter season.

The upside for travelers is that whichever week you land in, there's an evening plan waiting. This guide pins down, date by date, everything happening in Seoul (and in day-trip range an hour out) from October 13 through November 2, and lays out how to string them together week by week. The seasonal guide to silver grass and foliage spots themselves is in #1313 and #1314, and palace night programs are in #1316.

⚠ Note on timing: This is a 2026-only schedule (as confirmed on 2026-09-29). Outdoor events can change for rain or on-site conditions, so check the official channel (SOURCES below) before you head out. "Partially confirmed" means an event appears in Instagram notices or last year's pattern but its date or location hasn't been confirmed on an official channel.

🅰 Week Three (Oct 13–19) — Seoul Silver Grass Festival Opens, Children's Grand Park Traditional Performances

  • Seoul Silver Grass Festival (Oct 17–23): A festival timed to when the silver grass fields on Haneul Park's hill are at their silvery best. Open 10:00–21:00, free. Go up around sunset and you catch the grass glowing backlit plus the night landscape lighting in one visit. Walk from World Cup Stadium Station (월드컵경기장역) (Line 6) Exit 1, or take the park's little electric tram, nicknamed "maenggkkongi" (맹꽁이 전기차) — the stairs are long. The difference between silver grass and pink muhly, plus photo spots, is in #1313.
  • Gongyeon Bomnal Festival (Oct 16–17, partially confirmed): An outdoor performance at Seoul Children's Grand Park's Forest Stage. The second day (Oct 17, Sat) is free and first-come-first-served from noon, and since the lineup is traditional arts like samulnori percussion alongside multidisciplinary stage work, it's a show you can watch without understanding the language. Right outside Children's Grand Park Station (어린이대공원역) (Line 7) Exit 1. Check the official site's notice for the exact lineup.
  • Bongeunsa Temple Traditional Culture Festival (Oct 11–19): A fall festival at Bongeunsa Temple, a thousand-year-old temple right next to Coex (코엑스). Hanbok experience and Life Four-Cut photos run Oct 11–17 beside Jinyeomun Gate (진여문) (free, per Instagram notice), temple food is shared Oct 15 at Mireuk Plaza (미륵광장), and traditional performances such as Namsadang-paennori (남사당패놀이), Bukcheong Lion Dance (북청사자놀음), and Nanta (난타) follow on the 12th–16th. It's a walk from Samseong Station (삼성역) (Line 2) and Bongeunsa Station (봉은사역) (Line 9) — easy to combine with Coex shopping.
  • Gangdong Prehistoric Culture Festival (Oct 16–18): A festival held at the Amsa-dong Prehistoric Settlement Site, the site of a 6,000-year-old Neolithic village, and selected this year as a Ministry of Culture, Sports and Tourism pre-festival (예비축제). Opening-day (Oct 16) drone show, a Neolithic archaeology experience school, prehistoric barbecue, and a night light festival are added. Check the official notice for times and fees.
  • Jamsu Bridge Sundays (through Oct 25): Even after the first week of October's Gourmet Road (#1375), the car-free Jamsu Bridge continues every Sunday, 2–10 p.m. This is the last season under the name "Car-Free Jamsu Bridge."

🅱 Week Four (Oct 20–26) — Nodeul Island's Free Music Festival

  • Seoul Music Festival (Oct 24): A free one-day festival on Nodeul Island, right in the middle of the Hangang River. This year, in partnership with the Korean Music Awards, Poll Kim, Kwon Jin-ah, OKDAL, Nerd Connection, Chu Da-hye Charges, and the Kim Chang-wan Band take the main stage 17:00–21:00 (side program from noon). It's free, but advance reservation is required — see the ticketing guide on the official website (seoulmusicfestival.co.kr) for the booking site and opening schedule. Korean ticketing sites often block foreigners at the sign-up/ID-verification step, so check early (whether walk-up seats remain on the day is unconfirmed). It's a 10-minute walk from Nodeul Station (노들역) (Line 9) Exit 2 along the Hangang Bridge (한강대교) walkway; there's no parking lot on the island.
  • Seoul BBQ Festival (Oct 24–25): A festival grilling K-BBQ camping-style at Nanji Camping Ground. The self-BBQ zone is a paid reservation system (charcoal 20,000 won, gas 10,000 won; meat and vegetables bought separately) split into three sessions a day (starting at 12:00, 15:00, 18:00), and round 2 reservations open Tue Oct 6, 18:00 on Naver reservations. A chef BBQ performance, a market of six Seoul restaurants, and stage performances come along, and shuttles (1,500 won per ride) run from Digital Media City Station (디지털미디어시티역) (Line 6) Exit 2 and Mangwon Hangang Park (망원한강공원). To pair it with the Nodeul Island music festival the same day, do Nodeul Island in the afternoon and Nanji for lunch.
  • This week overlaps with the Silver Grass Festival's final days (through Oct 23) — string them together along the Hangang: Haneul Park through Friday, then Nodeul Island on Saturday.

🅲 Final Week (Oct 27–Nov 2) — Jeongdong Night Walk, Fortune Adventure, Bread-dong, and the Lantern Festival Opens

  • Jeongdong Night Walk (Oct 29–31): A free night festival where the Deoksugung Stone Wall Path (덕수궁 돌담길) and the modern-era buildings along Jeongdong-gil open at night. It comes with nighttime openings of Jeongdong-area cultural facilities, guided tours, and busking, and the Oct 30 (Fri) 19:00 Palace Concert in front of Deoksugung's Junghwajeon is the opening stage. Walk from City Hall Station (시청역) (Lines 1 & 2) Exit 5. Palace night programs overall — Starlight Night Tour (별빛야행) and Moonlight Tour (달빛기행) — are in #1316.
  • Fortune Adventure (Oct 29–Nov 1): A fair at the aT Center in Yangjae bringing Saju fortune-telling, Tarot, and face reading together in one place. It's the fastest way to experience the Korean custom of getting a year-end fortune reading, but since the sessions are in Korean, it's hard to follow anything in depth without an interpreter — set your expectations around soaking up the atmosphere and taking photos. ₩10,000 advance, ₩15,000 at the door; connects to Yangjae Citizens' Forest Station (양재시민의숲역) (Sinbundang Line (신분당선)) Exit 4.
  • Dongjak-gu Bread-dong Festival (Oct 31, partially confirmed): A free festival (12:00–17:00) where neighborhood bakeries in Dongjak-gu set up booths together. Last year it was held at the Dongjak-gu Office front yard (Jangseungbaegi Station (장승배기역), Line 7), but this year's location hasn't been officially confirmed. If you like a bakery tour, pair it with bakeries in Noryangjin (노량진) and Sangdo-dong (상도동).
  • Seoul Lantern Festival Opens (Nov 1–): The opening weekend of the winter festival where lanterns and media installations light up from Cheonggye Plaza to Supyo Bridge along Cheonggyecheon. Free, open until 23:00 on weekends. It's the most crowded on opening weekend — push it to a weekday evening if you can.

🎃 Halloween and All Month Long — Lotte World, Everland, Walkerhill, Night Museums

  • Lotte World Adventure 'Dark Fantasy Festival' (through Nov 15): The fall season at Lotte World Adventure in Jamsil. The Dark Fantasy parade, done in partnership with the Junji Ito Collection (이토 준지 컬렉션), runs daily at 20:20, and the day parade (14:00) and night parade (19:00) also run as usual. For foreign visitors, a KKday or Klook all-day pass (QR straight-in entry) is the easiest way to skip the ticket line. School uniform rental is in #1397.
  • Everland 'Blood City Zero' (through Nov 22, nearby in Yongin): A horror zone marking Everland's Halloween 10th anniversary. The immersive show runs daily 16:00–21:00 and is included with park admission alone (some experiences may need a separate reservation or fee). Limousine buses connect from Gangnam (강남) and other areas.
  • FALL in WALKERHILL (through Oct 31): The fall festival at the Walkerhill Hotel in Gwangjin-gu. It splits into free programs — a photo-tour map, a stamp challenge, music-listening benches — and paid fall dining plus a weekend wine fair (Oct 10–25). Since it's a hill overlooking the Hangang and the foliage, the free course alone fills half a day. Free hotel shuttle from Achasan Station (아차산역) (Line 5).
  • Seoul Culture Night (through Nov 30): Seoul's municipal cultural facilities stay open until 9 p.m. on Tue, Wed, Fri, Sat, and Sun — the Seoul Museum of History (Gyeongbokgung Station (경복궁역)), Seoul Museum of Craft Art (Anguk Station (안국역)), Unhyeongung (운현궁), Namsangol Hanok Village (Chungmuro Station (충무로역)), Hanseong Baekje Museum (한성백제박물관), and more. Permanent exhibits are mostly free. It's the go-to fallback for a weekday evening with nothing else on.
  • Suwon Arboretum Night Garden (Oct 2–31, every Fri–Sat, nearby Suwon, partially confirmed): A night opening (until 21:00) of the greenhouses and themed gardens at the Ilwol and Yeongheung arboretums. The greenhouse's fairy-tale concept needs no language. Admission is unconfirmed — it's not in the official materials. More than an hour from Seoul.

🗓 Week-by-Week Route — One Evening a Week

Week Three Saturday (Oct 17): Daytime Gongyeon Bomnal Festival at Children's Grand Park (from noon) → evening Silver Grass Festival opening at Haneul Park (arrive around sunset).

Week Four Saturday (Oct 24): Afternoon Seoul Music Festival at Nodeul Island (side program at noon, main stage at 17:00, reservation required) → night view along the Hangang Bridge walkway. If it's the day before (Fri), it's the last night for Haneul Park's silver grass.

Final Week Friday (Oct 30): Afternoon Fortune Adventure in Yangjae → evening Jeongdong Night Walk from City Hall Station, 19:00 Deoksugung Palace Concert. Saturday (Oct 31) is Bread-dong Festival by day plus Lotte World's 20:20 Dark Fantasy parade.

Default weekday evening: Seoul Culture Night museums (open until 21:00 Tue, Wed, Fri) — after Nov 1, switch to the Cheonggyecheon Lantern Festival.

⚠ 3 Common Mistakes

1. Just showing up at the Seoul Music Festival — it's free, but it runs on advance reservation (see the ticketing guide on the official website). Showing up without a reservation, you may not get in.

2. Looking for Itaewon Halloween (이태원 핼러윈) as a listed festival — this guide doesn't include the Itaewon Halloween crowd event. For Halloween atmosphere, it's safer to go with events that have an operating organizer, like Lotte World or Everland.

3. Planning a Monday night museum visit — Seoul Culture Night's nighttime openings run Tue, Wed, Fri, Sat, Sun. Most facilities are closed Mondays.

⚙ 3 FAQs

1. If you can only pick one? — In week three, the Seoul Silver Grass Festival (free, open into the night, great for photos); in the final week, Jeongdong Night Walk (free, Deoksugung Palace Concert, downtown location). Neither needs a reservation.

2. Which places can you go without a reservation? — The Silver Grass Festival, Jeongdong Night Walk, Jamsu Bridge, Lantern Festival, Culture Night museums, and Walkerhill's free programs are all walk-in. The Seoul Music Festival (advance reservation) and Lotte World (all-day pass) need advance preparation.

3. What if it rains? — Silver grass, Nodeul Island, and Jamsu Bridge are all outdoors. Switch to Fortune Adventure (indoors at the aT Center), Culture Night museums, or Lotte World's indoor zones.
