Missing the Last Train, the Zero-Panic Manual — Four Ways Home After Midnight

Subtitle: The subway's last train is set by arrival at the terminal station around 01:00 (earlier, near midnight, on Saturdays and holidays) · 14 night bus routes run 23:00–06:00, 2,500 won by transit card · taxi late-night surcharges run 40% from 23:00–02:00 and 20% from 22:00–23:00 and 02:00–04:00 · leave Seoul's city limits and another 20% out-of-city surcharge applies · only 4 night bus routes still run from Incheon Airport. Fares are current as of September 2026 — confirm again on the meter or app when you board

🎯 How to Read This Manual — Notation Convention

The moment you miss the last train looks the same everywhere. You run to the subway station and the shutter is already down; you open a taxi app and no car gets dispatched. This piece covers only what to look at and what to choose in the next 10 minutes.

  • Text that appears on apps or signage goes in corner brackets: 「Last Train」
  • A button on screen goes in brackets: [ Timetable ]
  • A hands-on action is bold: on the app, double-check based on your boarding station
  • A sentence to show a driver or staff member verbatim goes in code: 여기로 가주세요 (yeogiro gajuseyo)

⚠ Fare and Time Notation Principle: The numbers below are official Seoul city data as of September 2026. Taxi fares are under discussion for an increase and may change, so check the meter and app screen once more when you board. For individual route numbers and minute-level times, checking the app that day is more accurate — this piece only lays out the structure.

① Check This First — the Last Train Isn't Based on the Station You Board At

Let's clear up the most common misunderstanding about makcha (막차) — the last train — first. "Last train at 01:00" is based on the terminal station, jongchagyeok (종착역). It means the last train leaves the start of the line and reaches the terminal around 01:00, so at a station in the middle of the line it's much earlier. Heading the opposite direction is different again.

Category Basis Caution
Weekdays (Mon–Fri) Arrival at terminal station 01:00 Friday counts as a weekday too — there's no system that runs later just on Fridays
Saturdays & public holidays Earlier, around midnight If you're planning to stay out late on a weekend, this is the trap
Holidays & special events Temporary extended service sometimes runs There have been cases of extended service to 1am on Lunar New Year and Chuseok. Not a standing rule

How to check: Search for a station and open its timetable on Naver Map or Kakao Map. Kakao Map shows the train timetable and real-time train position in the station's info window; on Naver Map, go Public Transit → Subway Map → tap the station to see the timetable. There's no separate button labeled "last train" — you find the last train inside the timetable itself. Always check it for the station you're boarding from, in the direction you're heading.

② Night Buses (N-Bus) — the Default from Midnight to Dawn

Seoul has night buses — N-bus (심야버스) — that fill the gap after the subway shuts down. The route number is prefixed with N.

Item Detail
Number of routes 14 routes, about 140 buses
Operating hours 23:00–06:00
Dispatch interval 10–40 minutes (varies by route)
Fare 2,500 won by transit card

The dispatch interval is longer than a regular city bus. Plan around the possibility of waiting 30 minutes. Routes run along trunk axes that cross Seoul, so they favor the direction from downtown out toward outer residential areas.

  • Finding routes and stops: Drop a pin on your destination in a map app and search a route for the late-night hours, and an N route will come up. You can also look up routes on TOPIS (서울교통정보시스템), the Seoul Transportation Operation & Information System, at topis.seoul.go.kr.
  • Stops: They appear to use regular bus stops, but this piece could not confirm that — check the stop location by pinning it in the app.
  • Payment: Use your regular transit card. For card types and top-ups, see #1298.

Tip for foreign visitors — Even getting off at the back door, tap your card again on exit. That's where the transfer discount and fare settlement happen. For the full boarding procedure, see #1271.

③ Taxis — Know What Gets Added Before You Get In

The most reliable option late at night, but the fare isn't the same as daytime. In Seoul, the surcharge — haljeung (할증) — rate splits by time band.

Time Band Surcharge Base Fare (1.6km)
Day & evening None 4,800 won
22:00–23:00 20% 5,800 won
23:00–02:00 40% 6,700 won
02:00–04:00 20% 5,800 won
Mobeom / deluxe taxis Flat 20% from 22:00–04:00 8,400 won

On top of that, leaving Seoul's city limits adds another 20% — an out-of-city surcharge, sigyeoe (시계외) haljeung. Stack it onto the 40% late-night band and it can climb to as much as 60%. However, the shared-service area with Incheon Airport (Gwangmyeong, Bucheon, Gimpo, Goyang, Incheon) is exempt from the out-of-city surcharge — being in Gyeonggi Province doesn't automatically mean a surcharge. Highway tolls are paid by the passenger.

  • When you can't get a taxi: Kakao T has a call mode that searches up through higher tiers in order — Blue, Venti, Mobeom, Black. Late at night, regular cars are scarce, so a higher tier often comes through. It's not guaranteed to always work — treat it as an option worth trying, not a sure thing.
  • If your card doesn't work: A screen-by-screen guide to paying directly on the driver's terminal is in #1273.

Tip for foreign visitors — Late at night, it's faster to show your Hangul address on screen than to explain your destination out loud. Screenshot your lodging's Hangul address on your first day so it's ready.

④ Outside Seoul — Busan, Daegu, and the Airport Are Different

  • Busan: 14 night bus routes cover the gap after the subway shuts down. A late-night surcharge kicks in across all routes from 23:40, putting the transit-card fare at 2,000 won for a regular bus and 2,500 won for express/seated service.
  • Daegu: has no dedicated night bus. After 23:30, a good number of routes turn back partway instead of running to their terminus. Late at night, it's safer to plan around taking a taxi.
  • Incheon Airport → Seoul: 4 night bus routes remain. N6000 (Gangnam Express Bus Terminal, 강남고속버스터미널, 17,000 won) · N6002 (Cheongnyangni, 청량리, 18,000 won) · N6701 (DDP, 동대문디자인플라자, 18,000 won) · N6703 (Gwangnaru Station, 광나루역, 18,000 won). Each route runs 5–8 times between midnight and 04:50. The former N6001 was discontinued on August 1, 2025 — a lot of old timetables are still floating around online, so watch out for that.
  • Gimpo Airport: This piece could not confirm the subway's last-train time down to the minute. Check the app on the day.

Tip for foreign visitors — The airport night bus is much pricier than a regular city bus, but cheaper than a taxi. If you have a lot of luggage and are arriving before dawn, this is the realistic option. For the full picture on getting from the airport into the city, see #1285.

⑤ If You Still Can't Get Home — Options for Holding Out Until Morning

Sometimes you can't catch a taxi and the buses have stopped too. What's commonly recommended in that case is a 24-hour jjimjilbang (찜질방) or a café, but the assumption that "a jjimjilbang is always 24-hour" no longer holds. The number of bathhouses and jjimjilbangs fell by more than half, from about 8,800 in 2004 to about 4,350 in 2023, and some have given up 24-hour operation due to the burden of running costs.

  • Check that day's business hours before you go — especially whether entry is allowed in the middle of the night.
  • Seoul has a number of 24-hour cafés. That said, individual stores' hours change often.
  • For jjimjilbang procedure (wristband, locker, settling the bill), see the screen-by-screen guide in #1284.

Tip for foreign visitors — At a jjimjilbang, you get a wristband on entry and settle up on your way out. Some places ask for ID, so bring your passport.

📢 Request Cards

When you need to ask for help at night, lining up these three things in order makes the conversation much faster.

Who What to Prepare What to Say
Taxi driver A screen showing your lodging's Hangul address 여기로 가주세요 (yeogiro gajuseyo)
Station staff / bus driver The name of the neighborhood you're headed to "막차 끝났나요?" (makcha kkeutnannayo?)
Tourism Hotline 1330 Your current location "지금 어떻게 가야 하나요?" (jigeum eotteoke gaya hanayo?)

Tourism Hotline 1330 connects around the clock in multiple languages — it's the last resort when you're completely lost (#1299).

⚠ 3 Common Mistakes

1. Assuming "last train at 01:00" is based on your own boarding station — it's based on the terminal station. Mid-line stations are earlier, and Saturdays and holidays run around midnight.

2. Riding into Gyeonggi Province without knowing about the out-of-city surcharge — another 20% stacks on top of the 40% late-night rate. Gwangmyeong, Bucheon, Gimpo, Goyang, and Incheon are the exception, though.

3. Looking for the airport night bus using old information — N6001 has been discontinued. What remains are four: N6000, N6002, N6701, and N6703.

⚙ 3 FAQ

1. Night bus or taxi — which is better? — If your destination sits on an N route axis, the bus is overwhelmingly cheaper (2,500 won). If you're off the axis or carrying a lot of luggage, take a taxi.

2. What if the fare differs from this piece? — The numbers here are current as of September 2026. There's discussion of a taxi fare increase, so trust what the meter shows first.

3. Can I split a taxi with my group? — Taxi fare is charged per vehicle, so the more people you have, the less each person pays. You just can't exceed the seating capacity.
