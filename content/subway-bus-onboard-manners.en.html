Korea Subway & Bus, the Zero-Panic Manual — Where to Sit and What You Can Bring Aboard

Subtitle: How to board (#1271 · #1281) and why (#1169) live in other editions — this one covers only the 5 minutes between the doors closing and you getting off. From the pink seat to the end seats, backpacks, earphones, an uncapped cup, and single-file escalators — one table decides whether you sit, stand, or hold it.

ISSUE 06 · #1379 · 2026-09-17 · korea-first-timer series

🎯 How to Read This Manual — The Notation Rules

This uses the same notation rules set in #1270 (the kiosk edition).

  • Announcement and sign text appears in quotes: "This is a pregnancy priority seat."
  • Buttons to press and seats to leave open appear in brackets: [ Stop Bell ]
  • Hands-on actions are bold: on a packed train, turn your backpack to the front.

One angle only. Fares, transfers, and ticketing live in #1271 (bus) and #1281 (subway); why Koreans stay this quiet and leave priority seats empty lives in #1169. Here it's only what happens after the doors close — tagged by whether it's a rule or a custom ([Rule] = ordinance or law, [Custom] = almost everyone does it, [Optional] = up to you).

① The 3-Second Check Before Boarding — Hands, Back, Ears

Before the doors open, check just three things.

Check Subway Bus Tag
Drink in hand Lidded is fine; drinking it is your call An uncapped cup or a straw-poked can can get you refused boarding (Seoul city ordinance) Subway [Custom] · Bus [Rule]
Backpack on your back If it's crowded, turn it to the front or carry it by hand Same [Optional]
Ears Playing video or music without earphones makes you a "billeon" (빌런) — a villain Same [Custom]

Bus drinks are restricted under a Seoul city bus ordinance (in effect since 2018): uncapped takeout drinks and smelly food (fried chicken, tteokbokki, etc.) aren't allowed on board. The driver can refuse to let you on or ask you to get off. What's allowed: a capped tumbler, a thermos, a sealed container, or boxed takeout. No equivalent ban has been confirmed for the subway, but nobody eats smelly food down there either.

② The Seat-Judgment Master Flow — When You See an Open Seat

What You See Call Why Tag
Pink seat (imsanbu baeryeoseok / 임산부 배려석) Leave it empty — even if the train is packed No fine or legal enforcement, but almost everyone leaves it open. Sit there and eyes turn your way [Custom]
Priority seating (gyotong-yakjaseok, noyakjaseok / 교통약자석, 노약자석) at both ends of the car Leave it empty Priority for the elderly, disabled, pregnant, and those with infants. A young traveler sitting there can draw a comment [Custom]
Regular seat Sit If an elder is standing at the next stop, offering it up is [Optional] —
No seats + crowded Backpack to the front, hold the handrail, stand by the door — the people getting off go first at every stop Keeps the exit clear [Custom]
Front bus seats (marked yellow/pink) Leave it empty Same priority seating as the subway [Custom]

Priority seating at both ends of the car is the legally mandated seating — the Act on Promotion of Convenient Transportation for the Mobility Impaired requires a set share of every train's seats (at least one-tenth) to be reserved for it. The pink pregnancy priority seats are a separate layer within that: two per car on Lines 1-8, added by Seoul since 2013. For both, leaving them empty isn't law — it's an unwritten rule. That's what makes it stronger.

③ Sound — Calls, Video, Conversation

  • Calls [Custom] — If you have to answer, keep it short and quiet. "I'm on the subway right now, I'll call you back" is the local standard line.
  • Video & music [Custom] — Earphones are required. Playing sound through a speaker is exactly what the announcements are aimed at.
  • Conversation [Optional] — Talking with someone you're with is fine, but the whole car tends to stay quiet, so your tone naturally drops.
  • What you'll actually hear over the announcement: "Please refrain from using mobile phones on the train; if you must, switch the ringer to vibrate and keep the call short and quiet." (Seoul Metro) — Hearing this isn't aimed at anyone in particular; it's a routine announcement.

④ Bus Only — From Boarding to Getting Off

Stage What to Do The Trap
Boarding Front door, tag your card (#1271) An uncapped drink gets caught right here
Riding Hold the handrail or a pole Sudden stops are frequent. Plenty of visitors standing and staring at their phone end up falling

While exiting Wait for the person in front of you to get off first. In a hurry? Say "Naerilgeyo" (내릴게요) Someone standing by the door isn't necessarily getting off

⑤ Escalators, Corridors, Stairs — Where Custom and Official Policy Diverge

  • Escalators [Custom] — Inside stations, the custom is a single line on the right with walkers passing on the left. The official stance recommends standing in two lines for safety (a 2007 campaign → dropped in 2015 → back under discussion as of August 2026), but it isn't enforced. Standing on the left isn't a rule violation, but it leaves someone waiting behind you — it's just easier to stand on the right like everyone else.
  • Corridors & stairs [Optional] — Follow the keep-right arrows on the floor and walls. On the platform, stay behind the line; when a train arrives, the people getting off go first (step aside to both sides of the door).
  • Ticket gates [Custom] — Tag your card and go straight through. People are stacked up behind you, so don't go digging for your card right at the gate (#1281).

⑦ 3 Panic Scenarios

1. You sat in the pink seat and people are staring — Stand up and move aside, and it's over. No need to apologize. If you're pregnant, sitting there is fine, and a pink maternity badge clipped to your bag means nobody will ask.

2. You boarded the bus with coffee and the driver says something — The uncapped cup is the problem. Close the lid or finish it before boarding. If you're already on, say "Joesonghamnida" (죄송합니다) — sorry — get off at the next stop, finish your drink, and catch the next bus. That's the fastest fix.

3. You missed pressing the stop bell and rode past your stop — Get off at the next stop and catch a bus going the opposite direction from across the street. Transfer within 30 minutes (60 minutes between 9 p.m. and 7 a.m.) and the discount applies — but catching the same route back in the opposite direction can be excluded from the discount, depending on the route (#1271).

⑧ Plan B — When You're Not Sure

  • Not sure if you can sit? Stand. Standing on the Korean subway is never a mistake.
  • Holding a drink when the bus pulls up? Take the next one. Seoul city buses run at short intervals.
  • Can't follow the announcement? Check the display board or the station name on Naver Map (#1295). Most routes announce in Korean followed by English.
