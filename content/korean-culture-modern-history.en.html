Korean culture history/modern explainer

> Historical context that foreign tourists encounter while traveling in Korea — Joseon, Japanese colonial period, liberation, war, and division organized based on neutral facts

Foreigners who visit Korea for the first time encounter various historical spaces such as Gyeongbokgung Palace, Japanese-style houses in Gunsan, Gwangjang Market, the Demilitarized Zone (DMZ), and Jongno Hanok Village. However, the reasons why each space was preserved in that way and how Koreans remember a specific period are not often mentioned in the guidebook. This explainer is a meta guide organized to help foreign segment personas understand Korean history in a neutral and fact-based manner.

2. Frequently asked questions by foreigners (neutral description)

Q. Why are there so many Japanese-style buildings remaining in Korea?

Among the buildings built during the Japanese colonial period (1910-1945), those that are worth preserving or that can currently be used are operated as cultural assets or museums. Examples include Japanese-style houses in Sinheung-dong, Gunsan, and modern architecture in Jeong-dong, Seoul. The Korean government's official position is "preservation and education of historical facts" and is not biased towards either glorification or criticism.

Q. Why is there a historical conflict between Korea and Japan?

Differences in perceptions of unresolved history, such as forced mobilization and comfort women during the Japanese colonial period, are the main causes of diplomatic and public opinion conflicts between the two countries. From the perspective of foreign tourists, both countries are destinations that can be visited, and learning based on historical facts is recommended rather than expressing political views.

Q. Can I go to North Korea?

Direct visits are limited to foreigners as well (partially possible through group tours, but virtually impossible for Americans). DMZ Tour (Paju/Yeoncheon) is also open to foreigners after completing certain procedures, allowing them to experience the reality of division most directly.

Q. How should we understand the Korean War?

The Korean War from 1950 to 1953 was an international war in which South and North Korea, UN forces, and Chinese forces participated. War Memorial (Yongsan) exhibits various perspectives, including the contributions of the 16 UN participating countries. A meaningful visit destination for foreigners from participating countries such as the US, UK, Turkey, and Thailand.

  • Japanese colonial era heritage (Gunsan, Jeongdong, Seodaemun Prison) — shared history between the two countries
  • Encourage understanding of Korean perspectives

4. Information on sensitive topics (foreigner fit etiquette)

  • Comfort women/forced mobilization: An unresolved topic for both countries and the international community. Don't just bring it up as a topic.
  • North Korea: Focus on humanitarian aspects of division rather than political opinions.
  • Comparison between Korea and Japan: Recognition of each person’s unique value rather than a “better/worse” comparison.
  • Religious conflict: Religious freedom is guaranteed in Korea. Refrain from comparing and evaluating.
