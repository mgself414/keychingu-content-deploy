Seoul International Fireworks Festival 2026 — September 5 at Yeouido, How to Claim a Prime Viewing Spot Among 1 Million People

Subtitle: Not October this year — September 5 (Sat), three weeks earlier than usual. A complete guide to the free Hanwha (한화) Seoul International Fireworks Festival: the September 4 pre-show, fireworks teams from Korea, the US, and the UK, 6 best viewing spots compared, a subway exit strategy for stations that skip stops, and a pre-trip checklist.

It's the one day each year when the biggest crowd in Seoul packs into a single spot. Show up without a plan and you'll spend more time watching the crowd than the fireworks — this guide is that plan.

① 3 Mistakes Foreigners Often Make

1. Timing Your Arrival for Evening

The best lawn spots at Yeouido start filling up with picnic mats around lunchtime. If you want a front-row view, early afternoon arrival is the bare minimum — show up in the evening and you'll need to give up on the front row for one of the alternative spots below.

2. Heading Straight to Yeouinaru Station the Moment It Ends

Right after the show, crowd control has repeatedly turned Yeouinaru Station (여의나루역) into a stretch where trains pass straight through without stopping, or entry gets restricted (subject to same-day notices). Plan on a 20–30 minute walk home and head to a spread-out station instead — Saetgang (샛강), Singil Station (신길역), or National Assembly Station (국회의사당역) — that's the standard playbook.

3. Remembering "Fall Fireworks Festival = October"

Plan around the usual pattern (first week of October) and this year it'll already be over by the time you show up. 2026's date is September 5 — moved earlier to avoid the Chuseok (추석, Korea's autumn harvest holiday) break. If you're in Seoul during the first week of September, this is a must-add to your schedule.

🧳 Pre-Trip Checklist (Before You Travel · Morning-Of)

  • [ ] Double-check the date — main event 9/5 (Sat), pre-show 9/4 (Fri). Check the official channel (the Hanwha fireworks festival site) on the day for any rain-related changes or cancellations
  • [ ] Picnic mat / portable cushion — you can pick one up at a local convenience store, but Yeouido's stores commonly sell out on the day
  • [ ] Portable charger — 1 million people packed together means network lag and fast battery drain (set a meeting point with your group in advance, in case messaging apps go down on the day)
  • [ ] Dinner to go + water — on-site lines are brutal
  • [ ] Exit strategy — walk to Saetgang, Singil, or National Assembly Station instead of Yeouinaru, or wait 30 minutes after the show ends
  • [ ] Where you stay — for this one night, a hotel within walking distance of Yeouido or along Line 9 is worth its weight in gold. Mapo (마포) or Gongdeok (공덕) are realistic alternatives
