Korean Souvenirs, Chosen by Data — A Matrix by Recipient and Budget

Subtitle: This settles "what should I buy in Korea?" with data, not gut feeling. Cross-referencing 28 physical stores, 24 online platforms, and 26 social-media review sources, it keeps only the items that showed up on all three channels, then sorts them by recipient, culture, and budget. A decision matrix you can use right at the register, not just another list.

ISSUE · #1331 · 2026-08-24

🎯 The Frame — What People "Actually Buy" Shows Up in the Channel Overlap

Souvenir lists are everywhere online. The problem is evidence. This issue's ranking follows one principle: the more channels an item appears on — offline stores, online sales, and social-media reviews — the more likely it's something people actually bought and were actually happy with. We lead with whatever shows the strongest overlap.

③ Gift-Giving Grammar by Culture — Same Gift, Different Answer

  • Western markets: An item "with a story" reads as premium — artisan mother-of-pearl or hanji-paper craft, a category proven by real demand on handmade marketplaces. One meaningful piece for one person beats mass distribution.
  • Japan: Small, individually wrapped portions — the omiyage grammar — are the key: individually packaged snacks, travel-size beauty items, pretty stationery. Quantity per volume is the right answer.
  • Chinese-speaking markets: Formality and brand name win out — red ginseng, premium tea, department-store-wrapped gift sets, anything "displayable at home."

Choosing by the recipient's own grammar, not your own country's habits — that's what this matrix is for.

💡 Field Tips — At the Register and the Airport

1. Tax-free shopping: at any 「Tax Free」 store, spend ₩30,000 (~$22.5) or more and show your passport to qualify for an instant refund. Olive Young and department stores are the classic examples.

2. Check for 1+1 deals: Olive Young runs multi-buy promotions constantly — look for 1+1 or 2+1 on the shelf tags and cross-check against the app's promo price; the effective price can shift dramatically.

3. The 100ml liquid rule: sunscreen and toner count as liquids — pack them in checked luggage. Carry-on only allows containers of 100ml or less.

4. Watch customs rules: many countries ban importing processed meat snacks and fresh fruit — stick to sealed, processed foods for edible gifts.

5. Timing your purchases: beauty and food can wait until your last day, but boutique and craft items are often only sold in that specific neighborhood — if you're on the fence, buy it on the spot.
