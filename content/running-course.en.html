9 Seoul Running Courses — Hangang Parks, City Streams and Forest Paths Chosen by Public Vote, with Distances and How to Get There

Subtitle: The 9 Seoul courses that placed within the top 33 in the Korea Tourism Organization "100×100 Spot Discovery" public vote on the running course theme, grouped into 4 zones: Hangang, city center, parks and forest paths, and a Gangnam stream. The 8.4 km, 7.2 km and 11.5 km figures for the three Hangang parks are park lengths, not running course distances. Olympic Park ends inside access at 22:00, and Cheonggyecheon Stream closes entry when floodgate opening is expected. Banpo's Moonlight Rainbow Fountain runs only from March 15 to October. Distances, opening hours, stations and exits, and places to leave bags are laid out mainly with values given by the managing agencies. The Gyeongbokgung Palace course distance comes from news reports, and the walking time to Cheonggye Plaza is a Kakao Map value.

🎯 Who This Is For, and Why These 9

This list is for people who want to run for a day while traveling in Seoul. It mixes riverside parks, a city stream, the Gyeongbokgung Palace (경복궁) area, forest parks and a park on an old railway line.

There is one selection standard. We listed as they are the 9 Seoul courses that placed within the top 33 in the Korea Tourism Organization "100×100 Spot Discovery" public vote on the running course theme. The Hangang parks (Yeouido Hangang Park (여의도한강공원), Banpo Hangang Park (반포한강공원) and Ttukseom Hangang Park (뚝섬한강공원)) took 2nd, 6th and 9th place, followed by Seoul Forest (서울숲) in 15th, Cheonggyecheon Stream (청계천) in 18th, Olympic Park (올림픽공원) in 24th, the Gyeongbokgung Palace Daengdaeng Run (댕댕런) course in 29th, Yangjaecheon Stream (양재천) in 31st and Gyeongui Line Forest Park (경의선숲길) in 33rd. Namsan Northern Circular Road (남산 북측순환로), in 59th place (85 votes), is outside the 9, but it is a path with no cars or bicycles, so we added one more place at the end. The courses outside Seoul from the same vote are covered separately in #1445.

These 9 are not tracks made only for running. They are paths shared with walkers, bicycles and tourists. So we wrote down how long each is, when it opens and closes, and which exit of which station to take.

🚦 Before You Run — Common Safety and Manners

None of the 9 is a path made only for running. We write separately the rules set by facilities and the general manners to follow on paths that many people share.

  • Bicycle rules in Hangang parks. According to Seoul Metropolitan Government Hangang Project Headquarters, in Hangang parks bicycles ride only on the bike lane, at 20 km/h or less, keep to the right and ride in single file. At night, turn on the light.
  • Runners do not enter the bike lane. This is a manner, not a regulation. Because bicycles are supposed to ride only on the bike lane, runners using the walking path means fewer collisions. The Banpo Hangang Park introduction says the bike lane and the walking path are separated.
  • Slow down on crowded paths. Cheonggyecheon Stream, the Yeonnam-dong section of Gyeongui Line Forest Park and the Gyeongbokgung Palace area are in the middle of tourist areas. Walk in narrow sections.
  • Follow the rules set by each facility. At Cheonggyecheon Stream, you cannot ride bicycles or inline skates, and you cannot bring in animals (except guide dogs for people with disabilities). On the walking path inside the earthen fortress at Olympic Park, pedestrians have priority and dogs must be on a leash. Hangang parks are non-smoking, the fine is 50,000 won if a dog is not on a leash, and 100,000 won for leaving trash behind.
  • Cheonggyecheon Stream closes entry when floodgate opening is expected. If an evacuation announcement is broadcast, leave the stream right away. Do not shelter from rain under a bridge.
  • In an accident or emergency, call 119.

Places to Leave Bags and Change Clothes

  • Runner Station (러너스테이션) (Yeouinaru Station): A facility Seoul announced in May 2024. It is on floors B1 to M1 inside Line 5 Yeouinaru Station. It has a changing room, a powder room and 58 lockers (42 on floor M1, 16 on floor B1).
  • Runner Support Spaces (러너지원공간) at 3 subway stations: Seoul announced them in October 2025. They are at Line 5 Gwanghwamun Station, Line 4 Hoehyeon Station (회현역) and Line 6 World Cup Stadium Station (월드컵경기장역). They open daily 05:30~23:00 and run in line with subway service hours. Entry is by Naver QR or KakaoTalk verification, and Seoul stated there is no separate usage fee. There are men's and women's changing rooms, personal lockers, a powder room and a vending machine for running gear.
  • Open shower room at Yeouido Hangang Park: It is on the 1st floor of the Yeouido Hangang Park Information Center (안내센터). It opens daily 06:30~22:00, and 08:30~09:30 and 15:30~16:30 are cleaning times. It is free, and you bring your own towel and toiletries. Entry is by Naver app QR code verification. There are 5 shower booths each for men and women, 13 lockers and 2 changing rooms. Finish your shower within 20 minutes; laundry is not allowed. For inquiries, call the Yeouido Information Center at 02-3780-0563.

Runner Support Spaces are entered by Naver QR or KakaoTalk verification, and the shower room by Naver app QR code verification.

① Hangang — 3 Places

All 3 are Hangang parks managed by Seoul Metropolitan Government Hangang Project Headquarters. The km figures below are the park length stretching along the river, not the distance of a set running course. Runners choose their own start and turnaround points.

Yeouido Hangang Park (여의도한강공원)

  • Public vote: 162 votes · 2nd in the Korea Tourism Organization "100×100 Spot Discovery" public vote on the running course theme.
  • Park length: 8.4 km. It runs from the center of Hangang Railway Bridge (한강철교) to the Saetgang (샛강) side behind the National Assembly. Going end to end and back is 16.8 km.
  • How to get there: Each station reaches a different spot.
  • Line 5 Yeouinaru Station Exits 2 and 3: 1 minute on foot, the center of the park. The elevator is near Exit 2.
  • Line 9 National Assembly Station (국회의사당역) Exit 1: 1 minute on foot, behind the National Assembly.
  • Line 9 Saetgang Station (샛강역) Exit 4: 10 minutes on foot.
  • Lines 5 and 9 Yeouido Station (여의도역) Exit 1: 15~20 minutes on foot.
  • If you get off at the Yeouido pier of the Hangang Bus (한강버스), it connects directly to the park.
  • Facilities: There are 4 convenience stores, 24 restrooms, shade shelters and benches. The fountain at Mulbit Plaza (물빛광장) runs from April to October.
  • Bags and showers: Runner Station is inside Yeouinaru Station, and the open shower room is on the 1st floor of the park Information Center. The details are written in "Places to Leave Bags and Change Clothes" above.
  • Address and inquiries: 330 Yeouidong-ro, Yeongdeungpo-gu, Seoul. Yeouido Information Center 02-3780-0561~6.

Banpo Hangang Park (반포한강공원)

  • Public vote: 143 votes · 6th in the Korea Tourism Organization "100×100 Spot Discovery" public vote on the running course theme.
  • Park length: 7.2 km. The park introduction says the bike lane and the walking path are separated over 7.2 km and connect to Yeouido and Jamwon (잠원) Hangang Parks. Going end to end and back is 14.4 km.
  • Jamsugyo Bridge (잠수교): According to the park introduction, Jamsugyo Bridge has reduced car lanes, a widened sidewalk and a bicycle-only lane.
  • How to get there:
  • Line 9 Sinbanpo Station Exit 1: enter through Banpo Interchange (반포나들목), about 15 minutes on foot.
  • Lines 3, 7 and 9 Express Bus Terminal Station (고속터미널역) Exits 8-1 and 8-2 (or GOTO Mall (고투몰) G1): about 15 minutes on foot toward Banpo Bridge (반포대교).
  • Lines 4 and 9 Dongjak Station (동작역) Exits 1 and 2: walk along the Hangang toward Seoraeseom Island (서래섬).
  • Moonlight Rainbow Fountain (달빛무지개분수): A fountain installed on Banpo Bridge with a total length of 1,140 m. On November 7, 2008, it was entered in Guinness as the "world's longest bridge fountain". Viewing is free, with no reservation, on site.
  • Operating period: From March 15 to October. From November to March 14 of the next year is not an operating period. If you run Banpo in winter, you will be running along a riverside without the fountain.
  • Shows (20 minutes each): From March 15 to May, and in October: 12:00, 19:30, 20:00, 20:30 and 21:00. From June to September, 21:30 is added to these. There is no weekday and weekend distinction.
  • When it stops running: When wind speed is 7 m/s or more, when rain is forecast during operating hours, or when Hangang turbidity is 15 NTU or more. Times may change if there is an event. Inquiries 02-3780-0578~9.
  • Facilities: There are 2 convenience stores and 7 restrooms.
  • Address and inquiries: 40 Sinbanpo-ro 11-gil, Seocho-gu, Seoul. Banpo Information Center 02-3780-0541~4.

Ttukseom Hangang Park (뚝섬한강공원)

  • Public vote: 136 votes · 9th in the Korea Tourism Organization "100×100 Spot Discovery" public vote on the running course theme.
  • Park length: 11.5 km. It runs from Jungnangcheon Bridge (중랑천교) in Geumho-dong (금호동) to the Seoul boundary upstream of Gwangjin Bridge (광진교). It is the longest of the 3. Going end to end and back is 23 km.
  • How to get there:
  • Line 7 Jayang Station Exits 2 and 3: connects directly to the park (1 minute on foot). The Hangang park directions page writes "Jayang Station", and the park introduction page on the same site writes "Ttukseom Resort Station Exits 2 and 3".
  • Line 2 Konkuk Univ. Station (건대입구역) Exit 5: 1,000 m.
  • If you get off at the Ttukseom pier of the Hangang Bus, it connects directly to the park.
  • Facilities: There are 3 convenience stores, 18 restrooms, shade shelters and benches.
  • Address and inquiries: 2273 Gangbyeonbuk-ro, Gwangjin-gu, Seoul. Ttukseom Information Center 02-3780-0521~4.

② City Center — 2 Places

Gwanghwamun Station on Line 5 has a Runner Support Space, so it is a good place to leave bags before heading out.

Cheonggyecheon Stream (청계천)

  • Public vote: 128 votes · 18th in the Korea Tourism Organization "100×100 Spot Discovery" public vote on the running course theme.
  • Length: Seoul Facilities Corporation lists the total length as 8.12 km and the restored section as 5.84 km. The section list starts with Cheonggye Plaza to Mojeon Bridge (모전교), 150 m, and ends with Gosanja Bridge (고산자교) to Sindap Railway Bridge (신답철교), 307 m. The first bridge is Mojeon Bridge and the last bridge is Gosanja Bridge.
  • How to get there: By Kakao Map route, it is 3 minutes on foot (about 120 m) from Line 5 Gwanghwamun Station Exit 5 to Cheonggye Plaza.
  • Prohibited activities: The prohibited list in the citizen notices includes using bicycles and inline skates, bringing in animals (except guide dogs for people with disabilities), fishing, swimming, camping and cooking, and smoking and drinking. Running is not on this list. If you plan to run with a dog, choose another course.
  • On rainy days: Cheonggyecheon Stream is not a place to run on a rainy day.
  • Entry is closed when floodgates are expected to open because of rain.
  • Even if it is not raining around Cheonggyecheon Stream, leave the stream right away if an evacuation announcement is broadcast. Water also rises from rain that fell upstream.
  • Do not shelter from rain under a bridge. When it rains, floodgates suddenly open and under a bridge is dangerous.
  • There are emergency ladders at 39 places for people stranded by sudden rain. If you are stranded, call the Cheonggyecheon Comprehensive Control Room at 02-2290-7111~3.

Gyeongbokgung Palace "Daengdaeng Run" loop (경복궁 댕댕런 코스)

  • Public vote: 111 votes · 29th in the Korea Tourism Organization "100×100 Spot Discovery" public vote on the running course theme.
  • What kind of course it is (news reports): According to news reports, it is a running course around Gwanghwamun, Gyeongbokgung Palace, Cheong Wa Dae (청와대), Samcheong-dong (삼청동), Jongno and Cheonggyecheon Stream. When the GPS track of the run is joined into a line, it forms the shape of a puppy with ears and a tail, which gave it the name "Daengdaeng Run" (dog-shaped GPS route). The reports gave the distance as about 8 km, and said the course includes Gyeongbokgung Palace, Seochon (서촌) and Insa-dong (인사동).
  • Distance: About 8 km is a value from news reports.
  • The running route and the inside of the palace are different: The course in the reports passes around Gyeongbokgung Palace, Seochon, Insa-dong, Cheong Wa Dae and Samcheong-dong. Inside the palace is a viewing area you enter by paying admission. Plan running and sightseeing separately.
  • How to get there: Line 5 Gwanghwamun Station has a Runner Support Space. From Line 5 Gwanghwamun Station Exit 5 to Cheonggye Plaza is 3 minutes on foot (about 120 m, by Kakao Map route). The address of Gyeongbokgung Palace is 161 Sajik-ro, Jongno-gu, Seoul.
  • Nearby sight — Gyeongbokgung Palace: These are values from the Palaces and Royal Tombs Center of the Korea Heritage Service.
  • Closed days: Every Tuesday. If Tuesday overlaps with a public holiday or substitute holiday, it is open, and it is closed on the first non-holiday day after that.
  • Viewing hours: November to December and January to February, 09:00~17:00 (last entry 16:00). March to May and September to October, 09:00~18:00 (last entry 17:00). June to August, 09:00~18:30 (last entry 17:30). Hours may be shortened or adjusted depending on circumstances.
  • Fees: 3,000 won for individuals (foreigners aged 19 to 64). Foreigners aged 18 and under and aged 65 and over are free. Wearing a hanbok is free. Free admission on the Wednesday "Culture Day" does not apply to Gyeongbokgung Palace.

③ Parks and Forest Paths — 3 Places

Seoul Forest is open 24 hours, and Olympic Park ends inside access at 22:00. Gyeongui Line Forest Park is a long, narrow park along an old railway line.

Seoul Forest (서울숲)

  • Public vote: 130 votes · 15th in the Korea Tourism Organization "100×100 Spot Discovery" public vote on the running course theme.
  • Hours: The Seoul announcement (2026-10-30) says Seoul Forest is open 24 hours a day, year-round. Some facilities, such as the Ecological Forest (생태숲), have separate hours.
  • Park paths: According to the Seoul announcement, the core park paths inside Seoul Forest, 22,747.6 m in total, are pedestrian-only paths. On these paths you cannot ride a bicycle and must walk it. 22,747.6 m is the total of all the park paths added together, not the distance of a one-lap course.
  • How to get there: From Suin-Bundang Line Seoul Forest Station Exit 4 to the park is about 2 minutes on foot.

To protect cultural heritage, the Mongchontoseong Fortress (몽촌토성) zone blocks entry inside outside of operating hours. Using the park is free, and parking is charged separately.

Gyeongui Line Forest Park (경의선숲길)

  • Public vote: 104 votes · 33rd in the Korea Tourism Organization "100×100 Spot Discovery" public vote on the running course theme.
  • Length: The Seoul Parks guide lists the whole at about 6.3 km. The Yeonnam-dong (연남동) section is about 1.2 km. A round trip on the Yeonnam-dong section is about 2.4 km, and two round trips are about 4.8 km.
  • How to get there: Each section has different stations.
  • Yeonnam-dong section: Hongik Univ. Station (홍대입구역) (Line 2, Airport Railroad, Gyeongui-Jungang Line) and Gajwa Station (가좌역) (Gyeongui-Jungang Line). The Seoul announcement (2025-03) lists it as 438 m from Hongik Univ. Station Exit 3. The Seoul Parks guide also lists the busking spot in this section as "Hongik Univ. Station Exit 3 to Yeonnam Police Box (연남파출소) (about 200 m)".
  • Wau Bridge (와우교) section: Hongik Univ. Station, Sogang Univ. Station (서강대역) (Gyeongui-Jungang Line)

  • Saechanggogae and Wonhyo-ro (새창고개·원효로) section: Line 6 Hyochang Park Station (효창공원앞역)
  • Restrooms: The Seoul Parks guide lists 2 park restrooms. The management office restroom opens weekdays 09:00~18:00, and the Yeomni-dong restroom is open 24 hours. Use the restroom at the station before you run.
  • Parking: There is no parking lot inside the park. Seoul Parks recommends public transportation.

④ Gangnam Stream — 1 Place

Yangjaecheon Stream (양재천)

  • Public vote: 106 votes · 31st in the Korea Tourism Organization "100×100 Spot Discovery" public vote on the running course theme.
  • Length: Gangnam-gu Office lists the total length as 15.6 km and the Gangnam-gu section as 3.75 km. A round trip on the Gangnam-gu section is 7.5 km.
  • 2 kinds of paths: A Seoul article (2022-05) introduced the walking paths as 2 branches. The streamside path is shared by walkers and bicycles, and the embankment hill walking path is for walkers only. If you want to avoid bicycles, use the embankment path.
  • How to get there: Based on the Gangnam-gu guide (2020-07).
  • Line 3 Dogok Station (도곡역) Exit 3: exit toward Yangjaecheon Neighborhood Park (양재천 근린공원) and enter Yangjaecheon Stream.
  • Line 3 Hangnyeoul Station (학여울역) Exit 1: about 280 m, about 4 minutes on foot.
  • Suin-Bundang Line Gaepo-dong Station Exits 2 and 3: about 100 m, about 1~2 minutes on foot.
  • Facilities: Gangnam-gu Office introduces an observatory, scenic lighting and the Media Sapphire (미디어 사파이어). The Gangnam-gu guide says there are restroom signs here and there along the walking path.

➕ One More — Namsan Northern Circular Road (남산 북측순환로)

  • Public vote: 85 votes · 59th in the Korea Tourism Organization "100×100 Spot Discovery" public vote on the running course theme. It is outside the 9, but we add it because it is a path where you can run without worrying about cars and bicycles.
  • Distance: A Seoul article (2018-08) lists about 3.3 km from the Education Science Institute (교육과학원) to the National Theater of Korea (국립중앙극장). A round trip is about 6.6 km.
  • Cars and bicycles: Seoul introduced this road as a walking path where car and bicycle traffic is entirely banned and only pedestrians use it. The full ban on vehicles began in June 1996.
  • Do not confuse it with the Southern Circular Road: According to a Seoul article (2025-06), Namsan Southern Circular Road (남측순환로) is a road shared by pedestrians and cars, with vehicles and bicycles running one way. The place to run is the north side.
  • At night: The Seoul article says that when night falls, the lights come on one by one.

⚠ 3 Common Mistakes

  1. Treating the length of a Hangang park as the course distance — Yeouido 8.4 km, Banpo 7.2 km and Ttukseom 11.5 km are park lengths stretching along the river. They are not loop courses, so if you go to the end you have to come back along the same path. To run a set distance, measure with a watch or an app and choose your own turnaround point. If you want a place with a set lap distance, use Olympic Park's walking/jogging courses (1,302 m~4,290 m).
  2. Going to Cheonggyecheon Stream on a rainy day — With so many bridges it looks like you could shelter from the rain, but it is the opposite. When it rains, floodgates can suddenly open, making under a bridge dangerous, and entry itself is blocked when floodgate opening is expected. Even on a clear day, climb out right away if an evacuation announcement is broadcast.
  3. Getting the station and the time wrong at Olympic Park — Olympic Park Station is on Line 5 and Line 9. If you take Line 8, exit at Mongchontoseong Station Exit 1. Entry on foot is until 22:00, and after that you cannot enter deeper into the park. If you run in the evening, plan your time so that you enter before 22:00.

⚙ 3 FAQs

  1. Where can I leave my bags without going back to my accommodation? — On the Yeouido Hangang Park side, Runner Station inside Line 5 Yeouinaru Station has a changing room and 58 lockers. The Runner Support Spaces at Gwanghwamun Station (Line 5), Hoehyeon Station (Line 4) and World Cup Stadium Station (Line 6) open daily 05:30~23:00 and have changing rooms and personal lockers. Entry is by Naver QR or KakaoTalk verification. For a shower, use the open shower room on the 1st floor of the Yeouido Hangang Park Information Center (06:30~22:00, free, Naver app QR code verification).
  2. Where can I run after sunset? — Seoul Forest is open 24 hours a day, year-round. At Olympic Park, entry on foot is until 22:00, and only the plaza area is open until 24:00. Night lighting and closing times are covered separately in the #147 Seoul Night Running Course Guide.
  3. What changes between late November and winter? — Banpo's Moonlight Rainbow Fountain runs only from March 15 to October, so you cannot see it in winter. The Mulbit Plaza fountain at Yeouido also runs only from April to October. Gyeongbokgung Palace shortens its viewing hours to 09:00~17:00 in November to December and January to February, with last entry at 16:00.
