Myeongdong (명동) to Euljiro (을지로) Evening Walking Course — Seoul's Six Faces in Two Hours, Finished with Yajang (야장) Beer

Subtitle: Starts at Myeongdong Station (명동역), 19:00 · About 4 km, 6 stops — shopping in Myeongdong, historic architecture at Myeongdong Cathedral (명동대성당), industrial heritage in the Print Shop Alley (인쇄골목), modern architecture at Sewoon Sangga (세운상가), the urban stream Cheonggyecheon (청계천), finishing with draft beer in a yajang alley. For a full daytime Myeongdong itinerary, see the Myeongdong Full-Day Guide; for the full-day Euljiro course, see the Euljiro Full Loop Guide — this piece is the two-hour evening link between the two neighborhoods.

ISSUE 04 · #1309 · 2026-08-19

🎯 The Concept — Walking from "Tourist Seoul" into "Night Seoul"

Myeongdong and Euljiro are only one subway stop apart, yet their personalities are complete opposites. Myeongdong is the tourist district with the highest concentration of foreign visitors in Seoul; Euljiro is the still-working industrial alley district that Western travelers call "authentic Seoul." This course's whole point is that contrast — the mood shifts six times over two hours.

① Three Mistakes Foreign Visitors Often Make

1. Spending your whole evening in Myeongdong

Evening crowds peak in Myeongdong, but one hour is plenty. In this course, Myeongdong is the starting point — the real evening happens in Euljiro. If you want to dig deeper into Myeongdong, save that for daytime with the Myeongdong Full-Day Guide.

2. Turning back in Euljiro's alleys because "there's no shop here"

Many bars and restaurants in Euljiro have no sign at all, or sit hidden inside old print shop buildings. It's completely normal for a place to show up on a map app with no visible entrance — hunting for the door is half the Euljiro experience.

3. Assuming yajang means "a restaurant with outdoor tables"

Yajang (야장) is the culture of an entire alley filling up with folding tables once evening falls. Seating is first-come, first-served, sharing a table with strangers is normal, and the standard order is draft beer plus dried snacks. There's no such thing as a reservation.

② The 6-Stop Course (19:00 → 21:00+)

1. Myeongdong-gil (명동길) · K-Beauty Street — 19:00–19:20

By evening, street stalls on Myeongdong-gil — chicken skewers, hotteok, cream shrimp, ₩2,000–12,000 — turn the whole street into a night market. Big K-beauty stores like the Olive Young Myeongdong Central branch (올리브영 명동중앙점) stay open until 22:00, so just window-shop and note what you want — this isn't a route for carrying bags.

  • Check: street-side currency exchange rate boards (2–3% better than the airport) · tax refunds require a single receipt of ₩30,000+.
  • The waiting line outside the Myeongdong Kyoja (명동교자) flagship is a sight in itself — eating here is outside the scope of this course (see the Myeongdong Full-Day Guide).

2. Myeongdong Cathedral (명동대성당) — 19:20–19:35

Completed in 1898, this is Korea's first Gothic-style cathedral and the seat of the Korean Catholic Church. In the evening the facade is lit up, so photos come out better than in daytime. Looking down from the hilltop, the Gothic spire and Myeongdong's neon land in the same frame — a shot you only get at this hour. Viewing the exterior is free, 24 hours.

3. Euljiro's Print Shop Alley (인쇄골목) — 19:35–20:05 (including travel time)

Cross from Myeongdong into Euljiro 3-ga (을지로3가) and the texture of the city changes. In an alley where 1960s–70s print shops and metalworks are still in operation, bars and cafes are tucked inside old factory buildings left largely as they were. The 19:00 hour is the "transformation time" — print shop shutters coming down as neon signs switch on.

  • Unmarked cocktail bars (open from 18:00) are the alley's specialty — Negronis run about ₩15,000–22,000.
  • ⚠ Etiquette: craftspeople and residents still work in this alley. No photographing without permission — observe as you walk.
  • For a full-day Euljiro course, see the Euljiro Full Loop Guide; for one centered on retro bars, see the Euljiro Retro Hipster Tour.

4. Sewoon Sangga (세운상가) · Daerim Sangga (대림상가) — 20:05–20:25

Korea's first mixed-use megastructure, designed in 1968 by architect Kim Swoo-geun (김수근). An elevated pedestrian bridge links about 1 km between Jongno (종로) and Toegyero (퇴계로). The highlight is the Euljiro night view from the bridge, plus the rooftop view toward the Jongmyo (종묘)–Namsan (남산) axis — check on-site whether it's open. For architecture fans hunting "brutalist Seoul," this is the course's highlight.

  • Note the bridge-level shop layout — sticking to the ground floor means missing half the building.

5. Cheonggyecheon (청계천): Sewoon-gyo (세운교) to Supyogyo (수표교) — 20:25–20:35

An urban stream restored in 2005. Unlike the touristy stretch near Gwanghwamun, this section is where off-duty locals actually walk. Spend just 10 minutes along the lit-up waterside, then head back to Euljiro 3-ga — a pause before the yajang.

6. Euljiro's Nogari-golmok (노가리골목) Yajang — 20:35–, Finish 🍺

Euljiro 3-ga's beer-hall alley. It began in 1980 with "draft beer + nogari, ₩100" and grew into the whole alley turning into an outdoor bar — now a landmark of Seoul's yajang culture, recognized as Seoul Future Heritage (서울미래유산). The peak is around 21:00 — you'll arrive right when office workers' after-work dinners (회식) and travelers mix into the most Euljiro-like scene there is.

  • The standard order: draft beer + nogari (노가리, dried grilled pollack with gochujang mayo) + spicy whelk salad.
  • Seating is first-come, first-served · most places take cards, but some old-school spots (노포) prefer cash.
  • For a comparison of late-night food scenes (Euljiro vs. Jongno vs. Gangnam), see the Late-Night Snack Spots Compared guide.

④ Practical Tips

  • Restrooms: Using the station facilities at Myeongdong Station or Euljiro 3-ga Station is your safest bet. In the yajang alley, use the restroom inside a beer hall.
  • Payment: Some street stalls are cash-only — one ₩10,000 bill covers the cash needs of the whole course.
  • If it rains: the print shop alley and yajang lose half their appeal — on rainy days, swap this course for an indoor one like the Myeongdong Full-Day Guide.
  • Solo-friendly?: Solo diners are common at the yajang. If the table-sharing culture feels intimidating, a small spot deeper in the alley is more comfortable than a big place at the entrance.
