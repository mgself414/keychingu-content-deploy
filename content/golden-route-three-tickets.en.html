Korea Golden Route ③ Three Tickets — Seoul → Gyeongju → Busan → Seoul After the KTX–SRT Merger: Fares, Bus Alternatives, Whether the KORAIL PASS Pays Off, and Holiday Booking

Subtitle: On September 1, 2026, Korea's two high-speed rail firms merged — SRT is gone, Suseo trains are KTX too, one app (Korail Plus), fares down about 10%. This piece covers the Golden Route's three tickets, the bus alternative, whether the KORAIL PASS pays off, and holiday booking order.

ISSUE · #1374 · 2026-09-10

🎯 The Frame — Three Tickets and You're Done

The Golden Route (#1372) needs three tickets. Seoul (or Suseo) → Gyeongju Station (경주역), Gyeongju Station → Busan Station (부산역), Busan Station → Seoul (or Suseo). All three combined run around 100,000 won and take under 5 hours total. But how you buy those three tickets changed on September 1, 2026 — SRT (Suseo High-Speed Rail, 수서고속철도), which ran as a separate operator since 2016, was absorbed into Korail (코레일). Now the trains leaving Suseo are KTX too, and there's one app, Korail Plus (코레일+). The old advice — "install the SRT app separately" — has been wrong since this month.

This piece covers the tickets for all three legs (time, fare, alternatives), whether the foreigner-only KORAIL PASS (코레일패스) pays off on this route, and the order of steps when KTX sells out over the holidays — Chuseok (추석) and Seollal (설). App screens are #1287's job; bus terminal procedure is #1297's.

  • Three tickets combined — standard class (일반실), ~2026-09, approx.: Seoul–Gyeongju in the 40,000-won range + Gyeongju–Busan around 10,000 won + Busan–Seoul 54,400 won ≈ 100,000–110,000 won
  • KORAIL PASS 3-day pass: 186,000 won — loses money on this route alone (see 💳 below)
  • Holiday alternative: intercity buses (Gyeongju–Busan, about 50 runs a day) and more seats since the merger

Why the Math Matters — The "Unlimited" Trap and What the Merger Changed

The KORAIL PASS is an unlimited-ride KTX pass sold only to foreigners. The word "unlimited" is why most first-timers buy one. But the Golden Route is just three one-way legs — even combined, they run about 60% of the 3-day pass price. There are cases where the pass does pay off — adding Jeonju (전주) or Gangneung (강릉), or riding twice or more a day. This piece puts numbers on that break-even point.

The merger changed four things, and all four favor the Golden Route. ① One app: Korail Plus shows Seoul Station (서울역) and Suseo Station (수서역) departures on one screen. ② Lower fares: KTX fares dropped to the old SRT level, so Seoul–Busan standard class went from 59,800 won to 54,400 won (frozen for three years). ③ More seats: 15,000–17,000 more seats a day, with Suseo-departing seats up 30% — a bit less sellout pressure over holidays and weekends. ④ Name cleanup: the old SRT trains now run as "KTX-Sancheon (KTX-산천)." If SRT no longer shows on the timetable, it hasn't disappeared.

📖 Narrative 1 — Two Companies, Two Apps, Ten Years: KTX left Seoul Station starting in 2004, and SRT left Suseo Station starting in December 2016. Two companies ran the same track, with two apps and two mileage programs. For foreign travelers, the confusing question wasn't "Seoul Station or Suseo Station" — it was "Korail Talk (코레일톡) or SRT." That decade ended on September 1, 2026, when the two companies became one. This piece is how to buy the tickets in that first fall.

  • The Golden Route's three tickets combined run about 100,000–110,000 won — 70,000–80,000 won cheaper than the 186,000-won 3-day pass. If this route is all you're riding, the pass loses money.
  • When the pass does pay off: adding two or more extra legs — Jeonju, Gangneung, or Yeosu (여수) — or riding twice or more a day on a round trip or day trip, or riding first class often.
  • Note: the pass requires seat reservation (you can't board on the pass alone). Before the merger, it didn't work on SRT (Suseo-departing trains) — confirm with Korail officially whether it applies to Suseo-departing KTX after the merger before you buy. As of this writing, that hasn't been officially confirmed.

📖 Narrative 2 — The Great Holiday Migration: Chuseok falls on the 15th day of the 8th lunar month. In 2026, the holiday runs September 24–26. In the days around it, tens of millions of Koreans travel home, and KTX sells out right after booking opens. Korail runs a separate early-sale window for each holiday — for the 2026 Chuseok window, tickets sold September 7–11, daily 07:00–13:00, only through the Korail Plus app and a dedicated holiday-booking website, with different routes released on different days (#279). Foreign travelers rarely catch that window — so the order of steps changes over the holidays.

📱 Booking — One App, Three Channels

  • KTX: the Korail Plus app (Korail Talk was renamed this on August 3) or the English-language letskorail site. Overseas card payment works. Seoul Station and Suseo Station departures both show on one screen.
  • Intercity bus (Gyeongju–Busan): BusTago or T-money GO. It won't show in Kobus (코버스), the express bus app — Gyeongju–Busan is an intercity route, not express (#1297).
  • Express bus (alternative for Seoul–Gyeongju and Busan–Seoul): Kobus, T-money GO.
  • Timing: on weekdays, same-day booking usually works. Book ahead for weekends, holiday weekends, and holidays.

🗓 When Holiday Tickets Are Sold Out — The Order of Steps

1. Recheck leftover seats in Korail Plus: seats left over after the holiday special-sale window get released into general sale. With more seats since the merger, there's a bit more room than before.

2. Check standing tickets (입석) and unreserved seats: on a short leg (Gyeongju–Busan, 30 min), standing is enough.

3. Switch to an intercity bus: Gyeongju–Busan runs about 50 times a day. Seoul–Gyeongju and Busan–Seoul also have express buses (around 4 hours). Extra runs are standard practice over holidays.

4. Shift the date by a day: the day before or after the holiday itself works better than the holiday day.

🙏 Manners — Stations and Buses

  • Don't sit in someone else's reserved seat. If you're standing, stay near the car's connecting vestibule.
  • Cancel a booked ticket if you're not going to use it — over the holidays, that one seat is someone's trip home.
  • At bus terminals, line up by platform number. Hand bags for the luggage compartment to the driver.

💡 Where People Get Tripped Up

  • "Install the SRT app" is outdated advice: gone as of September 1, 2026. It's Korail Plus, one app.
  • Suseo Station trains are KTX too: the timetable shows them as "KTX-Sancheon." If your lodging is in Gangnam, Suseo is closer.
  • The pass requires seat reservation: you can't board on the pass alone. Reserve a seat in the app.
  • Gyeongju–Busan doesn't show in the express bus app: it's an intercity route. Use BusTago or T-money GO.
  • The holiday special-sale window is hard for foreigners to catch: go in order — leftover seats, then bus, then shift the date.
  • Fares change: the cut is frozen for three years, but there's variation by leg. The same-day app price is the real answer.

FAQ 6

Q1. Should I buy the KORAIL PASS?

  • Not if you're only riding the Golden Route's three legs. If you add two or more cities, run the math.

Q2. Seoul Station or Suseo Station — where do I board?

  • Go by lodging. Suseo for Gangnam, Songpa, or Jamsil; Seoul Station for Myeongdong, Hongdae, or Jongno. Fares and the app are the same after the merger.

Q3. When should I buy tickets?

  • Same-day works on weekdays. Book a few days ahead for weekends; over holidays, check leftover seats and buses together.

Q4. Do I need first class?

  • It's more comfortable if your bags are very large. Standard class is enough on legs under about 2 hours.

Q5. Which leg beats KTX with an intercity bus?

  • Just one — Gyeongju to Busan. Board right downtown, 50 minutes. KTX is better for the other two legs.

Q6. Does the merger leave travelers worse off anywhere?

  • Not so far. Fares dropped and seats increased. Mileage and discount programs are still changing, so check the app's notices.
