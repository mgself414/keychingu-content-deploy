Korean Photo Booth Culture — Why a Self-Photo Studio Sits on Every Korean Street Corner

insaeng-necut ("life four cuts," 인생네컷) · Photoism (포토이즘) · Haru Film (하루필름) · unmanned photo booths · instant prints · QR download — an explainer on Korea's self-photo studio culture, for foreigners. Not "go shoot here," but "why are there so many, what are they, and how do you shoot?"

Walk around Seoul and one of the questions foreigners ask most often is this: "Why are there three self-photo studios on a single block?" In Hongdae (홍대), Seongsu (성수), Gangnam (강남) — everywhere — tiny unmanned shops with pink-and-blue neon signs are packed in denser than cafés. There's no staff, just a few booths inside and a basket of props with headbands, sunglasses, and wigs. Koreans, on a date, meeting a friend, or after a concert — just walk in, shoot four cuts, and walk out holding two strips of paper. Foreigners usually see this as "just an old-school photo booth," but Korea's four-cut photo (insaeng-necut, 인생네컷) has a different flavor. It's a single "record-and-play" culture where the unmanned economy + the MZ generation's memory-keeping + SNS sharing + K-pop fandom are all tangled together. This article isn't "which shop is good" (→ cross-link); it unpacks why this craze took off, what it is, and how a foreigner can shoot it themselves and walk away with the digital files. The key: Korea's four-cut photo isn't a "service that hands you a photo." It's "the experience of playing together with a friend or partner and keeping that moment as a physical print." Prices, brands, and frames change often, so treat every number below as check-before-you-go.

⚠️ 3 Things Foreigners Often Misunderstand

1. "It's just the same as an old Western photo booth" ❌ — The form looks alike, but the Korean version lets you pick your own props, poses, and frame, and you choose your favorite 4 cuts out of 8–10 you've shot, then gives you a paper print + a QR digital file + a short looping clip all at once. It's less "receiving a photo that got taken" and more a mini-studio where you direct and edit it yourself.

2. "No staff — how do I even shoot? If I don't speak Korean I can't do it" ❌ — Almost every shop is an unmanned kiosk, and major-brand kiosks offer English, Japanese, and Chinese guidance and take card payment. Because there's no staff, foreigners actually shoot more comfortably, free of self-consciousness.

3. "Isn't this just a thing for teenage kids?" ❌ — It started with the MZ generation, but now it's spread to every age and every purpose — date courses for couples, friend meetups, families, K-pop fan pilgrimages, foreign tourist proof-shots. VisitKorea and Korea.net cover it as foreigner Hallyu (Korean Wave, 한류) experience content.

① What Is the Four-Cut Photo (insaeng-necut, 인생네컷)

"insaeng-necut" (인생네컷) combines "insaeng-sajin" (the best photo of my life, 인생사진) + "necut" (four cuts, 네컷). It was the name of one brand that started in Daegu (대구) in 2017, and as it spread so fast, the brand name itself became the "genre name." When a Korean says "let's go shoot insaeng-necut (인생네컷)," they usually don't mean a specific brand but "let's go to a four-cut self-photo studio" — similar to calling all tissues "Kleenex" in the US.

The core makeup goes like this.

  • A 2×6-inch vertical strip — the format comes from the classic Western photo booth, with 4 cuts stacked top to bottom as the default.
  • Black-and-white or color frames — you choose, from a clean black-and-white mood to pastel and character-collab frames.
  • 2 instant prints — usually the same photo prints twice, so a friend or partner each takes one. This "splitting" is the heart of the culture.

In other words, shooting a four-cut photo isn't simply printing a picture — it's the act of physically splitting and keeping that moment with the person who was there with you.

② Why Are There So Many — Unmanned Shops + Brand Competition

The direct cause of the "a photo studio on every block" phenomenon foreigners marvel at is the unmanned (무인) shop structure.

  • Unmanned kiosk operation: With no resident staff, a single kiosk handles shooting, payment, and printing. Low labor cost means a shop can fit even a tiny space, so density has exploded. It's a flagship case of Korea's unmanned economy (unmanned ice cream, unmanned cafés, unmanned photo studios, 무인경제).
  • Brand competition: The market leader insaeng-necut (Life4Cuts, 인생네컷) is reported to have expanded to hundreds of branches nationwide and into several countries abroad within a few years of launch. Behind it compete Photoism (포토이즘 — idol collabs, the "Photoism Box" flagship), Haru Film (Harupict, 하루필름 — went viral via influencers, with trendy frames that change monthly), Photo Signature (포토시그니처), Photogray (포토그레이), Photomatic (포토매틱), and more.
  • Each brand's color: Even for the same "four-cut," lighting, retouch tone, and frame design differ by shop. Some go for bright white and pastel, others for a film, editorial mood. Koreans pick where to go by "which tone shall I shoot in today."

Brand names, branch counts, and overseas-expansion scale differ by report and timing and change fast — the numbers above are for reference, check before you go.

③ The Shooting Process — Pick Props, Strike a Pose, Choose 4 Cuts, Get the QR

The actual flow foreigners are most curious about. Even with no staff, if you know the order it takes five minutes.

1. Pick frame & options (at the kiosk): Walk in, choose your language (English / Japanese / Chinese) on the kiosk screen, select the number of prints and frame design (black-and-white / color / collab), then pay by card.

2. Put on props: Beside or inside the booth are props like headbands, sunglasses, wigs, and hats. They're free, so use them freely. Pinning down a concept makes the photos pop.

3. Pose + countdown shooting: Step into the booth and the screen runs about a 1-minute countdown while it takes usually 8–10 shots in a row. Many shops also have a re-shoot (다시 찍기) option, so redo it if you don't like it. You watch the screen like a mirror and fix your expression and angle.

4. Choose 4 cuts + retouch: Out of the 8–10 you shot, you pick your favorite 4 cuts yourself. Some places have simple retouch options like brightness and filters. (There's a time limit, so dithering too long can auto-advance.)

5. Instant print + QR: 2 paper strips print out, and the QR code that comes with them — scan it with your phone to get the original digital files and a short clip (gif). This QR matters most to foreigners — you can leave the prints in Korea, but the digital files are kept forever and shared on SNS.

Tip: paper prints fade over time. Be sure to grab the digital files via QR so the originals survive even after your trip ends.

④ Why the Craze — MZ, Memory-Keeping, SNS, Dating

By form it's "the revival of the old photo booth," so why exactly did it explode in Korea right now? It's not a simple fad — several sentiments overlapped.

  • The identity of a "recording generation": Experts see the MZ generation as one that records itself and expresses its identity through photos. The four-cut photo is the lightest way to keep that moment as a physical print — cheaply, instantly.
  • The paradox of "memories you can hold": In an all-digital era, the analog feel of something printed on paper and stuck in a wallet, diary, or fridge feels fresh instead. The charm is getting both the digital (QR) and the analog (print).
  • The ease of no self-consciousness: Unlike a studio where a photographer shoots you, with no one watching your expression comes out natural. Even a goofy pose feels low-pressure. It's an angle that "looks like someone shot it for you" — but you control it.
  • SNS sharing culture: Post the QR file to Instagram or Stories and the shop, frame, and concept become content, and that post in turn pulls more people to the shop.
  • A cheap date/meetup course: An activity you can do together for a few thousand won in the gap between a café and a meal. It's become a "ritual of relationships" lightly slotted into dates, friend meetups, and after-parties.

⑤ The K-Pop Fandom Connection — Poca, Top-Kku, Idol-Collab Frames

For foreign K-pop fans, the four-cut photo is more than a simple proof-shot. It's directly interlocked with fandom culture.

  • Idol-collab frames: Brands like Photoism (포토이즘) often drop frames in collaboration with idols and idol groups. Special frames are released around comebacks, debut anniversaries, and member birthdays (birthday frames usually run about a month), and fans gather to shoot with them. The shop itself becomes a kind of fan experience site (팬 성지).
  • The link to poca (photo card) culture: K-pop albums include a member photocard (poca, 포토카드/포카). Fans put the poca in a top-loader (a clear protective case, 탑로더) and decorate it with stickers, ribbons, and beads ("top-kku / poca-kkumigi," 탑꾸/포카꾸미기), and they'll shoot a proof-shot of their own four-cut photo placed side by side with their bias's poca.
  • Rituals like the "yejeol-shot": Just as fans gathering for a meal stand a poca next to the food for a "yejeol-shot" (etiquette shot, 예절샷), the four-cut photo becomes a ritual of friendship and belonging — fans shoot the same frame and split it among themselves.

If you're a foreign fan: search ahead for whether your favorite group's collab/birthday frame is live, and you can snag a limited frame in Korea "shootable only that season." (Collabs and periods change constantly, so check before you go.)

⑥ A Foreigner's Practical Guide — How, Where, How Much

Prices, operations, and brands differ by timing and shop and change often — the below is for reference, check before you go.

  • How-to summary: ① pick language at the kiosk (many shops support English / Japanese / Chinese) → ② choose frame & quantity + pay by card → ③ put on props (free) → ④ shoot in the booth, about 1 minute, 8–10 shots (many shops allow re-shoots) → ⑤ choose 4 cuts + light retouch → ⑥ 2 paper prints + get digital files & clip via QR.
  • Group size: 2–4 people fits a frame's composition best. Solo (selfie concept) and large groups work too, but the 4-cut frame is optimal for small groups.
  • Where: Hongdae (홍대) is the densest (brands and indie shops on every alley). Seongsu (성수) has a trendy, editorial mood; Gangnam (강남) and Sinchon (신촌) are easy to reach from central lodgings; Hapjeong (합정) and Yeonnam (연남) lean a bit more design-forward. (For specific shop picks, see the place guide → not a cross-link, separate.)
  • How much: A basic 2 strips is about ₩4,000–5,000 (KRW), and special/collab frames commonly run ₩5,000+ (varies by brand/frame, check before you go). Card payment is almost always available.
  • Crowd tip: Hongdae (홍대) on a Friday/Saturday evening can mean a 20-minute-plus wait at popular shops. Avoid the busy hours or go a bit early for some breathing room.
  • Don't forget: Grab the digital files via QR. Paper fades and is easy to lose, but the digital originals are kept forever and shared on SNS. The best value for a travel proof-shot.

❓ Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

  • There's no staff — can I shoot in English? — Yes. Major-brand kiosks support English, Japanese, and Chinese guidance and take card payment. Being unmanned, you actually shoot more comfortably, free of self-consciousness. (Supported languages vary by shop, so check the language option on the screen's first step.)
  • How do I get the digital files? — A QR code comes printed alongside the paper. Scan it with your phone to download the original photos + a short clip. This is the most important step for foreigners — leave the paper behind, but the files stay.
  • Do I have to pay for the props? — Usually they're free. Use the headbands, sunglasses, wigs, and hats beside/inside the booth all you like. Pinning down a concept makes the photos come alive.
  • Can I go alone? — You can. That said, the 4-cut frame composition looks best with 2–4 people. Solo means a selfie concept; a group means the fun of squeezing in close.
  • How long does it take? — Payment, shooting, selecting, and printing usually take 5–10 minutes. But choosing the 4 cuts has a time limit, so dithering too long can auto-advance.
  • I heard the photo colors fade? — Instant print paper lightens over time. That's why grabbing the QR digital file is important.
