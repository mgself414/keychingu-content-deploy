🎯 Why This Deserves Its Own Guide

Korean street food is the #1 category on every international visitor's itinerary — it shows up at every tourist spot, from Gwangjang Market (광장시장) to Myeongdong (명동), Hongdae (홍대), Dongdaemun (동대문), and Itaewon (이태원). Before a full Korean meal, it's the top choice for a light bite, a snack, or just the experience itself. Netflix shows and YouTube vloggers feature it constantly, so international awareness is already high.

⚠ 3 Common Mistakes International Visitors Make

1. Ignoring hygiene — risk of an upset stomach

Hygiene standards vary stall to stall. Pick stalls with a visible turnover — a line is a good sign. Hand-washing isn't always easy, so bring wet wipes. If your stomach is sensitive, stick to hot dishes and fried food.

2. Not enough cash — payment fails

Most stalls are cash only. Withdraw ₩20,000–30,000 beforehand at an ATM near the market or at a convenience store.

3. Not checking spice levels ahead of time

Tteokbokki, dakgangjeong, sundae, and other Korean spicy dishes run very hot by international standards. Ask ahead — in Korean, "Maepji anheun geo isseoyo?" (맵지 않은 거 있어요?), or in English, "Not spicy?"

4. Hongdae (홍대) — Dak-kkochi, bungeoppang, and egg bread on the night streets

5. Dongdaemun (동대문) — Late-night tteokbokki and skewers alongside night shopping

⚙ FAQ

Q1. Which stalls are foreigner-friendly?

A. Gwangjang and Mangwon markets are mostly Korean-only, but pointing at photos or the display works fine. Myeongdong and Hongdae stalls are more likely to have English-speaking vendors.

Q2. Are there vegetarian or vegan options?

A. Tteokbokki, twigim, bungeoppang, egg bread, and hotteok can be vegetarian. Skip dak-kkochi and pork skewers. Yakgwa and traditional sweets are vegan-friendly.

Q3. What about payment and pricing?

A. Mostly cash. Withdraw ₩20K–30K beforehand. Larger stalls in Myeongdong and Hongdae take cards. Average item price is ₩2K–6K — even tasting 5 items stays under ₩30K.

Q4. What Korean phrases do I need?

A. "Igeo juseyo" (this one, please) / "Maepji anheun geo isseoyo?" (is there a non-spicy option?) / "Pojanghae juseyo" (to go) / "Multi-syu juseyo" (wet wipes, please).

Q5. What's the best season or time to go?

A. Spring and fall are best. In summer (July–August), daytime stalls can be uncomfortable — go in the evening instead. Winter (December–February) is prime season for hot hotteok, bungeoppang, and eomuk. Night hours (7–10 PM) are when Hongdae and Dongdaemun feel most like a night market.
