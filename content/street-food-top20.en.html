27_Street Food Top 20

One-Line Intro

Seoul street food ranked: the 20 must-eat items on every visitor's list.

Seoul's top 20 street foods — where to find them, how much they cost, and how to eat them like a local.

Who This Is For

  • First-time visitors to Korea
  • Travelers who want to experience a wide variety of Korean food on a budget

Recommended Route (2–3 Hours)

1. Myeongdong (명동) Street (40 min): Corn dogs, dak-kkochi (닭꼬치), and egg bread

2. Gwangjang Market (광장시장) (40 min): Mayak gimbap (마약김밥), sundae (순대), and eomuk (어묵)

3. Namdaemun Market (남대문시장) (30 min): Hotteok (호떡) and traditional snacks

4. Convenience Store Wrap-Up (10 min): Triangle gimbap (삼각김밥) and banana milk

Tips for International Visitors

  • Useful Korean Phrases
  • "Hana juseyo (하나 주세요)" = One, please
  • "Maepji anheun geo isseoyo? (맵지 않은 거 있어요?)" = Is there a non-spicy option?
  • "Pojanghae juseyo (포장해주세요)" = To go, please
  • Cultural Note: Street food is typically eaten standing or on a nearby bench. Dispose of trash in the bins provided nearby.
  • Payment: Most stalls are cash only. Keep small bills of ₩1,000–5,000 on hand.
  • Seasonal Items: Bungeoppang (붕어빵), roasted sweet potato (군고구마), and hotteok (호떡) are available in winter only (November–March).

Simple English Summary

Seoul's street food scene is one of Asia's best. The must-try items are tteokbokki (떡볶이, spicy rice cakes, ₩3,000), Korean corn dogs stuffed with cheese (₩3,000), and mayak gimbap (마약김밥) at Gwangjang Market (광장시장) (₩3,000). Most street food stalls operate on cash only — keep ₩10,000–20,000 in small bills handy. Myeongdong (명동) and Hongdae (홍대) have the highest concentration of street food vendors, with stalls open from noon until midnight.

Estimated Total Cost

  • Budget: ₩10,000 (small tastes of 5–6 items)
  • Standard: ₩25,000 (variety of 10 items)
  • Splurge: ₩50,000 (full list + makgeolli (막걸리) + café stop)
