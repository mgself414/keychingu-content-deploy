Locals' Picks ④ Jeonbuk — 13 Eateries Korean Viewers Named in the Comments, and Not All of Them Are in Jeonju

Subtitle: An axis untouched by Hanok Village (#255), the KTX 1-night-2-day trip (#1107), and the alleys outside Hanok Village (#1367) — from a crowdsourced list of regional eateries Korean viewers recommended in YouTube comments, only the 13 in Jeonbuk were picked, their existence, address, and business type checked on Naver Local Search, with no evaluation. Jeonju & Wanju 7 → Iksan 2 → Gunsan 4, with station/terminal access and one foreign-visitor tip per city. Iksan and Gunsan are cities KeyChinGu has barely covered.

ISSUE 06 · #1385 · 2026-09-18 · Locals' Picks ④

🎯 Why 'Comment Recommendations' Is the Bar

This corner has one source. Viewers left comments naming their neighborhood's eateries under a Korean YouTube channel's 'nationwide matjip' video, and the most systematically organized set of those comments, sorted by region into a table, covers 217 places. It's a list Koreans recommended to Koreans, so foreign reviews are almost nonexistent, and it's heavy on small-town restaurants that never make tourist lists. The corner's criteria and limits are laid out in the ① installment (#1382).

KeyChinGu checked exactly one thing on Naver Local Search — whether the name, address, and business type actually exist (2026-09-17). 13 of Jeonbuk's 15 places were confirmed, and the 2 that couldn't be (1 each in Gunsan and Jeonju) were dropped. There's no visit review, ranking, or star rating, and no hours or prices either — that's what Naver Place is for, on the day you go.

What defines the Jeonbuk installment is the world outside Hanok Village. KeyChinGu's Jeonbuk coverage has clustered around Jeonju's Hanok Village (한옥마을) and bibimbap until now (most of 11 pieces), but the places viewers wrote in were neighborhoods like Jeonju's Deokjin-gu (덕진구) and Pyeonghwa-dong (평화동), plus Iksan and Gunsan. Iksan is a transfer-station city where the Honam Line and Jeolla Line split, and Gunsan is a city where two Chinese restaurants landed side by side on the list.

① Jeonju & Wanju — 7 Places, the Jeonju Beyond Hanok Village

What foreign visitors see in Jeonju is Hanok Village and Nambu Market (남부시장) (#255·#642·#1107). The seven places viewers wrote in sit outside that — Deokjin-gu, the Jeonju Station/Jeonbuk National University (전북대) area, Wansan-gu's Pyeonghwa-dong and Seobu Sinsigaji, and Wanju-gun to Jeonju's northeast. Bus 79, a sightseeing route, connects Jeonju Station and Hanok Village.

  • Dugeori Usintang (두거리우신탕) · 1106 Dongbu-daero, Deokjin-gu, Jeonju-si (전주시 덕진구 동부대로 1106) · Korean · jjigae·jeongol (찌개·전골) · On the Dongbu-daero axis where Jeonju Station sits — check the stop and walking time on Naver Map

Naver's category is jjigae·jeongol (stew and hot pot). It sits on the road into downtown from Jeonju Station, so it works as a first meal on arrival day.

  • Maisan Pi Sundae (마이산피순대) · 8 Dugan 10-gil, Deokjin-gu (덕진구 두간10길 8) · Korean · sundae·sundaetguk (순대·순댓국) · Dugan-dong, Deokjin-gu — access: check Naver Map

Pi sundae (피순대) is a blood sausage packed generously with blood, giving it a reddish color — a common style in Jeonju. Sundaetguk can be ordered for one.

  • Geumam Myeonok (금암면옥) · 400-3 Girin-daero, Deokjin-gu (덕진구 기린대로 400-3) · Korean · kalguksu·mandu (칼국수·만두) · Geumam-dong, near Jeonbuk National University — check on Naver Map

Myeonok (면옥) is an old-fashioned name for a noodle/naengmyeon shop. Naver's category is kalguksu·mandu.

  • Baeksong Hoegwan (백송회관) · 177 Girin-daero, Wansan-gu (완산구 기린대로 177) · Korean · bibimbap (비빔밥) · Same Girin-daero axis as Hanok Village (기린대로 99) — check the walking time on Naver Map

A shop serving Jeonju bibimbap. The differences among the five bibimbap variations are laid out in #879. It's the only one on this list that slots directly into a Hanok Village itinerary.

  • Seito (세이토) · 13 Majeon-jungang-ro, Wansan-gu (완산구 마전중앙로 13) · Japanese · udon·soba (우동·소바) · Seobu Sinsigaji — check on Naver Map

A udon shop in Seobu Sinsigaji, Jeonju's newer commercial district. It made the list as an option for when Korean food gets old.

  • Tobang (토방) · 19 Pyeonghwa 18-gil, Wansan-gu (완산구 평화18길 19) · Restaurant · Korean · Pyeonghwa-dong residential streets — check on Naver Map

A Korean restaurant in a residential area completely off any tourist route. A taxi is the realistic way to get there.

  • Jongnam Sanjang (종남산장) · 429 Songgwangsuman-ro, Soyang-myeon, Wanju-gun (완주군 소양면 송광수만로 429) · Restaurant · Korean · City bus or taxi from Jeonju toward Soyang-myeon — check the bus number (only confirmed: Jeonju city buses do reach Soyang-myeon)

A mountain-lodge-style restaurant in Soyang-myeon, Wanju-gun, northeast of Jeonju, on the road to Songgwangsa (송광사). The Jongnamsan (종남산) in the name is the mountain in this area.

Foreign-visitor tip — In Jeonju, bibimbap, sundaetguk, and kalguksu can be ordered for one; hanjeongsik (Korean table d'hôte) defaults to 2 or more. Bus 79 runs between Jeonju Station and Hanok Village, so use it on arrival day if you're carrying luggage.

② Iksan — 2 Places, the City Where the Honam and Jeolla Lines Split

Iksan Station is a junction where the Honam High-Speed Rail, Honam Line, Jeolla Line, and Janghang Line all meet. Coming down from Seoul, this is where the route splits — toward Jeonju on the Jeolla Line, or toward Mokpo and Gwangju on the Honam Line. That makes Iksan easy to treat as a city you just pass through, but the two places on this list sit on the same Gobong-ro axis.

  • Byeolmijip (별미집) · 176-1 Gobong-ro, Iksan-si (익산시 고봉로 176-1) · Restaurant · Korean · Bus or taxi from Iksan Station — check the stop on Naver Map
  • Manpo Udon (만포우동) · 44 Gobong-ro 34-gil, Iksan-si (익산시 고봉로34길 44) · Japanese · udon·soba · Same Gobong-ro axis — check on Naver Map

A Korean restaurant and an udon shop sit in the same neighborhood. If you've got an hour of transfer time, check the walking distance on Naver Map first to see whether it's worth stepping outside the station.

Foreign-visitor tip — Iksan Station is a transfer hub, so luggage storage matters. Use the station lockers and go out light. Transferring between the Jeolla Line and Honam Line is covered in #1374 (Three Ticket Types).

③ Gunsan — 4 Places, Where the Terminal Beats the Station for Downtown Access

There's one thing that trips up foreign visitors in Gunsan. Gunsan Station moved to the city's outskirts (Naeheung-dong) in 2008 when the Janghang Line was relocated, and it's now a 20–30 minute bus ride to downtown. The Gunsan Intercity Bus Terminal, on the other hand, sits at 18 Haemang-ro, on the old downtown side of Gyeongam-dong. Two of the four places below sit in that terminal's area.

  • Gayajang (가야장) · 4 Gyeongam 4-gil, Gunsan-si (군산시 경암4길 4) · Chinese · Chinese restaurant · Gyeongam-dong, where the intercity bus terminal is
  • Yeonghwawon (영화원) · 112 Guyeong 5-gil, Gunsan-si (군산시 구영5길 112) · Chinese · Chinese restaurant · Old downtown, around Guyeong-gil

Gunsan is known as a city with a deep Chinese-restaurant scene that grew out of its Chinese merchant community, and two Chinese restaurants landed side by side on this list. Jjajangmyeon, jjamppong, and tangsuyuk can all be ordered for one.

  • Gunsan Jeonseol Sutbul Galbi (군산전설숯불갈비) · 9 Seosusong-an 2-gil, Gunsan-si (군산시 서수송안2길 9) · Korean · meat dishes (육류·고기요리) · Susong-dong, the newer district — about 15 min by bus from Gunsan Station to the new district
  • Myeongsan Yangpun Wang Galbi (명산양푼왕갈비) · 5 Geoseok-gil, Gunsan-si (군산시 거석길 5) · Korean · meat dishes · Downtown Gunsan — check on Naver Map

Two galbi places. Both sutbul galbi (charcoal-grilled ribs) and yangpun galbi (basin galbi) default to a 2-person minimum.

Foreign-visitor tip — It's easier to plan a Gunsan itinerary around the terminal and old downtown, not the station. Modern History Street (근대문화거리) and the Gyeongam-dong Railroad Village (경암동 철길마을) sit in that same area, and a Gunsan 1-night-2-day trip is in #1108.

🚄 How to Fold This Into a Regional Route

  • Working it into the Jeonju 1-night-2-day trip (#1107) — Baeksong Hoegwan sits on the same Girin-daero axis as Hanok Village; the other six Jeonju places are in Deokjin-gu, Pyeonghwa-dong, and Seobu Sinsigaji, so plan on one taxi ride.
  • Iksan = a transfer station — Where the Jeolla Line (toward Jeonju) and the Honam Line (toward Gwangju/Mokpo) split from Seoul. If your itinerary has you passing through, the two Gobong-ro places are an out-of-station option.
  • Gunsan (#1108) — Plan around the intercity bus terminal and old downtown. Jeonju↔Gunsan intercity buses run 7 times a day, and Iksan↔Gunsan is the most frequent, at 8 times a day.
  • Wanju's Jongnam Sanjang — Toward Soyang-myeon from downtown Jeonju. The bus number couldn't be confirmed, so plan on a taxi by default.
  • Continuing south into Honam — Head down from Iksan on the Honam Line and you're in Gwangju or Mokpo. That area is covered in the ⑦ installment (#1388).

⚠ 3 Common Mistakes

1. Trying to walk from Gunsan Station to old downtown — The station is on the outskirts, a 20–30 minute bus ride away. The terminal side is the one closer to downtown.

2. Bundling the six Jeonju places into a Hanok Village walking course — Aside from Baeksong Hoegwan, they're in Deokjin-gu and Pyeonghwa-dong. Plan on a taxi.

3. Setting off for Wanju's Jongnam Sanjang assuming a bus works — Only that Jeonju city buses reach Soyang-myeon has been confirmed. Check the number and schedule the same day.

⚙ FAQ 3

1. Who picked this list? — It's a table built from regional eateries Korean YouTube viewers named in the comments. KeyChinGu only confirmed that they exist, plus their address and business type — it didn't rank them.

2. Which Jeonju bibimbap place is the 'right' one? — This piece doesn't rank anything. The only bibimbap place on the list is Baeksong Hoegwan, and the differences among bibimbap types are in #879 and #881.

3. Can I visit just Iksan or just Gunsan on their own? — Iksan works as a stop along the way since it's a transfer station; Gunsan is better as an overnight (#1108). The intercity bus between them is the most frequent option, at 8 times a day.
