🎯 How to Use This Guide

This guide gathers 4 markets and alleys, 2 film and fashion streets, and 3 parks, observatories and staircases in the old downtown of Jung-gu, Busan, plus Yeongdodaegyo Bridge, which leads across the water to Yeongdo. Gukje Market, Mukja Alley and Bupyeong Kkangtong Market have hours registered, and most of the streets and parks are registered with no set hours.

The operating details are copied from values registered in the Korea Tourism Organization tourism data (TourAPI). Busan Tower admission fees and food prices in Mukja Alley are not in the Korea Tourism Organization data, so they are not listed.

📍 Gukje Market Area — Gukje Market, Mukja Alley, Bupyeong Kkangtong Market, Bosu-dong Book Street

Gukje Market (Junggu-ro B31, Sinchang-dong 4-ga, Jung-gu, ☎ 051-245-7389 Gukje Market Merchants Association) began, according to the Korea Tourism Organization, as a marketplace after liberation in 1945 where goods left behind by the Japanese and goods brought by overseas Koreans were traded. It grew as refugees did business there after the Korean War, was first called "Dotteogi Market", then "Jayu (Freedom) Market" after buildings went up in 1948, and became Gukje Market when it began handling goods from US military bases too. Today it has a second-hand clothing alley, a patbingsu alley, a cosmetics alley, Mukja Alley and a small goods alley.

  • Opening hours: 09:00~20:00 (varies by shop). Closed days: Every Sunday (varies by shop).
  • Finding your way: It is made up of 12 buildings, so it can feel like a maze at first. According to the Korea Tourism Organization, you can check where you are on the 2nd-floor footbridge that connects the 12 buildings.
  • Parking: Possible. Restrooms: Available.

Gukje Market Mukja Alley (Junggu-ro 36, Changseon-dong, Jung-gu) is an outdoor food alley that opened in May 1950. According to the Korea Tourism Organization, you sit on stall chairs and eat Chungmu gimbap (충무김밥) with seasoned squid, tteokbokki (떡볶이), mul-guksu (물국수) and sundae (순대), Busan's well-known bibim dangmyeon (비빔당면), patbingsu (팥빙수) and more.

  • Opening hours: 09:00~20:00 (varies by shop). Closed days: Every Sunday.
  • Parking: Possible. Strollers: Not allowed.
  • Dietary note: Ingredient information for each menu item is not registered.

Bupyeong Kkangtong Market (Bupyeong 1-gil 48, Bupyeong-dong 2-ga, Jung-gu, ☎ 0507-1416-1131) is, according to the Korea Tourism Organization, a market that formed when US military supplies and imported goods circulated after the 6·25 War. It is said to have been named "Kkangtong (can) Market" because many canned goods came in. Today it sells foreign liquor, clothing, decorations, accessories and some foreign-made electronics. Along a market street of about 110 m there are 11 local food stalls, 6 multicultural food stalls such as Japanese and Filipino, and 13 clothing and accessory stalls, and magic and guitar performances are held twice a day at the market entrance and the inner crossroads.

  • Opening hours: Market 08:00~20:00, night market 19:30~23:30 (varies by shop). Closed days: Varies by shop.
  • Parking: Possible. Restrooms: Available.

Bosu-dong Book Street (Daecheong-ro 67-1, Jung-gu, ☎ 051-600-6376 Bosu-dong Book Street Culture Center) began as a street stall where a couple who had fled to Busan, the temporary capital, during the 6·25 War spread out boxes and sold used books and magazines. According to the Korea Tourism Organization, it is the oldest used bookstore street in Korea, and each shop has its own specialty, from old books, novels, reference books and comics to children's books and foreign originals. It has a book street culture center and a children's library, and the Bosu-dong Book Street Culture Festival is held every autumn.

  • Opening hours and closed days: Vary by facility. Parking: Not possible.

📍 Film and Fashion Streets — BIFF Square, Gwangbok-ro Fashion Street

BIFF Square (Gudeok-ro B44, Nampo-dong 5-ga, Jung-gu, ☎ 1688-3010 Busan International Film Festival) was Nampo-dong's theater district, where more than 20 theaters gathered in the 1960s. According to the Korea Tourism Organization, when the 1st Busan International Film Festival was held on September 13, 1996, the area was named BIFF Square, and the festival's eve event and a handprinting event for film people were held there. After Busan Cinema Center (영화의전당) opened in Haeundae, the opening ceremony and other main events moved there, but traces of the early days remain and films are still shown.

  • Opening hours: Vary by shop. Closed days: Open every day of the year.
  • Parking: Not possible.

Gwangbok-ro Fashion Street (Gwangbok-ro 72-1, Gwangbok-dong 2-ga, Jung-gu, ☎ 051-254-5504 Busan Jung-gu Tourist Information Center) is a street with clothing shops on both sides of a road built over a covered stream. According to the Korea Tourism Organization, it runs from the Nonghyup Busan branch to the entrance of Gukje Market Mukja Alley, and is especially crowded at night and on weekends. Every year from December, the Jung-gu Office holds the Gwangbok-ro Winter Light Tree Festival, and during the festival the Gwangbok-ro area becomes a car-free street.

  • Opening hours: No set hours. Closed days: Open every day of the year.
  • Parking: Not possible.

📍 Yongdusan and the Old Downtown Hills — Yongdusan Park, Busan Tower, 40 Steps

Yongdusan Park (Yongdusan-gil 37-55, Jung-gu, ☎ 051-860-7820) is, according to the Korea Tourism Organization, a park on Yongdusan, one of Busan's 3 great mountains. It got its name because the shape of the mountain looks like a dragon, and it has the Busan Tower observatory, a statue of Admiral Yi Sun-sin, a bell pavilion, a flower clock, a dragon-shaped sculpture and more. An escalator runs from Nampo-dong to the park, and from the park you can look down on Busan Port and Yeongdo.

  • Opening hours: No set hours. Closed days: Open every day of the year.
  • Parking: Possible.

Busan Tower (Yongdusan-gil 37-30, Gwangbok-dong 2-ga, Jung-gu, ☎ 051-601-1800) is an observatory at the highest point of Yongdusan Park. According to the Korea Tourism Organization, you can see the night view of Busan from Busan Port out to Namhang in the distance. Sundries and other services are registered as sale items.

  • Opening hours: 10:00~22:00. Closed days: Not listed. Parking: Not possible. Cards: Accepted. Restrooms: Available.
  • Sale item prices (registered values): Masks 8,000~10,000 won, key rings 2,000~10,000 won, traditional dolls 8,000~30,000 won, mother-of-pearl fans and business card holders 5,000~15,000 won.

40 Steps Culture and Tourism Theme Street (Jungang-daero 73, Jungang-dong 4-ga, Jung-gu, ☎ 051-600-4046 Busan Jung-gu Office Culture and Tourism Division) is, according to the Korea Tourism Organization, a staircase that refugees climbed up and down with difficulty during the Korean War. Climbing the stairs, you can see an old streetscape, the 40 Steps Culture Center and various exhibits. It is also known as a filming location for films and dramas.

  • Opening hours: No set hours. Closed days: Open every day of the year.
  • Parking: Not possible.

📍 Across the Water — Yeongdodaegyo Bridge

Yeongdodaegyo Bridge (Daegyo-dong 1-ga, Yeongdo-gu, ☎ 051-419-4064 Yeongdo-gu Office Culture and Tourism Division) is a bridge connecting Yeongdo and mainland Busan. According to the Korea Tourism Organization, its biggest feature is a structure that opens at set times so that large ships can pass, and at night the bridge and its surroundings glow with lights. Crossing the bridge, you can see the sea view of Busan Port.

  • Opening hours: No set hours. Closed days: Open every day of the year.
  • Parking: Not possible.
  • The times when the bridge opens are not registered in the Korea Tourism Organization data.

⚠ 3 Things to Know Before You Go

  1. Gukje Market and Mukja Alley are closed on Sundays — both are registered as closed every Sunday (varies by shop). Bupyeong Kkangtong Market varies by shop.
  2. Closing times differ by place — Gukje Market is registered until 20:00, Busan Tower until 22:00 and the Bupyeong Kkangtong night market until 23:30.
  3. Many places have no parking — Bosu-dong Book Street, BIFF Square, Gwangbok-ro Fashion Street, Busan Tower, the 40 Steps and Yeongdodaegyo Bridge are registered as no parking.

⚙ FAQ 2

Q. Which places can I go to in the evening?

A. According to Korea Tourism Organization registered information, the Bupyeong Kkangtong Market night market is open 19:30~23:30 and Busan Tower until 22:00. According to the Korea Tourism Organization, Gwangbok-ro Fashion Street is especially crowded at night and on weekends.

Q. Which places are open on Sundays?

A. Gukje Market and Mukja Alley are closed on Sundays. BIFF Square, Gwangbok-ro Fashion Street, Yongdusan Park, the 40 Steps and Yeongdodaegyo Bridge are registered as open every day of the year. Busan Tower has no closed days registered.
