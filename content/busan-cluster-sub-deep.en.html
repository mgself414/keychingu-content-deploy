⚠️ 3 Common Mistakes Foreigners Make

Mistake 1 — Confusing BIFF season with BIFF Square

BIFF Square (the street in Nampo-dong) is open free, year-round. The handprint monuments and outdoor exhibits can be seen even outside the festival period (about 10 days in early October each year). Many foreigners mistakenly assume it's only worth a visit in October and skip it the rest of the year.

Mistake 2 — Climbing the stairs without knowing about the Yongdusan Park escalator

There's a free escalator at the entrance to Yongdusan Park, on the Gwangbok-dong (광복동) side. Many tourists don't know about it and climb the steep stairs instead. The escalator entrance is underground on the Gwangbokro side, toward Lotte Department Store's Gwangbok branch.

Mistake 3 — Visiting Gukje Market on a Monday

Some Gukje Market shops are closed on Mondays or open only after noon. Most eateries in the Gukje Market food alley operate Tuesday through Sunday. On a Monday visit, the ssiat-hotteok alley stays open, but the rest of the market runs at reduced capacity.

Axis 1: Gukje Market (3 Picks) — Authentic Since 1948

1. Gukje Market Main Street

Location: Sinchang-dong 4-ga, Jung-gu, Busan / 7-minute walk from Jagalchi Station (Line 1) Exit 7, or 5-minute walk from Nampo Station Exit 5.

Free/Paid: Free entry. Pay only for shopping and food.

Foreigner fit: Formed by refugees in 1948 after the Korean War, the market is Busan's history itself. Clothing, household goods, accessories, and souvenirs mix together, and each alley has its own mood. English signage is posted, and most shops accept cards.

Access & route: Nampo Station Exit 5 → Gwangbokro → Gukje Market entrance (5-minute walk). Or Jagalchi Station Exit 7 → 7-minute walk. The market is laid out in a grid of alleys, so it's hard to get lost.

Timing: Liveliest from 10am to 6pm. About 80% of shops close after 7pm. The food alley stays open into the evening.

BIFF season link: During BIFF in October, film-related pop-up booths and outdoor screenings appear around Gukje Market. Some merchants extend their hours at night to catch the festival crowd.

Practical tip: Gukje Market's nickname is "dotteogi sijang" — a chaotic bargain market. Bargaining is possible wherever there's no price tag. "How much?" works fine in English.

2. Gukje Market Food Alley (Ssiat-Hotteok & Eomuk)

Location: The alley near Gate 5 inside Gukje Market / the eomuk alley is at the entrance to Gwangbok-dong.

Free/Paid: One ssiat-hotteok costs about 1,500 won; 1–2 skewers of eomuk cost 1,000–2,000 won.

Foreigner fit: Busan's two signature street foods are here. Ssiat-hotteok (씨앗호떡) — a sweet pancake packed with sunflower seeds, peanuts, and other nuts — tastes different from Seoul-style hotteok. Busan-style eomuk (어묵, fish cake) is the real deal, and many stalls serve it with free broth on the side. A long line marks a good shop.

Access: Walk 50m straight from the main gate of Gukje Market (the Sinchang-dong 4-ga entrance) to find the ssiat-hotteok stalls.

Timing: 11am to 9pm. Lines are longest right after lunch, 1–3pm.

Gluten/allergies: Eomuk contains wheat flour — caution for gluten sensitivity. Ssiat-hotteok may trigger nut allergies.

3. Gukje Market 1948 Street (Historical Walk Zone)

Location: The historical signage zone at the center of Gukje Market, marked "1948 Street" on the market's own map.

Free/Paid: Free. Signage and mural walk.

Foreigner fit: A storytelling layer tied to Korean War (1950–1953) refugee history. Signage is posted in English, Japanese, and Chinese, so a self-guided walk works well. Especially suited to foreigners interested in Korean modern history — notably Japanese and American visitors.

Timing: Daytime visits are better for reading the signage.

Axis 2: BIFF & Film (3 Picks)

4. BIFF Square (Busan International Film Festival Square)

Location: Nampo-dong 5-ga, Jung-gu, Busan / 2-minute walk from Nampo Station (Line 1) Exit 6.

Free/Paid: The outdoor square is free. Screening programs during the festival period require paid tickets (purchased separately).

Foreigner fit: BIFF (Busan International Film Festival), one of Asia's largest film festivals, has been held in this district since 1996. The square's pavement holds handprint monuments of more than 100 Korean, Asian, and international filmmakers. These can be seen year-round, not just during the festival. Film fans can enjoy hunting for a favorite director's or actor's handprint.

Access: BIFF Square's sign is visible right outside Nampo Station Exit 6 — within a 2-minute walk.

Timing: Outdoor viewing is possible year-round. Night lighting makes handprint viewing possible after dark too. Crowds surge during the October BIFF season, so an early-morning visit is recommended.

BIFF season (October) notes: Held for about 10 days in early October each year. More than 300 domestic and international films screen, alongside red-carpet events, outdoor screenings, and GV (guest visit / Q&A) sessions with well-known directors and actors. Tickets must be booked online in advance (official site: biff.kr). On-site ticket sales sell out early.

Practical tip: The Nampo-dong fashion street near BIFF Square is a common spot to catch sight of directors and actors during the festival. Cafes and restaurants there extend their hours to midnight during BIFF.

5. BIFF Street & the Nampo-dong Film Culture Street

Location: Nampo-dong 5-ga and 6-ga around BIFF Square — a 300m stretch running from the square toward Jagalchi.

Free/Paid: Free to explore.

Foreigner fit: A street lined with Korean film poster murals, film-related sculptures, and independent cinema signage. Especially meaningful for foreigners interested in Korean film — Parasite, Oldboy, Train to Busan, and more. Nampo-dong has two or three small independent theaters that occasionally run programs with English subtitles.

Timing: Good for a daytime stroll; the lit-up mood at night is worth seeing too.

6. Busan Film Commission & Filming Location Tour

Location: The Busan Film Commission's headquarters is in Haeundae-gu, Busan, but its filming locations are scattered across Nampo-dong and Jung-gu.

Foreigner fit: Busan is a major filming location for Korean films such as Train to Busan and The Roundup. The Film Commission's website (bfc.or.kr) provides a free location map, including an English version, so film fans can build a self-guided location tour. Locations cluster around Nampo-dong, Yeongdo, and Jagalchi.

Practical tip: No guide is needed for the location tour — download the English filming-location map PDF ahead of time and walk it yourself.

Axis 3: Yongdusan & Busan Tower (3 Picks)

7. Yongdusan Park

Location: Gwangbok-dong 2-ga, Jung-gu, Busan / 5-minute walk from Nampo Station (Line 1) Exit 7, or take the escalator.

Free/Paid: The park is free, open 24 hours.

Foreigner fit: At 49m above sea level, this is the best vantage point over Busan's old downtown — Nampo-dong, Gwangbok-dong, Jagalchi, and Yongdusan itself. On a clear day, Yeongdo and Busan Port are visible in one sweep. The park holds a flower clock installed in 1983, a statue of Admiral Yi Sun-sin, and an octagonal pavilion. Suits both families and couples.

Access: Nampo Station Exit 7 → Gwangbokro → escalator entrance (underground). The escalator takes about 3 minutes to the summit; on foot it's about 10 minutes of stairs.

Timing: The night view (after 7pm) is the most beautiful. Spring (March–April) draws crowds for the blossoms, and October brings visitors linking their trip with BIFF Square. Early morning (6–8am) is quiet, with only local residents out for a walk.

Escalator location: The underground entrance is on the side toward Lotte Department Store's Gwangbok branch. Just follow the street-level signs.

8. Busan Tower

Location: At the summit of Yongdusan Park / 1-minute walk from where the Yongdusan Park escalator lets you off.

Free/Paid: Paid. Adults 12,000 won, teens 9,000 won, children 6,000 won (as of 2025). Card payment accepted.

Hours: 10:00–22:00, open year-round.

Foreigner fit: A 120m-tall observation tower with a 360-degree rotating view. It looks out over Busan's old downtown, Busan Port, and Yeongdo from every side. On clear days, Tsushima Island (Japan) is sometimes visible. Part of the observation floor is reinforced glass, letting you look straight down. Signage is complete in English, Japanese, and Chinese.

Recommended timing: Visit around sunset, 6–8pm, to catch both the daytime and night views. Entering just before the 10pm closing means a short visit, so enter at least an hour ahead.

BIFF season notes: During the October BIFF period, the tower's lighting sometimes switches to a festival theme.

9. Yongdusan Park Flower Clock & Instagram Spot

Location: The plaza at the summit of Yongdusan Park, next to Busan Tower.

Free/Paid: Free.

Foreigner fit: A large flower clock installed in 1983, set against a backdrop of Busan's old downtown — a strong photo spot. It's a fully working clock, and the flower design changes with the seasons. Especially popular with Japanese and Chinese tourists as an Instagram spot.

Timing: Daytime photos show the flowers and backdrop clearly; at night, the lighting gives it a different mood.

Axis 4: Nearby Cross (3 Picks)

10. Jagalchi Market (#590 cross)

Location: 10–15 minute walk from Yongdusan Park or BIFF Square / Jagalchi Station Exit 10.

Connection point: Busan's #1 seafood market, covered in detail in #590. The most efficient route is to visit Jagalchi for lunch or dinner after Gukje Market and BIFF Square. From Jagalchi Station, both Gukje Market (Exit 7, 7-minute walk) and BIFF Square (Nampo Station Exit 6, 5-minute walk) are just one subway stop away.

Practical tip: Jagalchi Market opens at 5am; the restaurants on Jagalchi's second floor start serving at 8am. The best order is: Jagalchi before lunch → Gukje Market and BIFF Square in the afternoon → Yongdusan night view in the evening.

11. Yeongdo Bridge & the Bridge-Lift Event

Location: 10-minute walk from Nampo-dong / on Yeongdo Bridge (영도대교).

Free/Paid: Free.

Foreigner fit: Yeongdo Bridge holds historical weight as a meeting point for refugees during the Korean War. Today, the bridge lifts every day at 2pm. For about 15 minutes the bridge opens, and you can watch boats pass through underneath. A familiar sight to locals, but a striking one for foreign visitors.

Timing: The lift happens exactly at 2:00pm. Arrive 10–15 minutes early for a good spot. The bridge's night lighting is also worth seeing.

12. Huinnyeoul Culture Village (Yeongdo)

Location: Yeongseon-dong, Yeongdo-gu, Busan / about 20 minutes by city bus 66 from Nampo-dong, or a 10-minute taxi ride.

Free/Paid: Free to explore the village.

Foreigner fit: A refugee village built on a cliff on Yeongdo (영도), known for its narrow alleys and sea views. It was also a filming location for the movie Nameless Gangster (범죄와의 전쟁). The village has small galleries, cafes, and craft studios, with English signage in place. It's less known than Gamcheon Culture Village but quieter, with a stronger local feel.

Timing: Best visited 10am–5pm. Some cafes and studios close before 6pm.

Practical Info (Transport, KTX, Language)

Getting to Busan

KTX: About 2 hours 15–40 minutes from Seoul Station or Suseo Station to Busan Station. As of September 1, 2026, the Suseo-departing SRT has also merged into KTX, so there's now just one app — Korail+. Fares start at 54,400 won for standard class (post-merger reduced rate). Book online at letskorail.com (English supported). Foreigners can purchase with a passport. See #1374 for the three-route breakdown.

Busan Station to Nampo-dong: Subway Line 1, Busan Station → Nampo Station, 4 stops, about 10 minutes. A single-ride fare is 1,500 won. T-money and Cashbee transit cards are sold at convenience stores.

Gimhae Airport to Nampo-dong: Light rail from Gimhae Airport Station → transfer at Sasang Station → Line 1 to Nampo Station. About 40 minutes, 2,400 won.

Getting Around Nampo-dong

The four spots in the Nampo-dong cluster — Gukje Market, BIFF Square, Yongdusan Park, Jagalchi — are all within a 15-minute walk of one another. By subway, Jagalchi Station (Exits 10 and 7) and Nampo Station (Exits 5, 6, and 7) are the hubs.

Jagalchi Station exits:

  • Exit 7 → Gukje Market (7-minute walk)
  • Exit 10 → Jagalchi Market (3-minute walk)

Nampo Station exits:

  • Exit 5 → Gukje Market (5-minute walk)
  • Exit 6 → BIFF Square (2-minute walk)
  • Exit 7 → Yongdusan Park escalator (5-minute walk)

Language Guide

Gukje Market, BIFF Square, and Busan Tower all have complete signage in English, Japanese, and Chinese. Busan Tower's interior runs automated announcements in three languages. Some Gukje Market merchants speak English or Japanese. If communication is difficult, use a translation app — Naver Papago or Google Translate.

Tourist interpretation hotline: 1330 (Korea Tourism Organization, 24h, English/Japanese/Chinese/Russian support).

Transit Cards & Payment

  • T-money card: Buy at Busan Station, subway stations, or convenience stores. Card fee 3,000 won. Once loaded, it works on both subway and bus.
  • Gukje Market shops: Most accept cards. Street stalls — ssiat-hotteok, eomuk — prefer cash; some accept QR payment.
  • Busan Tower: Both card and cash accepted.

FAQ 5

Q1. Is BIFF Square worth visiting outside the BIFF period?

Yes. BIFF Square is open free, outdoors, year-round. The more than 100 handprint monuments can be seen anytime, not just in October. In fact, the off-season lets you explore without the crowds.

Q2. How do you get into Yongdusan Park — is the escalator paid?

It's free. Walk 5 minutes along Gwangbokro from Nampo Station Exit 7 and you'll find the sign for the "Yongdusan Park Escalator" entrance. Head underground and ride it up to the park's summit.

Q3. Busan Tower vs. the Haeundae Centum observation deck — which is better?

For a view over Nampo-dong, Jagalchi, and BIFF Square, choose Busan Tower. For Haeundae and Gwangalli, choose the Haeundae Centum observation deck or Hwangnyeongsan. If you're covering the Nampo-dong cluster in a single day, Busan Tower is the pick.

Q4. What's a must-eat at Gukje Market?

Ssiat-hotteok (a nut-filled sweet pancake, 1,500 won) and eomuk skewers (1,000–2,000 won). Together for 3,000 won, that's a complete taste of Busan's signature street food.

Q5. What should a first-time visitor watch out for during October's BIFF season?

During the BIFF period (usually 10 days in early October), lodging prices around Nampo-dong, BIFF Square, and Jagalchi rise 2–3x, and crowds surge. Book accommodation at least 2–3 months ahead. BIFF ticket sales open on biff.kr 2–3 weeks before the opening, and popular screenings sell out within minutes.

Full-Day Courses 3

Course 1. Full Nampo-dong Set (1 Day)

Arrive at Jagalchi Station → Jagalchi Market (#10, 8–10am) → Gukje Market Food Alley: ssiat-hotteok and eomuk (#2, 10–11am) → Gukje Market Main Street walk (#1, 11am–12pm) → lunch at a Gukje Market Korean restaurant → BIFF Square handprint walk (#4, 1–2pm) → Yeongdo Bridge lift event (#11, exactly 2pm) → Yongdusan Park stroll and flower clock (#7, #9, 3–5pm) → Busan Tower sunset and night view (#8, 6–8pm)

Estimated cost: 1,500 won ssiat-hotteok + 2,000 won eomuk + 10,000–15,000 won lunch + 12,000 won Busan Tower + shopping optional.

Course 2. October BIFF Enthusiast Course

BIFF Square (#4, early morning) → BIFF Street walk (#5) → wait at a red-carpet viewing spot (afternoon) → evening outdoor screening. Shop at Gukje Market in between. Visit Busan Tower at night when it's running a BIFF lighting theme.

Advance BIFF ticket purchase (biff.kr) is essential. Very few tickets are sold on-site.

Course 3. Busan 1-Night Cluster

Day 1 morning: Jagalchi → Gukje Market → BIFF Square → Yongdusan night view. Day 2 morning: Yeongdo's Huinnyeoul Culture Village (#12) → Yeongdo Bridge lift event (#11) → afternoon at Haeundae and Gwangalli (#243 cross) or Gamcheon Culture Village (#245 cross).

5 Practical Tips for Foreigners

1. Jagalchi Station Exit 7 or Nampo Station Exits 5, 6, 7 — the two base camps for the Nampo-dong cluster.

2. Yongdusan Park escalator — free, so you don't have to climb the stairs. Just follow the underground entrance signs.

3. Busan Tower admission, 12,000 won — enter around 6pm to catch both sunset and the night view.

4. BIFF Square is free year-round — the handprint monuments can be walked even outside October.

5. Yeongdo Bridge lift at exactly 2pm — free, arrive 10 minutes early.

  • 112: Police
  • 119: Fire & ambulance

VAT refund: For purchases over 50,000 won at stores marked "Tax Refund," you can apply for a refund. Process it at the refund counter in the departure area of Incheon or Gimhae airport.
