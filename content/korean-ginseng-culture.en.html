Korean Ginseng & Red Ginseng — Why Koreans Are Serious About This Root, the Heart of "Boyang" Wellness

Korean ginseng (고려인삼) · susam (수삼)·baeksam (백삼)·hongsam (홍삼) · saponin (사포닌) & ginsenoside (진세노사이드) · yaksik-dongwon (약식동원) & boknal (복날) · Jeong Kwan Jang (정관장) & duty-free gifting — a Korean ginseng & red ginseng culture explainer for foreigners. Not "ginseng is good for you," but "why Koreans are so serious about this root, why there are so many kinds, and what a foreigner should actually buy and how."

Something shows up without fail in Korea's duty-free shops, souvenir stores, and K-dramas: a brown stick squeezed straight into the mouth, a red paste spooned from a jar, or a whole root tucked inside a chicken — insam (인삼, ginseng) and its processed form hongsam (홍삼, red ginseng). To Koreans, ginseng is more than a medicinal herb or health food; it is a symbol of "boyang (보양, restorative wellness) — the thing you reach for when you're worn out and your energy is low," a staple of holiday gifts, and an expression of jeong (정, affectionate care) handed to exam-takers, elders, and tired office workers.

This article is not about "which ginseng shop is best" (→ a place guide). It explains why Koreans are so serious about ginseng, how susam, baeksam, and hongsam differ, and what a foreigner should choose when buying. Health claims are handled honestly, kept to the line of traditional perception and functionality recognized by the MFDS (식품의약품안전처, Ministry of Food and Drug Safety) — no exaggeration. Key in one line: Ginseng (인삼) is the raw material; red ginseng (홍삼) is that ginseng steamed and dried. When a Korean says "have some hongsam," that red product is exactly this processed ginseng.

⚠️ 3 Things Foreigners Often Misunderstand

1. "Ginseng and red ginseng must be different plants" ❌ — They are the same ginseng (Panax ginseng). Hongsam (홍삼) is insam (인삼, usually susam) steamed (蒸) and dried (曝) — which is why it turns red and keeps for a long time. There is no separate "red-ginseng tree."

2. "Isn't ginseng a Chinese thing?" ❌ — Ginseng grows in several parts of East Asia, but the Korean kind has carried its own name, Korean ginseng (고려인삼), building a world reputation since the Three Kingdoms period — closer to a national flagship health brand.

3. "6-year-old root is always the best and pricier is better" ❌ — "6-year root" is a long-standing legal benchmark and marketing symbol (recently eased in part), but root age ≠ guaranteed quality. You should read it together with the labeled ginsenoside content and intended use (more in ② below).

① What Korean Ginseng Is — Why Korean Ginseng Gets Special Treatment

Ginseng began with sansam (산삼, wild ginseng), a deep-mountain herb. Because wild ginseng was so rare and costly, Korea developed field-cultivation techniques early and became a major ginseng producer.

  • The weight of the name — 'Goryeo insam (고려인삼)': In the East it's Goryeo insam, in the West Korean ginseng. Records of Baekje and Goguryeo ginseng appear in Chinese medical texts some 1,500 years ago, and its standing solidified through the Goryeo and Joseon eras — in the Joseon period it was a key export and a diplomatic gift.
  • 'The root that lifts your energy': In Korean traditional medicine, ginseng has long been ranked the foremost herb for replenishing (보, 補) one's vital energy, and this traditional perception carries on today as the red-ginseng health-food culture. So ginseng never stayed a mere medicine — it spread into food, liquor, tea, snacks, and gifts, becoming both a "special remedy" and a "familiar everyday."

② Susam, Baeksam & Hongsam — Same Root, Different Fates (and the 6-Year Root)

It all starts as one ginseng, and the name and character split by how it's processed. Susam (수삼) is the raw root just dug and rinsed of soil (70–80% water, so it can't last and goes straight into samgyetang, ginseng liquor, or raw eating); baeksam (백삼) is dried as-is (tea, herbal medicine); and hongsam (홍삼) is steamed and dried (jeungpo, 증포), turning red, keeping well, and reportedly developing components characteristic of red ginseng during processing — so in Korea, "health ginseng" is essentially this hongsam. Heuksam (흑삼) is a darker premium product processed by repeating the cycle, as in 9-steam-9-dry.

The '6-year root' story — symbol and misconception: Hongsam has long prized the 6-year root as the finest, and for a time only 6-year roots could be certified as hongsam (recently eased in part). But saponin content is not determined by root age alone — some saponins run high even in 4-year roots. "6-year = always best" is not true; it is just one quality indicator. Rather than the year-count, it's better to look at the labeled ginsenoside content.

③ Saponin & Ginsenoside — The 'Real Ingredient' Koreans Trust

A word never missing from red ginseng is saponin (사포닌), and specifically the ginseng saponins called ginsenoside (진세노사이드).

  • What ginsenoside is: The signature active component of ginseng and red ginseng. There are many kinds — Rg1, Rb1, Rg3, Re, and so on — and labels usually mark the sum of Rg1 + Rb1 (+ Rg3); this figure is the core indicator of quality and grade. During steaming and drying, some saponins are said to convert, creating a component profile characteristic of red ginseng, which is why Koreans specifically reach for 'hongsam.'
  • Yaksik-dongwon (약식동원, 藥食同源): "Food and medicine share one root" — the boyang idea that you govern the body through what you eat day to day. Ginseng and red ginseng are its representative restorative foods, settling in less as "medicine when sick" and more as "everyday boyang you take in advance when you're worn out."

④ How Far the Benefits Go — MFDS-Recognized Functionality (No Exaggeration)

A big reason red ginseng is trusted is that some of its benefits are officially recognized as health-functional-food functionality by the MFDS (식품의약품안전처, Ministry of Food and Drug Safety). The commonly introduced recognized functions include boosting immunity · improving fatigue · improving blood flow (inhibiting platelet aggregation) · improving memory · antioxidant action · and supporting women's health in menopause.

But points that must be made:

  • These are functionality claims at the level of "may help," not a medical assertion of curing disease — cure-all advertising is a regulated matter in Korea too. It may not suit those on blood-pressure or anticoagulant medication, or who are pregnant or nursing, research is still ongoing, and individual differences are large.
  • Bottom line: understand it as "a traditional restorative food + some officially recognized functionality," and for specifics, checking the product label and a professional is the accurate way.

⑤ Jeong Kwan Jang and Its Products — Paste, Sticks, Candy: Which to Pick

Red-ginseng products come in so many kinds it can feel overwhelming, and the most famous brand is Jeong Kwan Jang (정관장, KGC / Korea Ginseng Corporation) — a fixture of duty-free shops, department stores, and dramas. Sort by form and it gets easy to choose.

  • Hongsam-jeong (홍삼정, extract/concentrate): A thick paste of red ginseng simmered down. Scoop and stir into water, or eat as-is. The most concentrated restorative form, popular for the serious user · as a gift.
  • Stick type (pouch): A portable stick squeezed one dose at a time (e.g., the "Everytime" line). Less bitter and convenient, it's ideal for beginners · busy people and appears often in dramas.
  • Root ginseng (whole red-ginseng root): The processed red-ginseng root as-is. Its "real ginseng" look makes it favored for gifts · keeping.
  • Candy · jelly · pomegranate/red-ginseng sticks (Good Base, etc.) · red-ginseng tea · pills: From sweet entry versions for those put off by the bitter aroma, to teas and tablet forms. Easy as gifts for foreigners or for kids (only the form differs; the core is the same).

Picking tips: ① easy daily → stick, ② strong restorative · gift → hongsam-jeong / root ginseng, ③ put off by ginseng taste → candy · jelly. Compare the ginsenoside (Rg1+Rb1) content on the label and the value becomes visible.

⑥ Ginseng in Food — Samgyetang and the 'Fight Heat with Heat' of Boknal

Ginseng runs deep into the Korean table as food, too. The clear standout is samgyetang (삼계탕, 蔘鷄湯).

  • What samgyetang is: A restorative soup of a young chicken stuffed with ginseng, glutinous rice, jujube, and garlic and slow-simmered. The name itself is sam (蔘) + gye (鷄) + tang (湯) — ginseng is the lead.
  • Why eat something hot on the hottest day — iyeolchiyeol (이열치열, 以熱治熱): On the sambok (삼복, the three hottest days: chobok·jungbok·malbok) of the year, people eat piping-hot samgyetang. "Govern heat with heat" — the wisdom of recovering a worn-out body with restorative food while sweating it out (→ for boknal · seasonal food, see #1194 Four Seasons Culture).
  • Beyond that: insam-ju (인삼주, ginseng liquor) made with susam, honeyed jeonggwa, ginseng fritters, and more. In Geumsan (금산), the production hub, you can taste ginseng makgeolli and ginseng dishes, and in autumn there's even the Geumsan Insam Festival (금산 인삼축제).

⑦ Gifting & Duty-Free Culture — Why Red Ginseng Is the 'No. 1 Holiday Gift'

In Korea, red ginseng is a core item of gift culture. Come Seollal and Chuseok, department stores pile up red-ginseng gift sets like mountains, and it's a go-to handed to parents, elders, bosses, and business partners.

  • Why it's good as a gift: Red ginseng carries a message of jeong (정) and care — "I'm looking after your health and energy." In Korea's gift culture, which prizes the giver's heart (→ #1193), red ginseng is a formal yet safe choice that says I wish you good health.
  • A popular souvenir for foreigners: It's steadily popular as a Korea-travel gift, and preferences split by country — duty-free data shows Chinese visitors favor root ginseng, while American visitors lean to hongsam-jeong. For a first try, a stick · candy set is foolproof.
  • Where to buy: airport · downtown duty-free shops (foreigner pricing · curated lineups) · department stores / Jeong Kwan Jang stores (trust · varied sets) · a production-area market (Geumsan Ginseng Market, 금산 인삼시장) (cheaper via direct trade).

❓ Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

  • What's the difference between ginseng and red ginseng? — They're the same ginseng. Hongsam (홍삼) is insam (인삼, susam) steamed and dried — red in color and long-keeping. When people say "for health," they usually mean red ginseng.
  • Do the benefits really exist? — Red ginseng is an MFDS-recognized health functional food, with some functionality officially recognized for immunity, fatigue, blood flow, memory, and more. But it's at the "may help" level, not a treatment, and individual differences are large.
  • I'm new — what should I buy? — Start with a stick type (easy) or candy/jelly (sweet). For the serious option or a gift, hongsam-jeong or root ginseng.
  • Who shouldn't take it? — Those on blood-pressure or anticoagulant medication, the pregnant or nursing, and those with certain conditions may find it unsuitable — consulting a professional before taking it is safest.
