Sindang-dong (신당동) — The Neighborhood People Are Starting to Call "the Next Seongsu," a Three-Layer Mix of Markets, Hip Bars, and Tteokbokki

Subtitle: A neighborhood showing early signals of Western travelers naming it as a place to stay — three axes from daytime Seoul Jungang Market (서울중앙시장) to nighttime Ssajeon-geori (싸전거리) bar alley to the original Tteokbokki Town, plus a walkable route to Euljiro (을지로) and Dongdaemun (동대문), and why to go now

ISSUE 04 · #1311 · 2026-08-20

🎯 Why Sindang, Why Now

Sindang-dong (신당동) is tracing a path similar to what Seongsu-dong (성수동) looked like two or three years ago. Bars and restaurants have started moving into worn-out storefronts along the old rice-shop alley, Ssajeon-geori (싸전거리), and Western travel videos have begun calling it "a neighborhood I want to stay in when I visit Seoul" — the tourist crowds haven't arrived yet. Walking a neighborhood before it blows up is the core value here.

① 3 Mistakes Foreign Visitors Often Make

1. Coming in thinking "Sindang = tteokbokki" and nothing else

Tteokbokki (떡볶이, stir-fried Korean rice cakes) Town is just one of three axes. What's actually making Sindang right now is the market and the bar alley.

2. Assuming "I already did Gwangjang Market, so I've covered the markets"

Seoul Jungang Market (서울중앙시장) is the opposite of Gwangjang Market (광장시장) — it's a market for merchants and residents, not tourists. No touting, and prices are local (it's the go-to alternative featured in the Overrated vs Underrated Seoul guide).

3. Only coming during the day

The bars on Ssajeon-geori don't open until evening. You have to link the daytime market with the evening bar alley to see both faces of Sindang.

② Walking Sindang's Three Layers

Layer 1. Seoul Jungang Market (서울중앙시장) — the Daytime Axis

Right outside Sindang Station (신당역) Exits 1 and 2. One of Seoul's three great markets, founded in 1911 — it grew out of rice and dried-seafood wholesaling, and that everyday-market texture is still alive today.

  • Food alley: old-school spots (노포, decades-old traditional eateries) selling jeon (전, savory Korean pancakes), sundae (순대, Korean blood sausage), kalguksu (칼국수, hand-cut noodle soup), and grilled fish line a narrow passage, shoulder to shoulder. A meal inside the market runs about ₩7,000–12,000.
  • There's also a local move of buying nogari (노가리, dried baby pollack) and hwangtae (황태, semi-dried pollack) snacks at the dried-seafood shops and carrying them to the evening bar alley (check with each shop whether outside food is allowed).
  • Foreign-language menus are rare — pointing is the default way to order. That's part of the market's charm.

Layer 2. Ssajeon-geori (싸전거리) Bar Alley — the Nighttime Axis

Behind Jungang Market, an alley of old rice shops — ssajeon (싸전) — that gave the street its name. Bars and restaurants have moved in, keeping the worn-out storefronts mostly as they were, creating a "market hip" that's a different flavor from Euljiro's hip.

  • Jushindang (주신당) — the concept cocktail bar that became the symbol of Sindang's hip scene. Its musok (무속, Korean shamanism)-themed interior and cocktails named after the 12 zodiac animals show up often in foreign reviews. Waits are common. (Proven enough as the original location to have spawned branches in Gangnam (강남) and Yongsan (용산).)
  • Deeper into the alley, small bars and restaurants continue — Sunak (수낙), Uyukmi (우육미), Taetae-samgyeop (태태삼겹), Gyeryugwan (계류관) — with signs small enough that you'll end up walking the alley twice.
  • Tip: on weekend evenings, put your name down at Jushindang first, do a loop of the alley, grab market night snacks, then head back for your table — that order is the most efficient.

Layer 3. Tteokbokki Town — the Original Axis

The tteokbokki alley toward Sindang Station Exit 8. This is the birthplace of jeukseok-tteokbokki (즉석 떡볶이, "instant tteokbokki" simmered right at your table), started in the 1950s by Grandma Ma Bok-rim (마복림).

  • With jeukseok-tteokbokki, you order first, then simmer it at the table, adding sari (사리, extra add-ins like ramyeon noodles, jjolmyeon noodles, or dumplings) as you go — ₩15,000–25,000 for two or more.
  • The key point: this is a different genre from bunsik-style tteokbokki (street-food chains and general bunsik guides). It pairs well with the Dongdaemun Market Shopping guide.

④ Practical Tips

  • Payment: Some market old-school spots (노포) prefer cash — carrying ₩20,000 in cash is a good idea. The bar alley is fine with cards.
  • On staying here: It's just 1–2 subway stops from the Myeongdong (명동) and Dongdaemun tourist zones, yet lodging is cheaper — a good fit for the "stay like a local" mindset. There are no big hotels, though — mostly small stays.
  • Photo etiquette: A quick nod to market vendors before photographing their stalls is standard. Most Ssajeon-geori bar interiors are fine to photograph freely.
  • Getting there: Sindang Station, Lines 2 and 6. Walking over from Euljiro, crossing Cheonggyecheon makes for a nice route.
