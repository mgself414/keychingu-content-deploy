① What This Piece Covers — Why Confusing "Install" and "Activate" Gets You Stuck at the Airport

Most eSIM horror stories start from exactly one mistaken assumption: "I'll just install the eSIM once I land at the airport."

Wrong. Embedding the eSIM onto your phone (install) means scanning a QR code to download a profile — which needs an internet connection. But the moment you land, you don't have roaming or Wi-Fi yet — you're stuck in a catch-22: "scanning the QR needs data, but turning on data needs the QR scanned first." You might think grabbing airport Wi-Fi solves it, but in practice, fumbling through its login screen and dealing with slow speeds during that short window between baggage claim and walking out is the single most common way people end up sweating it out.

The fix is splitting the order.

  • Install before you leave home, on your home Wi-Fi — get the QR scan done while you still have time to spare
  • Activate after you land — data actually starts working the moment you get off the plane and turn on roaming

Remember just that one line and you're already halfway through this piece. The other half is ③④⑥.

② eSIM or Physical SIM — Check Your Phone First

eSIM doesn't work on every phone. It needs to be an eSIM-supported model (most iPhones from the XS onward, recent 3–4 years of Galaxy/Pixel flagships, etc.) and either carrier-free or unlocked. If it's eSIM-locked to your home carrier or an older model, the option won't even show up.

  • If you're not sure whether your phone supports it, the fastest check before you leave is Settings → General (or About) → eSIM support, or checking your model name against your vendor's compatibility list.
  • If your phone doesn't support it, or you'd rather not risk it, go with a physical USIM instead — buying and swapping in a physical SIM at an airport carrier booth or convenience store sidesteps this whole QR/timing problem entirely.
  • The full comparison of eSIM vs. physical SIM — pricing, data plans, where to buy — is covered in #250. This piece focuses only on install/activation for people who've already decided on eSIM.

③ Install Master Flow — Before You Leave, on Your Home Wi-Fi

Your eSIM usually arrives as a QR code by email (issued by the vendor/purchase site). Scan that QR in the order below and the install is done. Do this before you leave, while connected to your home Wi-Fi, without exception.

Step iPhone Android
1 Open the Settings app Open the Settings app
2 Tap [ Cellular ] (or [ Mobile Data ]) Tap [ Network & Internet ]
3 Tap [ Add eSIM ] or [ Add Cellular Plan ] Tap [ SIM Manager ]
4 Select [ Use QR Code ] Tap [ Add eSIM ]
5 Scan the QR code from the email with your camera Tap [ Download instead using a QR code ] → scan the QR
6 Confirm "Cellular Plan Added" → set a label (e.g., "Korea") Confirm "eSIM Download Complete"
  • The QR is often scannable only once — don't refresh the screen before scanning, and scan directly from the original email, not a screenshot.
  • Finishing the install doesn't mean data starts working right away. At this point the eSIM is only embedded in your phone — you need the next step (④) to actually turn it on.

④ Post-Arrival Activation Checklist — 3 Steps

Once you land and power your phone back on, just follow this order.

1. Set that eSIM as your mobile data line — Settings → Cellular/Mobile Data → select the "Korea" line you just installed as your default data line

2. Turn on Data Roaming — flip on the [ Data Roaming ] toggle on that same screen. With many providers, the data won't work even with the eSIM installed if this toggle is off — the step people miss most often

3. Toggle Airplane Mode off and on — once both settings are done, switch Airplane Mode ON briefly, then back OFF. In most cases the antenna picks up the new settings and connects

Once all three steps are done, the carrier name and signal indicator (LTE/5G) appear at the top of the screen. It can take a minute or two, so don't jump straight to rebooting if it doesn't show up right away — give it a moment first.

⑤ 6 Survival Screen Terms — Just the Confusing Ones

A few of these look similar enough that people tap the wrong one. Come back to this table when in doubt. The Korean in parentheses is what your phone shows if it ever auto-switches to Korean — and what you can show a clerk at an airport telecom counter.

Term Meaning Why it's confusing
Cellular / Mobile Data (sellyulleo 셀룰러 / 모바일 데이터) The menu name for managing your data line overall iPhone calls it "Cellular," Android calls it "Mobile Data" — same place, different name
Add eSIM (eSIM chuga eSIM 추가) The install button for embedding a new QR Don't tap this again for an eSIM you've already installed — ④ is a separate menu
Data Roaming (deiteo roming 데이터 로밍) The switch that allows data use while abroad Separate from Mobile Data being ON — both need to be on for it to work
Default Line (gibon hoeseon 기본 회선) Which of your two SIMs actually gets used for data If your home SIM stays set as default, the eSIM won't be used even after installing it
APN Carrier connection settings (통신사 접속 설정값) Only enter manually if auto-configuration fails (⑥)
SIM Manager (SIM gwallija SIM 관리자, Android) The screen that manages both eSIM and physical SIM together Android's name for what iPhone calls "Add eSIM"

⑥ 3-Step Troubleshooting When It Won't Work

If it still isn't working after doing all of ④, check in this order — one at a time, in sequence.

1. Double-check Roaming is ON — the most common cause. Settings → Cellular/Mobile Data → the eSIM line you just turned on → look again with your own eyes and confirm [ Data Roaming ] is really switched on

2. Enter the APN manually — auto-configuration may have failed. Enter the APN value exactly as written in your purchase email/vendor instructions into Settings → Cellular → eSIM line → [ APN Settings ]. Don't improvise — enter the vendor's value exactly as given

3. Toggle Airplane Mode → then reboot if it still fails — repeat step 3 of ④ once more, and if it's still not working, power the phone itself off and back on

If all three steps still don't fix it, there's a good chance the problem is with the eSIM itself (failed install, expired QR) — head to ⑨ Plan B.

⑦ Keeping Your Home Line — Avoiding a Roaming Bill Shock

Adding an eSIM doesn't remove your original home SIM — it's still sitting in your phone. Leave it alone and your home carrier can automatically grab roaming data even while you're in Korea, leaving you with a surprise bill after you get home.

  • Make sure Data Roaming on your home SIM (or physical SIM) is switched OFF — the roaming you turned on in ④ was for the new eSIM; this one goes the other way
  • If you want calls/texts to still go through your home line, you can set data to the eSIM and calls to your home line separately with dual-SIM settings (set data/calls independently in the ⑤ "Default Line" menu)
  • Once you're home, flip it the other way — turning off roaming on the eSIM side stops any remaining data from being wasted for nothing

⑧ 3 Panic Scenarios

1. Landed but no data — go back through the 3 steps of the ④ checklist, and if that doesn't fix it, move to ⑥ troubleshooting. Most of the time, it's Data Roaming still switched off.

2. Can't find the QR email — you can usually re-check it through your order history after logging into the purchase site's account. Check spam too. If you can get airport Wi-Fi, you can also ask for a reissue on the spot — but finishing ③ before you leave in the first place is what prevents this scenario entirely.

3. Your phone turns out not to support eSIM — no need to panic. Go back to ② and switch to a physical USIM instead — you can buy and activate one right at the airport (⑨).

⑨ Plan B — When eSIM Just Won't Work

  • Get a physical USIM at an airport carrier booth — past immigration, you'll find booths for the three carriers (SKT, KT, LG U+). Show your passport and buy a SIM that activates on the spot.
  • Use public Wi-Fi to hold you over first — hands-on use of airport, subway, and major tourist-spot public Wi-Fi is covered in #1302. Useful as a stopgap until your eSIM issue is sorted out.
