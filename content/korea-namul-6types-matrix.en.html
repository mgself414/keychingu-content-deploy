Korean Namul (나물) 6-Type Overview — Spinach, Bellflower Root, Bean Sprouts, Bracken Fern, Aster Leaf & Water Dropwort: The Complete Set for Foreign Travelers

Subtitle: A foreign traveler's guide to 6 namul types — the complete hanjeongsik banchan, temple-cuisine, and bibimbap tradition

ISSUE 03 · #876 · 2026-06-09

🎯 Why a Korean Namul Overview

The foreign traveler's pain point with Korean namul (나물, seasoned vegetable side dishes) = knowing only "namul = greens" without knowing the devoted fan base. Korean namul = the hanjeongsik (한정식, Korean course meal) banchan (반찬, free Korean side dishes) staple + temple-cuisine tradition + the complete 5-color bibimbap (비빔밥) set + globally vegetarian-friendly. Even under the same label "Korean vegetable side," Sigeumchi (spinach, #1 nationwide · bibimbap) · Doraji (bellflower root, #1 wild green · a ginseng-family root) · Kongnamul (bean sprouts, #1 haejang (해장, hangover-cure food) · #807 cross) · Gosari (bracken fern, wild-green tradition · gosari-tang (고사리탕) soup) · Chwi-namul (aster leaf, #1 spring wild green) · Minari (water dropwort, spring aromatic green · detox) each have their own color, season, and devoted fans. Foreign travelers typically don't realize that (a) bibimbap is a 5-color namul set, (b) temple cuisine is entirely a namul cluster, (c) Kongnamul crosses over with haejang soup, or (d) wild mountain greens are a spring tradition. This guide covers a 6-type overview + an emphasis on vegetarian- and halal-friendly options.

🌿 6-Type Deep Dive

🥬 Sigeumchi-namul (시금치나물) — #1 Starter

  • Signature: year-round · #1 tradition in the 5-color bibimbap · globally familiar
  • Ingredients: spinach + sesame oil, garlic, soy sauce & salt
  • Foreigner-friendliness: ★★★★★ (#1 starter; globally recognized as spinach)
  • Recommended venues: hanjeongsik (#837) · home-style meals · Jeonju bibimbap · MZ cafes
  • Season: year-round · winter tradition
  • Price: free (unlimited refill) with hanjeongsik / free with home-style meals / ₩3,000–6,000 (~$2–4) at markets

🌱 Doraji-namul (도라지나물) — Spring Wild Green · Hanjeongsik

  • Signature: #1 spring wild green · ginseng-family root · medicinal · hanjeongsik tradition
  • Ingredients: doraji (도라지, bellflower root, dried or fresh) + seasoning
  • Foreigner-friendliness: ★★★★ (hanjeongsik tradition; globally recognized as a medicinal root)
  • Recommended venues: traditional hanjeongsik restaurants · Gangwon (강원) wild-green nofo (노포, restaurants in business 30+ years)
  • Season: spring (March–May) · available year-round dried
  • Price: free with hanjeongsik / ₩5,000–15,000 (~$4–11) per pack at markets

🌿 Kongnamul (콩나물) — #1 Haejang

  • Signature: #1 haejang · #807 gukbap (국밥, Korean rice soup) cluster cross · kongnamul-gukbap (콩나물국밥, bean sprout soup with rice)
  • Ingredients: bean sprouts + sesame oil, salt & soy sauce (simple)
  • Foreigner-friendliness: ★★★★★ (#1, globally recognized as bean sprouts)
  • Recommended venues: hanjeongsik · home-style meals · Jeonju kongnamul-gukbap · haejang cluster
  • Season: year-round
  • Price: free with hanjeongsik / ₩2,000–4,000 (~$1–3) at markets

🍂 Gosari-namul (고사리나물) — Spring & Ancestral Rites Tradition

  • Signature: #1 spring wild green and ancestral-rites food · a traditional noodle-side delicacy
  • Ingredients: gosari (고사리, dried bracken fern) + perilla oil, soy sauce & garlic
  • Foreigner-friendliness: ★★★ (favored by wild-green enthusiasts)
  • Recommended venues: traditional hanjeongsik restaurants · ancestral-rites cuisine · Gangwon nofo
  • Timing: spring · ancestral rites (lunar traditional holidays (#846))
  • Price: free with hanjeongsik / ₩10,000–25,000 (~$7–19) per dried pack at markets

🌳 Chwi-namul (취나물) — Spring Aroma Tradition

  • Signature: #1 in spring (April–May) · aromatic · wild green
  • Ingredients: chwi-namul (spring aster greens) + seasoning
  • Foreigner-friendliness: ★★★★ (spring tradition; aroma globally appreciated)
  • Recommended venues: traditional hanjeongsik restaurants · Gangwon & Jeolla (전라) spring tradition
  • Season: #1 in spring (April–May)
  • Price: free with hanjeongsik / ₩8,000–20,000 (~$6–15) per pack at markets

💧 Minari-namul (미나리나물) — Spring Aroma & Detox

  • Signature: #1 in spring (March–April) · detox · aromatic · hanjeongsik
  • Ingredients: minari (미나리, water dropwort) + seasoning
  • Foreigner-friendliness: ★★★ (spring-aroma fans; globally known via the American film Minari, 2020)
  • Recommended venues: hanjeongsik restaurants · Cheongdo (청도) minari (Daegu (대구) tradition) · MZ (MZ, Korea's Millennial+Gen Z generation) Korean restaurants
  • Season: #1 in spring (March–April)
  • Price: free with hanjeongsik / ₩8,000–15,000 (~$6–11) per pack at markets

🍽 Hanjeongsik, Temple Cuisine & Bibimbap Signatures

Hanjeongsik (한정식) Banchan Full Set (#837)

  • The 5-course meal = 4–6 types of namul + kimchi & pickled sides
  • Shilla Hotel (신라호텔)'s La Yeon (라연), Samcheonggak (삼청각), Insadong (인사동) — traditional venues
  • Unlimited free refills

Sachal Eumsik (사찰음식, Temple Cuisine)

  • Temple cuisine = the #1 6-type namul cluster · traditional vegetarian food
  • Recommended venues:
  • Jingwansa (진관사) (Seoul) — temple-cuisine tradition
  • Jogyesa (조계사) — temple-cuisine cluster
  • Bongeunsa (봉은사) & Gwanaksan (관악산) — for temple-cuisine enthusiasts
  • Halal, kosher & vegetarian = OK (no meat or osinchae (오신채, five pungent vegetables banned in temple food: garlic, onion, chives, leek & asafoetida))

Bibimbap (비빔밥) 5-Color Tradition (#856)

  • 5 colors = spinach (green) · carrot (red) · Kongnamul (white) · Gosari (brown) · Doraji (white) + egg (yellow)
  • Jeonju bibimbap = the #1 tradition · royal-court bibimbap cluster

Jesa Eumsik (제사음식, Ancestral Rites Food)

  • Gosari, Doraji & Sigeumchi = the top 3 namul for ancestral rites
  • Lunar New Year (Seollal, 설날) & Chuseok (추석) (#846) — tradition

⚠ 5 Common Mistakes Foreign Travelers Make

1. "Namul = greens": namul = a hanjeongsik banchan tradition · a dish with devoted fans · globally vegetarian-friendly.

2. "Bibimbap = just a main dish": bibimbap = the complete 5-color namul set (spinach, carrot, Kongnamul, Gosari, Doraji).

3. "Temple cuisine = just greens": temple cuisine = a 6-type namul cluster + tofu & mushrooms · traditional vegetarian cuisine.

4. "Kongnamul = only for haejang": Kongnamul = served in hanjeongsik, haejang, and home-style meals alike.

5. "Wild greens = year-round": wild greens (Doraji, Chwi-namul, Minari, Gosari) = #1 in spring (March–May).

⚙ 8 FAQs

1. Top starter picks for foreign travelers: Sigeumchi + Kongnamul (globally familiar) → Doraji (hanjeongsik tradition).

2. Instagram & vlog: the full hanjeongsik banchan set · Jeonju bibimbap · temple cuisine.

3. MZ trend: MZ Korean restaurants' spring namul · Jeonju bibimbap · temple-cuisine tradition.

4. Haejang fans: Kongnamul (#1) + haejang soup (#870 · #807).

5. Spring wild greens: Doraji, Chwi-namul, Minari & Gosari (March–May).

6. Halal, vegetarian & vegan: all 6 types are OK (not OK for temple cuisine if osinchae garlic is included).

7. Allergies: declare garlic and soy sauce gluten in advance.

8. Payment & tips: hanjeongsik & home-style meals = card OK / no tipping.

⚠ Medical, Safety & Dietary Disclaimer

Namul = an ordinary, plant-based food. Pregnant women & children = OK (spice level can be adjusted). Declare allergies (garlic, soy sauce gluten, Doraji as a medicinal root) in advance. If food poisoning is suspected, call ☎ 1339 (medical, English available). This guide is general information, not medical advice.

Dietary restriction fallback:

  • Halal & Kosher: all 6 types = OK (plant-based).
  • Vegetarian & vegan: all 6 types = OK (#1 vegetarian tradition).
  • Gluten-free: soy sauce seasoning = contains gluten (substitute tamari).
  • Temple cuisine (no osinchae): all 6 types = OK once garlic seasoning is excluded.
