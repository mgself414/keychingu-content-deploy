Gyeongju Bulguksa and Seokguram — Last Admission Times, Bus 12 and the 2.8 km Mountain Path Between the Two, and Buses 10 and 11 from Downtown

Subtitle: For travelers who want to see Bulguksa and Seokguram in Gyeongju in one day, this guide groups the hours, fares and directions. Admission to both places is free and both are open all year. Entry starts at 09:00 and last admission is 17:00 in November–January (months 11–1), 17:30 in February and October, and 18:00 in March–September (months 3–9). Parking at both places is 2,000 won for a passenger car. From the Terminal to Bulguksa you take city bus 10 or 11. From Bulguksa to Seokguram you change to bus 12 or walk the 2.8 km trail in about 1 hour. From the Seokguram parking lot to Seokguram it is about 10 minutes on foot. In the autumn forest fire watch period (2026-11-15 to 2026-12-15) the Bulguksa–Seokguram trail is partly open. The 1.4 km section from the Seokguram Guard Station to the Tohamsan summit is closed for construction from 2026-11-01 to 2027-03-31. The values were read on 2026-11-25.

ISSUE 06 · #1052 · 2026-11-25 · Gyeongju Bulguksa·Seokguram

🎯 Who This Is For, and Why These Two Places

This guide is made for foreign travelers who want to see Bulguksa Temple (불국사) and Seokguram Grotto (석굴암) in Gyeongju (경주). It is written so that you can match times and bus numbers before you leave, even if you do not know Korean.

Seokguram is on the mid-slope of Tohamsan (토함산). The official Seokguram website (누리집) (read 2026-11-25) says that artificial grottoes were built "using white granite on the mid-slope of Tohamsan". Korea National Park Service (국립공원공단) introduces the path from Bulguksa to Seokguram as a visit course of Tohamsan. The Seokguram website says "Seokguram was jointly registered as a UNESCO World Heritage Site together with Bulguksa in December 1995." That is why the two places are grouped in one guide.

This guide answers four questions.

  • Until what time can you enter: the last admission time differs by month.
  • Does it cost money: admission is free. Parking and the bus cost money.
  • How do you get between the two places: three ways — bus 12, the 2.8 km trail, or your own car.
  • What changes in winter: last admission moves earlier and the path toward the Tohamsan summit is closed for construction.

Sites, restaurants and lodging in downtown Gyeongju are not covered in this guide. The night routes, markets and hanbok of the downtown are in the guides in the Related section below.

⚠ Notation principle: Hours, fares, distances and directions are values read on 2026-11-25. Sources are the Bulguksa website, the Seokguram website, Gyeongju City Hall (경주시청), Korea National Park Service and the National Heritage Portal (국가유산포털). Fares are adult list prices. 17:00, 17:30 and 18:00 are not closing times but last admission times. The visit news screen of Korea National Park Service does not show a posting date. Operating hours, fares, bus service and closed sections can change.

🚌 How to Get There

1. From the Terminal to Bulguksa — City Bus 10 or 11

These are the values of the official guide of Gyeongju City Hall (read 2026-11-25).

  • Bus 10: it starts at the Terminal (터미널), passes Cheomseongdae (첨성대) and Bomun (보문), and turns around at Bulguksa.
  • Bus 11: it runs the same route as bus 10 in the opposite direction. The Gyeongju city guide also lists Bulguksa as the turnaround point of bus 11.
  • Time: measured at the Terminal, the first bus 10 is 06:00 and the last bus is 21:40. It comes about every 20 minutes.
  • Time if you go straight through: Gyeongju City Hall writes "If you go straight from the Terminal, the starting point, to Bulguksa, the turnaround point, without intermediate stops, it takes about 35 to 45 minutes." City Hall writes that this value changes with traffic. The route passes sightseeing places in the downtown, so it takes longer if the bus stops along the way.
  • Where to get off: the "Bulguksa (불국사)" stop. From the stop to Bulguksa it is 3–5 minutes on foot.
  • Fare: adult 1,500 won, youth 1,200 won, child 800 won. With a transit card, adults get 50 won off, and children and youth ride free (including residents of other regions).
  • Do not take bus 10-1: bus 10-1 does not go to Bulguksa. It turns around at Gyeongju World (경주월드).

The directions page of the Bulguksa website also lists the buses to Bulguksa as 10 and 11.

2. From Bulguksa to Seokguram — Bus 12

These are the values of the directions guide on the official Seokguram website (read 2026-11-25).

  • Going: change to bus 12 at Bulguksa.
  • Coming back: the bus 12 time given by the Seokguram website is "departs every hour on the hour" from Seokguram to Bulguksa.
  • At Seokguram: from the Seokguram parking lot to Seokguram it is about 10 minutes on foot.

Decide the on-the-hour bus you will take back before you go into Seokguram. This is this guide's own advice.

3. From Bulguksa to Seokguram — 2.8 km on Foot

The official visit news of Korea National Park Service (read 2026-11-25) introduces the "Bulguksa–Seokguram visit course" (2.8 km in total, about 1 hour). Bulguksa and Seokguram are inside Gyeongju National Park. The mountain path between the two is a national park trail.

In the autumn forest fire watch period (2026-11-15 to 2026-12-15), the Park Service partly opens the Bulguksa–Seokguram section of the Tohamsan trails. If you plan to walk in this period, also decide on a bus 12 time. This is this guide's own advice.

4. By Your Own Car

  • From downtown to Bulguksa: following the Bulguksa website, take National Route 7 toward Ulsan. Turn left at Gujeong-dong Samgeori (구정동 삼거리) junction. Go about 2.5 km on Local Road 902.
  • From Bulguksa to Seokguram: following the Seokguram website, follow the Seokguram signs and drive the Tohamsan road for about 20 minutes. You arrive at the Seokguram parking lot.
  • Parking fee: Bulguksa is 2,000 won for a passenger car. Seokguram is 2,000 won for small and medium vehicles.

① Bulguksa Temple (불국사)

  • Address and phone: 385 Bulguk-ro (불국로), Gyeongju-si, Gyeongsangbuk-do (Jinhyeon-dong (진현동) 15-1). 054-746-9913.
  • Hours and fare: the official Bulguksa website (read 2026-11-25) says "Under the revised Cultural Heritage Protection Act, admission is free from May 4, 2023." Entry starts at 09:00. Last admission is 17:00 in November–January (months 11–1), 17:30 in February and October, and 18:00 in March–September (months 3–9). It is open all year.
  • Parking: passenger car 2,000 won, large vehicle (bus) 4,000 won.
  • Rules: the Bulguksa website says "Pets are not allowed."

When the Temple Was Built

The history guide on the official Bulguksa website (read 2026-11-25) introduces the record of its founding and the record of its large renovation together.

  • 528: there is a record that the temple was first built in the time of King Beopheung (法興王) of Silla (신라). The website gives the source of this record as the Bulguksa Gogeum Changgi (불국사고금창기). The same guide also says this record has a "weak documentary basis".
  • 751: the website says "In 751 (the 10th year of King Gyeongdeok), it was greatly renovated by Kim Dae-seong (金大城), and a pagoda, a stone bridge and so on were also made."

Later events are in the same guide.

  • 1593: "Bulguksa, destroyed by the Japanese army in 1593, began to be restored and rebuilt again from around 1604."
  • 1970–1973: restoration work started in February 1970 and finished in June 1973. At this time Museoljeon Hall (무설전), Gwaneumjeon Hall (관음전), Birojeon Hall (비로전), Gyeongnu Tower (경루) and the corridors were restored.

What to See — 6 National Treasures

The 6 National Treasures (국보) below are the ones listed in the sacred treasures guide on the official Bulguksa website (read 2026-11-25).

  • The two pagodas: the Bulguksa website calls Dabotap and Seokgatap "the most representative stone pagodas of our country". The heights of the two pagodas are close, 10.29 m and 10.75 m. The two pagodas face each other in the front courtyard of Daeungjeon Hall. The Bulguksa website says "The east pagoda is Dabotap" and calls the west pagoda the "Three-story Stone Pagoda". The same guide explains Seokgatap as the "Gyeongju Bulguksa Three-story Stone Pagoda".
  • Designation record of Dabotap: the National Heritage Portal (read 2026-11-25) gives the category as National Treasure, the name as "Gyeongju Bulguksa Dabotap Pagoda", and the designation date as 1962.12.20. The period is the 8th century of Unified Silla.
  • Cheongungyo and Baegungyo Bridges: the Bulguksa website says these bridges have "the symbolic meaning of linking the world of ordinary people below the bridge and the world of the Buddha above the bridge."
  • The two Buddha statues: the Gilt-bronze Seated Vairocana Buddha is enshrined in Birojeon Hall and the Gilt-bronze Seated Amitabha Buddha in Geungnakjeon Hall.

② Seokguram Grotto (석굴암)

  • Address and phone: 238 Seokgul-ro (석굴로), Gyeongju-si, Gyeongsangbuk-do (Jinhyeon-dong (진현동)). 054-746-9933.
  • Hours and fare: as written on the official Seokguram website (read 2026-11-25), admission is free from 2023-05-04. Entry starts at 09:00. Last admission is 17:00 in November–January (months 11–1), 17:30 in February and October, and 18:00 in March–September (months 3–9). It is open all year.
  • Parking: as written on the official Seokguram website, small and medium 2,000 won, large vehicle (bus) 4,000 won. The parking lot is run by the Gyeongju Facilities Management Corporation (경주시 시설관리공단).
  • From the parking lot to Seokguram: about 10 minutes on foot.
  • Rules: pets cannot enter.
  • Weather: when a heavy rain, typhoon or heavy snow advisory or warning is issued, entry to the grounds can be closed.

When It Was Built

These are the values of the history guide on the official Seokguram website (read 2026-11-25).

  • Start: it says "Seokguram was begun in the 10th year of King Gyeongdeok of Silla (751) by Kim Dae-seong, who was prime minister at the time".
  • Completion: it says "It was completed in the 10th year of King Hyegong (774)".
  • First name: when it was built, "it was called Seokbulsa (석불사)."

What to See

  • The grotto: the Seokguram grotto is a National Treasure.
  • The main Buddha: the structure guide on the Seokguram website says the main Buddha is "a huge Buddha statue about 3.4 meters tall."

🏛 UNESCO World Heritage Listing

The history guide on the official Seokguram website (read 2026-11-25) says "Seokguram was jointly registered as a UNESCO World Heritage Site together with Bulguksa in December 1995."

The two places were listed together at one time. Seokguram was begun in 751 and completed in 774. Dabotap of Bulguksa is a pagoda of the 8th century of Unified Silla.

❄ What Changes in Winter

  • Last admission is 17:00: in November, December and January, last admission at both Bulguksa and Seokguram is 17:00. In February it is 17:30.
  • The path toward the Tohamsan summit is closed: the notice on the Gyeongju National Park screen of Korea National Park Service (read 2026-11-25) gives the closed section as "Seokguram Guard Station (석굴암지킴터) to Tohamsan summit (1.4 km)" and the closure period as "26.11.1 ~ 2027.3.31". The reason for the closure is the "Gyeongju Seokguram Grotto Danger Zone Reinforcement Work (Phase 2)" carried out by Gyeongju City (Park Service Gyeongju National Park Office (경주국립공원사무소) Notice No. 2026-26, 2026-10-22). The notice also says "The closure period may change depending on conditions on site". It will be opened temporarily for the 2027-01-01 sunrise event.
  • Bulguksa–Seokguram trail: in the autumn forest fire watch period (2026-11-15 to 2026-12-15), the Park Service partly opens the Bulguksa–Seokguram section of the Tohamsan trails. If you plan to walk in this period, also decide on a bus 12 time. This is this guide's own advice.
  • Seokguram can be closed when a weather warning is issued: the Seokguram website says that when a heavy rain, typhoon or heavy snow advisory or warning is issued, entry to the grounds can be closed. In the notice list of the Seokguram website there is a notice shown only with the date 03-04, titled "March 4 entry not possible due to heavy snow. Entry possible from 2:00 on the 5th." It is an example that heavy snow can block entry.
  • To see both places in a day: set out in the morning in winter. This is this guide's own advice.
  • Inquiries: Seokguram 054-746-9933. Bulguksa 054-746-9913. Gyeongju National Park Visitor Center (경주국립공원 탐방안내소) 054-778-4100.

⚠ 6 Common Mistakes

  1. Reading 17:00 as the closing time. 17:00, 17:30 and 18:00 are last admission times. After that time you cannot enter.
  2. Taking bus 10-1. Bus 10-1 turns around at Gyeongju World. The buses that go to Bulguksa are 10 and 11.
  3. Trying to reach Seokguram by bus 10 or 11. For Seokguram, you change to bus 12 at Bulguksa.
  4. Trying to walk on from Seokguram to the Tohamsan summit. The 1.4 km section from the Seokguram Guard Station to the Tohamsan summit is closed for construction from 2026-11-01 to 2027-03-31.
  5. Thinking Seokguram is right there when you get off at the Seokguram parking lot. From the parking lot to Seokguram it is about 10 minutes on foot.
  6. Bringing a pet. Pets cannot enter either Bulguksa or Seokguram.

⚙ 6 FAQs

  1. Do the two places cost money? — Admission is free at both from 2023-05-04. If you bring a car, parking is 2,000 won per place for a passenger car. The city bus fare is adult 1,500 won, youth 1,200 won, child 800 won. With a transit card, adults get 50 won off, and children and youth ride free (including residents of other regions).
  2. Until what time can I enter? — Last admission is the same at both places. It is 17:00 in November–January (months 11–1), 17:30 in February and October, and 18:00 in March–September (months 3–9). Entry starts at 09:00.
  3. How do I get from Bulguksa to Seokguram? — There are three ways. Change to bus 12 at Bulguksa. On foot, the trail is 2.8 km and about 1 hour. In the autumn forest fire watch period (2026-11-15 to 2026-12-15) this section is partly open. By car, drive the Tohamsan road for about 20 minutes.
  4. Is there a day off? — Both Bulguksa and Seokguram are open all year. Seokguram can close entry to the grounds when a heavy rain, typhoon or heavy snow advisory or warning is issued.
  5. When was Bulguksa built? — The history guide on the Bulguksa website introduces the record of its founding and the record of its large renovation together. There is a record that the temple was first built in 528 in the time of King Beopheung. The website writes that this record has a "weak documentary basis". There is a record that in 751 Kim Dae-seong greatly renovated the temple and made a pagoda and a stone bridge.
  6. Can I go from Seokguram to the Tohamsan summit? — From 2026-11-01 to 2027-03-31 you cannot. The 1.4 km section from the Seokguram Guard Station to the summit is closed for construction. The Park Service writes that it plans to open it temporarily for the 2027-01-01 sunrise.
