Why Jungbu Market Matters Now — Korea's No. 1 Dried Seafood Wholesaler Since 1957

Jungbu Market (중부시장) opened in 1957 as Korea's largest dried seafood wholesale market, sitting in Euljiro (을지로) 4-ga in Seoul's Jung District. Compared with Gwangjang Market (traditional food, vlog-famous) and Namdaemun Market (authentic old-school eateries), it has one clear point of difference: the center of gravity here is wholesale dried seafood, seaweed, sea mustard, kelp, anchovies, and dried shrimp. The real advantage for foreign visitors is that even retail shoppers can buy at wholesale prices.

The market covers roughly 13,000 pyeong. Dried seafood wholesalers are concentrated on the ground floor of the main building, and old-school restaurants with 60-plus years in business line the surrounding alleys. Jungbu Market packs dried seafood stalls, food-ingredient wholesale, and Euljiro's old-school dining culture into a single block.

This matrix covers 3 dried seafood stall picks + 3 authentic old-school eatery picks + 3 Euljiro cross picks + 3 foreign enthusiast picks = 12 picks across 4 axes.

12 Picks Across 4 Axes

Axis 1: Dried Seafood Stalls (3 picks)

1. Jungbu Market dried seafood wholesale, main building

Location: 118 Euljiro, Jung District, Seoul (main cluster on the ground floor of the main building)

Hours: Weekdays/Saturdays 06:00–18:00 / mostly closed Sundays (varies by stall)

Prices (2026): Soup anchovies 1kg ₩12,000–18,000 / medium dried shrimp 500g ₩8,000–14,000 / kelp 500g ₩6,000–10,000 (about 40–60% of retail prices)

Foreigner fit: ★★★★★ — the top wholesale destination for Chinese and Japanese tourists buying "Korean dried seafood gifts" in person. Southeast Asian visitor traffic is also rising.

Access: 3-minute walk from Exit 3, Euljiro 4-ga Station (Lines 2, 5)

English: Limited. Google Translate's camera feature is practical. Printing a Korean item list lets you point and buy.

Timing tip: 07:00–09:00 is wholesale-trader peak → 10:00–13:00 is the recommended window for retail foreign visitors

Taking it home: Some stalls offer vacuum packing (ask for "vacuum packaging" or use a translation app). Dry, unprocessed dried seafood is generally fine for air travel — but check your destination country's quarantine rules in advance.

2. Jungbu Market seaweed/laver stall cluster

Location: Main building and adjacent annex (roughly 10-plus laver specialty stalls)

Hours: Weekdays/Saturdays 07:00–17:00

Prices (2026): Wholesale roasted laver, 100 sheets, ₩3,500–5,000 / seasoned laver gift set (30 packs) ₩15,000–22,000 / green laver 200g ₩4,000–7,000

Foreigner fit: ★★★★★ — Korean laver (gim) is one of the most popular Korean food souvenirs across Chinese, Japanese, Southeast Asian, and Western visitors alike. Jungbu Market sets Seoul's wholesale floor price.

Timing tip: Buying by the wholesale pack (100-sheet units) opens room to negotiate an extra discount. Smaller quantities are also allowed.

Taking it home: Roasted seasoned laver generally clears air travel — just make sure it's dry, with no liquid components.

3. Jungbu Market nuts/grains stall cluster

Location: Outer edge of the main building and the right-hand section of the ground floor

Hours: Weekdays/Saturdays 07:00–18:00

Prices (2026): Domestic pine nuts 100g ₩5,000–8,000 / shelled walnuts 500g ₩7,000–12,000 / black sesame 200g ₩3,000–5,000

Foreigner fit: ★★★★ — an easy add-on after buying dried seafood. Some Chinese and Japanese enthusiasts come specifically for hangwa ingredients or grains for seasoned vegetable side dishes.

Axis 2: Authentic Old-School Eateries (3 picks)

4. Jungbu Market authentic old-school eatery cluster

Location: Alleys near the main building (side streets south of Euljiro 4-ga Station)

Hours: 06:00–14:00 (lunch-focused, many close early in the afternoon)

Prices (2026): Kalguksu (칼국수)/sundae-guk-bap ₩7,000–9,000 / one plate of sundae ₩5,000–7,000

Foreigner fit: ★★★★ — these eateries got their start feeding wholesale traders and merchants. Expect to sit shoulder-to-shoulder with locals. Be considerate before taking photos.

English: Limited. Korean-only menus plus a translation app. Pointing at a neighboring diner's dish also works.

Note: Some old-school spots don't take reservations — no seats means you wait in line. Before 9 a.m., wholesale traders get priority.

5. The restaurant alley near Jungbu Market

Location: The alley east of the market (toward Cheonggye 4-ga)

Hours: 07:00–15:00 (lunch only)

Prices (2026): Baekban (rice, soup, and 5 side dishes) ₩7,000–10,000

Foreigner fit: ★★★ — low menu variety and Korean-only ordering. Recommended for foreign visitors up for a challenge.

6. Traditional Korean pantry goods (banchan, jeotgal, doenjang wholesale)

Location: Inside the main building and the adjacent banchan alley

Hours: 07:00–17:00

Prices (2026): Jeotgal (salted shrimp, 500g) ₩6,000–10,000 / wholesale doenjang (1kg) ₩5,000–8,000

Foreigner fit: ★★★ — jeotgal and doenjang count as liquid contents for checked baggage on flights home. Not recommended as a last-day purchase.

Note on taking it home: Liquid fermented items like salted shrimp and fish sauce are barred from carry-on (over 100ml) — pack them in checked luggage.

3 Common Mistakes Foreign Visitors Make

Mistake 1: Visiting on a Sunday

Most dried seafood wholesalers at Jungbu Market are closed on Sundays, and many of the main old-school eateries close too. Check the day of the week before you go — weekday mornings or Saturday mornings are best.

Mistake 2: Bringing only cards

Dried seafood wholesale tends to run on cash. Some stalls take cash only for smaller transactions (under ₩10,000). Withdraw ₩50,000–100,000 in advance from a nearby ATM (there are four inside Euljiro 4-ga Station).

Mistake 3: Trying to carry jeotgal or fish sauce as carry-on

Salted shrimp, fish sauce, and doenjang can't go in carry-on luggage (liquids over 100ml are barred). If you're flying home the same day you buy them, use a checked-baggage packing service near the market, or save the purchase for a later visit.

Practical Info — Transport, Payment, Language

Transport

  • Subway: Lines 2 and 5, Euljiro 4-ga Station, Exit 3 → 3-minute walk (Jungbu Market main building)
  • Bus: Routes 162, 262, 301, and others stop at Cheonggye 4-ga (청계4가) → 2-minute walk
  • Parking: On-street parking nearby is limited. The Euljiro public parking lot (toward Cheonggye 4-ga, paid) is an option, but public transit is recommended.

Payment

  • Cash preferred: Wholesale stalls and old-school eateries favor cash. Smaller stalls sometimes refuse cards.
  • Cards accepted: Larger stalls (laver specialists carrying 10-plus varieties) generally accept cards — worth checking on-site.
  • ATM: KB and Woori Bank ATMs are inside Euljiro 4-ga Station. Foreign card withdrawals are possible (fee ₩2,000–3,000 per transaction).

Language

  • English: Mostly limited across the market. Google Translate's camera feature is highly practical (★★★★★).
  • Chinese/Japanese: Some large laver stalls have Chinese signage. Full communication is still a stretch.
  • Tourist interpretation hotline: ☎ 1330 (Korea Tourism Organization, 24h, English/Chinese/Japanese/French) — can connect you with a stall owner over the phone on-site.

FAQ Expanded (5 Questions)

Q1. What's a good first-visit route through Jungbu Market?

Exit 3, Euljiro 4-ga Station → 3-minute walk → enter the dried seafood zone on the ground floor of the main building. From the entrance, the left (west) side is dense with dried seafood wholesalers; the right (east) side has laver, seaweed, and nuts. A full loop takes about 40–60 minutes.

Q2. How is it different from Gwangjang Market and Namdaemun Market?

  • Gwangjang Market: centered on food (yukhoe, bindae-tteok) and clothing, the highest foreign-tourist density of the three
  • Namdaemun Market: household goods, clothing, general retail, plenty of foreigner-friendly English menus
  • Jungbu Market: specializes in wholesale dried and dried seafood. Less of a food destination, more focused on buying ingredients.

Q3. Can you fly home with dried seafood?

Dry, unprocessed dried seafood (anchovies, kelp, sea mustard, roasted laver) generally clears checked baggage. Since quarantine rules vary by country, checking your destination's regulations in advance is recommended. Japan sometimes requires declaring the quantity and packaging of dried seafood. Liquid or fermented items (salted shrimp, fish sauce) can't go in carry-on and need checked-baggage packing.

Q4. Can retail shoppers buy at wholesale prices too?

In principle, yes — most wholesale stalls also allow smaller retail purchases. Some stalls do set a minimum (for example, 500g of anchovies or 50 sheets of laver). If you want a smaller amount, confirm with a translation app first.

Q5. Is there somewhere cheap to eat nearby?

Kalguksu and sundae-guk-bap at old-school eateries near the main building run ₩7,000–9,000. Convenience stores (CU, GS25) toward the Euljiro 4-ga Station exit also work for something quick. Lunch peak (11:30–12:30) gets competitive for seats — arriving before 11:00 is recommended.

Seasonal Tips

  • Spring (March–May): New-season anchovies arrive. Prices are favorable.
  • Summer (June–August): Watch for heat and humidity. Bring a storage bag for dried seafood. Some sections of the market lack air conditioning.
  • Autumn (September–November): Peak dried seafood season. Kelp, sea mustard, and laver are at their best quality. The best time to visit.
  • Winter (December–February): Seasonal items like semi-dried yellow corvina, pollack, and dried pollack arrive. Wholesale price negotiation is more favorable.
  • Public holidays: Closed on Lunar New Year and Chuseok eve and the holiday itself. The day before a long holiday often brings inventory clearance and better prices.
