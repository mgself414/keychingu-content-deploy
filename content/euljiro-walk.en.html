3 Common Mistakes Foreign Travelers Make

1. Thinking "Euljiro = just a shopping/office street"

Euljiro racks up an unusual number of 4.8-star old eateries, 5.0-star bakeries, and 5.0-star dumpling shops in foreign-language reviews — in other words, it's the heart of the food-and-cafe cluster locals actually visit. Skip the global chains and build your route around old-school eateries and hidden shops instead — you won't regret it.

2. Thinking "Daelim Sangga (대림상가) is just a shopping mall"

Pao (dumplings) sits on the bridge level of Daelim Sangga. A foreign English review: "Shop is on the bridge level and not on street level! We only realised this after walking the entire stretch of building twice" — it's not on the ground floor but up on a bridge level, and travelers often only find it after circling the whole building. Know this ahead of time and save yourself the walk.

3. Thinking "Euljiro is mainly an evening scene"

In fact, the standout eateries are the ones open for breakfast and lunch — Sarangbang Kalguksu (opens 11:00) and Asoto Bakery (opens 09:00). You need to start in the morning to complete the full loop. Coming only in the evening gets you half the experience.

2. The 8-Spot Loop, by Time of Day

09:00–11:00 · Morning Start

1. Asoto Bakery (아소토베이커리)

  • Category: Bakery/cafe (80s retro concept)
  • Location: 19 Supyo-ro 10-gil, Jung-gu, Seoul (6 min walk from Euljiro 3-ga station)
  • Foreign English reviews (1,079 on Google Maps / 5 foreign-language):

"The atmosphere embodies the retro 80s. Siphon coffee and pastries are the pride of this place. They serve cafe au lait instead of caffe latte."

"A very cute cafe with a cozy atmosphere. I ordered the melon bread, strawberry parfait, and coffee, and everything was delicious and so cute."

  • Foreign Japanese reviews: ★ 5.0/5.0 (5 reviews) — very well known among Japanese visitors (the melon bread is popular as a "diet dessert")
  • Signature: Melon bread / dango desserts / siphon coffee / cafe au lait
  • Foreign-traveler advantage: 80s Japanese-dessert-shop vibe + English menu + solo-friendly
  • Hours: 09:00–22:00 (daily)
  • Budget: Melon bread ₩4,500 + siphon coffee ₩7,000 = ₩11,500/person

→ Next: 8 min walk (Supyo-ro → Euljiro → Daelim Sangga)

11:30–12:30 · Lunch, Round 1 (Snack)

2. Pao (빠우) — On the Bridge Level of Daelim Sangga

  • Category: Dumplings/donuts (freshly fried, single items)
  • Location: 157 Euljiro, Daelim Sangga, Jung-gu, Seoul (3 min walk from Euljiro 3-ga station, 2nd-floor bridge level!)
  • Foreign English reviews (127 on Google Maps / 8 foreign-language, ★ 5.0/5.0):

"Best bread I've tried in Seoul! I loved how they fry the bread after ordering. We tried the original, cheese and red bean flavored donuts."

"Shop is on the bridge level and not on street level! We only realised this after walking the entire stretch of building twice before heading up."

"It's situated on the top floor of Daelim plaza. The items are fried-to-order so expect a little wait time but it's totally worth it."

  • Signature: Freshly fried donuts (original / cheese / red bean) — about 5 min fry time after ordering
  • Foreign-traveler advantage: English menu / solo-friendly / card and easy-pay accepted
  • Key tip for foreign travelers: On the 2nd-floor bridge level (not the ground floor). Take the upper staircase from the Daelim Sangga entrance
  • Hours: 12:00–19:00 (closed Mon, Tue)
  • Budget: 3 donuts = ₩9,000/person

→ Next: 8 min walk (Daelim Sangga → Chungmuro → Sarangbang Kalguksu)

13:00–14:30 · Main Lunch

3. Sarangbang Kalguksu (사랑방칼국수)

  • Category: Korean old eatery (broiled chicken and kalguksu, 50 years in business)
  • Location: 46 Toegye-ro 27-gil, Jung-gu, Seoul (5 min walk from Chungmuro station)
  • Foreign English reviews (1,266 on Google Maps / 9 foreign-language, ★ 4.2/5.0):

"The broiled chicken is the best of its kind I've ever had in my life. The kalguksu is average alright but for the value, no one would really complain."

"I liked this local restaurant which feels like your grandma is cooking for you. Chicken was amazing and all experience is very cool."

  • Foreign Japanese reviews: ★ 4.8/5.0 (5 reviews) — overwhelmingly praised by Japanese visitors
  • Signature: Dak-bulgogi (닭불고기) — broiled chicken — plus kalguksu (foreign travelers' top pick is the broiled chicken)
  • Foreign-traveler advantage: Old-eatery atmosphere + partial English menu (friendly staff) + solo-friendly
  • Key tip for foreign travelers: Foreign English reviews unanimously point to the broiled chicken as the signature dish. Don't order kalguksu alone.
  • Hours: 11:00–21:00 (closed Sundays)
  • Budget: Broiled chicken + kalguksu = ₩18,000/person

→ Next: 6 min walk (Toegye-ro 27-gil → Toegye-ro 20-gil)

15:00–16:00 · Afternoon Break (Secondary Pick)

4. Ruri Coffee (루리커피) — A Hidden Cafe Inside a Comic Book Shop

  • Category: Modern roastery (Geisha hand-drip)
  • Location: 31 Toegye-ro 20-gil, Jung-gu, Seoul (6 min walk from Euljiro 3-ga station)
  • Foreign English reviews (42 on Google Maps / 5 foreign-language, ★ 5.0/5.0):

"Found the best Geisha in Korea, here they have it! Better go experience it before you're too late!"

"Almost seems like a hidden world tucked inside an anime store."

  • Signature: Geisha hand-drip (world top-10 beans), seasonal single origins
  • Foreign-traveler advantage: Hidden inside a comic-book and game shop — the fun of discovery + English menu
  • Hours: 12:00–21:00 (daily)
  • Budget: Geisha hand-drip ₩12,000–₩18,000

→ Next: 5 min walk (Toegye-ro → Chungmuro 4-gil)

16:30–17:30 · Afternoon Snack / Early Evening

5. Oldies Taco (올디스타고)

  • Category: Global concept (Mexican tacos + 80s music bar)
  • Location: 5, 1F, Chungmuro 4-gil, Jung-gu, Seoul (4 min walk from Chungmuro station; a 2nd Euljiro branch also exists)
  • Foreign English reviews (463 on Google Maps / 9 foreign-language, ★ 4.6/5.0):

"Items #1 and #2 were so good.... OMG. #1, the Oldies Taco, was packed with meat, and the sauces inside were amazing. #2 was the birria taco, super juicy."

"Nothing like having good tacos on the street in the cold! What a vibe! Service was good and efficient!"

"Oldies Taco was such a fun surprise. They were playing oldies music and it just lifted the whole atmosphere."

  • Foreign Japanese reviews: ★ 4.2/5.0 (5 reviews)
  • Signature: Oldies Taco (#1) + Birria Taco (#2) + 80s oldies music BGM
  • Foreign-traveler advantage: Mexican food meets K-vibes / English spoken / outdoor seating on Chungmuro
  • Hours: 12:00–22:00 (daily)
  • Budget: 2–3 tacos = ₩15,000–₩22,000/person

→ Next: 2 min walk (same Chungmuro 4-gil cluster)

18:30–20:00 · Dinner (Hidden Pick 1)

6. Hanju Diary (한주다이어리) — Korean Dining Hidden from Foreign Travelers

  • Category: Korean dining (casual modern)
  • Location: 2, 1F, Chungmuro 4-gil, Jung-gu, Seoul (4 min walk from Chungmuro station)
  • Foreign-traveler signal: ★ 0 foreign-language reviews (1 review total on Google Maps) — a true local hotspot foreign travelers barely know
  • Signature: Casual Korean dining paired with hanju (Korean alcohol) — the name "Diary" hints at the intimate, journal-like dining experience
  • Foreign-traveler advantage: Low foreign-visitor exposure → a genuine slice of Korean everyday dining / solo-friendly / menu mostly in Korean (ask staff for recommendations)
  • Key tip for foreign travelers: With zero English reviews, actively ask staff for menu recommendations. You can also order by pointing at photos.
  • Hours: 17:00–24:00 (closed Mondays)
  • Budget: ₩25,000–₩35,000/person (drinks + meal course)

→ Next: 1 min walk (same Chungmuro cluster)

20:30–22:00 · Drinks, Round 1 (Hidden Pick 2)

7. Kasu (카수) — A Japanese Izakaya Hidden from Foreign Travelers

  • Category: Japanese izakaya (sushi and sake)
  • Location: 301, 3F, 46 Chungmuro, Jung-gu, Seoul (4 min walk from Chungmuro station)
  • Foreign-traveler signal: ★ 0 foreign-language reviews (barely registered on Google Maps) — hidden, known only to local enthusiasts
  • Signature: Japanese small plates paired with sake
  • Foreign-traveler advantage: Almost no foreign visitors → a genuine taste of Japanese-Korean crossover dining / small space, 1–2 parties at a time
  • Key tip for foreign travelers: Reservations recommended (3rd floor, limited seating). Confirm English availability directly with the shop; Japanese helps more if you speak it.
  • Hours: 18:00–02:00 (closed Sundays)
  • Budget: ₩30,000–₩50,000/person (drinks included)

→ Next: 7 min walk (Chungmuro → Inhyeon-dong)

22:00–23:00 · Night (Wrap-up, Round 1 or 2)

8. Inhyeon Golbang (인현골방)

  • Category: An intimate music-bar concept (small space, song requests welcome)
  • Location: 3F, around Inhyeon-dong, Jung-gu, Seoul (7 min walk from Chungmuro station)
  • Foreign English reviews (23 on Google Maps / 1 foreign-language, ★ 5.0/5.0):

"Highly recommend this place!! The music and atmosphere was very good!! More than that, the experience in this bar was great. We were able to request 2~3 songs."

  • Signature: A cozy, closet-sized atmosphere plus a music bar where you can request songs
  • Foreign-traveler advantage: A genuine local bar few foreigners find / great BGM and music curation / solo-friendly
  • Key tip for foreign travelers: Only 6–10 seats. Confirm a seat ahead via Instagram DM or phone. No foreign-language menu, but cocktails and highballs are easy to order regardless.
  • Hours: 20:00–02:00 (closed Mondays)
  • Budget: ₩15,000–₩25,000/person (1–2 cocktails)

3. FAQ — 6 Questions Foreign Travelers Often Ask

Q1. Can you really walk the whole loop?

  • The full course is about 4–5km over 13 hours (09:00–22:00+), with 30–60 minutes of walking between stops and 30–90 minutes at each shop.
  • If you're not up for all the walking, you can take the subway one stop between Chungmuro and Euljiro 3-ga stations.

Q2. Payment — can I use WeChat Pay or Alipay?

  • Pao, Asoto Bakery, Oldies Taco, Ruri Coffee, Sarangbang Kalguksu: card, KakaoPay, Samsung Pay all accepted.
  • Kasu, Inhyeon Golbang: card accepted / check with the shop for WeChat or Alipay.
  • Kasu, Hanju Diary, and Inhyeon Golbang see few foreign visitors, so English payment guidance is limited — hold out your card with a bit of Korean and hand gestures.

Q3. Is there an English menu?

  • Pao / Asoto Bakery / Oldies Taco / Ruri Coffee: English menu ✓
  • Sarangbang Kalguksu: Partial English menu (friendly staff who can manage some English)
  • Hanju Diary / Kasu / Inhyeon Golbang: Mostly Korean (point at photos, use gestures, or Japanese if you speak it)

Q4. Can I go solo?

  • Pao, Asoto, Sarangbang, Ruri, Oldies Taco: ✓ (solo dining feels natural)
  • Hanju Diary, Kasu, Inhyeon Golbang: limited seats + reservations recommended → weekday evenings are best

Q5. Vegan, halal, or other dietary restrictions?

  • Vegan: Only drinks at Ruri Coffee and Asoto Bakery (americano, tea). Pao's donuts contain animal-derived ingredients.
  • Halal: No certified shops. Drinks only recommended.

Q6. Kid-friendly?

  • Sarangbang Kalguksu, Asoto, Oldies Taco: fine with kids
  • Kasu, Inhyeon Golbang, Hanju Diary: tight spaces + drink-focused → not recommended for kids
