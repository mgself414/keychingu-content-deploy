If Spicy Food Scares You — the No-Spice Korean Food Map: 20 Menu Items and Ordering Phrases for Kids, Seniors, and Spice Beginners

Subtitle: A field guide that busts the myth that "all Korean food is spicy" — 20 menu items that are "never spicy" broken down by soup, rice, meat, and side dish · 3 color traps that fool people · 5 ordering phrases that actually work at restaurants · what to feed kids in a country where kids' menus are rare

🎯 Why a "No-Spice Korean Food Map"

On Japanese travel forums, "I can't eat spicy food — what do I eat in Korea?" is a question that keeps coming back — a single thread from a family traveling with kids gets read almost 3,000 times and pulls 11 replies. Western travelers with families or seniors in tow face the same problem. And the answer always stops at "Samgyetang (삼계탕) or Gimbap (김밥), I guess" — but in reality, about half of Korean food is non-spicy from the start. This guide lays out that half by category, flags the traps you can fall into while dodging spice, and gives you the exact phrases that actually work at a restaurant counter. If you're ready to work your way up to spicy, flip to the companion guide (#201).

⚠ 3 Color Traps That Fool People

1. Red doesn't always mean spicy — brown, deep-red glazes like tteokgalbi or soy-braised galbijjim are sweet-savory, and even gochujang-based dishes like yangnyeom chicken (양념치킨) are often sweet enough for a kid to eat. If you're nervous, just ask: "Is this spicy? (이거 매워요?)"

2. White doesn't always mean mild — some places add cheongyang chili to white gyeranjjim or doenjang-jjigae. For soup dishes, it's safest to add "not spicy (안 맵게)" when you order.

3. "Not spicy" only works on dishes made to order — pre-made side dishes like kimchi, kkakdugi, and mumallaengi can't be changed. Just don't eat them — skipping a side dish isn't rude (#1339).

🗣 5 Ordering Phrases That Actually Work

Situation Korean Pronunciation
Make it not spicy from the start Maepji anke haejuseyo (맵지 않게 해주세요) maep-ji an-ke hae-ju-se-yo
Just a little less spicy Deol maepke haejuseyo (덜 맵게 해주세요) deol maep-ke hae-ju-se-yo
Skip the gochujang/sauce Gochujang (or yangnyeom) ppaejuseyo (고추장 빼주세요) go-chu-jang ppae-ju-se-yo
Tell them you can't eat spicy Maeun geo mot meogeoyo (매운 거 못 먹어요) mae-un geo mot meo-geo-yo
Double-check Igeo maewoyo? (이거 매워요?) i-geo mae-wo-yo?
  • One sentence is enough — Korean restaurants are used to "not spicy" requests, especially for soups and stir-fries. If you have a kid with you, "A-i-ga meo-geul geo-ye-yo (아이가 먹을 거예요)" ("this is for my child") works best.
  • Chain restaurants (fried chicken, tteokbokki) list spice levels right on the menu — just pick "mild / level 1" (#65, #66).

👶 Feeding Kids in a Country Where Kids' Menus Are Rare

Korean restaurants almost never have a "kids' menu." Instead, these items act as the de facto kids' menu: Gimbap, jumeokbap (rice balls), Gyeranjjim, udon, donkatsu (Western-style set), so-gogi-mu-guk (beef radish soup), Tteokguk, Bulgogi. Korean BBQ restaurants are extremely kid-friendly — a combo of Doenjang-jjigae (not spicy) + rice + Gyeranjjim makes a full meal. Convenience stores are a safe zone too: tuna-mayo or ham triangle gimbap (samgak-gimbap), cup udon.

⚙ 3 FAQs

1. Is Korean food really mostly spicy? — No. The spicy dishes are mostly the gochujang/gochugaru (chili flake) family — some jjigae, stir-fries, and mixed dishes — while most soups, grilled dishes, jeon, porridge, and namul (seasoned vegetables) are non-spicy from the start. The table above is that half.

2. Where doesn't "not spicy" work? — Anything pre-marinated (like yangnyeom galbi or pre-marinated spicy pork) and pre-made side dishes. For those, switching to a different menu item is faster than asking for "less spicy."

3. Want to work up to spicy food gradually? — The order is: soy-sauce-based (Bulgogi) → mild gochujang (the sweet version of yangnyeom chicken) → mild tteokbokki → jjigae. After that, move on to the #201 spicy challenge guide.
