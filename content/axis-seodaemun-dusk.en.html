Seoul E-W Axis ② Seodaemun and Independence Gate, at Dusk — The Walking Order from Independence Gate to Yeongcheon Market

Subtitle: The western starting point of Seoul's E-W Axis, walked through the site where a gate once stood. From Dongnimmun Station through Seodaemun Prison History Hall, Independence Gate and the Seo Jae-pil statue, to Dongnimmun Yeongcheon Market, ending at Seodaemun Station or the Bus 720 stop — starting in the afternoon and following the light down, about two hours. A neighborhood KeyChinGu has never covered before. Not a single shop is named. Seoul E-W Axis, Part 2.

ISSUE · #1357 · 2026-09-07

🎯 The Frame — The Axis's Western End, Where Two Gates Stood

The Seoul E-W Axis overview (#1356) boarded the bus in this neighborhood. This piece doesn't ride — it walks. The Seodaemun area once had two gates. One was Donuimun (돈의문), the west gate of the Joseon capital's fortress wall, demolished in 1915 and now marked only by its former site. The other is Independence Gate, built in 1897 and still standing. Between them, over a stretch of a little more than a kilometer, sit a former prison turned history hall, a park, and a traditional market, layered one after another.

This piece walks that ground late in the afternoon. You enter the history hall while the sun is still up, stand at Independence Gate as it starts to set, and pass through the market as evening customers gather. A neighborhood that tourist routes always skip — walked once, in order.

  • Total time: about 2 hours (about 40 minutes walking + up to 1 hour at the history hall + the market)
  • Best time: depart between 3 and 4 PM — this times you for the history hall's last admission (30 minutes before closing: 17:30 in summer, 16:30 in winter) and puts you in the market at dusk. The history hall is closed on Mondays
  • Incline: gentle to moderate — the park is flat, with a gentle climb toward Seodaemun Station. The optional Ansan Jarak-gil trail (안산 자락길) climbs uphill

🚶 The Route — In the Order You Walk It

The starting point is Dongnimmun Station Exit 5 on Line 3. Come out and the history hall's front gate is 50m to your left.

① Dongnimmun Station Exit 5 → Seodaemun Prison History Hall (🚶 about 2 minutes)

  • Built in 1908 and used as a prison until 1987, the building is now a history hall. Independence activists were held here under Japanese colonial rule, and pro-democracy activists after liberation. The exhibit presents the facts as they are — the cell blocks, execution chamber, and women's prison building all remain.
  • Hours: 09:30–18:00 March–October, 09:30–17:00 November–February, last entry 30 minutes before closing. Closed Mondays, January 1, and on Lunar New Year and Chuseok. Admission 3,000 won for adults. An hour is enough to see it, and even if you're short on time, just seeing the red brick wall and watchtower from outside carries the weight of this neighborhood.
  • 📖 Story 1 — What Remains Where a Gate Disappeared: This spot sat on the road climbing from Donuimun, the fortress's west gate, toward Muakjae (무악재). The Japanese Government-General demolished Donuimun in 1915, citing tram double-tracking and road straightening, and a prison had already stood on the hill to its west for seven years by then. The gate vanished; the prison remained — the reason the western end of Seoul's E-W Axis endures as "memory" rather than as a "gateway."

② History Hall → Seodaemun Independence Park (서대문독립공원) → Independence Gate and the Seo Jae-pil Statue (🚶 about 10 minutes)

  • From the history hall's front gate, into the park. Independence Gate stands at the park's southeast corner. Completed on 1897-11-20, 14.28m tall and 11.48m wide, modeled on the Arc de Triomphe in Paris — Korea's first Western-style stone structure. It was moved to its current spot, about 70m from its original location, during the 1979 Seongsan-ro construction.
  • West of the gate stands a statue of Seo Jae-pil (1990), from the Independence Club that built it. As the sun sets, the gate's stone turns red — one of the reasons this piece departs late in the afternoon.
  • Optional: 340m north of the park is the National Memorial of the Korean Provisional Government (국립대한민국임시정부기념관) (free, closed Mondays — confirm hours on the official site). Worth 30 minutes if you have the time.

③ Independence Gate → Dongnimmun Yeongcheon Market (🚶 about 10 minutes)

  • Cross Tongil-ro (통일로) in front of Independence Gate and you're right at the arcade entrance of Dongnimmun Yeongcheon Market. Running since the 1960s, this Seodaemun-gu traditional market has about 200 stalls, specializing in snack food like tteokbokki (떡볶이), kkwabaegi (꽈배기), and donuts. The market shows its true face in the evening rush, when commuters heading home pack the arcade.
  • No shop names here — walk the arcade once from entrance to end and pick by smell. This is the same entrance the overview (#1356) glimpsed through a bus window. There's a Bus 720 stop on Tongil-ro right in front of the market.

④ Yeongcheon Market → Toward Seodaemun Station (🚶 about 10 minutes · gentle uphill)

  • Leave the market and head south along Tongil-ro. The road climbs gently as it enters what was once inside the fortress wall. Geumhwa Elementary School on your left, and straight ahead, the Seodaemun Station intersection — the site where Donuimun once stood.
  • 📖 Story 2 — The Site of the West Gate: the area around the Seodaemun Station intersection is the site of Donuimun. A single marker stone and, across the street, Donuimun Museum Village (돈의문박물관마을) are what remain. Jongno (종로) begins east from here — the same road the overview rode by bus. This is the point where the walking feet reach the axis's 0km mark.

⑤ Wrap Up at Seodaemun Station — or Re-board Bus 720 (🚶 about 10 minutes)

  • End at Seodaemun Station (Line 5). If you want to keep walking inside the fortress wall, Jeongdong-gil (정동길), Gyeonghuigung (경희궁), and the Seoul Museum of History (서울역사박물관) are 10 minutes away (#141, #1244).
  • To keep tracing the axis: head back to the stop in front of the market and board Bus 720 toward Dapsimni, and you can ride #1356's segments ② through ⑧ in an evening version — past Bosingak and the night-lit Heunginjimun, on to Cheongnyangni.

🙏 Manners — the Core of This Course

  • The history hall is a memorial space. Keep your voice down inside the cell blocks and execution chamber, and follow posted signage on photographing exhibits. No selfie sticks or flash.
  • The park is a space for residents to walk. If you're taking extended photos in front of Independence Gate, keep the walkway clear.
  • The market arcade is narrow. Don't block the path as a group, and only sample food when a vendor offers it.
  • This neighborhood's history carries weight. Skip the playful poses in and around the history hall.

💡 Where You Might Get Thrown Off

  • The course is cut in half on Mondays — both the history hall and the Provisional Government memorial are closed. Go Tuesday through Sunday, afternoon.
  • Last admission is earlier than you'd expect: 30 minutes before closing (17:30 in summer, 16:30 in winter). Arrive after 4 PM and the visit gets tight — in that case, stick to the exterior and the park.
  • Pick the right exit: Dongnimmun Station Exit 5 leads toward the history hall and park. Come out a different exit and you'll have to cross Tongil-ro.
  • The market gets crowded in the evening: foot traffic inside the arcade slows down. If you're carrying bulky bags, stick to the edges.

6 FAQs

Q1. Is the history hall appropriate for foreign visitors?

  • Yes. The exhibit is fact-centered and has multilingual signage. It's a rare space that compresses the weight of modern Korean history into a single hour. That said, sections like the execution chamber are heavy, so adjust the course if you're with young children.

Q2. Independence from what?

  • The Independence Club built it in the 1890s to commemorate Joseon's sovereign independence from Qing influence. That's why it takes the shape of a triumphal arch. It's a different era from the independence movement under Japanese colonial rule — the history hall's exhibit picks up that later story.

Q3. Why is Donuimun gone?

  • It was demolished in 1915. It's the only one of the fortress's four main gates that no longer stands. A marker stone and the museum village now occupy the site.

Q4. What should I eat at the market?

  • Tteokbokki, kkwabaegi, and donuts are this market's specialty snacks. No specific shop is named — wherever there's a line is the answer.

Q5. Can I continue on to the Ansan Jarak-gil trail?

  • Yes. From Dongnimmun Station Exit 5, it's about 1.2km and 18 minutes through the park to the trailhead. The view over the Seodaemun area is good if you get there before sunset. Doing this, though, changes this piece's market-ending order.

Q6. How does this connect with the overview's (#1356) bus?

  • Board Bus 720 toward Dapsimni at the stop on Tongil-ro in front of the market. Walking this piece first, then riding the evening bus through Jongno on to Cheongnyangni, is the natural order.
