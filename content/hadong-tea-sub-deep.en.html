Hadong Wild Tea Guide — Hwagae Wild Tea Fields, Hadong Wild Tea Museum, Ssanggyesa Tea Tree First Planting Site, Hwagae Market, Choi Champan House

Subtitle: Hwagaedongcheon Wild Tea Fields (화개동천 야생차밭), open at all times · Hadong Wild Tea Museum (하동야생차박물관), free admission, closed Mondays · Tea Cafe Hadong (티카페하동) · Ssanggyesa Temple (쌍계사), 08:00–17:30 · Tea Tree First Planting Site (차나무 시배지) · Hwagae Market (화개장터), permanent market · Choi Champan House (최참판댁), 09:00–18:00 — confirmed via Korea Tourism Organization tourism data (TourAPI)

🎯 How to Use This Guide

Hwagae-myeon (화개면) in Hadong, Gyeongnam (경남), is the first tea planting site in Korea according to the Korea Tourism Organization, a place with 1,200 years of wild tea history. The wild tea fields that grew among the rocks of a Jirisan (지리산) valley, the Hadong Wild Tea Museum where you experience tea culture, and Ssanggyesa Temple, said to be where tea trees were first planted, are all in Hwagae-myeon. Where the Seomjin River (섬진강) and Hwagaecheon Stream (화개천) meet there is Hwagae Market, and in Pyeongsa-ri (평사리), Akyang-myeon (악양면), there is Choi Champan House, the setting of the novel "Toji" (『토지』).

This guide covers the six items in the table and the May festival, and the operating details are copied from values registered in the Korea Tourism Organization tourism data (TourAPI).

🍃 Hwagaedongcheon Wild Tea Fields

Hwagaedongcheon Wild Tea Fields (694 Jeonggeum-ri, Hwagae-myeon, Hadong-gun (하동군 화개면 정금리 694), ☎ Hadong County Office Tourism Promotion Division 055-880-2375) are fields of wild tea trees that grew in the valley and among the rocks of Hwagae-dong (화개동) on Jirisan. The tea trees grow in cloud-shaped clumps, so many photographers visit. They are one of the Ten Scenic Views of Hadong (하동 10경) chosen by Hadong County, and you can look down on the tea fields from a pavilion on a hill near Jeonggeum Tea Garden (정금다원). Hwagae-myeon tea was a tribute offering to the king.

  • Opening hours: Open at all times, open year-round. Parking available.

🏛 Hadong Wild Tea Museum — Where Experience Fees Are Set

Hadong Wild Tea Museum (571-25 Ssanggye-ro, Hwagae-myeon, Hadong-gun (하동군 화개면 쌍계로 571-25), ☎ museum 055-884-2955, experience hall 055-884-2896) is a museum built to preserve Hwagae's tea culture, and it runs participatory experience exhibits.

  • Fees: Museum admission is free. Tea ceremony experience 5,000 won, tea-leaf picking experience 3,000 won, pan-roasting (deokkeum, 덖음) experience 9,000 won, doncha (돈차) experience 9,000 won.
  • Opening hours: March–October (months 3–10) 10:00–18:00 (last entry 17:30), November–February (months 11–2) 10:00–17:00 (last entry 16:30).
  • Closed days: Every Monday, January 1, the days of Seollal and Chuseok.

Tea Cafe Hadong (티카페하동)

Hadong Wild Tea Healing Center Tea Cafe Hadong (571-27 Ssanggye-ro (쌍계로 571-27), ☎ 070-4171-8873) is a teahouse run directly by Hadong County. Its signature menu item is jaksaltang (잭살탕), and it also has Hadong traditional tea, green tea (ujeon), and Byeolcheonji waffles (별천지와플).

  • Opening hours: March–October (months 3–10) 09:30–18:30, November–February (months 11–2) 09:00–18:00. Last order 17:50.
  • Closed days: Every Monday, January 1, the days of Seollal and Chuseok.
  • Cards accepted, takeout available, parking available.

🛕 Ssanggyesa Temple and the Tea Tree First Planting Site

Ssanggyesa Temple (59 Ssanggyesa-gil, Hwagae-myeon, Hadong-gun (하동군 화개면 쌍계사길 59), ☎ 055-883-1901) is a temple built in the reign of King Seongdeok of Silla. In 840 the monk Jingam (진감선사) called it "Okcheonsa," and it was later renamed Ssanggyesa; it burned down in the Imjin War and was rebuilt in 1632. Its cherry blossoms are especially famous, and it has the calligraphy "Ssanggyeseokmun" (쌍계석문) by Choe Chiwon (최치원), the Stele of Master Jingam (진감선사 대공탑비), and Buril Falls (불일폭포). A temple stay is registered in the experience information.

  • Opening hours: 08:00–17:30, open year-round. Parking available.

The Ssanggyesa Tea Tree First Planting Site (쌍계사차나무시배지) is said to be where Kim Daeryeom (김대렴), who had gone to Tang China as an envoy, brought back tea tree seeds in 828 (year 3 of King Heungdeok of Silla) and first planted them by royal order. There is a memorial stele for the tea planting by Daeryeomgong (대렴공 차시배 추원비), and it was registered as the official tea first planting site in July 2008 (month 7). The tea here is called jungnocha or jaksaltea, and it is introduced as being made from leaves picked for about a month starting from Gogu (곡우). Open at all times.

🛒 Hadong Hwagae Market

Hadong Hwagae Market (15 Ssanggye-ro, Hwagae-myeon, Hadong-gun (하동군 화개면 쌍계로 15), ☎ 055-883-5722) is a traditional market where the Seomjin River and Hwagaecheon Stream meet, a marketplace where people of Yeongnam (영남) and Honam (호남) bought and sold goods. It is known from Kim Dongri's (김동리) novel "Yeokma" (『역마』) and the song "Hwagae Market" (화개장터). It reopened in 2001 with traditional market buildings and green tea specialty shops, and it is now a permanent market that opens without market days.

  • What is sold: Chestnuts, wild mountain greens, farm produce, medicinal herbs.
  • Opening hours and closed days: They differ by shop. Parking available.

🏯 Choi Champan House — The Setting of "Toji"

Choi Champan House (66-7 Pyeongsa-ri-gil, Akyang-myeon, Hadong-gun (하동군 악양면 평사리길 66-7), ☎ Hadong County Office Tourism Promotion Division 055-880-2825) is a recreation, in 14 hanok buildings, of the Choi Champan family house in Park Kyung-ni's (박경리) epic novel "Toji." There is also a drama set that recreates life in the late Joseon period, and the Toji Literature Festival is held every autumn.

  • Opening hours: 09:00–18:00, open year-round. Parking available.

📅 Hadong Wild Tea Culture Festival (하동야생차문화축제) — May (month 5)

The Hadong Wild Tea Culture Festival is held at the Hadong Wild Tea Healing Center and the Hadong Wild Tea Museum. In 2026 it ran May 1–5, 10:00–18:00, with free admission and some paid experiences. The 2027 schedule is not yet posted in the Korea Tourism Organization information.

⚠ 3 Common Mistakes

1. Booking the museum and the teahouse on a Monday — The Hadong Wild Tea Museum and Tea Cafe Hadong are both closed every Monday.

2. Thinking you can do experiences until 18:00 in winter — The museum closes at 17:00 in November–February (months 11–2), and last entry is 16:30.

3. Thinking Hwagae Market has market days — It is now a permanent market that opens without market days.

⚙ FAQ 2

Q. When can I pick tea leaves?

A. According to the Korea Tourism Organization, Ssanggyesa tea is made from leaves picked for about a month starting from Gogu. The tea-leaf picking experience at the Hadong Wild Tea Museum is 3,000 won, and its operating period is not listed in the Korea Tourism Organization information.

Q. Can I use a card?

A. According to the Korea Tourism Organization, cards are accepted at Tea Cafe Hadong. For the other places, card information is not listed.
