Why Golf, Why Now — the #1 Draw for JP, Middle Eastern, and CN Segments

Korean golf is the #1 market for Japanese (JP) golf enthusiasts, plus a Middle Eastern segment (#325) and a CN (Chinese) luxury segment. What sets this piece apart from the #542 luxury-golf deep dive is the full foreign-visitor round lineup (9-hole ₩60K to 18-hole ₩170K, plus luxury and screen golf). All within an hour of Gangnam or Gyeonggi, with no-caddie self-carry options and rounds for two or three players.

Korean golf's core edge over Japan is price. Japan's public 18-hole average runs ¥12,000–18,000 (roughly ₩110,000–160,000), while Korea's greater-Seoul public courses run ₩120,000–150,000 on weekdays — similar or cheaper. Even with caddie fees added, the total often still comes in below Japan. That's the #1 reason JP golfers pick Korea for a golf trip.

This matrix covers public 9-hole (3 picks) + regulation 18-hole (3 picks) + luxury golf (3 picks, cross-ref #542) + screen & indoor golf (3 picks) = 12 picks across 4 axes.

3 Common Mistakes Foreign Visitors Make

Mistake 1 — Assuming the caddie fee is included in the green fee

Most Korean golf courses bill the caddie fee separately from the green fee. For a 4-person team, that's an extra ₩80,000-100,000, or roughly ₩20,000-25,000 per person. Some public courses run no-caddie self-carry rounds, so always confirm whether a caddie is required before booking. Check the booking screen for "Walking only / No caddie available."

Mistake 2 — Trying to book a weekend round on short notice

Popular greater-Seoul public courses fill up 2-4 weeks ahead for weekends and holidays. Visitors who try to book a weekend round after arriving in Korea usually find nothing available. It's more realistic to book through kimcaddie.com or the Kakao Golf Reservation app before arrival, or plan for a weekday round instead.

Mistake 3 — Ignoring the dress code

Korean golf courses, especially at the semi-luxury tier and above, enforce a strict dress code. Jeans, sneakers, and sleeveless shirts are grounds for being turned away. Even public courses generally require a collared shirt and golf shoes. Golf shoe rental (₩5,000-10,000) is often available at the course's rental shop, but size availability is limited.

Axis 1: Public 9-Hole (3 Picks) — Value at ₩60K

1. Korea Public CC (Yongin)

Location: Cheoin-gu, Yongin, Gyeonggi. 30-40 minutes from Gangnam by car.

Green fee (2026): 9-hole ₩60,000-75,000, 18-hole ₩110,000-130,000. Runs as a no-caddie self-carry course — no caddie fee. One of the few courses near Seoul where you can play a full 18-hole grass round at this price point.

Foreigner fit: ★★★★★. Night rounds available, no-caddie self-carry, two-player bookings accepted. Low language barrier and a simple check-in process. Good for first-time visiting golfers.

Booking: kimcaddie.com or Korea Public CC's official website. Weekday bookings can sometimes be made 3-7 days ahead, but weekends are best booked 2 weeks out.

English support: Staff have partial English ability. The booking site is mostly in Korean, so a translation app helps.

Access: 20 minutes by car from Bundang or Pangyo. No convenient direct public transit — a rental car or Kakao T is recommended.

2. Lakeside CC (public, Goyang)

Location: Deogyang-gu, Goyang, Gyeonggi. 30-40 minutes by car from Hongdae or Mapo.

Green fee (2026): 18-hole weekday ₩130,000-150,000. A public course, but the condition is solid. The lake-and-mountain backdrop makes it a favorite among JP and CN golfers who value the photo opportunity.

Foreigner fit: ★★★★. Caddie required. English names can be entered at booking. Good clubhouse facilities.

Booking: Kakao Golf Reservation app or Lakeside CC's official site. Confirm before booking.

Access: By car toward Susaek or Neunggok stations. Can be combined with an airport bus transfer (Incheon Airport → airport bus → Neunggok → car).

3. Blue Heron CC (Yangju)

Location: Yangju, Gyeonggi. 20 minutes by car from Uijeongbu, 40-50 minutes from northern Seoul.

Green fee (2026): 18-hole weekday ₩120,000-145,000. On the more affordable end for the greater-Seoul area.

Foreigner fit: ★★★★. Solid course condition. English support is limited, so a translation app is essential. Handy if you're based in the northern Seoul metro area.

Access: By car toward Uijeongbu Station, via the national road toward Dongducheon.

4. Bear Creek GC (Yongin)

Location: Giheung-gu, Yongin, Gyeonggi. 30-40 minutes by car from Gangnam or Bundang.

Green fee (2026): 18-hole weekday ₩140,000-170,000. Certain weekday time slots carry discounted rates — check kimcaddie for details.

Foreigner fit: ★★★★. Good access from the metro area, and two-player bookings are relatively easy to secure. Reportedly one of the courses with a high JP-golfer repeat-visit rate, based on local golf community reviews — not an official statistic.

Booking: kimcaddie.com or East Valley CC's official site.

6. Pine Creek CC (Gwangju)

Location: Gwangju, Gyeonggi. 40-50 minutes by car from Gangnam.

Green fee (2026): 18-hole weekday ₩130,000-160,000.

Foreigner fit: ★★★. The course itself is good, but English guidance is limited. Easier with a Korean-speaking companion along.

Access: By car toward Gyeonggi-gwangju Station, or via a transfer at Moran Station.

Location: Yeoju, Gyeonggi. 1 hour to 1 hour 20 minutes by car from Seoul.

Green fee (2026): Primarily members-only. Non-member rounds run ₩200,000 and up — confirm usage terms before your visit. Ranked among Asia's top 100 golf courses, with design and scenery to match its luxury tier.

Location: Yeongjongdo, Jung-gu, Incheon. 15-20 minutes by car from Incheon International Airport.

Green fee (2026): Non-member play runs ₩200,000-250,000. Its direct airport link makes it ideal for a round right after arrival or right before departure.

Foreigner fit: ★★★★. Its direct Incheon Airport link is the core strength. A high share of staff can handle English. JP and Middle Eastern segments frequently use it for an airport-layover round. Cart-based play, top-tier turf condition.

Booking: Jack Nicklaus GC Korea's official site has an English version. International card payment accepted.

English support: English-language booking available. English scorecards provided.

Axis 4: Screen & Indoor Golf (3 Picks)

10. GOLFZON Screen Golf

Brand overview: GOLFZON is Korea's #1 screen golf brand by market share, with over 7,000 locations nationwide. Many sit in foreigner-heavy areas like Gangnam, Hongdae, Myeongdong, Sinchon, and Itaewon.

Rate (2026): ₩10,000-20,000/hour per person. Varies by location, time of day, and round vs. practice mode. A bit pricier during evening peak hours (7-10pm).

Foreigner fit: ★★★★★. English UI supported. Play virtual rounds on famous world courses (Augusta, Pebble Beach, and more). Weather- and season-independent. Playable with just two people. Many locations allow you to bring your own drinks.

Booking: Walk-in same-day or via the GOLFZON app. Book ahead for evenings at core commercial-district locations.

English support: The machine's UI has English. Staff English varies by location — Gangnam and Itaewon branches often handle English well.

How to find one: Search "GOLFZON near me" on Naver Map or Google Maps. Several locations sit within 500m of Gangnam Station alone.

11. Kakao VX Friends Screen

Brand overview: A screen golf brand run by Kakao VX. GOLFZON's competing brand, with roughly 3,000 locations nationwide. Its Kakao character interface (Ryan, Apeach, and more) makes it approachable even for non-golfer foreign visitors.

Rate (2026): ₩15,000-20,000/hour per person.

Foreigner fit: ★★★★. English UI. The friendly Kakao character interface suits beginners, couples, and families. For foreign visitors trying screen golf for the first time, it feels less intimidating than GOLFZON.

Booking: Kakao VX app or walk-in.

12. Indoor Golf Practice Ranges (Gangnam, Apgujeong, Itaewon)

Overview: Rented by the bay, by the hour. Unlike screen golf, the focus is real ball-striking practice. Club rental is available at many locations.

Rate (2026): ₩5,000-15,000/hour per bay. Varies by location and facilities.

Foreigner fit: ★★★. Good for golfers focused on improving their swing. Explanations tend to be Korean-only though, so beginners may find screen golf more convenient.

Finding one in Gangnam: Search "golf practice range Gangnam" on Naver Map. Indoor ranges near Apgujeong and Cheongdam tend to have higher-end facilities.

Practical Info

Transportation & Access

Rental car: Most greater-Seoul courses are inconvenient by public transit. A day's rental car (₩50,000-80,000) plus free parking (at most courses) is the realistic option. An international license plus card payment works fine.

Kakao T / Uber: A one-way ride from Gangnam to a Yongin course runs roughly ₩40,000-60,000. Book your return ride in advance — cars are scarce near rural courses after a round.

Screen golf reachable by transit: Gangnam, Hongdae, and Myeongdong screen golf locations are directly accessible by subway. No rental car needed if screen golf is your only goal.

Booking

kimcaddie.com: Korea's #1 platform for comparing and booking greater-Seoul public courses. Mostly Korean, but usable by foreign visitors with Google Translate running alongside. Real-time green-fee comparisons by date.

Kakao Golf Reservation: Booking tied to Kakao Map and Kakao T. A relatively simple interface.

Course official sites: Courses targeting foreign visitors, like Jack Nicklaus GC, run an English booking page.

Booking timing: Weekday = 3-7 days ahead / Weekend = 2-4 weeks ahead / Spring or fall peak-season weekend = 4+ weeks ahead.

Language & Communication

Courses with English booking: Jack Nicklaus GC Korea (Yeongjongdo), Haesley Nine Bridges (confirm with the official site), and select resort-affiliated courses.

Using translation apps: Naver Papago's camera translation can instantly translate Korean scorecards, menus, and signage.

Emergency communication: 1330 (24-hour tourist interpretation) — connects you to an interpreter for booking disputes or language issues at the course.

Equipment Rental

Club rental is available at most public courses and screen golf locations. Public-course club rental runs ₩10,000-20,000. Some locations offer shoe rental too, but sizing runs to the Korean standard (mostly 250-280mm), so visitors with larger feet should check ahead.

For golf apparel, you can buy international brands (Titleist, TaylorMade, PXG, and more) in Gangnam, along Garosu-gil in Sinsa-dong, and at IFC Mall (Yeouido). Korea ranks among the world's top markets for golf apparel spending, with many Korea-exclusive designs.

FAQ 6

Q1. Where should a foreign visitor start their first round?

Korea Public CC's (Yongin) 9-hole, or a greater-Seoul public 18-hole on a weekday, is the realistic starting point. Choosing a no-caddie self-carry course minimizes the language barrier. On kimcaddie.com, filter for "self-carry course + weekday + green fee under ₩140,000" to surface options.

Q2. What total budget should I plan for?

  • 9-hole value round: Green fee ₩60,000-75,000 + transport (round-trip Kakao T ₩80,000-120,000) = total ₩140,000-200,000/person
  • 18-hole public (with caddie): Green fee ₩130,000-170,000 + caddie fee ₩20,000-25,000 + transport = total ₩200,000-280,000/person
  • 18-hole luxury (Jack Nicklaus and similar): ₩200,000-250,000 + caddie fee + transport = total ₩300,000-400,000/person
  • Screen golf (2 hours): ₩20,000-40,000/person + subway fare

Q3. Which courses suit the JP and Middle Eastern segments best?

JP golfers: Korea Public CC (value, no-caddie) and Bear Creek GC (intermediate). More price-competitive than Japan with solid course condition. The kimcaddie app is mostly Korean, so pair it with Papago.

Middle Eastern segment: Jack Nicklaus GC Korea (English support, airport link) and Haesley Nine Bridges (luxury course). Combine luxury golf with a broader Korea experience for the luxury cluster (cross-ref #542, #325).

Q4. How do you bundle this into a full luxury series package?

#542 luxury golf + #595 rounds + #577 luxury hotels + #576 luxury cars together form the luxury cluster. A concrete route: Jack Nicklaus GC (golf) → airport pickup by Mercedes or BMW (#576) → Signiel Seoul (#577) → dinner in Cheongdam = a full 1-night, 2-day package for enthusiast visitors.

Q5. What's the best season for a round?

Spring (Mar-May): April's cherry blossom season gives the best course scenery. It's also when Korean golfers crowd the courses most, so booking competition is fierce — book 4-5 weeks ahead.

Fall (Sep-Nov): Rounds set against autumn foliage. Gyeonggi and Gangwon courses peak in color mid-October through early November. Peak season = book weekends 4+ weeks ahead.

Summer (Jun-Aug): An early-morning round (6-7am start) is recommended to beat the heat. During monsoon season (mid-July to early August), switch to screen golf.

Winter (Dec-Feb): Courses south of the greater-Seoul area sometimes stay open through winter, but many in Gangwon and North Chungcheong close for the season. Screen golf runs year-round.

Q6. Can you enjoy screen golf even if you can't play golf?

Yes. Both GOLFZON and Friends Screen have difficulty-adjustment modes in their English UI (mulligans included, error-forgiving settings). Foreign couples and families who've never played real golf often use it for 2-3 hours of entertainment. Gangnam and Hongdae locations often allow drink orders (beer, soju), making it a good fit for a social outing.

5 Tips for Enthusiast Visitors

1. Bookmark kimcaddie.com — Real-time green-fee comparison across greater-Seoul public courses. Check the lowest rate by date. Use Google Translate's browsing mode.

2. Pick weekdays, and early weekday slots — ₩20,000-30,000 cheaper than weekends, with less booking competition.

3. Jack Nicklaus GC Korea = direct airport link — Yeongjongdo location, English support, international card payment. The only major course where a same-day arrival or departure round is realistic.

4. Screen golf = a rain backup in the city — GOLFZON's Gangnam and Hongdae locations take weekday-daytime walk-ins with no booking needed. A quick fallback if a real-course round gets rained out.

5. Know the caddie-tip culture — Korean caddie fees are billed as a flat team charge at the counter; a separate tip isn't mandatory. A ₩5,000-10,000 cash tip for good service is customary, but never required.
