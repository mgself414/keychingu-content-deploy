All Guides › Where Locals (and Civil Servants) Actually Eat in Jongno & Gwanghwamun — Data-Verified Spots Tourists Miss

Restaurants in Jongno and Gwanghwamun that locals and government officials really go to – a well-established data verification store that tourists don’t know about

Gwanghwamun·Jongno Lunch Belt — When walking through Gyeongbokgung Palace and Insa-dong, the question you face at lunch time is: “Apart from restaurants for tourists, where do people who work here eat?” Lunchtime favorites for government officials and office workers in the Seoul Government Complex and Jongno Office District were selected after verifying with visitation data. Not a list of the BEST 10, but places that locals really go to.

As you walk along the stone walls of Gyeongbokgung Palace, you will see endless crowds of people wearing hanbok and tour buses. However, inside one block, in the alley between the Seoul Government Complex and the office buildings in Jongno, there is a completely different scene. At 12 noon, civil servants and office workers with employee ID cards around their necks line up at this long-established restaurant. They eat here every day — not the expensive Korean food for tourists, but the 30-year-old kimchi stew and gomtang restaurant that opens at 7:30.

This guide compiled the 'Local Lunch Belt' by verifying it with data. The source was Korea's 'Civil Servant Restaurant' curation (kofficer-guide), which received visit count data for 57 places in Jongno-gu, and selected only places accessible within the tourist flow line (Gyeongbokgung Palace, Insa-dong, Gwanghwamun). The number of visits and business hours for each store are actual data. Unlike the "Seoul Best 10" that foreign guidebooks have compiled 10,000 times, the houses featured here have been verified by the votes of people working in the neighborhood.

💡 Why ‘Civil Servant Restaurants’ is a signal: Employees of government buildings and public offices eat ① every day ② at their own expense ③ within an hour of lunch time. In other words, only houses that have proven cost-effectiveness, speed, and consistent taste survive. It is a list that is filtered out of pure ‘re-visit’, beyond the reach of tourism marketing.

1. Gomtang and Seolleongtang — At 7:30 in the morning, a civil servant’s day starts with soup.

Gwanghwamun office workers' daily routine on the way to work is a bowl of gomtang. Slurp up the rich beef bone broth with rice and radish radish radish - this is why they open from 7 to 8 o'clock.

  • Iyeo Gomtang · 70 Yulgok-ro 4-gil, Jongno-gu · Open at 07:30 · Number of visits: 19

A bowl of clear and rich gomtang. Since it is within walking distance of Insa-dong and Unhyeongung Palace, it is a good place to fill your stomach before visiting the palace early in the morning.

  • Najuso Najugomtang · 87 Saemunan-ro, Jongno-gu · Open at 08:00 · Number of visits 17

Jeolla-do Naju style gomtang. Since it is right next to the Seoul Government Complex, you can stop by immediately after seeing Gwanghwamun Square and the Statue of King Sejong.

  • 🔗 Deeper: For a full comparison of Gomtang and Gukbap, refer to [Seoul Gukbap Guide]. The 120-year-old Imun Seolnongtang is the oldest restaurant in Korea and is located one stop from Jongno.

Tip for foreigners — Gomtang is usually seasoned by adding salt and green onions. The salt shaker and chopped green onions on the table are for that purpose. A local way to eat radish radish with a little bit of soup.

2. Loach stew and health food — a bowl of ‘recovery food’

Chueotang (a stew made with ground loach) may be unfamiliar to foreigners, but for Korean office workers, it is synonymous with energy replenishment. A ‘locals only’ menu that competes with taste rather than appearance.

  • Gureumsan Chueotang · B1, 92 Saemunan-ro, Jongno-gu · Number of visits: 23

It is located in the underground shopping mall of Gwanghwamun D Tower and Government Complex, so it is crowded with office workers during lunch. A thick soup with plenty of perilla seeds.

  • Damso Nurungji Baeksuk · 81 Sambong-ro, Jongno-gu · Open at 10:00 · Number of visits: 21

Baeksuk (chicken health food) with added scorched rice. Recommended for group travelers as it is easy to share.

  • Hwang’s Daechueotang · 81 Sambong-ro, Jongno-gu · Number of visits: 18 — Another great loach soup in the same Sambong-ro block.

3. Baekban·Jjigae — 30-year-old taste, happiness worth 10,000 won

The most ‘civil servant restaurant’ category. It's not fancy, but it's a home-cooked meal that you won't get tired of every day.

  • Honggeum Sikdang · 77-3 Jibong-ro, Jongno-gu · Visits 49 (No. 1 in Jongno)

The most visited house in this dataset. With a simple, home-cooked meal style, it’s the ultimate in ‘local verification’.

  • Jongga Kimchi Jjigae · 64 Sambong-ro, Jongno-gu · 11:00~22:00 · Number of visits 18

Kimchi stew boiled with ripe old kimchi. It is best to eat it with egg rolls and pork.

  • Gwanghwamun House / Gwanghwamun Ryu — A long-established group of ‘Gwanghwamun kimchi stew and pork’ that appears repeatedly in SERPs. The signature spicy kimchi stew + egg rolls from a 30-year-old recipe.

Tip for foreigners — Baekban restaurants usually require one person to order a la carte, and many have unlimited refills (self-service bar) for side dishes. Some long-established stores only accept cash, so bring small amounts of cash.

4. Meat, noodles, cafe — lunch specials and morning coffee

  • Gwanghwamun Sirloin · 2nd floor, 34 Jongno 5-gil · 11:00~ · Number of visits 22 — Lunch special sirloin and ribs. No. 1 for company dinner.
  • Myeongdong Kalguksu (Insadong Branch) · 29 Insadong-gil · Number of visits 23 — It’s in the middle of Insadong, so it overlaps exactly with the tourist route. Combination of hand kalguksu + dumplings. 🔗 For information on Jongno Grandma Kalguksu, etc., refer to [Seoul Kalguksu/Noodle Guide].
  • Cafe Maggio · 103 Jongno 5-gil · Open at 06:30 · Number of visits 37 — A mecca for morning coffee on the way to work. One of the earliest cafes in Jongno.
  • Coffee@Works · Ima Building, 42 Jongno 1-gil · Open at 07:00 · Number of visits 23 — A cup of specialty. A caffeine charging station for office workers in Gwanghwamun.

5. Extras — Gwangjang Market: Tourist destination and locals’ dining table

Gwangjang Market · 88 Changgyeonggung-ro, Jongno-gu · 100+ year traditional market

It is a must-see for tourists, but it is also a place where locals really eat. Bindaetteok (mung bean pancake), drug gimbap, raw meat, and kalguksu at street stalls. It's a good place to stop by at the end of a tour of Jongno's long-established stores. 🔗 The entire market is [Full guide to the Korean market].

  • Transportation: Gwanghwamun Station (Line 5), Jonggak Station (Line 1), and Anguk Station (Line 3) form a triangle within walking distance. Most stores are within a 10-minute walk.
  • Timing: Exactly 12:00~13:00 rush for civil servants and office workers — The line is long. For foreigners, it is more comfortable to arrive at 11:30 or after 13:30 (paradox of locals’ verification: it means it’s that good).

Key summary for foreigners

  • The value of this list is not 'fame' but 'return visits by people who go every day'.
  • Most menus are single menu, around 10,000 won — Enjoy a local meal without the burden.
  • The three top tips are preparing cash, avoiding the rush (11:30/13:30), and searching Korean name on Naver Map.
  • Same route as Gyeongbokgung Palace, Insa-dong, and Gwanghwamun tour — No need to go far, just one block away.
