Seoul Craft Cocktail Bar Guide — Asia’s 50 Best Gourmet Cocktail Bars

> What makes it different: Unlike Seoul's guide to local bars, pochas, and makgeolli, this article only collects gourmet (craft) cocktail bars that world bar critics visit every year. Hanok bar, traditional liquor cocktail, hidden speakeasy — a drink only available in Korea.

> ⚠️ Ranking, address, and operation information are subject to change. Before visiting, search for the business name on Naver Map/Instagram to check sales and reservations.

Seoul has emerged as one of Asia's best cocktail cities over the past decade. Every year, several Seoul bars are included in Asia's 50 Best Bars, and one is also included in World's 50 Best. The background of “Why Seoul became the cocktail capital” is covered in the sister edition (Korean cocktail bar culture explainer), and this article provides guidance by neighborhood where to actually go.

⚠️ 3 things foreigners should know

1. Reservations are the answer — Famous bars have long lines or limited seating. Make a reservation in advance via Catch Table, Instagram DM, or phone. In particular, reservations are required for hotel tasting bars (H Bar, etc.).

2. OK even if you don’t know cocktails — Seoul bars are famous for their hospitality. Just tell us your preference in the form of “refreshing/smoky/not-sweet” and the bartender will make a recommendation. There are many bars without menus.

3. Be sure to try soju and makgeolli cocktails — The global differentiation of Seoul bars is traditional liquor and Korean ingredients. This is a combination you can only drink in Korea, so be sure to give it a try.

🍸 Gangnam (Cheongdam, Apgujeong, Sinsa) — The birthplace of Seoul’s cocktail scene

This is the neighborhood where Seoul’s craft cocktail scene first blossomed. Attractions are gathered around Dosan-daero in Cheongdam-dong, making it great for bar hopping.

  • Zest — Cheongdam. Seoul No.1, Asia #2·World #16 (2025, Check recommended). An exquisite cup made with Zero Waste philosophy of reusing discarded ingredients and ingredients from Korea (Jeju tangerines, Gujwa carrots, etc.). If I had to go to just one place in Seoul, it would be the number one candidate. Reservation recommended.
  • Le Chamber — Cheongdam. You have to press the button on the bookshelf to open the door. Original of the Seoul scene, run by a world champion bartender and featuring over 200 types of whiskey (listed continuously since 2016).
  • Alice (Alice) — Cheongdam/Apgujeong. ‘Alice in Wonderland’ themed bar accessed through secret door inside the flower shop. Molecular cocktail and fantastic production. 2025 Asia #13 (Rising Award).
  • Pine & Co (Pine & Co) — Apgujeong. Retro sensibility of Seoul in the 1980s and 1990s + cutting-edge techniques such as vacuum and rotovape + traditional Korean materials. Asia expansion list #52.
  • Cricket Seoul (Cricket) — Near Apgujeong. Specialized in Korean traditional liquor education and tasting. Experience little-known Korean alcohol from all over the country with tap cocktails. Ideal for introduction to Korean alcohol.
  • Elements — Cheongdam. A dining bar where you can enjoy cocktails + pasta together. Pasta changes every day.
  • Villa Records — Sinsa-dong basement. Vinyl listening lounge with a space age sensibility from the 1960s and 1970s. Recommended for groups of more than 4 people as it is one of the largest scales in Seoul. Classic and molecular versions together.
  • MIXOLOGY — Gangnam. A place where cocktails are treated like a science. Famous for ice sculpture and master classes.

🏯 Jongno (Seochon, Bukchon, Gwanghwamun) — Hanok and traditional cocktails

A neighborhood steeped in tradition. Hanok bar and historical concept bar come together, making both the photos and atmosphere special.

  • Bar Cham — Seochon (Jahamun-ro area). In a renovated hanok space, the menu is divided into 6 regions across the country and cocktails are made with traditional liquor and products from each region. A bar that best displays Korean culture. Asia #6. Get there early as the line can be long.
  • Bar Pomme — Tongin-dong, Seochon. Bar specializing in apple terroir from the Bar Cham team. Based on Korean apple brandy and Calvados directly connected to Chungju apple farmers. (If the line at Bar Cham is long, it is also a great nearby alternative.)
  • Cham in Season — Sajik-ro. The Bar Cham team's Seasonal Special 2nd branch. The entire menu changes each season using seasonal Korean ingredients (fall = apples, pears, pumpkin, etc.).
  • Gong-Gan (space) — Anguk·Bukchon Hanok Village alley. In the Hanok Courtyard structure, unwind your childhood memories with a cocktail and practice upcycling. Asia #63 (large increase). Good to combine with Bukchon sightseeing.
  • Charles H (Charles H) — Basement of Four Seasons Hotel in Gwanghwamun. 1920s-30s Art Deco Speakeasy. It's comfortable, unlike a hotel bar, and the service is excellent. Asia #96.
  • H Bar — Four Seasons Hotel, inside Charles H. Tasting course (seasonal cocktail + pairing, approx. 150,000 won per person, confirmation recommended) in 8-seat exclusive space. Reservation required, for special occasions.
  • Cobbler — Yeonhui-dong Hanok. Upon entering, you will receive Blueberry Cobbler Pie as a welcome snack. A classic drink made through conversation with the bartender without a menu (daiquiri recommended). Small space with 20 seats.

🌃 Other neighborhoods — Mapo, Yongsan, Eunpyeong

Neighborhood bars outside the city center. To enjoy local sensibility and high quality together.

  • Antidote — Mapo Gongdeok and Daeheung (near Gyeongui Line Forest Road). Prescription (antidote) concept — Korean ingredients in signature cocktails for each symptom. Transparency that even shows the production process video through each cocktail QR. Instagram sentiment.
  • Notes — Near Yongsan Hyochang Park Station. Although it is a small bar close to the neighborhood, it offers high-end cocktails. When you want a quiet place away from tourist attractions.
  • Kissk (foot) — Eunpyeong Galhyeon-dong, Bukhansan Mountain ('foot'=mountain foot). Open until 5 a.m. with a disco-era feel. Even though it is located in the outskirts, most recommended by bartenders “Bartender’s Bar”. Good for a leisurely finish.
  • MMS — A whiskey-centric design bar, known for its most experimental (eccentric) menu (anchovy foam, smoked duck, etc.). It is recommended to check the exact location before visiting.

🗺️ Practical guide for foreigners

  • Reservation: Popular bars and hotel tasting bars can be reserved in advance via Catch Table/Insta DM/phone. Compliance with number of people is required as there are many bars with few seats.
  • English: The more famous the bar, the more likely it is that English will be provided. All you have to say is your preference (taste, alcohol content).
  • Cost: Approximately 20,000-25,000 won for a cocktail, higher for hotels and tastings (recommended to check timing and exchange rate). Service charge included, so no tip required.
  • ID Card: Entry only for those over 19 years old — bring Passport.
  • Recommended route (bar hopping): Cheongdam (Zest → Le Chamber → Alice) or Seochon (Bar Cham → Pomme → nearby). Jongno is connected to Bukchon Tourism (Gong-Gan), and Gwanghwamun is connected to Four Seasons (Charles H).
  • Timing: Weekdays (Sun~Thurs) when evenings are quiet. It's crowded on Fridays and Saturdays, so visit right after opening.

❓ FAQ

1. How many places per day is appropriate? — 2-3 places. Slowly, one drink at a time. If you group them in the same neighborhood (Cheongdam, Seochon), the trip will be shorter.

2. Dress code? — Mostly casual OK. However, it is best to avoid wearing too casual clothes (slippers, sportswear) at hotel bars (such as Charles H).

3. Even if you go alone? — If you sit at the bar, it's natural to visit alone. It's also nice to chat with the bartender.

4. Can I take pictures? — In most cases, it is possible, but be considerate of seating and other guests. Some speakeasies restrict filming at the entrance or inside, so ask.

5. Recommended bars for traditional liquor cocktails? — Bar Cham (6 regions), Cricket (traditional liquor tasting), and Zest (Korean ingredients) are representative.
