Seoul E-W Axis ① Bus 720, Independence Gate to Cheongnyangni — 600 Years of the East-West Road, Passing by the Window

Subtitle: If the north-south axis was a straight line people built fifty years ago, the east-west axis is Seoul's original one — Jongno, the road that has run through the old walled city since the Joseon dynasty. This piece boards city bus 720 in front of Independence Gate, passes the site of the old west gate, cuts through Gwanghwamun and Jongno, watches Dongdaemun go by outside the window, and rides on to Cheongnyangni, just outside where the old city wall ended. About two hours, with one stop at Jegi-dong. Gwanghwamun, Jongno, and Dongdaemun all go by outside the window only. Seoul E-W Axis, Part 1.

ISSUE · #1356 · 2026-09-07

🎯 The Frame — Reading Seoul as One Horizontal Line

Seoul's north-south axis (#1353) rode bus 140 through Gangnam-daero, Hannam Bridge, and Namsan 1st Tunnel to see "the order in which Gangnam was built." That road went down between 1969 and 1976. This piece's road is different. Jongno (종로) has cut east-west through the walled city since Hanyang became the capital in 1394. From the west gate, Donuimun (돈의문), to the east gate, Heunginjimun (흥인지문), people, horses, and shops have stood on this road for 600 years.

Switch between subway lines and Seoul looks like a set of separate islands. What lies between Gyeongbokgung and Dongdaemun stays invisible underground. This course crosses that gap above ground — boarding city bus 720 at Independence Gate and watching the site of the old west gate, Gwanghwamun (광화문), Jongno 1-ga through 5-ga, and Dongdaemun go by outside the window, all the way to Cheongnyangni, just outside the old city wall.

  • Total time: about 2 hours (about 45–55 minutes on the bus + 15 minutes off at Jegi-dong + buffer)
  • Best time: depart on a weekday at 10 AM — window seats open up on the Jongno stretch once the morning rush ends. During evening rush (5–7 PM), even the bus lane backs up through Jongno
  • Incline: none — the only walking segment is 15 minutes on flat ground

Why 720 — One Route Crosses the West Gate, the East Gate, and the Eastern Gateway

Trunk route 720 runs between Jingwan-dong in Eunpyeong-gu and Dapsimni (Chotgyeongso Intersection) in Dongdaemun-gu, crossing Muakjae Pass and running in a single line through Dongnimmun Station (독립문역) → Seodaemun Station stop (서대문역) → Gwanghwamun → Jongno 1-ga through 6-ga → Dongdaemun → Dongmyo (동묘앞) → Sinseol-dong (신설동) → Jegi-dong → Cheongnyangni Station (청량리역). It's the one route that passes the site of the Joseon capital's west gate, Donuimun, the east gate, Heunginjimun, and Cheongnyangni, the eastern gateway just outside the wall, all in one trip. The first bus leaves at 4 AM, and the weekday headway is about 6–10 minutes (as of the April 2026 service cut, subject to change).

📖 Story 1 — The Road Where People and Horses Passed Beneath the Bell Pavilion: In 1413, Joseon moved the bell pavilion, Jongnu, to what is now the Jongno intersection, and when it was rebuilt in 1440, the bell hung upstairs while people and horses passed through underneath. The main street in front of that pavilion was Unjongga (운종가), "the street where people gather like clouds." Six licensed shops, Yugui-jeon (육의전), granted a monopoly in exchange for supplying goods to the state, lined this street. The bell pavilion received the name 'Bosingak (보신각)' on March 15, 1895. Today, a bus-only lane runs right over that spot.

🚶 The Route — In the Order It Passes the Window

The starting point is Dongnimmun Station on Subway Line 3. Stand on the main road and you'll see a stone gate that resembles the Arc de Triomphe — Independence Gate, built in 1897.

① Dongnimmun Station → Facing Independence Gate → the 720 Stop (🚶 about 3 minutes)

  • Come out of the station, face Independence Gate once, then board a bus headed toward Dapsimni (Chotgyeongso Intersection) at the Dongnimmun Station stop on Tongil-ro (통일로). Check the stop name against the app's labels.
  • A proper dusk course walking Independence Gate, the Seodaemun Prison History Hall, and Yeongcheon Market exists separately at #1357. Here, you just board.

② Yeongcheon Market → Seodaemun Station Stop (🚌 about 4 minutes · no stop)

  • Right after departure, the arcade entrance of Dongnimmun Yeongcheon Market (독립문영천시장) flashes by on the left (#1357). Tongil-ro ends and the Seodaemun Station stop comes up — this is where Donuimun, the west gate of the Joseon capital, once stood.
  • 📖 Story 2 — From the West Gate to the East Gate: The Japanese Government-General tore down Donuimun in 1915, citing the double-tracking of the streetcar line and the straightening of the road. Heunginjimun, the east gate, survived. This bus runs the old east-west road of the walled city exactly as it was, from where the gone gate stood to where the surviving one still stands. Jongno's total length, from Sejong-daero Intersection to Sinseol-dong, is 4.2km — as measured after the Heunginjimun–Sinseol-dong stretch was folded into Jongno in 2010.

③ Jeongdong Intersection → Gwanghwamun (🚌 about 5 minutes · no stop)

  • Past the Seodaemun Station stop, the Seoul Museum of History (서울역사박물관) and, behind it, Gyeonghuigung (경희궁) pass by on the right, and Donuimun Museum Village (돈의문박물관마을) on the left. It's the sign that you've entered the walled city.
  • The Gwanghwamun stop. Through the right-side window, Gwanghwamun Square and Gwanghwamun itself at its far end appear layered against Bugaksan behind them. This piece doesn't get off here — Gyeongbokgung and Gwanghwamun Square are covered by #141 and #1244, and the Jongno 3-ga (종로3가) transfer hub by #1254.

④ Jongno 1-ga → Jongno 3-ga → Jongno 5-ga (🚌 about 5 minutes · no stop)

  • Jongno 1-ga: Bosingak on the right — where the bell is rung every year at midnight on December 31. Jongno 3-ga: the alley on the left is Ikseon-dong, and Tapgol Park is on the right (#294, #1254). Jongno 5-ga: the entrance to Gwangjang Market (광장시장) on the right (#1257).
  • Jongno is one of the streets in Seoul where buses stop most densely. The rhythm of passengers getting on and off at every stop is itself the scenery on this stretch.

⑤ Dongdaemun (🚌 about 6 minutes · window climax)

  • Past Jongno 6-ga, a fortress gate appears in the middle of the road. Heunginjimun, the walled city's east gate, designated a Treasure in 1963. Traffic splits and flows around both sides of the gate, and the fortress wall climbs up Naksan behind it on the left (the #1340 Changsin-dong course walks that side).
  • This is where the walled city ends. It's the climax of this piece, but you don't get off — Dongdaemun shopping and the DDP are covered by #172, #31, and #1252.

⑥ Dongmyo → Sinseol-dong → Get Off at Jegi-dong (🚌 about 8 minutes)

  • Wangsan-ro (왕산로), outside Heunginjimun. Past Dongmyo (#049, the secondhand market) and Sinseol-dong, the smell of medicinal herbs reaches you before anything else, on both sides of the road. Get off at the Jegi-dong Station (제기동역) stop.

⑦ Jegi-dong Mini Walk — 15 Minutes at the Gyeongdong Market (경동시장) Entrance (🚶 about 15 minutes)

  • Walk from the stop to the entrance of Gyeongdong Market and circle the edge of the traditional market district before heading back. The market opened in June 1960, and the stretch west of Gosanja-ro was designated Seoul Yangnyeongsi (서울약령시), a street of medicinal herb shops, in 1995. A proper morning course walking the market is at #1358 — here, you only taste the smell and the sound.
  • Catch Line 720 toward Dapsimni again from the same stop.

⑧ Get Off at Cheongnyangni Station — Wrap-Up (🚌 about 3 minutes)

  • Get off at the next stop, Cheongnyangni Station. The eastern gateway just outside the walled city — where Line 1 and the Gyeongui-Jungang Line meet, and KTX trains stop. Seoul's east-west axis ends here. Line 720 continues on to Dapsimni, but this piece's traverse of Seoul wraps up at Cheongnyangni.
  • From here: Line 1 back to downtown, the Gyeongui-Jungang Line to the Han River (#1248), or KTX toward Gangwon-do.

🚌 5 Tips for Riding and Seating

1. One card: T-money or a foreign contactless card (#1298). Tag on and tag off — don't forget the tag-off, since it's what triggers the transfer discount.

2. Sit on the right (south-facing) side: heading toward Dapsimni, a right-side seat puts Gwanghwamun Square, Bosingak, Gwangjang Market, and Heunginjimun all in your window.

3. Ring the bell at Sinseol-dong: ring it while passing through Sinseol-dong, before the "Jegi-dong Station" announcement plays (in Korean and English).

4. A faster alternative: if you're short on time, you can cross the city underground on Line 5 (Seodaemun Station stop → Gwanghwamun → Dongdaemun History & Culture Park) and Line 1 (Dongdaemun → Cheongnyangni). You'll miss the entire Jongno window view, though.

5. The reverse direction works too: board Line 720 toward Jingwan-dong from Cheongnyangni and you enter the walled city facing Heunginjimun head-on. An evening reverse-direction ride is a candidate for a series expansion.

🙏 Bus Manners — the Core of This Course

  • Jongno stops sit like islands in the middle of the road. Cross only at the crosswalk signal.
  • The Jongno stretch carries a lot of elderly passengers. Leave the priority seats up front open, and put large luggage near the rear door.
  • Keep other passengers out of the frame when shooting out the window. Keep phone calls quiet.

💡 Where You Might Get Thrown Off

  • Stop names can differ by direction: even at the same intersection, the stop name toward Jingwan-dong and the one toward Dapsimni can differ. Check the direction in your app before ringing the bell.
  • Jongno backs up in the evening: between 5 and 7 PM, even the bus lane through Jongno 1-ga to 5-ga gets stuck. The course stretches to 3 hours.
  • Only 15 minutes at Jegi-dong: go too deep into the market and it's easy to lose your way back to the 720 stop. Stick to one loop around the entrance.
  • The last bus and headway change: headway has widened since the April 2026 service cut. Check arrival times in your app late at night.

6 FAQs

Q1. It's a regular city bus, not a tour bus — is that right?

  • Yes. This is trunk route 720, the bus Seoulites ride every day, not a city tour bus. There's no narration, but the view out the window is the narration. The fare is a regular city bus fare too.

Q2. How is this different from the N-S Axis (#1353)?

  • The N-S axis runs through a new road built between 1969 and 1976 (Gangnam-daero, Hannam Bridge, the tunnel), while the E-W axis runs through an old road that's been there since 1394 (Jongno). Ride both in sequence and you read Seoul as a cross.

Q3. Can't I get off at Gwanghwamun or Jongno?

  • You can. But this piece's principle is to pass through, so see the separate pieces (#141, #1254, #172) for guides to those neighborhoods. Re-board Line 720 in the same direction within 30 minutes and the transfer discount still applies.

Q4. How many stops in total, and how long does it take?

  • From Dongnimmun Station to Cheongnyangni Station, roughly 15 stops, about 45–55 minutes on the bus (a weekday-daytime estimate). It varies a lot by time of day.

Q5. Shouldn't I get off to see Heunginjimun properly?

  • It stands in the middle of the road, so the angle from the bus window is actually a good one. To see it up close, get off at Dongdaemun Station and head toward the fortress wall — the #1340 Changsin-dong course walks that side.

Q6. Can I ride it at night?

  • Yes. Bosingak and Heunginjimun are both lit up at night. But this piece is built around Jongno's daytime rhythm, and a nighttime reverse-direction ride is covered in an expansion piece.
