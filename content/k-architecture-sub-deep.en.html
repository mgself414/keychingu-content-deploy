🎯 How to Use This Guide

This guide gathers 2 modern architecture sites, 2 hanok villages, and 4 UNESCO World Heritage sites where you can see Korean architecture in person. DDP has an architecture tour where a docent takes you around the building.

The operating details are copied from values registered in the Korea Tourism Organization tourism data (TourAPI). Eunpyeong Hanok Village and Yangdong Village are places where residents live, so entering the hanok and houses is restricted, and Bukchon Hanok Village has set visiting hours. Hanok villages and hanok experiences are covered in the #226 Korean Hanok Deep Dive Guide.

📍 Modern Architecture — DDP and Bonte Museum

Dongdaemun Design Plaza (DDP) (281 Eulji-ro, Jung-gu, Euljiro 7-ga (을지로7가), ☎ 02-2153-0000) is a complex cultural space that opened on March 21, 2014, designed by the architect Zaha Hadid. It is divided into Allimteo (알림터), Baeumteo (배움터), Sallimteo (살림터), the DDP Design Museum (DDP 디자인 뮤지엄), the Design Market (디자인 장터), Eoullim Plaza (어울림 광장), and Dongdaemun History and Culture Park (동대문 역사 문화공원). According to the Korea Tourism Organization, Zaha Hadid focused on the dynamism of Dongdaemun and created the space with an architectural language of curves and curved surfaces, diagonals and slanted planes, and the dynamism of the curves shows once the lights come on at dusk. The Korea Tourism Organization names the entrance path to Eoullim Plaza as a good spot for night photos.

  • Opening hours: 10:00~20:00. Some spaces operate flexibly depending on the program.
  • Closed days: January 1, the day of Seollal and Chuseok. It is registered that this differs by facility.
  • Parking: Available.

The DDP Architecture Tour (☎ 02-2153-0310, 0311, 12:00~13:00 lunch break) is a guided tour hosted by the Seoul Metropolitan Government and the Seoul Design Foundation, in which you look around the buildings designed by Zaha Hadid and the history of the Dongdaemun area with a docent. During the 60-minute regular course, it passes the space frame structure of Eoullim Plaza, Igansumun Water Gate (이간수문), the Hanyangdoseong city wall and lighting tower, the exterior aluminum panels, and the spiral sculptural staircase.

  • Operation: January 1~December 31, 2026, Tuesday~Sunday. 3 times a day (2 in Korean, 1 in English), and 2 times a day during the rooftop tour period. Closed days: Every Monday, January 1, the day of Seollal and Chuseok.
  • Meeting point: Tour desk on the 1st floor of the DDP Museum. Duration: About 60 minutes.
  • Fee: General 10,000 won, groups of persons of national merit, soldiers, persons with disabilities and teenagers 8,000 won, children 5,000 won.
  • Participation: Ages 13 (middle school) and over; children under 13 must be accompanied by a guardian. Online reservation, or on-site registration for remaining seats from 10 minutes before the tour starts. The English tour can be booked only by foreigners or Koreans accompanied by foreigners. You cannot join in the middle of a tour, and if you miss a tour without cancelling in advance, future reservations are restricted. A receiver and headphones are provided.

Bonte Museum (69 Sanrok-namno 762beon-gil, Andeok-myeon, Seogwipo-si, ☎ 064-792-8108) is a museum whose name means "original form", designed by the architect Tadao Ando. According to the Korea Tourism Organization, it brings light and water in as architectural elements on Tadao Ando's trademark exposed concrete, and it was designed under the concept "tradition and modernity that yield to the land of Jeju Island". The space is divided into two areas, displaying traditional crafts and modern artworks (Picasso, Yayoi Kusama, Nam June Paik, Dalí and others).

  • Opening hours: 10:00~18:00 (last entry 17:00). Closed days: Open all year.
  • Fee (permanent exhibition): Adults 30,000 won, teenagers 20,000 won, children 10,000 won.
  • Parking: Free. Credit cards: Accepted.

📍 Hanok Villages — Bukchon and Eunpyeong

Bukchon Hanok Village (37 Gyedong-gil, Jongno-gu, Gye-dong (계동), ☎ Bukchon Village Information Center 02-2148-4161) is a residential area where traditional hanok cluster between Gyeongbokgung Palace, Changdeokgung Palace and Jongmyo Shrine, and Gahoe-dong, Songhyeon-dong, Anguk-dong, Samcheong-dong and others belong to it. According to the Korea Tourism Organization, it is a representative traditional residential area of Seoul, and because it has many historic sites and cultural heritage items it is called a "street museum in the city center". The Korea Tourism Organization's description points to the connected eave lines and alleys.

  • Visiting hours: 10:00~17:00. 17:00 to 10:00 the next day is a restricted visiting period (special management area, red zone). Closed days: Open all year.
  • Etiquette: No trespassing, unauthorized photography, illegal dumping, public urination or noise; observe visiting hours.
  • Parking: Not available.

Eunpyeong Hanok Village (7-10 Yeonseo-ro 50-gil, Eunpyeong-gu, Jingwan-dong (진관동), ☎ 02-351-6114) is a modern-style hanok village newly created after Bukchon and Seochon, set in harmony with Bukhansan Mountain and Jingwansa Temple. According to the Korea Tourism Organization, the land was divided and sold so that only hanok could be built, so each hanok differs in shape and wood color according to the owner's taste. It is a place where residents live, so you cannot see the inside of the hanok, and for some houses you can see the inside if the owner allows it.

  • Opening hours: Open at all times. Closed days: Open all year.
  • Parking: Available.

📍 UNESCO World Heritage — Jongmyo Shrine, Changdeokgung Palace and Huwon, Hahoe Village, Yangdong Village

Jongmyo Shrine (157 Jong-ro, Jongno-gu, Hunjeong-dong (훈정동), ☎ 02-765-0195) is the national shrine that enshrines the spirit tablets of the kings and queens, emperors and empresses of Joseon and the Korean Empire, and it was built on its present site in 1395 (the 4th year of King Taejo). At first it had only the main hall, and as the number of enshrined tablets grew it was extended several times into today's main hall of 19 bays and Yeongnyeongjeon of 16 bays. Within the grounds there are annex buildings such as Mangmyoru (망묘루), Hyangdaecheong (향대청), Jaegung (재궁) and Jeonsacheong (전사청).

  • Opening hours: Feb~May and Sep~Oct 09:00~18:00 (last entry 17:00), Jun~Aug 09:00~18:30 (last entry 17:30), Nov~Jan 09:00~17:30 (last entry 16:30).
  • Closed days: Every Tuesday. If Tuesday is a public holiday, it stays open that day and closes on the next weekday instead.
  • Parking: Available.

Changdeokgung Palace and Huwon (99 Yulgok-ro, Jongno-gu, Waryong-dong (와룡동), ☎ 02-3668-2300): Changdeokgung Palace was built in 1405 (the 5th year of King Taejong) as a secondary palace of Gyeongbokgung Palace, and after it burned during the Imjin War it was the first of the palaces to be rebuilt, in 1610 (the 2nd year of King Gwanghaegun). According to the Korea Tourism Organization, it was built to blend with the surrounding terrain rather than follow an artificial structure, so it was regarded as the most Korean of the palaces, and it was inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage site in 1997. The rear garden (Huwon) makes use of the natural terrain, with pavilions built in each valley, and the 4 valleys have the areas of Buyongji (부용지), Aeryeonji (애련지), Gwallamji (관람지) and Okryucheon (옥류천).

  • Opening hours: Feb~May and Sep~Oct 09:00~18:00 (last entry 17:00), Jun~Aug 09:00~18:30 (last entry 17:30), Nov~Jan 09:00~17:30 (last entry 16:30).
  • Closed days: Every Monday. If Monday is a public holiday, it stays open that day and closes on the next weekday instead.
  • Parking: Not available.

Andong Hahoe Village (186-8 Jeonseo-ro, Pungcheon-myeon, Andong-si, ☎ General Information Center 054-840-3795) is a clan village where the Pungsan Ryu family has lived for generations. According to the Korea Tourism Organization, the Nakdong River wraps around the village in an S shape, which gave it the name "Hahoe", and the houses face lower ground around a low hill, so the direction of the houses is not uniform. At the center of the village are the large tile-roofed houses of the Ryu family, and around them are thatched houses whose original form is well preserved. On July 31, 2010 it was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage list together with Gyeongju Yangdong Village as "Historic Villages of Korea", and it holds the Hahoe Byeolsin Gut Mask Dance Drama (하회별신굿탈놀이) as a regular performance.

  • Opening hours: Apr~Sep 09:00~18:00 (last entry 17:30), Oct~Mar 09:00~17:00 (last entry 16:30). Closed days: Open all year.
  • Experiences: Traditional wedding experience, seasonal customs, Seowon Sohwakhaeng (서원소확행), family motto calligraphy experience and others.
  • Parking: Free.

Gyeongju Yangdong Village (93 Yangdongmaeul-gil, Gangdong-myeon, Gyeongju-si, ☎ 054-762-2630) is a clan village formed by the Wolseong Son family and the Yeogang Yi family, and the whole village is designated a National Folklore Cultural Heritage. According to the Korea Tourism Organization, it is the largest clan village in Korea, and 54 old houses more than 200 years old are preserved, so you can see the structure of traditional houses from the mid-Joseon period onward at a glance.

  • Opening hours: Apr~Sep 09:00~19:00 (last entry 18:00), Oct~Mar 09:00~18:00 (last entry 17:00). Closed days: Open all year.
  • Entering houses: You cannot enter a house without the owner's permission. For designated houses such as Treasures and Important Folklore Materials, only the outer spaces other than the anchae (inner quarters) are open.
  • Experiences: Rice cake pounding, yakgwa making, traditional etiquette learning, seodang (village school) experience and others.
  • Parking: Available.

⚠ 3 Things to Know Before You Go

  1. Jongmyo Shrine closes on Tuesdays and Changdeokgung Palace on Mondays — the closed days are registered differently.
  2. Bukchon Hanok Village restricts visits after 17:00 — 17:00 to 10:00 the next day is a restricted visiting period.
  3. Eunpyeong Hanok Village and Yangdong Village are places where residents live — at Eunpyeong Hanok Village you cannot view the inside of the hanok, and at Yangdong Village you cannot enter a house without the owner's permission.

⚙ FAQ 2

Q. Which architecture tour has an English session listed in the Korea Tourism Organization's registered data?

A. According to the Korea Tourism Organization's registered data, the DDP Architecture Tour has an English session. It can be booked only by foreigners or Koreans accompanied by foreigners.

Q. Which places are open all year?

A. According to the Korea Tourism Organization's registered data, the Bonte Museum, Bukchon Hanok Village, Eunpyeong Hanok Village, Andong Hahoe Village and Gyeongju Yangdong Village are open all year.
