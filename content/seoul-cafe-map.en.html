Seoul Cafe Guide — 14 Coffee and Space Destinations (Hakrim, Eulji Dabang, In the Mass, Chuso Bookshop) Roasteries, Old Tea Rooms, Warehouses and Views (Official Values, October 2026)

Subtitle: Hakrim (학림) running since 1956, roasting its own beans (VisitKorea) · Eulji Dabang (을지다방) opened in 1985, opens at 06:00 Mon-Sat (VisitKorea) · In the Mass Majang (인더매스 마장) shows the roasting process in an old warehouse (VisitKorea) · Inwangsan Chuso Bookshop (초소책방), a former police guard post, 08:00-22:00 (VisitKorea) · Prices not published for all 14 places — as of 2026-10-21

🎯 How to Use This Guide

Climb the wooden stairs to the second floor and the smell of roasted beans is already waiting, with a Buam-dong (부암동) alley outside the window (Club Espresso, VisitKorea). This guide covers only Seoul cafes where the coffee itself and the space are the reason to go. There are four groups: cafes that roast their own coffee, old dabang (traditional tea rooms), cafes converted from warehouses and old buildings, and mountain and river view cafes. Every shop is listed in the Korea Tourism Organization's tourism data (VisitKorea, TourAPI), and the addresses, hours and closing days are copied as listed. Shops where cake and bread are the stars are split off into #914 Dessert Shop Guide.

The old guide attached rankings such as "best in the world" and "best in Korea" to Blue Bottle, Anthracite and Fritz, but they had no source, so they were deleted. For well-known brand cafes and neighborhood lists, see the Seoul Cafe Meta Guide for Foreigners. This edition focuses on places whose space history is written in VisitKorea.

☕ Cafes That Roast Their Own — 4 Places

  • Hakrim (학림) — 2F, 119 Daehak-ro (대학로 119), Jongno-gu, Seoul. 10:00-23:00 (last order 22:00), closed Seollal (Lunar New Year) and Chuseok. A cafe running since 1956 that roasts its beans on site and serves hand-drip coffee. The signature is Vienna coffee (coffee topped with whipped cream), with cream cheesecake and parfait also on offer. Classical music plays. Tel 02-742-2877 (VisitKorea)
  • Club Espresso (클럽에스프레소) — 132 Changuimun-ro (창의문로 132), Jongno-gu, Seoul (Buam-dong). 09:00-19:00 (last order 18:50), open every day. It opened in Daehangno in 1990 and moved to Buam-dong in 2001. The first floor is an order counter like a roasting workshop, and the second floor is wood-finished seating. It also sells beans and coffee equipment. The Yun Dong-ju Literary Museum (윤동주문학관) is nearby. Tel 02-764-8719 (VisitKorea)
  • Coffee Chingu (커피친구) — 81 Sambong-ro (삼봉로 81), Jongno-gu, Seoul (Susong-dong, 수송동). Weekdays 10:00-20:30, Sat 12:00-17:30, closed Sun. A hand-drip specialty shop opened in 2005 that uses beans roasted fresh each day. The menu is organized by origin names such as El Salvador, Kenya, Rwanda and Colombia, and it also has decaf coffee. Tel 02-725-0179 (VisitKorea)
  • Heo Hyeong-man's Apgujeong Coffee House (허형만의 압구정커피집) — Unit 107, Building 2, Shinhyundae Shopping Arcade, 151 Apgujeong-ro (압구정로 151), Gangnam-gu, Seoul. Mon-Fri 09:00-18:00, Sat 09:00-17:00, closed Sun, public holidays, and the Seollal and Chuseok holiday periods. Opened in July 2001. Roaster and instructor Mr. Heo Hyeong-man serves coffee he roasts daily and also teaches coffee classes. The signature is Apgujeong Coffee. Tel 0507-1367-5078 (VisitKorea)

🕰 Old Dabang — 3 Places

  • Eulji Dabang (을지다방) — 2F, 124-1 Eulji-ro (을지로 124-1), Jung-gu, Seoul (Euljiro 3-ga, 을지로3가). Mon-Sat 06:00-21:00, Sun 09:00-20:00, open every day. Opened in 1985. The signature is ssanghwa-cha (a herbal tea) topped with an egg yolk, and it also has iced coffee and fresh fruit juice. It moved to its current spot because of redevelopment. Tel 02-2272-1886 (VisitKorea)
  • Teobangnae (터방내) — 101-7 Heukseok-ro (흑석로 101-7), Dongjak-gu, Seoul. 12:00-23:00, closed Sun. It keeps the look of the music dabang that opened in 1983. Coffee is brewed with a siphon. Popular items are Café Royal (카페 로열), in which whiskey is poured over a sugar cube and set alight, and a coffee float with ice cream on top. Tel 02-813-4434 (VisitKorea)
  • Jebi Dabang (제비다방) — 24 Wausan-ro (와우산로 24), Mapo-gu, Seoul (near Sangsu Station, 상수역). 11:30-02:00, open every day. Named after the dabang that the writer Yi Sang (이상) once ran. Besides coffee it serves doraji-cha (bellflower root tea) and black tea, with garaetteok (rice cake sticks) as a snack. There is an indie band stage in the first basement level. Tel 02-325-1969 (VisitKorea)

🏭 Cafes Converted from Warehouses and Old Buildings — 3 Places

  • In the Mass Majang (인더매스 마장) — Gyeongdong Warehouse (경동창고), 270 Majang-ro (마장로 270), Seongdong-gu, Seoul (near Exit 4 of Majang Station, 마장역). 09:00-21:30 (last order 21:00), open every day. A renovated old warehouse. The ceiling is high, and you can watch the roasting process and barista training in the shop. You can pick beans and taste them right away, and pets are welcome. Tel 0507-1489-9031 (VisitKorea)
  • Coffee Hanyakbang (커피한약방) — 16-6 Samil-daero 12-gil (삼일대로12길 16-6), Jung-gu, Seoul (Euljiro 2-ga, 을지로2가). 1F weekdays 10:00-22:00, weekends 11:00-22:00 (last order 21:00); 2F and 3F 10:00-21:30, open every day. VisitKorea introduces it as a cafe converted from the site of Hyeminseo (혜민서), a Joseon-era medical institution. It left old props and antiques as they were, and its signature items are hand-roasted coffee and filter coffee. The alley is narrow and hard to find. Tel 070-4148-4242 (VisitKorea)
  • Bean Brothers Hapjeong (빈브라더스 합정) — 35-1 Tojeong-ro (토정로 35-1), Mapo-gu, Seoul (near Exit 7 of Hapjeong Station, 합정역). 10:00-22:00 (last order 21:30), open every day. A detached building with a high ceiling and industrial interior, where baristas brew coffee at the bar in the middle of the shop. The signature is drip coffee. Tel 0507-1428-2001 (VisitKorea)

⛰ Mountain and River View Cafes — 4 Places

  • Inwangsan The Forest Chuso Bookshop (인왕산 더숲 초소책방) — 172 Inwangsan-ro (인왕산로 172), Jongno-gu, Seoul (halfway up Inwangsan). 08:00-22:00 (last order 21:30), open every day. A bookshop-cafe converted from a police guard post used for more than 50 years since 1968, keeping part of the outer wall and the iron door. Through the floor-to-ceiling windows you can see N Seoul Tower. Tel 02-735-0206 (VisitKorea)
  • Dongjak Noeul Cafe (동작노을카페) — 335 Dongjak-daero (동작대로 335), Dongjak-gu, Seoul (Line 4 Dongjak Station, south end of Dongjak Bridge). Mon-Thu 10:00-23:00, Fri-Sun 10:00-01:00, open every day. The second floor has seats facing Yeouido, the third floor faces Banpo, and the fourth is a rooftop. On the other side of the bridge, near Exit 1, there is a same-shaped Cloud Cafe (구름카페). Tel 0507-1324-7279 (VisitKorea)
  • Sanmotung-i (산모퉁이) — 153 Baekseok-dong-gil (백석동길 153), Jongno-gu, Seoul (Buam-dong). 11:00-18:50 (last order 18:20), open every day. It was a filming location for an MBC drama, and the second floor displays filming props and photos. Through the window you can see downtown Seoul and Bukhansan. Tel 02-391-4737 (VisitKorea)
  • Hanok Cafe Seonungak (한옥카페 선운각) — 223 Samyang-ro 173-gil (삼양로173길 223), Gangbuk-gu, Seoul (Ui-dong, 우이동). 11:00-19:00 (last order 18:00), closed Wed. A hanok (traditional Korean house) below Bukhansan. The VisitKorea description reports as hearsay that it was built by the late Chairman Chung Ju-yung (정주영) and is said to be the largest private hanok in Seoul apart from the palaces. It was used only as a hanok wedding and photo venue before opening as a cafe. About a 20-minute walk from Bukhansan Ui Station (북한산우이역) on the Ui-Sinseol Line, and parking is paid. Tel 0507-1399-1105 (VisitKorea)

🥗 Dietary Notes

  • Price range: None of the 14 places has a price in its VisitKorea listing. Prices not published — choose from the menu board or check by phone
  • Milk alternatives: Places with almond drinks listed are Chuso Bookshop (Almond Breeze latte) and Sanmotung-i (Almond Breeze coffee) (VisitKorea). At other places, ask when you order whether they can swap in oat or soy milk
  • Caffeine: The place with decaf coffee listed is Coffee Chingu (VisitKorea). The ssanghwa-cha at Eulji Dabang is a herbal tea, not coffee
  • Eggs and alcohol: The ssanghwa-cha at Eulji Dabang is topped with an egg yolk. The Café Royal at Teobangnae contains whiskey (VisitKorea) — vegetarian and halal travelers should ask them to leave it out or pick something else
  • Halal: VisitSeoul introduces Itaewon's Eid (이드) (15 Usadan-ro 10-gil (우사단로10길 15), Yongsan-gu, daily 12:00-21:00) as one of the few restaurants recognized as halal by the Korea Muslim Federation (KMF). Certification can change, so check before you visit. None of the cafes in this guide carries a halal label
  • For show-and-tell cards see #1300 Dietary Request Manual

🧰 Cafe Practicalities — Solo, Reservations, Payment, Ordering, Getting Around

  • Solo: Cafes are built around one-person orders, so going alone is easy. For a map of solo meals and solo cafes, see #348 Seoul Solo Diner Map
  • Reservations: None of the 14 places has reservation information in its VisitKorea listing. Join the line or just walk in
  • Payment: For cards and mobile payment, see #527 Korea Payment Systems
  • Ordering: Many places have you order at the counter and then hand you a vibrating pager — #1288 Cafe Pager Manual. For mugs, return stations and holding a seat, see #1378 Cafe In-Store Rules. When you cannot make yourself understood, see #1339 Ordering Etiquette
  • Getting around: For subway and bus, see #1298 Choosing Among 3 Transit Cards. Chuso Bookshop, Sanmotung-i and Seonungak sit on mountain slopes, so allow plenty of walking time

⚠ 4 Common Mistakes

1. "The old dabang are all gone" — Eulji Dabang (1985) and Teobangnae (1983) are still listed in VisitKorea as operating

2. "Cafes are open any day of the week" — Coffee Chingu and Teobangnae close on Sunday, Heo Hyeong-man's Apgujeong Coffee House on Sunday and public holidays, and Seonungak on Wednesday (VisitKorea)

3. "Cafes stay open late" — Club Espresso closes at 19:00, Sanmotung-i at 18:50 and Seonungak at 19:00. For late nights pick Jebi Dabang (02:00) or Dongjak Noeul Cafe (Fri-Sun 01:00) (VisitKorea)

4. "View cafes require a hike" — Dongjak Noeul Cafe connects directly to the exit of Line 4 Dongjak Station (VisitKorea)
