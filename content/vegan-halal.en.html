5 Mistakes Foreigners Often Make

1. Believing "Korea has no vegan options"

Korean vegan restaurants have exploded since 2020 — 100+ vegan venues across Itaewon, Hannam, Hongdae, Yeonnam, and Seongsu. Including Korean temple food and vegetarian options, foreigner vegans can survive a full week of eating.

2. Assuming temple food = vegan by default

Korean temple food is no meat / seafood + no five spices (green onion, garlic, leek, scallion, asafoetida). It's a separate category from typical vegan food. Korean temple-food restaurants are limited but represent the most authentic Korean vegan style.

3. Trusting "vegan" labels in regular Korean restaurants

"Vegan-possible" claims at general Korean restaurants often don't verify broth, oil, or fermented sauces. For true vegan compliance, choose certified vegan restaurants or expat-oriented vegan venues.

4. Thinking halal = Itaewon only

Halal restaurants exist in Itaewon's mosque district + select venues in Gangnam, Myeongdong, and Hongdae. The core is Southeast Asian, Middle Eastern, and Turkish food. KMF (Korea Muslim Federation) certified venues can be searched on their official site.

5. Relying on allergy labels

Korean restaurant allergy labels are only partially in English. For gluten / nuts / shellfish / egg / dairy / soy allergies, confirm via English menu + direct staff confirmation + photo.

1️⃣ Vegan — No Animal Products

Korean vegan restaurant distribution:

  • Itaewon / Hannam: 30+ foreigner-friendly vegan cafes / brunches
  • Yeonnam / Seongsu / Hongdae: 20+ MZ-trendy vegan venues
  • Insadong / Jongno: 5+ authentic temple food

Verified Korean vegan lineup (reference):

  • Plant (Planty): Vegan Korean (Hannam / Itaewon)
  • Lush Vegan Cafe: Vegan brunch (Hongdae)
  • Vatted: Vegan western food (Seongsu / Yeonnam)
  • Whole Foods Market (some Korean stores): Vegan ingredients
  • Seonjae: Authentic temple food (Insadong)

Vegan Korean menu picks:

  • Bibimbap (request no meat / egg + verify gochujang is vegan)
  • Gimbap (request no egg / ham / tuna)
  • Janchi guksu (request no anchovy / meat broth — difficult)
  • Temple food: Tofu / wild greens / multigrain rice = 100% vegan

Confirmation keywords:

  • "Vegan-ieyo (비건이에요)" = "I'm vegan"
  • "Please remove meat, fish, egg, dairy, honey"

2️⃣ Halal — Islamic Diet

Korean halal restaurant distribution:

  • Itaewon mosque district: Main halal alley (5~10 min walk from Itaewon Station)
  • Some venues in Gangnam, Myeongdong, Hongdae: Indian, Turkish, Malaysian, Indonesian

Verified Korean halal lineup (reference):

  • Itaewon mosque district: 20+ Turkish, Pakistani, Egyptian, Malaysian, Indonesian restaurants
  • Eid Halal restaurant: Korean-style halal (some Seoul locations)
  • Indian restaurants (Itaewon / Gangnam): Bombay Curry, Saffron, etc.

Korean mosque-certified venues:

  • Search KMF (Korea Muslim Federation) certified venues = official site
  • "Halal Certified" sign at venue entrance

Halal Korean food options (limited):

  • Korean galbi / samgyeopsal = pork / beef slaughter often non-halal → confirm halal-certified Korean restaurant in advance
  • Korean dakgalbi / seafood = some venues halal-possible
  • Korean bibimbap / janchi guksu = request no broth / meat (effectively vegan)

3️⃣ Gluten-Free

Korean gluten-free restaurants (limited):

  • Korean soy sauce / gochujang often contain wheat → verify directly
  • 10+ gluten-free cafes / restaurants in Hannam / Apgujeong / Gangnam
  • Rice-based Korean food (rice / multigrain / rice cakes) = gluten-free possible

Confirmation keywords:

  • "Gluten allergy-ieyo"
  • "Please remove flour / soy sauce" — soy sauce is core to Korean cooking, so difficult

4️⃣ Kosher — Jewish Diet

Korean kosher restaurants (very limited):

  • Some Itaewon venues offer kosher options
  • Israeli / Middle Eastern restaurants (some)
  • Officially kosher-certified venues are nearly nonexistent in Korea → self-prepare or use vegan options

FAQ

Q1. Will English work?

Vegan cafes / halal restaurants = 95% English-capable. Temple food / authentic Korean = limited English menus.

Q2. Where to verify vegan / halal certification?

  • Halal: KMF (Korea Muslim Federation) official site / venue entrance signage
  • Vegan: Korea Vegan Association website / Happy Cow app

Q3. "Vegan-possible" claims at general Korean restaurants?

Verification needed. Broths, oils, fermented sauces, fish sauce may be included. 100% vegan guarantee only at certified venues.

Q4. Family vegan / halal?

Itaewon / Hannam / Hongdae = many family-friendly vegan / halal venues. English menus + allergy labels + child seats common.

Q5. Korean food adapted for vegan / halal?

  • Vegan: Temple food / bibimbap (no meat / egg) / tofu Korean
  • Halal: Halal-certified Korean restaurants (limited) → Korean food + verify certification

Q6. Convenience-store vegan / halal?

Korea Vegan Association certified convenience-store line (some CU / GS25 / 7-Eleven branches — vegan gimbap / lunch boxes / snacks). Halal nearly nonexistent → fruits / nuts / water.
