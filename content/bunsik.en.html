Seoul Bunsik Guide — Where to Eat Tteokbokki, Gimbap, Sundae and Mandu: 5 Snack Streets and 6 Listed Spots (Official Info as of October 2026)

Subtitle: Sindang-dong Tteokbokki Town (Maboklim since 1953, weekdays 09:00–23:00, closed 2nd and 4th Mondays) · Gwangjang Market food alley daily 09:00–23:00 (Jongno 5-ga Exit 8) · Sillim-dong Sundae Town (Sillim Station Exits 3 and 1, 168 m, around 10,000 won posted) · Myeongdong Kyoja (Michelin Bib Gourmand, daily 10:30–21:00) · Mandujip (Apgujeong, Michelin) · Sinsa Market Tteokbokki (secondary) — as of 2026-10-08 per VisitSeoul and Michelin. Chain store counts and price lists are left out because there is no official source.

🎯 How to Use This Guide

The old "Top 8" called Maboklim the "No. 1 birthplace of Korean tteokbokki" (VisitSeoul only calls it the original of Sindang-dong Tteokbokki Town), included a shop that does not exist ("Anjanjanchi Rabokki" — the old text itself admitted the name was fictional), and had no source for "Sindang Station Exit 1, 3 minutes," "6,000–8,000 per person," chain store counts (Jaws 80+, Gimbap Cheonguk 1,500+, etc.) or price lists. This time we list only places whose VisitSeoul or Michelin pages we opened directly on 2026-10-08, and Time Out is marked "secondary." Prices are given only where the page posts them.

🍜 What Is Bunsik? — 5 Types

  • Tteokbokki (떡볶이) (rice cakes in gochujang sauce) · Gimbap (김밥) (rice, vegetables and ham rolled in seaweed; at Gwangjang Market it is the mini Mayak Gimbap (마약김밥)) · Sundae (순대) (pork intestine stuffed with glass noodles and blood; stir-fried in Sillim) · Fried snacks and Gimmari (김말이) · Mandu and Kalguksu (칼국수) (Myeongdong Kyoja and Mandujip are more restaurants than snack shops). Spice level and ingredients differ by shop, so ask before ordering. For the chains (Jaws, Gukdae, Gimbap Cheonguk, Gobongmin) we found no official source for store counts or vegetarian options, so we do not list them.

🗺 Two Routes

  • A. Half day at the market: Jongno 5-ga Exit 8, Gwangjang Market food alley (Mayak Gimbap, Bindaetteok) → toward Euljiro → Myeongdong Kyoja (before 21:00).
  • B. Tteokbokki comparison: Sindang-dong Tteokbokki Town, Maboklim (weekdays; avoid 2nd and 4th Mondays) → evening at Sillim Sundae Town (Sillim Station Exit 3).

⚠ 5 Common Mistakes

1. Going to Maboklim on the 2nd or 4th Monday — closed.

2. Going to the Gwangjang main market on Sunday — closed. The food alley is open.

3. Going to Mandujip on Sunday — closed.

4. Trusting old price lists — officially posted prices are limited to around 10,000 won in Sillim, BBQ and a few others.

5. Looking for "Anjanjanchi Rabokki" — it does not exist.

⚙ FAQ 6

1. Where is the original tteokbokki? — Maboklim, the original of Sindang-dong Tteokbokki Town (since 1953, VisitSeoul).

2. What about gimbap? — Mayak Gimbap at the Gwangjang Market food alley (VisitSeoul).

3. Any Michelin bunsik? — Myeongdong Kyoja (Bib Gourmand, daily 10:30–21:00).

4. What about sundae? — Sillim-dong Sundae Town, Sillim Station Exits 3 and 1, 168 m.

5. How much will it cost? — The only posted figures are around 10,000 won in Sillim and 3,000 won for Sinsa Market tteokbokki (secondary).

6. What if I can't handle spicy food? — Check the spice level before ordering. Kalguksu and mandu are safe choices.
