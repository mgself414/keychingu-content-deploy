Subtitle: Old-school gukbap and haejangguk shops that open at 6–8 a.m. · Tofu jeongol and kimchi jjigae for groups · Winter snacks (Dec–Feb) come from street stalls with no fixed spot, and prices are checked on site · Dongji 2026-12-22 red bean porridge · Seollal 2027-02-07 (provisional, data published before the official almanac announcement) · Only seolleongtang and gomtang are confirmed as beef broth, so ask about the broth base for everything else · Shop details and hours are in gukbap #477, jjigae #486, noodles #480, snacks #476 and health foods #827

🎯 How to Use This Guide

On a cold morning, once you leave your lodging and start walking, what you want most is a bowl of hot broth. But if you search for "Korean winter food," you only get a list of menu names. They do not tell you what is open right now, or what to order if you want to avoid pork broth. This guide is a hub that answers by situation. Instead of explaining menus, you pick the branch that fits your situation right now (morning, lunch, dinner, walking, a specific date) and get sent to detail guides where shop names, opening hours and closing days are confirmed on official pages.

The numbers this guide states itself are the Korea Astronomy and Space Science Institute calendar data (the dates of Dongji and Seollal) and a few opening hours, opening years and daily preparation quantities that the detail guides confirmed in October 2026 on VisitKorea and Michelin pages. No detail guide has a food price posted in won anywhere, so this guide gives no amounts — every shop with a Michelin price symbol is ₩ (the lowest tier) (#477, #480, #486). For the rest, asking before you order is the honest answer.

🍲 A Bowl of Gukbap or Tang — Old-School Shops Open in the Morning and Sell Only What They Prepared

  • What is most useful on a winter morning is an old-school shop that opens early. In Jongno, Cheongjinok (청진옥) is open 06:00–22:00 (last order 21:30) year-round and serves Yangseonji haejangguk (양선지 해장국), with sagol bone broth (사골 육수) and congealed blood and beef offal. Behind City Hall, Mugyo-dong Bugeoguk-jip (무교동 북어국집) is open 07:00–20:00 on weekdays and 07:00–15:00 on weekends, year-round, and serves a single dish, bugeo haejangguk (#486, confirmed on VisitKorea 2026-10). In Myeongdong, Hadongwan (하동관) serves gomtang (곰탕) Monday–Saturday 07:00–16:00 (closed Sundays) and closes once the day's prepared amount runs out (#477, confirmed on Michelin 2026-10).
  • "If you go in the afternoon, it is closed" is the default for old-school shops. Some close at 4 p.m., like Hadongwan, and some stop when about 100 servings a day run out, like Hapjeong-ok (합정옥) (#477), so going in the morning or at lunch is safer. Hours, closing days and menus for 8 places in Seoul and 3 in Busan are in #477, the differences among six kinds of gukbap are in #807, and what to do once you sit down is in #808.
  • The broth base differs from shop to shop. What #477 lists as beef-bone and beef broth is seolleongtang and gomtang, and pork gukbap is pork, as the name says. Haejangguk is either sagol bone broth with congealed blood and beef offal (Cheongjinok) or dried pollack (Bugeoguk-jip). If you need to avoid an ingredient, ask about the broth base, not the menu name — see 🥗 below.

🍜 Kalguksu and Jeongol — Winter Around the Table With a Group

  • If you are alone, kalguksu is quick. Myeongdong Gyoja (명동교자) in Myeongdong is open daily 10:30–21:00, has been family-run since 1966, and has just four menu items, so it is easy to choose (#480, confirmed on Michelin 2026-10). Hours and closing days for Hwangsaengga Kalguksu (황생가 칼국수) in Bukchon (sagol bone broth) and Im Byeong-ju Sandong Kalguksu (임병주 산동 칼국수) in Seocho (clear clam broth) are in the same guide.
  • If you are with a group, jeongol and jjigae are the winter dinner standard. In Mapo, Hwanggeum Kongbat (황금콩밭) boils tofu jeongol with tofu made in-house from domestic soybeans (Monday–Friday 11:30–21:00, Saturday and Sunday 11:00–21:00), and in Bangsan Market, Eunjujeong (은주정) serves kimchi jjigae that you wrap in leaves, 11:30–22:00 (last order 21:30), closed Sundays and over the Seollal and Chuseok holidays (#486, confirmed on VisitKorea and Michelin 2026-10). The officially recognized origin of budae jjigae (부대찌개), with ham and sausage, is Uijeongbu (Odeng Sikdang (오뎅식당)), and in Seoul, Daewoo Budae Jjigae (대우부대찌개) in Yeoksam is in the same guide.
  • Jeongol and jjigae are dishes boiled on the table and shared by several people. For one person, a jjigae baekban set meal or kalguksu is easier.

🐟 Street Snacks — Stalls Move, and You Check the Price on Site

  • Bungeoppang, roasted sweet potatoes, roasted chestnuts, gyeran-ppang and hotteok are the basic winter snacks for December to February. Street stalls have no fixed spot, so "it is at this place" cannot be written, and no official page posts a price for any of them. So the detail guide #476 carries over only the types of sellers (pojangmacha, convenience stores, inside subway stations) and the officially listed fixed shops (Bungeorang Odeng (붕어랑 오뎅) in Pyeongchang, hotteok shops in Gunsan, Cheongju and Busan, and Jeju Dongmun Market Hotteok Alley (제주 동문시장 호떡골목)).
  • The spot VisitKorea points to as a place where stalls gather is Myeongdong Station Food Street (명동역 푸드 스트리트) (go straight to the left from Exit 6 of Myeongdong Station (명동역) on Line 4) (#476).
  • Roasted chestnuts and roasted sweet potatoes are now also sold at convenience stores and inside subway stations, and jjinppang and wang-mandu are easy to buy at convenience stores in winter (VisitKorea, #476). That is the alternative when you cannot find a stall.
  • Hotteok filling contains nuts, and gyeran-ppang has a whole egg in it. Whether the batter contains milk or eggs is not on the official pages, so if you have an allergy, ask before you buy (#476 🥗).

🥣 Dongji Red Bean Porridge — December 22, 2026

  • Dongji in 2026 is December 22 (Tuesday) at 05:50 (Korea Astronomy and Space Science Institute, Astronomy and Space Knowledge Information 2026 calendar data), while the official announcement comes in the Wollyeok Yohang official almanac (월력요항). It is the day with the longest night; people believed that eating red bean porridge made you a year older, and they sprinkled it on the gate to ward off bad luck (National Folk Museum of Korea, #476).
  • Red bean porridge specialty shops are not open only on Dongji. Sindangdong Cheon Patjuk (신당동천팥죽) in Seoul (10:30–20:00, closed Sundays), Yetnal Patjukjip (옛날팥죽집) in Jeonju (10:00–20:00, open year-round) and Munhori Patjuk (문호리팥죽) in Yangpyeong (11:00–19:20, closed Mondays) are among five places whose addresses and hours are in #476, and the price is not posted at any of the five.
  • Whether the porridge broth uses meat stock is not on the shops' official pages. If you are vegetarian, ask before you order (#476 🥗).

🍗 Samgyetang and Health Foods — Often Eaten on Boknal, but Old-School Shops Are Marked Open Year-Round

  • Samgyetang is a soup of a young chicken stuffed with ginseng, jujubes, glutinous rice and nuts (VisitKorea, #827) and is known as a summer Boknal food, but Tosokchon Samgyetang (토속촌 삼계탕) in Jongno is posted as 10:00–22:00 (last order 21:00) and open year-round, and Gobong Samgyetang (고봉삼계탕) and Baekje Samgyetang (백제삼계탕) in Myeongdong are posted as daily 09:00–22:00 (#827, confirmed on VisitKorea 2026-10). The price is not posted at any of the three. For nut allergies, see 🥗 below.
  • For warming up with hanbang tea, see #681 Hanbang tea and the herbal medicine market — Seoul Hanbang Promotion Center and Gyeongdong Market are in one guide.

🧧 Seollal Holiday — February 6–9, 2027 (Provisional), Check the Closing Notes First

  • Seollal (설날) in 2027 is Sunday, February 7, and the holiday runs from Saturday, February 6 to Monday, February 8, with Tuesday, February 9 shown as a substitute holiday (Korea Astronomy and Space Science Institute 2027 calendar data). However, that page states that it is data from before the Wollyeok Yohang official almanac (월력요항) was officially announced through the Korea AeroSpace Administration, so these are provisional values — check the public holidays again right before you travel.
  • Some shops in the detail guides are marked closed for Seollal and Chuseok: Eunjujeong (closed over the Seollal and Chuseok holidays, #486), Hwangsaengga Kalguksu (closed on the day of Seollal and Chuseok, #480), and Yetnal Patjuk in Seongeup, Jeju (closed Mondays and over the Seollal and Chuseok holidays, #476). If you have picked out an old-school shop for the holiday, look at the closing notes in the detail guide first, and confirm by phone.
  • Seollal food and customs are covered in the #752 Seollal guide.

🥗 Dietary Needs — If You Have an Ingredient to Avoid, Ask About the 'Broth Base', Not the 'Menu'

  • Avoiding pork: What the detail guides confirm as beef-bone and beef broth is seolleongtang and gomtang (#477). Pork gukbap and sundae-guk (순대국) are pork, and kimchi jjigae and budae jjigae often contain pork. Other soups, jjigae and kalguksu have a different base at each shop, so ask "Is it beef broth or pork broth?" Kimchi seasoning mostly uses salted fish or fish sauce or meat stock (#477, #486).
  • Seafood allergy: Fish cake is fish meat, and clam kalguksu (Im Byeong-ju) and bugeo haejangguk are seafood themselves. Most sundubu jjigae (순두부찌개) and dubu jorim (두부조림) contain saeujeot (새우젓, salted shrimp) (#486), and kimchi also uses salted fish or fish sauce, so ask "Is there jeotgal (젓갈) in it?"
  • Vegetarian: No shop in this branch is confirmed fully vegetarian. Tofu jeongol, doenjang jjigae (된장찌개), red bean porridge, hotteok and bungeoppang are candidates, but the stock, jeotgal and batter ingredients are not posted on the official pages, so confirm before you order (#486 lists assuming "tofu jjigae is vegetarian" as a common mistake). For a card to show staff, see #1300 Dietary request manual.
  • Other allergies: Kalguksu and milmyeon (밀면) contain wheat (#480), tofu, doenjang and cheonggukjang (청국장) contain soy (#486), hotteok filling contains nuts, gyeran-ppang contains egg (#476), and samgyetang contains nuts (#827).
  • Halal: The official pages of the shops this guide carries over carry no halal marking. The halal restaurant that all five detail guides list in common is Eid (이드) in Itaewon (Usadan-ro 10-gil 15, Yongsan-gu (용산구 우사단로10길 15), daily 12:00–21:00, recognized by the Korea Muslim Federation — VisitSeoul, #476), and the four halal categories by nationality are in #192 Guide for Southeast Asian Muslim travelers.
  • Price: No shop in any of the detail guides has an official price posted in won. Every shop with a Michelin price symbol is ₩ (the lowest tier) (#477 11 places, #480 12 places, #486 Hwanggeum Kongbat). For street snacks, look at the price board on site, and for restaurants, look at the menu at the entrance.

⚠ 4 Common Mistakes

  1. "Old-school shops are fine to visit in the evening" — Hadongwan ends at 16:00, Bugeoguk-jip ends at 15:00 on weekends, and Hapjeong-ok closes when the 100 servings run out. Go in the morning or at lunch (🍲).
  2. "Bungeoppang and hotteok batter are all pretty much the same" — Hotteok filling contains nuts, and gyeran-ppang has a whole egg in it (VisitKorea). If you have an allergy, ask before you buy (🐟).
  3. "Tofu dishes are vegetarian" — Most sundubu jjigae and dubu jorim contain saeujeot (#486). Ask about the stock and jeotgal (🥗).
  4. "Markets will surely be open over the Seollal holiday" — Some shops are marked closed for Seollal and Chuseok (Eunjujeong, Hwangsaengga, Jeju Yetnal Patjuk). If you visit on February 6–9, 2027 (provisional), look at the closing notes first (🧧).

⚙ 4 FAQs

Q1. Is there a broth shop open at 7 a.m.?

Yes. Cheongjinok in Jongno (from 06:00), Mugyo-dong Bugeoguk-jip behind City Hall (from 07:00) and Hadongwan in Myeongdong (Monday–Saturday from 07:00) are in #486 and #477 with addresses and closing days. If you are near your lodging, search for "haejangguk (해장국)" in a map app and check the opening hours.

Q2. I don't eat pork. Can I still eat gukbap?

Seolleongtang and gomtang are confirmed as beef-bone and beef broth (#477). Pork gukbap and sundae-guk are pork. Haejangguk has a different base at each shop, so ask "Is it beef broth or pork broth?" (🥗). Do not choose by menu name alone.

Q3. How much does bungeoppang cost?

No price is posted on an official page, and it differs from stall to stall. Look at the price board on site, and it is safe to carry a little cash (even among the fixed shops in #476, some are marked as not accepting cards).

Q4. What is Dongji, and what do you eat that day?

It is December 22, 2026, the day with the longest night. There is a custom of eating red bean porridge, and the hours and closing days of the five red bean porridge specialty shops are in #476.
