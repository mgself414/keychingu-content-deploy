From Incheon Airport to Seoul, Follow the Step Sheet Exactly — Express or All-Stop

Subtitle: 3 ways from Incheon Airport to downtown Seoul, fully mastered — the AREX Express (jiktong, 직통) vs. All-Stop (ilban, 일반) decision table (there's a twist if your destination is Hongdae!) · the ticket machine's screen flow · finding your limousine bus boarding point · moving with luggage · 12 survival words

🎯 How to Read This Guide — Screen Notation Rules

This piece reuses the notation set in #1270 (the kiosk-ordering piece) as-is.

  • Buttons on a screen appear in brackets: [ English ]
  • Screen titles and prompts appear in corner brackets: 「Express Train / All Stop Train」
  • Actions you do by hand are bold: insert your card into the slot all the way

This is written so you can move purely by reading machine screens and signage — from the moment you step out of arrivals to the moment you set your bags down at your accommodation in the city.

Method What It Is Covered in This Piece

This piece is centered on what to press in front of the AREX ticket machine specifically — if you've already read #398 for connectivity and currency exchange setup right after landing, or #1249 for the station-by-station AREX guide, this piece picks up from "after that, in front of the machine."

② Express vs. All-Stop — the Decision Table (Here's Where the Twist Comes In)

AREX runs two types of trains on the same line: Express (jiktong, 직통) and All-Stop (ilban, 일반). Going by the name alone, Express sounds like it's always better — but the right answer changes depending on your destination.

Category Express (jiktong, 직통) All-Stop (ilban, 일반)
Stops No stops until Seoul Station 13 stops along the way (Gimpo Airport, Hongik Univ. Station, etc.)
Travel time 43 min 56–60 min
Fare (one-way) About ₩9,500 About ₩9,000
Seating Reserved seat (seat number printed on ticket) Unreserved (stand or sit, like the subway)
Frequency Timetable-based intervals — check ahead As frequent as the subway
Right for you if your destination is Seoul Station, City Hall, Myeongdong Hongdae, Hapjeong, Gongdeok, and other stops along the way

The twist: if your destination is Hongdae, "Express" is actually the longer way around. Because Express doesn't stop until Seoul Station, getting to Hongdae means transferring to the subway there. All-Stop, on the other hand, stops directly at Hongik Univ. Station — so if Hongdae is where you're headed, All-Stop, despite its name, is the direct route.

The rule of thumb is simple: if your accommodation is north of Seoul Station (downtown), take Express; if it's near Hongdae, Hapjeong, or Gongdeok, take All-Stop. If you're still unsure, both options appear side by side on the ticket machine screen — just check the table in ③ and choose on the spot.

③ The AREX Ticket Machine, Master Flow — Screen by Screen

The AREX ticket machines at the Incheon Airport Transportation Center never deviate from the sequence below.

Step What You See on Screen Button / Action to Press Trap
1 「Please Select Language」 Touch the flag icon → [ English ] Some machines start on the Korean screen — don't panic, just find the language button first
2 「Express Train / All Stop Train」 Choose based on your destination per the table in ② Pick wrong here and every screen after this changes
3 Departure/arrival station or ticket screen Set the number of passengers with the [ + ] / [ − ] buttons For Express, your seat gets auto-assigned at this step
4 「gyeolje sudan seontaek (결제 수단 선택) — Select payment method」 [ Card ] or the cash slot Both KRW cash and card work. If a foreign card gets rejected, see #1270 ⑤
5 Ticket printout Take your receipt-style ticket For Express, the seat number is printed on the ticket — check it and keep it

After you get your ticket, tag through the ticket gate (the same way as a transit card) and head down to the platform — that's it. The staffed counter runs the same procedure on your behalf; the options themselves don't change.

④ What Express's "Reserved Seat" Actually Means

Like KTX, Express prints a seat number on your ticket. The seats on the train are numbered too, so just sit in the one that matches. Since it isn't unreserved seating, if you find someone else sitting in your seat, you may need to compare tickets and swap — no need to panic, just match the numbers again.

Also remember that Express runs less frequently than All-Stop. Check the next train's departure time on the platform display first — if the timing looks awkward, taking All-Stop in the meantime is also an option (All-Stop runs frequently, so the wait is short).

⑤ Riding the Limousine Bus — Buying a Ticket and Finding Your Boarding Point

  • Buying a ticket: at the limousine ticket booth (counter) on the arrivals floor, or at a nearby ticket machine. At the booth, a staff member is there — just say your destination. The machine works similarly to the AREX ticket machine: select route → pay.
  • Finding your boarding-point number: every limousine bus route has an assigned boarding-point number. It's shown on the arrivals-floor signage or on the ticket itself — just wait at the stop post marked with that number. If you missed the number, check the route name again at a nearby information desk or the stop's display board.
  • Frequency: 20–40 minutes between buses, depending on the route. The display board shows the next bus's expected arrival time.
  • Advantage: costs more than AREX, but goes straight to a stop near your accommodation with no transfers. This is the better option if you have a lot of luggage or your destination is far from a subway station.

⑥ Moving with Luggage — General Route Notes

  • From arrivals to the Transportation Center (shared by AREX and limousine buses), just follow the elevator/escalator signage. With a large suitcase, always head for the elevator signage instead of the stairs.
  • AREX Express: there's a dedicated large luggage rack inside the train for suitcases. Legroom is tight, so it's easier to load your bag onto the rack before you sit down.
  • AREX All-Stop: it's a subway-type car with no separate luggage rack. Stand your bag by the door or next to your seat, and since there are many stops, stand near your luggage ahead of time so you're not rushed at your stop.
  • Limousine bus: luggage goes in the cargo compartment under the bus, not under your seat. The driver often loads it for you, so remember your arrival stop and collect your luggage when you get off.

⑧ 3 Panic Scenarios — What Actually Happens

1. You boarded Express but your destination is Hongdae — don't panic. Get off at Seoul Station and transfer to the subway. If you'd checked the table in ② ahead of time, this wouldn't have happened in the first place — but if you're already on board, follow the station signage at Seoul Station to the subway transfer corridor.

2. The last-train time is unclear — for both AREX and the limousine bus, the last departure changes by season and day of the week. Checking the platform display board or the ticket counter on the day itself is the most accurate way to confirm it. If you miss the last one, just switch to ⑨ Plan B (taxi).

3. You lost your ticket / the gate won't open — AREX tickets are receipt-style, so reissuing one is a hassle. If the gate won't open, step out of line, go to the staffed window or ticket counter nearby, and show them the situation — they'll let you through in most cases.

⑨ Plan B — Switching to a Taxi

  • You can line up directly at the taxi stand outside Incheon Airport arrivals.
  • If you'd rather call one by app, Kakao T — the call procedure and late-night fare structure are covered in detail in #1273.
  • In the pre-dawn hours after the last train/bus, both AREX and the limousine buses stop running, so a taxi becomes practically the only way to move. If your itinerary has you arriving during this window, it's worth mentally preparing for it in advance.
