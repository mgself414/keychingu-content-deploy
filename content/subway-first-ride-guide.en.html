Riding Korea's Subway for the First Time, Follow the Step Sheet Exactly — Zero Panic at the Ticket Machine

① What This Piece Covers — Not the Route Map, but "What Your Hands Do"

Even memorizing the route map ahead of time doesn't stop plenty of people from freezing in front of the ticket machine screen, in front of the gate, at a fork in the transfer corridor. The reason is simple: route information and procedure information are completely different kinds of knowledge.

  • Which station to transfer at → knowledge of the route itself → #237 Seoul Subway, Fully Mastered
  • Where your finger needs to press once you're there → knowledge of the procedure → this piece

Assuming you've already decided your destination, this is written as one continuous flow — from the ticket machine's first screen to the final moment of getting your deposit back after you get off.

② T-money vs. Single-Journey Ticket — Which One Fits Your Trip

Situation Recommendation Why
Staying 3+ days / planning to ride the subway multiple times T-money transit card Load it once and you never need to buy a ticket again, and transfer discounts apply automatically → for where to buy it and how to top up, see #238 T-money, Fully Mastered
Only here 1–2 days, or riding just once or twice today Single-journey ticket (dansugwon, 단수권) Pay a ₩500 deposit and ride, then feed it into the refund machine when you get off to get it straight back — sections ③–⑥ of this piece are that exact procedure
If you already bought T-money at the airport or downtown Skip ③–④ and start reading from ⑤ Getting Through the Gate

③ The Single-Journey Ticket Machine, Master Flow — Screen by Screen

The blue self-service ticket machines inside every station aren't brand-specific — they all move through the same 6 steps, station to station.

Step What You See on Screen Button / Action to Press Trap
1 First screen, language icons Touch the 🇺🇸 flag → [ English ] Some machines respond slowly to touch — press firmly to make sure it advances
2 「mokjeokjiyeok seontaek (목적지역 선택) — Select destination station」route map / search box Search for the station name in English or tap your destination directly on the route map Both Korean and English station names are shown together — confirm by the English spelling
3 「inwonsu (인원수) — Number of passengers」popup Select the number of adults with [ + ] / [ - ] Child and senior fare buttons are separate — general tourists only need to press the adult button
4 「yogeum hwagin (요금 확인) — Fare confirmation」screen Confirm the amount shown = fare + ₩500 deposit The ₩500 deposit isn't a loss — it's money you get back after you get off. Refund details are in ⑥
5 Payment method screen Insert cash into the slot (some machines also take card) Older machines are cash-only — if card doesn't work, go to the ticket window next door
6 Bottom dispensing slot Take your coin-shaped single-journey ticket This card is both your gate tag and, later, your refund-machine card — lose it and there's no refund

④ Getting Through the Gate — Where to Tag, and What You'll Hear

  • Facing the direction you're entering the gate, lightly tap your single-journey ticket (or T-money) on the round reader on the right side. It's tap, not insert.
  • A normal pass: a short beep + a green arrow, and the gate opens.
  • Insufficient balance or a failed read: a long warning tone ("beeeep") + a red X, and the gate stays shut → recharge or rebuy at the nearby ticket window or machine.
  • The gate closes automatically after one person passes through. If you're with a group, tag one at a time and go through.

⑤ Finding Your Direction & Transferring — Follow the Board, the Color, the Sign

Before you head down to the platform, the one thing you must check is the "bound-for" direction sign.

  • At the stairway entrance and on the platform display board, the direction is shown by the terminal station name as "OO-haeng (OO행)" / "OO Direction." Check that your direction and the terminal station shown are on the same line.
  • Even at the same station, the up and down platforms are often on opposite sides — you only need to confirm that the terminal station name on the board and your destination are on that same line (the direction is shown by the terminal station's name even for a station in the middle of the line).
  • Follow the line's color plus its number for transfers. For example, if you see a sign for "hwanseung (환승) — Transfer" pointing you to "Line 2 (green)," just keep following that color and number's arrows, and a new platform appears at the end of the transfer corridor. Even if the corridor is long, the same colored sign repeats at every fork, so there's no need to worry about losing your way.

⑥ Getting Off → The Deposit Refund Machine — Getting Your ₩500 Back

If you rode on a single-journey ticket, you need to get your deposit back before you exit through the ticket gate once you arrive at your destination.

  • Location: not outside the ticket gate, but in the area just before you pass through it, there's a separate yellow or blue deposit refund machine (a different machine from the ticket machine).
  • Procedure: insert the single-journey ticket into the slot → the refund amount appears on screen → a ₩500 coin comes out of the return slot below.
  • After the refund, the machine keeps the card (it doesn't come back out — that's normal).
  • Can't find the refund machine, or already went through the gate? Most stations have one more refund machine nearby, just outside the ticket gate. At stations without one, show your card to the ticket-window staff and they'll refund it manually.

⑧ 3 Panic Scenarios — What Actually Happens, and How to Handle It

1. You boarded going the wrong direction — get off at the next station and cross to the opposite platform. At most stations you can cross over via stairs or a corridor within the same ticket gate, but at some smaller stations the opposite platform only connects by going back outside the ticket gate. In that case, exiting the gate can charge your fare again — before you exit, check the screen or signage for a "no transfer corridor" notice first, or ask a station staff member by saying "bandae banghyang (반대 방향)" — "wrong direction" — which is the fastest way to sort it out.

2. You got off at the wrong station — just catch the next train on the same platform and go back. If it's within the valid transfer window, there's no extra charge.

3. The gate sounded a warning tone — nine times out of ten, it's janaek bujok (잔액부족) — insufficient balance. Don't panic; recharge or rebuy at the nearby ticket machine or window, then tag again to get through.

⑨ Plan B — When the Machine or Signage Just Won't Cooperate

  • Calling a station staff member: there's always someone in the glass booth near the ticket gate. Say "dowajuseyo (도와주세요)" — "please help me" — and show them the screen or your card, and they'll help almost immediately.
  • The platform safety-door intercom: the red-button intercom mounted on the screen door is for emergencies only (someone caught, a medical emergency). It's worth remembering it isn't for asking directions — for that, go to the station staff booth instead.
  • Showing a screen instead of speaking: type your destination station's name in Korean into Papago or Google Translate, then enter it exactly as shown into the ticket machine's search box — no risk of a typo.
