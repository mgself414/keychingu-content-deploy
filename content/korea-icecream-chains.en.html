Why Ice Cream Chains — Not the Land of 31 Flavors, but the Land of the "Flavor of the Month"

Baskin Robbins (배스킨라빈스) exists everywhere in the world. But Korea's Baskin Robbins is different — with 1,500+ stores nationwide it's one of the brand's top markets globally, flavors invented in Korea like Almond Bon Bon (아몬드봉봉) and Mom Is an Alien (엄마는외계인) top the sales charts, and a new "Flavor of the Month" goes viral on social media every single month. It's that rare case where tourists deliberately seek out a sign they already have at home.

Add Korean-born premium soft serve brands like Softree (소프트리), which crowns its ice cream with honeycomb, and Baekmidang (백미당), which bets everything on the milk itself — and Korea's ice cream chains have become a genre of their own. If bingsu is your goal, #510 is your answer; this guide is about how to choose "the brand of cones and cups."

Brand Profiles — Starting with a One-Line Identity

🩷 Baskin Robbins — Same Sign, Different Country

  • One line: 1,500+ stores nationwide. Korea-only flavors and a cake culture have turned it into a brand entirely different from the original.
  • Signatures (Korea-only): Almond Bon Bon — Korea's best seller. Mom Is an Alien — a triple-chocolate national favorite. Shooting Star (슈팅스타) and Strawberry in Love (사랑에빠진딸기) are Korean down to their very names.
  • What defines it: A new "Flavor of the Month" every month · ice cream cake culture (the birthday standard) · a take-home system from pint (3 flavors) to half gallon (6 flavors) · a size-up promotion on the 31st of each month
  • Price range: Single regular ₩4,000~ (~$3.0) · pint ₩11,000~ (~$8.2)
  • Why visitors love it: "Unknown flavors under a familiar sign" — the same rule as the global exclusive menus in the burger edition (#1264). The Gangnam (강남) flagship Baskin Robbins Brown (배스킨라빈스 브라운) serves 100 flavors and is a pilgrimage site for flavor completionists.

🍯 Softree — The Original Honeycomb Soft Serve

  • One line: The original brand that tops organic-milk soft serve with a whole chunk of honeycomb.
  • Signature: Honey Chip soft serve — the one known on social media as "Korean honey ice cream."
  • What defines it: Small shops in major districts like Hongdae (홍대) and Gangnam · premium pricing · takeout-focused
  • Price range: ₩5,000~7,000 (~$3.7~5.2)
  • Why visitors love it: The destination for travelers who arrived by googling "Korean honey ice cream."

🥛 Baekmidang — Premium Milk, Pure and Simple

  • One line: A milk soft serve specialist created by Namyang Dairy (남양유업) — fewer additives, letting the milk flavor do the talking.
  • Signature: Milk soft serve cone · half-milk, half-chocolate swirl.
  • What defines it: Common along tourist routes like Myeongdong (명동) and department stores · clean hanok-style branding · experiments with steamed bun (호빵) and roasted sweet potato lines in winter
  • Price range: ₩4,000~6,000 (~$3.0~4.5)
  • Why visitors love it: A perfect rest stop on a shopping route (department store basements). The rich milk flavor is especially popular with travelers from Chinese-speaking regions.

🍦 Natuur — Lotte's Premium Scoop

  • One line: Lotte's premium ice cream — positioned as the calm, grown-up flavor compared to Baskin Robbins.
  • Signature: Korean-ingredient flavors like green tea and black sesame.
  • What defines it: Mostly located in department stores and malls · far fewer stores than Baskin Robbins
  • Why visitors love it: "Korean-flavor scoops" like green tea and black sesame, enjoyed in a quiet store.

🏪 The Convenience Store Channel — An Ice Cream Powerhouse Outside the Chains

Store brands aren't the whole story. Convenience store ice creams like Melona (메로나), World Cone (월드콘), and Boongeo Samanco (붕어싸만코) run ₩1,000~2,000 (~$0.7~1.5) and make up half of Korea's ice cream culture — see #1262 (convenience store food roundup) for details and #1261 for choosing between brands.

🎯 Know Before You Order — Universal Basics

  • 🥄 Tasting spoons: Baskin Robbins lets you request sample spoons — just say "tasting spoon" or point at the showcase with your finger.
  • 📏 Size system (Baskin Robbins): Single (1 flavor) → Pint (3 flavors) → Quart (4 flavors) → Family (5 flavors) → Half Gallon (6 flavors). For groups, pint and up gives you better value per scoop.
  • 💳 Payment: Cards work at every brand. Happy Point app discounts require a Korean account — travelers can safely ignore them.
  • 🧊 Dry ice: Orders of a pint or more get free dry ice packing — ask for it if your accommodation is more than an hour away.

3 Situational Picks

Situation 1. "I heard Korean Baskin Robbins is different — what do I order?"

  • A double of Almond Bon Bon + Mom Is an Alien — the combo of Korea's top two sellers. Add the Flavor of the Month if it catches your eye.
  • If you're serious, head to Baskin Robbins Brown in Gangnam (the 100-flavor flagship).

Situation 2. "That honey ice cream I saw on Instagram"

  • Softree Honey Chip. Walking distance in the Hongdae and Gangnam districts — the proper way is to chew the honeycomb whole.

Situation 3. "I'm shopped out, I need something sweet"

  • A Baekmidang milk soft serve cone (Myeongdong · department store basements). If the line is long, switch to Natuur — green tea and black sesame are the alternative.

5 Usage Tips — Get 200% Out of Korea's Ice Cream Chains

Tip 1. At Baskin Robbins, check the "Flavor of the Month" first

Few countries launch new flavors every month — start with this month's flavor on the POP display by the showcase. Viral flavors sell out fast.

Tip 2. The 31st of every month is size-up day

Baskin Robbins 31 Day — a promotion on the 31st of each month (size-ups and more; terms vary). If your trip includes a 31st, remember it.

Tip 3. Ice cream cakes are content even if you're just window shopping

The standard of Korean birthday culture — character collab cake showcases take up half of every Baskin Robbins store. You can also pack a mini cake instead of a pint.

Tip 4. Always work in at least one Korean flavor

Natuur's black sesame and green tea, Baskin Robbins' seasonal Korean dessert collabs — the rule is "vanilla exists everywhere."

Tip 5. Bundle convenience stores and chains as a set

Chain scoops by day, a Melona from the convenience store near your accommodation by night (#1262) — bouncing between the ₩1,000 tier and the ₩5,000 tier is the Korean way to travel through ice cream.

FAQ 6 — Ice Cream Chain Questions Foreigners Actually Ask

Q1. What's different about Korean Baskin Robbins?

  • More than half the flavors are developed in Korea. Almond Bon Bon and Mom Is an Alien don't exist in other countries — the store count, cake culture, and monthly new-flavor cycle are all unique too.

Q2. What is Almond Bon Bon?

  • A vanilla base + almonds + malted chocolate balls — Korea's best-selling flavor. "Bon Bon" is the signature naming of Korean Baskin Robbins.

Q3. Are tastings free?

  • Yes. Just ask for a tasting spoon — sampling three or four flavors without anyone batting an eye is the standard culture.

Q4. Why isn't Sulbing here?

  • Sulbing is a bingsu chain, so it's covered in #510 (the complete guide to bingsu in Seoul), not here — if snowflake bingsu is your goal, that's your answer.

Q5. Do Koreans eat ice cream in winter?

  • They do — Korea has a "winter ice cream" culture (a Melona in a heated room). Chain stores stay open year-round too.

Q6. Can I pack it and take it back to my accommodation?

  • For a pint or more, dry ice packing is standard — it holds easily for 1~2 hours. Tell the counter your travel time and they'll pack accordingly.
