🎯 How to Use This Guide

This guide gathers places to visit in Yongsan-gu (용산구), Seoul. They are large museums such as the National Museum of Korea (국립중앙박물관) and the War Memorial of Korea (전쟁기념관), parks opened on the site of a former base, Nodeul Island (노들섬) in the middle of Hangang Bridge (한강대교), Hyochang Park (효창공원) with its graves of independence activists, and the streets of Itaewon (이태원) and Haebangchon (해방촌) below Namsan (남산). The museums and parks are registered as free to enter. Paid special exhibitions at the National Museum of Korea are excluded.

Many places close on Mondays (War Memorial of Korea, Yongsan History Museum, Yongsan Park Partially Opened Site, Yongsan Children's Garden and Nodeul Island). The National Museum of Korea is open on many Mondays (the first Monday of March, June, September and December is a regular closure day) and stays open until 21:00 on Wednesdays and Saturdays. Ichon Hangang Park (이촌한강공원) is in the #706 Hangang Parks Guide: 9 Hangang Parks, Nanji, Mangwon and Ichon on the North Bank, Gangseo, Yanghwa, Banpo, Jamwon, Jamsil and Gwangnaru on the South Bank.

The operating details are copied from values registered in the Korea Tourism Organization tourism data (TourAPI). This guide covers only places registered in the Korea Tourism Organization tourism data.

📍 Museums — National Museum of Korea, War Memorial of Korea and Yongsan History Museum

The National Museum of Korea (국립중앙박물관) [Seobinggo-ro 137 (서빙고로 137), Yongsan-gu (용산구), ☎ 02-2077-9000] is a museum that moved to Yongsan and opened in 2005. It has 7 permanent exhibition halls divided by era and theme, a special exhibition hall and a children's museum, and it displays 9,884 artifacts in the permanent exhibition halls. The exhibits are changed periodically.

  • Opening hours: Indoor galleries Monday, Tuesday, Thursday, Friday and Sunday 09:30~17:30 (last entry 17:00), Wednesday and Saturday 09:30~21:00 (last entry 20:30). The outdoor exhibition area (garden) is 07:00~22:00.
  • Closed days: January 1, Lunar New Year and Chuseok days. Separately, the first Monday of March, June, September and December is registered as a "regular closure day".
  • Fee: Free. Paid special exhibitions are excluded. Parking: 900 won for the first 30 minutes for a passenger car, then 300 won every 10 minutes.

The National Museum of Korea Children's Museum (국립중앙박물관 어린이박물관) [same address, ☎ 02-2077-9647] is a hands-on museum where children can touch replicas made in the same shape and size as artifacts in the permanent exhibition halls.

  • Opening hours: 10:00~17:50. Closed days: January 1, Lunar New Year and Chuseok holidays, days set by the museum director. Fee: Free.

The War Memorial of Korea (전쟁기념관) [Itaewon-ro 29 (이태원로 29), Yongsan-gu (용산구), ☎ 02-709-3114] is a memorial hall built on June 10, 1994. It displays about 10,000 of its more than 33,000 holdings. Indoors there are six exhibition rooms: the Memorial Hall for Patriotic Dead (호국추모실), the War History Room (전쟁역사실), the Korean War Room (6·25전쟁실), the Overseas Dispatch Room (해외파병실), the Armed Forces Development Room (국군발전실) and the Large Equipment Room (대형장비실). Outdoors there are large weapons such as K-1 tanks, howitzers, missiles, helicopters and transport aircraft, as well as the Gwanggaeto the Great Stele (광개토대왕릉비), the Statue of Brothers (형제의 상) and the Peace Clock Tower (평화의 시계탑). The corridors on both sides have the Memorial Wall of the Fallen, which carries the names of more than 200,000 fallen soldiers of the Korean and UN forces.

  • Opening hours: 09:30~18:00 (last entry 17:00). Closed days: Every Monday.
  • Fee: Free. Visit time: About 2 hours. Parking: Small cars 4,000 won for up to 2 hours, then 1,500 won every 30 minutes.
  • Guided tours: Group visits and exhibition tours can be booked on the website, according to the registration.

The Yongsan History Museum (용산역사박물관) [Hangang-daero 14-gil 35-29 (한강대로14길 35-29), Yongsan-gu (용산구), ☎ 02-2199-4620] was reborn in March 2022 as a museum specializing in local history. The building is a railway hospital built during the Japanese colonial period, and the hospital building has been preserved and restored for use. It has exhibition spaces by era, a hands-on corner and a rooftop garden.

  • Opening hours: 10:00~18:00 (last entry 17:30). Closed days: Every Monday, January 1, Lunar New Year and Chuseok days. Fee: Free.

📍 Parks on a Former Base Site — Yongsan Family Park, Yongsan Park Partially Opened Site and Yongsan Children's Garden

Yongsan Family Park (용산가족공원) [Seobinggo-ro 185 (서빙고로 185), Yongsan-gu (용산구), ☎ 02-792-5661] is a park with a wide lawn and a pond. The Seoul city government took over the place where the UN forces and the U.S. Forces Korea Command had been during the Korean War (6·25전쟁) in 1992 and turned it into a park. It has walking paths, outdoor sculptures and exercise facilities.

  • Opening hours: Always open. Closed days: Open year-round. Fee: Free. Parking: Available.

The Yongsan Park Partially Opened Site (용산공원 부분개방부지) [Seobinggo-ro 221 (서빙고로 221), Yongsan-gu (용산구), ☎ 070-4224-1708] is the site of the former U.S. military officers' housing Complex 5. It is where the "Seobinggo" (서빙고), an ice storehouse of the Joseon dynasty, once stood, and after liberation a U.S. military unit moved in and the area is said to have been called "Camp Seobinggo" (캠프 서빙고). Outdoors there are photo spots such as the Deulnae Bomnae Lawn Yard (들내 봄내 잔디마당), a round sign with a red border and the Duruduru Pavilion (두루두루 파빌리온).

  • Opening hours: Tuesday to Sunday 09:00~18:00 (last entry 17:00).
  • Closed days: Every Monday, January 1, Lunar New Year and Chuseok days. If Monday is a public holiday, it closes the next day.
  • Fee: Free. Parking: Available.

Yongsan Children's Garden (용산어린이정원) [Yongsan-dong 5-ga 2-1 (용산동5가 2-1), Yongsan-gu (용산구), ☎ 070-8833-5800] is a newly landscaped part of the Yongsan base. It has Ieum Madang (이음마당), an archive (기록관), a viewing hill (전망언덕), Bobo's House (보보의 집) and a children's environmental ecology education center (어린이 환경생태교육관), plus a cafe and an infirmary. It is registered as open to visit without advance reservation.

  • Opening hours: Tuesday to Friday and Sunday 09:00~18:00 (last entry 17:00), Saturday 09:00~21:00 (last entry 20:00).
  • Closed days: Every Monday, January 1, Lunar New Year and Chuseok days. If Monday is a public holiday or a substitute holiday, it closes the next day.
  • Fee: Free. Parking: Not available.

📍 An Island on the Han River and the Graves of Independence Activists — Nodeul Island and Hyochang Park

Nodeul Island (노들섬) [Yangnyeong-ro 445 (양녕로 445), Yongsan-gu (용산구), ☎ 02-749-4500] is an oval island in the middle of Hangang Bridge (한강대교). Its name is said to come from "Nodol" (노돌), meaning "the stone where herons played". It reopened in September 2019 as a "complex cultural base centered on music".

  • Opening hours: Winter (November to February) Tuesday to Sunday 10:00~20:00. Summer (March to October) Tuesday to Friday 10:00~21:00, weekends and public holidays 10:00~22:00. Outdoor areas are always open, and each facility has its own operating hours.
  • Closed days: Every Monday, January 1, Lunar New Year and Chuseok.
  • Fee: Free. Parking: 1,000 won for the first 30 minutes, then 300 won every 10 minutes, up to 15,000 won per day.

Hyochang Park (효창공원) [Hyochangwon-ro 177-18 (효창원로 177-18), Yongsan-gu (용산구), ☎ Yongsan-gu Office Parks and Greenery Division (용산구청 공원녹지과) 02-2199-7594] is where the remains of independence activists are enshrined. It was originally the site of Hyochangwon (효창원), the tombs of Crown Prince Munhyo (문효세자), the eldest son of King Jeongjo (정조), and others. It became a park during the Japanese colonial period, and in 1944 the tombs were moved to the Seosamneung royal tombs (서삼릉) in Goyang (고양) to build a memorial tower for the spirits of the dead. After liberation, Baekbeom Kim Gu (백범 김구) enshrined here the remains of the Three Patriots (삼의사), Yi Bong-chang (이봉창), Yun Bong-gil (윤봉길) and Baek Jeong-gi (백정기), and made an empty tomb for the patriot Ahn Jung-geun (안중근). In 1948 the remains of Yi Dong-nyeong (이동녕), Cha Ri-seok (차리석) and Jo Seong-hwan (조성환) were enshrined, and in 1949 those of Kim Gu.

  • Opening hours: Always open. Closed days: Open year-round. Fee: Free. Parking: Available.

📍 Itaewon and Haebangchon — Special Tourist Zone, Antique Furniture Street, Usadan-gil, Muslim Street and Haebangchon Village

The Itaewon Special Tourist Zone (이태원 관광특구) [Itaewon-ro 150 (이태원로 150), Yongsan-gu (용산구), ☎ Itaewon Tourist Information Center (이태원 관광안내소) 02-3785-0942] is, according to the Korea Tourism Organization, the first tourist special zone designated by the Seoul city government, in 1997, and a multinational, multicultural area where more than 20,000 foreigners live. It is known for leather goods, discount clothing and accessory shops, and has a World Food Street (세계음식거리) where restaurants run directly by foreigners are gathered.

  • Opening hours and closed days: Vary by shop. Parking: Available.

The Itaewon Antique Furniture Street (이태원 앤틱 가구거리) [Bogwang-ro 109-1 (보광로 109-1), Yongsan-gu (용산구), ☎ Yongsan-gu Office Local Economy Division (용산구청 지역경제과) 02-2199-6784] began in the 1960s when soldiers from nearby U.S. military units returning home put their used furniture up for sale. Besides furniture, clocks, teacups and accessories are popular items, and Western antiques and vintage goods are sold too. It is described as holding the Itaewon Antique Festival (이태원 앤틱 페스티벌) in spring and autumn.

  • Opening hours and closed days: Vary by store. Parking: Not available.

Itaewon Usadan-gil (이태원 우사단길) [Hannam-dong (한남동), Yongsan-gu (용산구), ☎ Itaewon Tourist Information Center (이태원 관광안내소) 02-3785-0942] is a road where restaurants from Pakistan, Turkiye, Egypt, Lebanon, India and other countries and Arabic signs mix with old residential alleys. Young artists and young entrepreneurs began moving in from the early 2010s.

  • Opening hours and closed days: Vary by facility. Parking: Not available.

Itaewon Muslim Street (이태원 이슬람거리) [around Usadan-ro (우사단로), Yongsan-gu (용산구), ☎ Yongsan-gu (용산구) 02-2199-6114] begins when you go up the Bogwang Elementary School (보광초등학교) alley from Exit 3 of Itaewon Station (이태원역) on Subway Line 6. Centered on the Seoul Central Mosque (서울중앙성원), it has an Islamic school, halal butcher shops, Islamic grocery marts, halal restaurants, clothing stores selling hijabs, and Islamic bookstores and libraries.

  • Opening hours and closed days: Vary by business.

Haebangchon Village (해방촌마을) [Sinheung-ro 3-ga-gil 66 (신흥로3가길 66), Yongsan-gu (용산구), ☎ Yongsan Tourist Information Center (용산 관광안내소) 070-7731-7050] formed after liberation in 1945, when refugees, people returning from abroad and people fleeing the war settled on the hillside below Namsan (남산). Narrow alleys and hills remain. In 2021 an inclined elevator was installed at the "108 Steps" (108계단) as an urban regeneration project.

  • Opening hours: Always open. Parking: Available.

⚠ 3 Things to Know Before You Go

  1. Several places close on Mondays — the War Memorial of Korea, Yongsan History Museum, Yongsan Park Partially Opened Site, Yongsan Children's Garden and Nodeul Island are registered as closed on Mondays.
  2. The National Museum of Korea is open until 21:00 on Wednesdays and Saturdays — on other days the indoor galleries close at 17:30.
  3. Parking is not available at Yongsan Children's Garden, the Antique Furniture Street or Usadan-gil — Yongsan Family Park, Yongsan Park Partially Opened Site and Hyochang Park have parking.

⚙ FAQ 2

Q. Which museums can be visited for free?

A. According to the Korea Tourism Organization registration, the National Museum of Korea (paid special exhibitions excluded), the Children's Museum, the War Memorial of Korea and the Yongsan History Museum are all free.

Q. Which places are open in the evening?

A. The indoor galleries of the National Museum of Korea are open until 21:00 on Wednesdays and Saturdays, and the outdoor exhibition area until 22:00. Yongsan Children's Garden is open until 21:00 on Saturdays. Nodeul Island is open until 20:00 Tuesday to Sunday in winter (November to February), until 21:00 Tuesday to Friday in summer (March to October), and until 22:00 on weekends and public holidays, and its outdoor areas are always open.
