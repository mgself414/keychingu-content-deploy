Korea Solo Female Travel Guide: Seoul Night-Walk Safety Services, Women-Only 24-Hour Jjimjilbang, Solo Dining Seats, Selfie Booths and Hanbok Palaces (Official Values, October 2026)

Subtitle: Seoul's Safe Return Scout (안심귀가스카우트) offers free escorts Monday-Friday after 10 p.m. · Seoul Ansimi app (서울 안심이) supports English, Chinese and Japanese (Seoul Metropolitan Government) · 1330 tourist interpretation hotline in Korean, English, Japanese and Chinese · Spa Lei (스파레이), a women-only 24-hour jjimjilbang (찜질방, Korean sauna lounge), first 12 hours ₩16,000 (~$12) (VisitKorea) · Gyeongbokgung Palace (경복궁) is free for hanbok wearers and closed on Tuesdays (Royal Palaces and Tombs Center (궁능유적본부)) · Bukchon tourist hours 10:00-17:00 (VisitSeoul) · Climate Card (기후동행카드) 1-day pass ₩5,000 (~$4) (Seoul Metropolitan Government) — as of 2026-10-28

ISSUE 03 · #460 · 2026-05-26

🎯 How to use this guide

At 11 p.m., as you come out of Exit 4 of Seongsu Station (성수역), the white steam from a 24-hour gamjatang (감자탕, pork bone stew) restaurant drifts into the alley, and a guest who arrives alone skips the line and is shown straight to a counter seat (VisitSeoul). This guide covers the four things women travelling to Korea alone ask about most: night walks and emergencies, solo dining and 24-hour places, selfies, hanbok and palaces, and K-beauty and jjimjilbang. Opening hours, prices and addresses are only the values we read directly on 2026-10-28 from the Seoul Metropolitan Government, the Royal Palaces and Tombs Center (궁능유적본부), VisitKorea, VisitSeoul and each venue's official page, and where a value is not published, we say so. The Seoul safety notice also says "most areas of Seoul are safe for women travelling alone, day and night," but adds that you should avoid dark alleys and parking lots (VisitSeoul, updated 2019) — this guide turns that "how to avoid" into service names and hours.

🛡 Night walks and emergencies: Seoul services and phone numbers

  • Safe Return Scout (안심귀가스카우트, Seoul Metropolitan Government): A free service in which two staff members walk with citizens who are heading home late at night, all the way to their door. Hours are Monday 22:00-24:00 and Tuesday-Friday 22:00-01:00 the next day; in January-February and July-August the hours shorten to Tuesday-Friday 22:00-24:00 and Monday is closed. Booking is 100% advance reservation through the Seoul Ansimi app (서울 안심이) (Seoul Metropolitan Government welfare site, updated 2026-08-24). The districts listed as operating the service are 10: Yongsan (용산), Seongdong (성동), Dongdaemun (동대문), Jungnang (중랑), Seongbuk (성북), Gangseo (강서), Dongjak (동작), Seocho (서초), Songpa (송파) and Gangdong (강동) — Jung-gu (중구), Jongno-gu (종로구) and Mapo-gu (마포구), which include Myeongdong (명동), Jongno (종로) and Hongdae (홍대), are not on the list, so check your lodging's district first. Inquiries: 02-2133-5349
  • Seoul Ansimi app (서울 안심이, Seoul Metropolitan Government): Supports English, Chinese and Japanese. One app holds "Safe Return Monitoring" (귀가모니터링), where you enter a destination, walk, and the district CCTV control center watches over your trip home; "Emergency Report" (긴급신고) for when you feel threatened; and the Scout booking above (reservations possible 24 hours). Free (Seoul Metropolitan Government welfare site, updated 2026-03-13). Download it from the App Store or Google Play by searching for "Seoul Ansimi (서울 안심이)"
  • 1330 tourist interpretation hotline: Dial 1330 inside Korea, or +82-2-1330 from abroad. Korean, English, Japanese and Chinese (each VisitKorea venue page lists "1330 Travel Hotline"). We confirmed 24-hour operation only from the title of a VisitKorea notice ("1330 Offers 24/7 Travel Assistance"); the article body would not open — if you are lost late at night or a taxi fare seems wrong, call here first
  • Tourist Police: They patrol 7 major tourist areas in Seoul, including Myeongdong (명동), Itaewon (이태원) and Dongdaemun (동대문), and help in English, Japanese and Chinese. Ask 1330 and they will tell you where the nearest Tourist Police are (VisitSeoul safety notice, updated 2019)
  • Emergencies: Police 112 · fire and ambulance 119 (VisitSeoul). For hospital, pharmacy and ER routes, see #433 Korea Emergency Map

🍚 Solo dining and 24 hours: places where one is fine

  • Somunnan Seongsu Gamjatang (소문난성수감자탕, Seongsu): 45 Yeonmujang-gil (연무장길), Seongdong-gu, Seoul. Daily 00:00-24:00, open all year. It has solo-only seats, so if you are alone you skip the line and go straight to the counter. 229m from Exit 4 of Seongsu Station (성수역) on Line 2. Phone +82-2-465-6580 (VisitSeoul, updated 2026-08-31). Prices are not published. Gamjatang is a stew boiled from pork spine bones, so if you avoid pork, choose another dish
  • Gwangjang Market Food Alley (광장시장 먹거리골목, Jongno 5-ga): 88 Changgyeonggung-ro (창경궁로), Jongno-gu, Seoul. The food alley runs 09:00-23:00 and operates all year (general shops 09:00-18:00, closed Sundays). Phone +82-2-2267-0291 (VisitKorea). You sit one seat at a time on long benches in front of the stalls, so it is easy to eat alone. Prices differ by stall and are not on the page
  • Idol-favorite restaurants with solo-dining signals confirmed (secondary): Yeongcheon Yeonghwa Cheongdam branch (영천영화 청담점) has 6 "eating alone" (혼밥) entries in Dining Code's visit-purpose tags, yukhoe bibimbap (육회비빔밥, rice bowl with seasoned raw beef) ₩21,000 (~$16) · Aga Gelato (아가젤라또, Seongsu) cup ₩6,000 (~$5) (Dining Code (다이닝코드), secondary, 2026-10-28). For the detailed price list, see #221 K-pop Fan Full Guide
  • 24-hour convenience stores: You can eat lunch boxes, triangle kimbap and cup ramen at the tables inside the store. Prices differ by store and are not listed in this guide
  • Map of restaurants good for sitting alone: #348 Seoul Solo Diner Map

📸 Selfies, hanbok and palaces

  • Seongsu self photo booth: 43-1 Yeonmujang-gil (연무장길), Seongdong-gu, Seoul. 24 hours, open all year. 237m from Exit 4 of Seongsu Station (성수역) on Line 2 (VisitSeoul, updated 2026-08-21) — it is on the same alley as the gamjatang restaurant above, so one night stroll covers both
  • How to use a four-cut booth: Pay at the unmanned booth → choose a frame → shoot to the timer → your chosen shots are printed in two strips, and you download the originals and video by QR code (VisitKorea article, 2025-07). The price per session differs by brand and shop, and we could not open an official price list — around ₩4,000 (~$3) (secondary). Life Four Cuts (인생네컷), Photoism (포토이즘) and Haru Film (하루필름) are common brands
  • Gyeongbokgung Palace (경복궁, Royal Palaces and Tombs Center (궁능유적본부) official): Closed on Tuesdays (if Tuesday is a public holiday it opens and closes the next weekday). Viewing hours are 09:00-17:00 (last entry 16:00) in November-December and January-February, 09:00-18:00 (17:00) in March-May and September-October, and 09:00-18:30 (17:30) in June-August. Adults ₩3,000 (~$2) (foreigners aged 19-64); free for foreigners aged 18 and under and 65 and over. The combined ticket for the 4 Grand Palaces and Jongmyo Shrine (종묘) is ₩6,000 (~$5) (valid for 6 months). Gyeongbokgung Management Office (경복궁관리소) 02-3700-3900
  • Conditions for free entry in hanbok (한복, traditional Korean clothing) (Royal Palaces and Tombs Center (궁능유적본부)): Both traditional hanbok and everyday hanbok count, for Koreans and foreigners alike. But you must wear both a jeogori (저고리, hanbok jacket) and a skirt (or trousers) — wearing only a durumagi (두루마기, hanbok overcoat), a jeogori with jeans, hanbok bottoms with a T-shirt, or a one-piece hanbok dress without a jeogori does not qualify for free entry. A T-shirt-style everyday hanbok jeogori is also not accepted
  • Bukchon Hanok Village (북촌한옥마을): 37 Gyedong-gil (계동길), Jongno-gu, Seoul. Tourist hours are 10:00-17:00 (measured at Bukchon-ro 11-gil (북촌로11길)), and since the November 2024 designation as a special management area, entering the restricted zone is prohibited from 17:00 until 10:00 the next day (VisitSeoul, updated 2026-09-11). Violators are subject to fines (confirmed from a VisitKorea article title; body not read). 516m from Exit 3 of Anguk Station (안국역) on Line 3. Free entry. Inquiries +82-2-2133-1372. Residents live here, so keep your voice low in the alleys
  • For all the photo spots, see #339 Seoul Photo Spots

💄 K-beauty and jjimjilbang

  • Olive Young Myeongdong Town (올리브영 명동타운, official): 53 Myeongdong-gil (명동길), Jung-gu, Seoul. Daily 10:00-22:30 (including weekends and public holidays; may change depending on the store). Floor 1 has skincare, suncare, sheet masks and dermocosmetics; floor 2 has makeup, hair, perfume, body and health foods. On floor 2 there is a tax refund desk with foreign-language guidance, plus free Wi-Fi and a rest area (Global Service Lounge). If you bring your passport, purchases of ₩15,000 (~$11) or more are refunded immediately in the store (excluding hygiene products, shopping bags and dried fruit). Exit 8 of Myeongdong Station (명동역) on Line 4 · Exit 5 of Euljiro 1-ga Station (을지로입구역) on Line 2. There is no restroom inside the store
  • Daiso Myeongdong Station branch (다이소 명동역점, VisitKorea): 134-1 Toegye-ro (퇴계로), Jung-gu, Seoul. 10:00-22:00, closed on Lunar New Year and Chuseok (추석). Cards accepted, tax-free shop with after-purchase refund. Phone 1522-4400. The VisitKorea page does not give the floor, so we do not list it
  • Spa Lei (스파레이, VisitKorea): A women-only jjimjilbang (찜질방) and sauna. 5 Gangnam-daero 107-gil (강남대로107길), Seocho-gu, Seoul (Jamwon-dong (잠원동)). 24 hours. First 12 hours ₩16,000 (~$12) (robe rental included), then ₩1,000 (~$1) per hour. Phone +82-2-545-4002. The nearest station is not on the page. This is the place this guide ranks first for a solo late-night rest
  • Gangbyeon Spaland (강변스파랜드, VisitKorea): Basement floor 2, 45 Guuigangbyeon-ro (구의강변로), Gwangjin-gu, Seoul. 24 hours, open all year. Sauna for adults ₩12,000 (~$9) by day and ₩14,000 (~$11) by night; using the jjimjilbang area costs an extra ₩2,000 (~$2). Phone +82-2-455-3737. The jjimjilbang is mixed-gender, so only the bathhouse is separated by gender
  • We do not list dermatology, hair or personal color here because we could not open official prices — see #346 Seoul K-beauty Map, and for templestay and meditation see #345 Seoul Wellness Map

🥗 Dietary notes

  • Pork: Gamjatang (감자탕, pork spine stew), Gwangjang Market bindaetteok (빈대떡, mung bean pancake) and sundae (순대, Korean blood sausage) are made with pork by default. If you avoid it, picking a dish with no pork (yukhoe bibimbap (육회비빔밥), gelato, bibimbap-type dishes) is faster than asking "Dwaejigogi ppago doenayo? (돼지고기 빼고 되나요)" before ordering
  • Halal and vegetarian: None of the restaurants in this guide carry a halal label. For 12 halal and vegetarian restaurants, see #453 Korea Vegan and Halal Guide, and for request cards to show in restaurants, see #1300 Dietary Request Manual
  • Sense of prices: This guide has no officially published meal prices. Only two were confirmed as secondary: yukhoe bibimbap ₩21,000 (~$16) and a gelato cup ₩6,000 (~$5) (Dining Code (다이닝코드)); check gamjatang and Gwangjang Market prices on site

🧰 Practical tips for solo women: alone, booking, payment, ordering, getting around

  • Alone: Solo-only seats (Seongsu gamjatang), market stalls and convenience stores work right away, while some places for hot pots and grilled meat have 2-3 servings as the basic unit (for example, Suwon Wonjo Galbi (수원원조갈비) in #221 has 3 servings as the basic order unit, Dining Code (다이닝코드) secondary). Alone, soup-rice (gukbap (국밥)), stews, bibimbap and bunsik (분식, Korean snack food) places are the ones that will not turn you away
  • Booking: Safe Return Scout (안심귀가스카우트) takes app advance reservations only. Jjimjilbang (찜질방), markets and photo booths need no booking
  • Payment: Cards are the default, and for market stalls and older restaurants, carry a little cash — #527 Korea Payment Systems
  • Ordering: For sentences to use when you are not understood and tips on asking for more banchan (반찬, side dishes) and water, see #1339 Ordering Etiquette
  • Getting around: If you stay 2-3 days, the Climate Card (기후동행카드) short-term pass is convenient — 1-day ₩5,000 (~$4) · 2-day ₩8,000 (~$6) · 3-day ₩10,000 (~$8) · 5-day ₩15,000 (~$11) · 7-day ₩20,000 (~$15). You buy a physical card and load the short-term pass at a charger in a station. It works on the Seoul subway, city buses licensed in Seoul, village buses, night buses and Ttareungi (따릉이, Seoul public bikes), but not on the Shinbundang Line, intercity buses or airport buses (Seoul Metropolitan Government transport site, updated 2024-08-07). For a comparison of the 3 cards, see #1298 Choosing Among 3 Transit Cards
  • One-line night route: Olive Young Myeongdong (올리브영 명동) (~22:30) → Line 2 to Seongsu Station (성수역) → self photo booth (24 hours) → gamjatang solo seat (24 hours) → if your lodging is in an operating district such as Seocho or Songpa, book the Safe Return Scout (안심귀가스카우트); if not, rest at Spa Lei (스파레이, women-only, 24 hours)

⚠ 4 common mistakes

1. "Just wearing a hanbok gets me into the palace free" — you must wear both a jeogori (저고리) and a skirt (trousers). A durumagi (두루마기) alone, a jeogori with jeans, or a one-piece hanbok dress alone is not free (Royal Palaces and Tombs Center (궁능유적본부)). And Gyeongbokgung (경복궁) is closed on Tuesdays

2. "Bukchon is open at night too" — tourist hours are 10:00-17:00, and entering the restricted zone after 17:00 is subject to fines (VisitSeoul and VisitKorea). For night photos, go outside the palace walls, or to Seongsu or Myeongdong

3. "The Safe Return Scout comes right away if I call" — it is 100% advance booking through the Ansimi app (안심이), Monday-Friday after 10 p.m., and only in the 10 operating districts (Seoul Metropolitan Government). Check the district before you book your lodging

4. "Every jjimjilbang is women-only" — the bathhouse is separated by gender, but the jjimjilbang area where you wear the sauna clothes is usually mixed-gender. If you want women-only, choose Spa Lei (스파레이, Jamwon-dong (잠원동), VisitKorea)
