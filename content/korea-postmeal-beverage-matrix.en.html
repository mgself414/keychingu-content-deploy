Korean Meal-Ending Beverages (마무리 음료) 6-Type Overview — From Sik-hye & Sik-hae to Sujeong-gwa, Dong-dong-ju, Maesil-cha & Haejang-su: The Complete After-Meal & Hangover-Cure Drink Guide for Foreign Visitors

Subtitle: A foreign visitor's guide to Korea's 6 meal-ending beverages — Sik-hye, Sik-hae, Sujeong-gwa, Dong-dong-ju, Maesil-cha, Haejang-su — plus the final drink of a hanjeongsik (한정식, Korean course-meal) course and the next-day hangover cure

ISSUE 03 · #870 · 2026-06-09

🎯 Why a Korean Meal-Ending Beverage Overview

The foreign visitor's pain point with Korean beverages = "I only know meal drinks and soju — I have no idea what to drink after the meal." Korean meal-ending beverages = the final drink of a hanjeongsik course + a home-style dessert drink + a hangover-cure signature. Even under the same label "Korean traditional beverage," Sik-hye (식혜, sweet · the #1 hanjeongsik finale) · Sik-hae (식해, fermented fish · a Gangwon (강원) tradition) · Sujeong-gwa (수정과, cinnamon, ginger & dried persimmon) · Dong-dong-ju (동동주, a meal-time rice wine that doubles as a drink) · Maesil-cha (매실차, hangover relief & digestion) · Haejang-su (해장수, part of the bean-sprout hangover-soup cluster) each play a different role for a different type of fan. What sets this guide apart from the Traditional Beverage cluster (#840) = the "after-meal finishing drink" angle (the last drink of a hanjeongsik or home-style meal, plus the next-day hangover cure). Foreign visitors typically don't know that (a) Sik-hye and Sik-hae are completely different drinks, (b) hanjeongsik courses end with a specific finishing drink, (c) Dong-dong-ju is alcoholic even though it looks like a soft drink, or (d) Haejang-su belongs to the hangover-cure cluster. This guide covers a 6-type overview + finishing drinks + hangover cures.

🍹 6-Type Deep Dive

🍚 Sik-hye (식혜) — Hanjeongsik Finale #1

  • Signature: the #1 finishing drink of a hanjeongsik course · sweet · traditional
  • Ingredients: rice + malted barley (엿기름) + sugar (with rice grains floating in it)
  • Foreigner-friendliness: ★★★★★ (#1 starter pick; sweet and globally approachable)
  • Recommended venues:
  • Hanjeongsik restaurants (#837) — the #1 course finale
  • Shilla Hotel's La Yeon (라연), Samcheonggak (삼청각), Insadong (인사동) — the traditional spots
  • Supermarkets and convenience stores (편의점, Korea's ubiquitous minimarts) — canned or bottled
  • Temperature: served cold (with ice)
  • Price: free with a hanjeongsik course / supermarket = ₩2,000–5,000 (~$1–4) per can

🐟 Sik-hae (식해) — Gangwon Tradition

  • Signature: a Gangwon (강원) tradition — fermented fish + rice, a fan-favorite dish (not to be confused with Sik-hye, despite the similar name)
  • Ingredients: pollock, sailfin sandfish or flounder + rice, chili flakes & radish, fermented
  • Foreigner-friendliness: ★★★ (strong fermented flavor; for adventurous fermentation fans)
  • Recommended venues: nofo (노포, restaurants in business 30+ years) and markets in Gangwon
  • Season: year-round · a Gangwon classic
  • Price: market = ₩10,000–25,000 (~$7–19) per pack
  • Note: Sik-hye = a sweet rice drink / Sik-hae = a fermented side dish, a Gangwon fan favorite — completely different foods

🍂 Sujeong-gwa (수정과) — Winter Classic

  • Signature: a winter classic — cinnamon and ginger punch with dried persimmon and pine nuts
  • Ingredients: cinnamon & ginger + dried persimmon + pine nuts (as garnish)
  • Foreigner-friendliness: ★★★★ (a winter classic; spiced yet sweet)
  • Recommended venues: hanjeongsik courses · traditional tea houses · MZ cafes · supermarkets
  • Temperature: served cold (summer) or warm (winter)
  • Season: the #1 winter pick, but available year-round
  • Price: free with a hanjeongsik course / supermarket = ₩3,000–7,000 (~$2–5) per can

🍶 Dong-dong-ju (동동주) — Traditional Liquor, Meal-Time Drink

  • Signature: a meal-time liquor — a traditional makgeolli-type drink, a cloudy fermented rice liquor
  • Ingredients: rice + nuruk (fermentation starter), fermented (rice grains float on top — "dong-dong" means "floating")
  • Foreigner-friendliness: ★★★★ (rides the MZ makgeolli trend; traditional)
  • Recommended venues: hanjeongsik restaurants · traditional liquor bars · the MZ makgeolli scene
  • Season: year-round · a meal-time drink
  • Alcohol content: 6–8%
  • Price: ₩5,000–15,000 (~$4–11) per bottle

🌿 Maesil-cha (매실차) — Hangover Relief & Digestion

  • Signature: a hangover-relief and digestive drink; a traditional Korean herbal-style beverage
  • Ingredients: plum syrup + water (served hot or cold)
  • Foreigner-friendliness: ★★★★ (digestion and hangover relief translate well globally)
  • Recommended venues: home-style meals · traditional tea houses · supermarkets · convenience stores
  • Temperature: warm (for digestion) or cold (summer)
  • Season: year-round
  • Price: free with a home-style meal / supermarket = ₩2,000–5,000 (~$1–4)

🍵 Haejang-su·Kongnamul-guk (해장수·콩나물 국물) — The Morning-After Cure

  • Signature: crosses into the hangover-cure cluster — a bean-sprout broth for the morning after
  • Ingredients: bean sprouts + radish, scallion, chili & soy sauce
  • Foreigner-friendliness: ★★★ (a hangover-cure regulars' favorite)
  • Recommended venues: 24-hour Bonjuk (본죽, a porridge chain) · gukbap (국밥, rice-and-soup) restaurants · the hangover-cure cluster
  • Season: the early morning after drinking · hangover relief
  • Price: home-style meals and gukbap restaurants = ₩8,000–15,000 (~$6–11)
  • See also: the Gukbap (국밥) cluster (#807)

🍽 Hanjeongsik Finale, Home-Style Dessert & Hangover-Cure Signatures

Hanjeongsik Course Finale (#837)

  • The finale of the 6-course meal = Sik-hye + Sujeong-gwa (the dessert course)
  • Shilla Hotel's La Yeon (라연), Samcheonggak (삼청각), Insadong (인사동) — the traditional spots
  • Included free

Home-Style Dessert Drinks

  • Sik-hye and Maesil-cha = free home-style dessert drinks
  • Baekban-jip (백반집, home-style set-meal diners) and MZ Korean restaurants — great value

Next-Day Hangover-Cure Cluster

  • Maesil-cha + Haejang-su = the classic next-day hangover-cure combo
  • 24-hour Bonjuk and gukbap restaurants — hangover-cure regulars' spots

Traditional Liquor Bars & the MZ Makgeolli Scene

  • Dong-dong-ju and makgeolli (막걸리) = the traditional liquor cluster (#840)
  • Gwangjang Market (광장시장) and Insadong (인사동) traditional liquor bars — the classic spots

⚠ 5 Common Mistakes Foreign Visitors Make

1. "Sik-hye and Sik-hae are the same thing": Sik-hye = a sweet rice drink (rice + malted barley) / Sik-hae = a fermented fish side dish (a Gangwon tradition) — completely different foods.

2. "Dong-dong-ju is a regular soft drink": Dong-dong-ju = a 6–8% alcohol liquor (a meal-time drink).

3. "Sujeong-gwa is a tea": Sujeong-gwa = a cinnamon-ginger-persimmon punch (not a tea — a fruit-based drink).

4. "Hanjeongsik drinks cost extra": the hanjeongsik finishing drink = included free (Sik-hye or Sujeong-gwa).

5. "Maesil-cha is just a regular tea": Maesil-cha = a medicinal-style drink (a favorite for digestion and hangover relief).

⚙ 8 FAQs

1. Top starter picks for foreign visitors: Sik-hye (the hanjeongsik finale) + Sujeong-gwa (a winter classic) → Maesil-cha (for digestion).

2. Instagram & vlog: the #1 hanjeongsik Sik-hye, Sujeong-gwa with dried persimmon garnish, and traditional Dong-dong-ju.

3. MZ trend: Sik-hye fusion drinks at MZ cafes, Dong-dong-ju, and sparkling plum drinks.

4. Digestion & hangover relief: Maesil-cha + Haejang-su.

5. Allergies: declare nut allergies (pine nuts) in advance.

6. Alcohol: Dong-dong-ju = 6–8% — not for pregnant women or drivers.

7. Payment & tipping: hanjeongsik and home-style meals = card OK / no tipping.

8. VAT refund: food and beverages are generally not eligible for refund.

⚠ Medical, Safety & Dietary Disclaimer

Meal-ending beverages are everyday foods. Pregnant women and children = Sik-hye, Sujeong-gwa and Maesil-cha are OK (Dong-dong-ju = not OK, it's alcoholic). Declare allergies (nuts, ginger) in advance. If food poisoning is suspected, call ☎ 1339 (medical, English available). This guide is general information, not medical advice.

Dietary restriction fallback:

  • Halal & Kosher: Sik-hye, Sujeong-gwa, Maesil-cha, Haejang-su and Sik-hae (depending on region) = OK / Dong-dong-ju = not OK, contains alcohol.
  • Vegetarian & vegan: 5 of the 6 types = OK / Sik-hae = fermented fish (not vegetarian).
  • Gluten-free: all 6 types = OK (use tamari as a substitute where soy sauce is used).
