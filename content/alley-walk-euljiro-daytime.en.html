Alley Walk ⑦ Euljiro (을지로), Daytime — The Street of Makers: Tools, Lighting, and Bangsan Market

Subtitle: Euljiro's (을지로) night belongs to Nogari Alley (노가리 골목) and Hipjiro (힙지로) — that's #1309. This piece comes before all that: daytime Euljiro — from Tool Street (공구거리), where the materials that rebuilt postwar Seoul still gather, to Lighting Street (조명거리), where about 210 lighting shops light up 600 meters, to Bangsan Market (방산시장), the wholesale alley for paper and packaging. About 2 hours from Euljiro 4-ga Station (을지로4가역), walked while the shutters are still up — the "Euljiro of the people who make things." Seoul Alley Walks, Part 7.

ISSUE · #1346 · 2026-09-03

🎯 The Frame — Euljiro (을지로) Is a Different Neighborhood by Day Than by Night

The Euljiro tourists know is an evening one — the outdoor tables (야장) of Nogari Alley (노가리 골목), the bars of Hipjiro (힙지로). But what actually built this street was daytime work. After the Korean War flattened Seoul, lumber, hardware, paint, wallpaper, and tools naturally gathered in this area to rebuild it, and the street that took the name "Euljiro" in 1946 has been Seoul's materials warehouse ever since. This course walks that daytime Euljiro, during the hours when the shop shutters are up. The night course is handed off to #1309 — it isn't covered here at all.

  • Total time: about 2 hours · Best time: start on a weekday between 10 AM and 3 PM — the wholesale alleys start closing in the late afternoon, and most are closed on weekends and public holidays
  • Incline: low — flat ground. The alleys form a grid, so there's no risk of getting lost.

🚶 The Route — In Walking Order

① Euljiro 4-ga Station (을지로4가역) Exit 6 → the Mouth of Lighting Street (조명거리) (about a 3-minute walk)

  • Come up to street level and an alley begins where every display window is lit with chandeliers, LEDs, and pendant lights.

② Lighting Street (조명거리) — Euljiro 3-ga (을지로3가) to 4-ga (을지로4가), about 600m (about an 8-minute walk)

  • 📖 Story 1 — The Street That Sold Korea's Light: Euljiro's Lighting Street was the center of Korean lighting in the 1970s–80s — interior decorators and builders came here to buy lighting wholesale, and about 210 lighting shops still line the street today. It's a street designated as Seoul Future Heritage (서울미래유산). Even with nothing to buy, the display windows alone are worth the walk.
  • Take photos from outside the display windows — the shop interiors are active wholesale workplaces.

③ Lighting Street (조명거리) → Bangsan Market (방산시장) (about a 7-minute walk)

  • A wholesale market for paper, packaging materials, printing supplies, and baking ingredients. From wedding favor boxes to cake boxes — Korea's "packaging" comes from here. The name Bangsan (芳山, "fragrant mountain") is said to be an ironic reversal — a nod to the stench that once hung over this area in the Joseon dynasty, flipped into a character meaning "fragrance."
  • Many shops sell in small quantities, so travelers can buy too — Korean-style wrapping paper and ribbon make a light, distinctive souvenir.

④ Bangsan Market (방산시장) → Tool Street (공구거리) along Cheonggyecheon (청계천) (about an 8-minute walk)

  • 📖 Story 2 — A Market Built by War: A tool market took shape along Cheonggyecheon right after the Korean War. Street stalls that had dealt in military surplus goods settled in and hardened into a tool district after Cheonggyecheon was covered over in 1961 — from a single screw to industrial machine tools, this is the alley people mean when they say "if you can't find it here, it doesn't exist in Seoul."
  • Between alleys, the route continues into the tool and parts shops on the lower level of Sewoon Sangga (세운상가). Sewoon Sangga's own story belongs to #1309 — here, it's just a pass-through.

⑤ Tool Street (공구거리) → Euljiro's Tile and Sanitary Ware Alley (타일·도기 골목) (about a 10-minute walk)

  • The last piece of Euljiro's specialty-street cluster — an alley of tile and sanitary-ware shops. A trace of the postwar reconstruction era, when lighting, tile, and furniture markets all flourished together. The tile displays, lined up like showrooms, make for an unexpectedly good photo spot.
  • Finish: from here, head back to Euljiro 3-ga Station (을지로3가역), or, if it's turned evening, hand the baton naturally to Nogari-golmok (노가리골목) from the #1309 course.

🙏 Alley Manners — The Core of This Route

  • The customers in these alleys are businesses. Don't take photos in front of a counter mid-negotiation, and give way to forklifts and hand carts.
  • If you're only browsing, don't step inside the shop — the displays are visible enough from outside.
  • Many wholesale shops pull their shutters down at lunchtime (12–1 PM) — treat that hour as time to take in the alley's layout instead.

💡 Where You Might Get Thrown Off

  • This course doesn't work on weekends or public holidays — the wholesale alleys are closed. Weekdays only.
  • Search "Lighting Street (조명거리)," "Bangsan Market (방산시장)," and "Tool Street (공구거리)" separately in your map app and drop three pins — that's what gives you your bearings in the grid of alleys.
  • You can start anywhere between Euljiro 3-ga Station (을지로3가역) and Euljiro 4-ga Station (을지로4가역), but for this course, Euljiro 4-ga Station (을지로4가역) Exit 6 is closest to the mouth of Lighting Street (조명거리).
